# Real Estate & Construction — Prospecting Guide

## 1. Industry Overview

Real estate and construction together represent a **$15 trillion global industry** characterized by:
- **Local regulation** — every jurisdiction has zoning, building codes, licensing
- **Relationship-driven** — decades-long relationships between developers, brokers, contractors
- **Slow digital adoption** — many firms still run on Excel + email; construction is 2nd least digitized industry
- **Cyclical** — interest rate sensitive; boom/bust cycles; 7-10 year typical cycle
- **Project-based** — revenue is lumpy; project pipeline matters more than ARR
- **Material cost volatility** — lumber, steel, copper prices swing wildly; 20-40% annual variation
- **Labor shortage** — 650K+ construction worker shortage in US; aging workforce; immigration dependency

### 2.1 Commercial Real Estate (CRE)

**Market Shape**
- Global CRE market: ~$35T in total value; US CRE ~$20T; transaction volume $800B-$1.2T annually (varies by cycle)
- Market structure: Institutional investors (pension funds, REITs, sovereign wealth) own 40%+; private equity 20%; family offices 15%; remainder fragmented
- Property types: Office (declining — 20% vacancy post-COVID), industrial/logistics (booming — 4% vacancy), multifamily (strong — 5% vacancy), retail (polarizing), hospitality (recovering), life science (niche growth)
- REITs: ~$1.5T market cap globally; 200+ US REITs; largest: Prologis, American Tower, Equinix, Public Storage
- Distress: $1.5T+ in US CRE debt maturing 2024-2026; office sector most at risk; opportunity for workout/distressed investors

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Rental income (primary — 70-80%), tenant reimbursements (10-15%), parking/ancillary (5-10%), management fees
- Margins: NOI margin 50-70% (varies by property type); cap rate 4-8% (varies by market and type); levered IRR target 12-18%
- Valuation: Income approach (NOI / cap rate = value); sales comparison; cost approach; DCF for institutional
- Leverage: LTV 50-75%; DSCR 1.25x+ required; interest rate sensitivity is existential
- Key metric: NOI, occupancy rate, tenant retention, cap rate, rental rate growth, same-store NOI growth

**Tech Stack**
- Property management: Yardi (dominant — 40%+ market share), MRI Software, RealPage (multifamily), AppFolio (small-mid), Buildium (small residential)
- Leasing: VTS (dominant for office/industrial), CoStar (data + leasing), CompStak (comp data), Reonomy (ownership)
- Accounting: Yardi, MRI, Sage Intacct, QuickBooks (small firms), JD Edwards (enterprise)
- CRM: Salesforce, HubSpot (brokerage), VTS (owner/landlord)
- Market intelligence: CoStar (dominant — $30B+ company), CBRE data, JLL data, Reonomy
- Tenant experience: HqO, Lane, Equiem, Zoom (common area tech)
- Energy/sustainability: Measurabl, EnergyStar, GridPoint

**Decision-Making**
- Asset Manager: Portfolio strategy; capital allocation; technology investment for asset performance
- Property Manager: Day-to-day operations technology; tenant satisfaction; building systems
- Head of Leasing: Leasing technology; market data; virtual tours; CRM
- CIO/CTO: Enterprise IT strategy; data platform; cybersecurity
- CFO: Budget approval; ROI requirements; capital vs. operating expense
- Investment committee: Major technology CapEx; tied to property acquisition/disposition
- Brokerage teams: Independent contractors; technology adoption varies widely

**Budget Cycles**
- Annual budget: Q3-Q4 planning for following year; property-level budgets consolidated to portfolio level
- CapEx vs OpEx: Property improvements are CapEx (5-10 year depreciation); technology can be either
- Tenant improvement (TI) budgets: Per-lease; often $20-100/sf; timing tied to lease execution
- Acquisition integration: Technology budget included in acquisition underwriting; post-close deployment 6-12 months
- Refinancing triggers: Pre-refi property improvements; technology for energy efficiency (green financing)

**Regulatory**
- Zoning: Municipal; determines use, density, height, setbacks; rezoning process 6-24 months
- Building codes: IBC (International Building Code) + local amendments; energy codes (ASHRAE 90.1, IECC)
- ADA/accessibility: Commercial properties must comply; ongoing litigation risk; website accessibility
- Environmental: Phase I/II ESAs; CERCLA/Superfund; brownfield; wetlands; NEPA for federal actions
- Fair Housing Act: Prohibits discrimination in housing; applies to multifamily; advertising restrictions
- REIT regulations: 75%+ income from real estate; 90%+ taxable income distribution; asset and income tests
- Local rent control: Growing in major metros; impacts revenue projections; technology for compliance
- ESG/energy disclosure: Local Law 97 (NYC), BERDO (Boston), benchmarking ordinances; growing mandates
- SEC climate disclosure: Public REITs must disclose climate risk; ESG technology demand

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- Office vacancy: 20%+ national average (post-COVID); $50-100/sf rent loss; tenant improvement costs rising; flight-to-quality
- Interest rate pressure: 300-400 bps increase since 2022; refinancing at higher rates erodes returns; $1.5T+ debt maturing 2024-2026
- Data fragmentation: Average institutional owner uses 8-15 property management systems (legacy of acquisition); data consolidation is massive project
- Tenant expectations: COVID raised bar for building experience; smart building tech, air quality, touchless access; 10-20% premium for Class A+
- ESG compliance: Local Law 97 penalties $268/sf for non-compliance (NYC); carbon reporting mandates expanding; ESG technology is urgent
- Maintenance/operations: Aging building stock; deferred maintenance $1T+ in US; capital planning technology needed
- Insurance costs: Property insurance up 20-40% since 2020; climate risk pricing; insurance technology for risk management
- Talent shortage: CRE professionals aging; younger workforce expects modern technology; firms competing with tech for talent

**Buying Triggers**
- Property acquisition: Due diligence technology; property management onboarding; tenant communication
- Refinancing: Property improvement budget; energy efficiency investment; ESG compliance
- Tenant vacancy spike: Leasing technology; marketing automation; virtual tours
- New asset management hire: Technology modernization; data consolidation; reporting improvement
- ESG regulation (Local Law 97, BERDO): Energy monitoring; carbon reporting; sustainability technology
- Building system upgrade: Smart building technology; IoT; access control; energy management
- M&A (portfolio acquisition): System consolidation; data migration; integration
- Insurance renewal: Risk management technology; climate analytics; building resilience
- Lease expiration cluster (3+ major leases within 12 months): Retention technology; tenant experience investment

**Channels**
- Industry events: ICSC (retail), NAIOP (industrial/office), ULI Fall Meeting, CBRE/JLL conferences
- Email: Asset Manager, CIO, Head of Leasing — must reference specific property type and market
- Trade media: CoStar, GlobeSt, Commercial Observer, Real Capital Analytics, The Real Deal
- LinkedIn: CIO, Asset Manager, Head of Leasing — property-type-specific content performs
- Brokerage introductions: CBRE/JLL/Cushman tech teams influence buying decisions
- Advisory firms: Gartner Real Estate, JLL Technologies, CBRE Build

**Vocabulary**
- NOI, cap rate, IRR, cash-on-cash return, equity multiple, DSCR, LTV
- TI (Tenant Improvements), LC (Leasing Commission), rent abatement, base year, CAM (Common Area Maintenance)
- NNN (Triple Net), gross lease, modified gross, percentage rent, escalation
- Class A/B/C, trophy, value-add, core, core-plus, opportunistic
- Same-store NOI, rent roll, occupancy, vacancy, absorption, deliveries
- Phase I/II, ESA, CERCLA, brownfield, LEED, ENERGY STAR, WELL, Fitwel
- LOI (Letter of Intent), PSA (Purchase and Sale Agreement), due diligence, closing
- Rent concession, free rent, TI allowance, work letter, base building
- ESG, GRESB, carbon intensity, Scope 1/2/3, net zero, embodied carbon

### 2.2 Residential Brokerage

**Market Shape**
- US residential real estate: ~$50T total value; ~5M transactions/year; $2T+ transaction volume
- Market structure: 1.5M+ Realtors; 100K+ brokerages; top 10 brokerages = 25% market share; highly fragmented
- Major players: Compass (tech-forward), Keller Williams (largest agent count), RE/MAX, eXp (cloud-based), Sotheby's (luxury), Anywhere (Coldwell Banker, Century 21, Better Homes & Gardens)
- iBuyer retreat: Zillow Offers, Offerpad, RedfinNow — mostly exited or scaled back; model proved unprofitable
- Commission pressure: NAR settlement (2024) decoupling buyer/broker commission; industry restructuring; technology efficiency needed

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Commission income (5-6% of sale price, split 50/50 between buy/sell side), referral fees, ancillary services (title, mortgage, insurance)
- Margins: Gross commission split 50-80% to agent; brokerage margin 20-40% after agent split; net margin 5-15%
- Agent model: Independent contractor (not employee); commission split (70/30 typical for experienced agents); desk fee model (flat monthly)
- Revenue per agent: $50-150K GCI for average agent; $500K+ for top producers; 80/20 rule applies strongly
- Key metric: Agent count, transaction volume, GCI per agent, agent retention, market share

**Tech Stack**
- CRM/lead management: Follow Up Boss (dominant for mid-market), KVCore (inspired by eXp), Top Producer, Sierra Interactive, BoomTown
- Transaction management: dotloop (dominant), SkySlope, Brokermint, Real Geeks
- Marketing: Mailchimp, Canva, Adobe, kvCORE marketing suite, Meltwater
- Showing management: ShowingTime (Zillow), BrokerBay, Showami
- CMA/appraisal: RPR (Realtors Property Resource), CoreLogic, House Canary
- Document/e-signature: DocuSign (dominant), Dotloop, Authentisign
- Website/IDX: Real Geeks, kvCORE, Sierra Interactive, custom WordPress
- Social media: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube — agent personal brand is primary marketing channel

**Decision-Making**
- Broker/Owner: Strategic technology decisions; brokerage-wide platform selection; budget approval
- Team Lead: Team-level technology decisions; CRM selection; marketing technology
- Head of Growth/Marketing: Lead generation technology; marketing automation; agent recruitment tech
- IT Director: Infrastructure; security; compliance; integrations
- Agent advisory board: Influences technology selection; agent satisfaction critical for retention

**Budget Cycles**
- Annual budget: Q4 planning for following year; technology as % of GCI (typically 2-5%)
- Per-agent technology: $200-500/month per agent for tech stack; broker may subsidize or mandate
- Spring selling season: Technology readiness by February; hiring season January-February
- Conference cycle: NAR Annual (November), Inman Connect (January and August), local association meetings
- Market-dependent: Hot market = investment in scaling; slow market = investment in efficiency

**Regulatory**
- Real estate licensing: 50 state licensing requirements; continuing education; broker supervision requirements
- Fair Housing Act: Prohibits discrimination; applies to advertising, showing, lending; training required
- RESPA (Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act): Kickback prohibition; affiliated business arrangement disclosure; CFPB enforcement
- NAR settlement (2024): Decoupling buyer-broker commission; written buyer agreements required; industry restructuring
- TCPA: Cold calling restrictions; DNC compliance; SMS consent required; $1,500 per violation
- State-specific: Disclosure requirements, agency relationships, escrow handling, advertising rules
- CAN-SPAM: Commercial email; identification; unsubscribe; penalties
- Data privacy: CCPA/CPRA, state laws; consumer data handling; lead data retention

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- Agent turnover: 70%+ of new agents leave within 2 years; $3-5K cost to recruit each new agent; training investment lost
- Lead conversion: Average conversion rate from lead to closed transaction: 1-3%; 50%+ of leads never receive follow-up
- Commission compression: NAR settlement pressure; discount brokerages; flat-fee models eroding traditional 5-6% commission
- Technology fragmentation: Average agent uses 8-12 tools; data silos; no single view of customer; CRM adoption <50%
- Market slowdown: Transaction volume down 30-40% from 2021 peak; fewer transactions = pressure on GCI
- Lead quality: Zillow/Realtor.com leads expensive ($200-1,000 per lead); conversion rate low (1-2%); ROI negative for many agents
- Compliance burden: Fair housing training; RESPA compliance; state-mandated disclosures; NAR settlement requirements
- Competition: 1.5M+ agents competing; low barriers to entry; technology differentiation is key

**Buying Triggers**
- Agent recruitment drive: CRM/lead management platform; onboarding technology
- Market share decline: Technology upgrade; marketing automation; lead generation investment
- New Broker/Owner hire: Platform evaluation; technology modernization; 90-day review period
- NAR settlement implementation: Process re-engineering; buyer agreement technology; commission transparency tools
- Agent satisfaction survey: Technology gaps identified; retention-driven investment
- Merger/acquisition of brokerages: System consolidation; agent migration; platform standardization
- Spring selling season: Lead management scaling; marketing technology; mobile readiness
- Office expansion/new location: IT infrastructure; communication technology; agent onboarding

**Channels**
- Industry events: NAR Annual Conference, Inman Connect, RESO Conference, local association events
- Email: Broker/Owner, Team Lead — must reference specific market and peer brokerage references
- Trade media: Inman, Real Trends, RISMedia, HousingWire
- LinkedIn: Broker/Owner, Head of Growth — agent productivity content performs well
- Agent communities: Facebook groups, Reddit r/realtors, BiggerPockets
- Referral networks: Title company introductions; mortgage broker referrals; MLS contacts
- Vendor partnerships: Zillow Premier Agent, Realtor.com, and lead provider account managers

**Vocabulary**
- GCI (Gross Commission Income), net commission, commission split, desk fee, cap
- Listing, buyer representation, dual agency, transaction brokerage, designated agency
- CMA (Comparative Market Analysis), BPO (Broker Price Opinion), appraisal, comp
- MLS (Multiple Listing Service), IDX, VOW (Virtual Office Website), RESO (Real Estate Standards Organization)
- Lead, lead conversion, sphere of influence, farming, open house, door knock
- Absorption rate, days on market, list-to-sale ratio, inventory months, price per sq ft
- FSBO (For Sale By Owner), expired listing, pre-foreclosure, short sale, REO
- Buyer agreement, exclusive representation, NAR settlement, cooperative compensation
- Transaction coordinator, escrow, title, closing, earnest money, contingency

### 2.3 Real Estate Developers

**Market Shape**
- US development market: ~$1T+ in annual construction put in place; development pipeline $500B+ at any time
- Market structure: Highly fragmented; local/regional dominance; few national players (Lennar, D.R. Horton, Pulte for residential; Related, Hines, Boston Properties for commercial)
- Product types: Single-family residential, multifamily, mixed-use, industrial/logistics, office, retail, hospitality, senior living
- Capital structure: Developer equity 10-25%; mezzanine 10-15%; senior debt 60-80%; construction loans then perm financing
- Cycle sensitivity: Starts decline 30-50% in recessions; entitlement timelines 2-5 years; development is long-cycle

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Sale of completed units (residential), lease-up (commercial), management fees, promote/carried interest
- Margins: Development margin 15-25% (residential); 10-20% (commercial); promote 20% of profits above hurdle
- Return structure: IRR target 15-25% (development); equity multiple 1.5-2.5x; promote after preferred return (8-12%)
- Capital stack: Equity (developer + investors) 20-35%; debt (construction + perm) 65-80%
- Key metric: Development pipeline value, pre-sales/pre-leasing, cost per unit/sf, construction timeline, IRR

**Tech Stack**
- Project management: Procore (dominant for construction), Autodesk Build, CoConstruct (residential), BuilderTrend (home building)
- Financial: Sage Intacct, Yardi, MRI, QuickBooks (smaller developers), Juniper Square (investor reporting)
- Design: Revit, AutoCAD, SketchUp, Enscape (rendering), Lumion
- Entitlement/permitting: Accela, OpenGov, custom; often still paper-based
- Sales/marketing: Salesforce, HubSpot, MRI Software (residential), real estate-specific CRMs
- Investor relations: Juniper Square, CrowdStreet, RealPage IMS
- Scheduling: Microsoft Project, Primavera P6, Procore scheduling

**Decision-Making**
- Principal/Managing Partner: Strategic direction; capital allocation; go/no-go on projects
- Development Director: Day-to-day project management; design coordination; permitting
- Construction VP/Director: Construction technology; contractor management; quality control
- VP Finance/CFO: Project financing; investor reporting; cost control technology
- VP Sales/Marketing: Sales technology; marketing automation; CRM; virtual tours

**Budget Cycles**
- Project-based: Technology budget tied to project lifecycle; 2-5 year planning
- Pre-development: Technology for design, entitlement, permitting; 6-24 months before construction
- Construction phase: Project management technology; cost tracking; schedule management; safety
- Lease-up/sell-out: Sales and marketing technology; CRM; virtual tours
- Annual corporate: Overhead technology; accounting; HR; investor reporting

**Regulatory**
- Zoning/entitlement: Municipal; comprehensive plan; rezoning; variances; PUD; 6-24 month process
- Environmental review: NEPA (federal), SEPA (state); EIS/EIR; wetlands; endangered species; historical preservation
- Building permits: Local building department; plan review; inspections; occupancy permits
- Impact fees: Municipal; $5-50K per unit; varies widely; affects project economics
- Affordable housing: Inclusionary zoning; density bonuses; LIHTC (Low-Income Housing Tax Credit); compliance requirements
- ADA: Accessibility requirements; Fair Housing Act design requirements; ongoing compliance
- Stormwater/water quality: EPA/state permits; SWPPP; low-impact development requirements
- Energy codes: IECC; ASHRAE; stretch codes; net-zero requirements in some jurisdictions; electrification mandates
- Historic preservation: Section 106; state/local historic districts; tax credits for rehabilitation

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- Entitlement timeline: 2-5 years for complex projects; each month of delay = $50-200K in carrying costs; community opposition common
- Construction cost escalation: 20-30% increase since 2020; labor shortage 650K+ workers; material price volatility 20-40%
- Interest rate risk: Construction loan rates up 300-400 bps; project feasibility strained; some projects paused/cancelled
- Permit delays: Average 6-12 months for commercial; residential 3-6 months; varies dramatically by jurisdiction
- Contractor defaults: 10-15% of subcontractors fail during project; performance bonds required; replacement costs 20-50% premium
- Cost tracking: 40-60% of developers still use Excel for cost tracking; real-time cost visibility is rare; budget overruns 10-20% common
- Investor reporting: Monthly/quarterly reporting burden; manual data aggregation from 5-10 systems; accuracy issues
- Sales cycle: Pre-sales target 50%+ before construction; slow pre-sales delay project start; marketing technology critical

**Buying Triggers**
- New project acquisition: Full project management stack; design coordination; permitting technology
- Entitlement milestone: Moving from entitlement to construction; technology needs shift
- Construction start: Project management; cost tracking; schedule management; safety technology
- Pre-sales launch: Sales CRM; marketing automation; virtual tours; investor reporting
- Cost overrun on active project: Cost control technology; change order management; real-time tracking
- Investor reporting burden: Automation; data consolidation; investor portal
- New VP Construction/Director hire: Technology evaluation; process modernization
- Regulatory change (energy code, zoning): Compliance technology; design adjustment
- Partnership/JV formation: Reporting requirements; investor communication technology

**Channels**
- Industry events: ULI Fall Meeting, NAIOP Development Conference, ICSC, NAHB International Builders' Show
- Email: Principal, Development Director, VP Construction — must reference specific project type and market
- Trade media: GlobeSt, Commercial Observer, The Real Deal, Builder Magazine, Development Magazine
- LinkedIn: Principal, Development Director — project milestone content performs
- Lender/broker introductions: Construction lenders often refer technology partners
- Architect/engineer referrals: AEC firms influence developer technology decisions

**Vocabulary**
- Entitlement, rezoning, variance, conditional use, PUD, comp plan, general plan
- Plat, subdivision, site plan, conditional map, tentative map, final map
- Pre-sales, pre-leasing, absorption, lease-up, stabilization, NOI stabilization
- Promote, carried interest, preferred return, hurdle rate, waterfall, look-back
- Construction loan, perm loan, bridge loan, mezzanine, preferred equity, joint venture
- Hard cost, soft cost, contingency, general conditions, GC fee, overhead
- TCO (Temporary Certificate of Occupancy), CO (Certificate of Occupancy), punch list, substantial completion
- Impact fee, connection fee, proffer, exaction, density bonus
- LIHTC, inclusionary, affordable, AMI (Area Median Income), compliance period

### 2.4 General Contractors (GCs)

**Market Shape**
- US construction market: ~$2T annual put in place; GCs manage ~$1.2T of that
- Market structure: Top 10 GCs (Turner, Bechtel, Skanska, DPR, Suffolk, Clark, Hensel Phelps, McCarthy, Whiting-Turner, Mortenson) = ~10% market share; highly fragmented below that
- Segments: Commercial building, industrial, infrastructure, institutional, residential
- Project delivery: Design-bid-build (50%), CMAR (25%), design-build (20%), IPD (5%)
- Workforce: 8M+ construction workers; aging workforce (average age 42); 650K+ worker shortage

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Construction revenue (primary — fee on top of project cost), preconstruction services, self-perform work, equipment rental
- Margins: Gross margin 5-15% (fee on project cost); net margin 2-5%; large GCs rely on volume; self-perform work has higher margins
- Fee structure: Guaranteed Maximum Price (GMP) with shared savings; lump sum; cost-plus; CMAR fee 2-5%
- Backlog: Revenue visibility from backlog; target 12-18 months of backlog; backlog = health indicator
- Key metric: Backlog, revenue growth, gross margin, EMR (Experience Modification Rate — safety), project schedule performance

**Tech Stack**
- Project management: Procore (dominant — 60%+ market share in mid-large GCs), Autodesk Build (formerly PlanGrid + BuildingConnected), CMiC (enterprise), Viewpoint
- Preconstruction: Autodesk Takeoff, BuildingConnected (bidding), Procore Prequalification, SmartBid
- Field: Procore (field management), OpenSpace (photo documentation), DroneDeploy (site survey), Hilti (tool tracking)
- Cost management: Procore, CMiC, Viewpoint, Hard Dollar, DESTINI Estimator
- Scheduling: Primavera P6 (Oracle — dominant for large projects), Microsoft Project, Procore
- Safety: Procore Safety, SafetyCulture (iAuditor), Smartvid.io (AI safety), Alcumus
- BIM: Revit, Navisworks, BIM 360 (Autodesk), Trimble Connect
- Quality: Procore Quality & Safety, OpenSpace, Fieldwire (autodesk)
- Submittals/RFIs: Procore (dominant), Autodesk Build, Bluebeam Revu

**Decision-Making**
- Project Executive: Project-level technology decisions; field technology adoption; team preferences
- VP Operations: Enterprise technology strategy; Procore vs. Autodesk decision; standardization
- VP Preconstruction: Estimating technology; bidding platforms; quantity takeoff tools
- Director of BIM/VDC: BIM technology; model coordination; clash detection; field layout
- Director of Safety: Safety technology; compliance; reporting; wearable technology
- CFO/VP Finance: Cost management technology; ERP; financial reporting
- IT Director: Infrastructure; security; integrations; mobile deployment

**Budget Cycles**
- Annual budget: Q4 planning for following year; technology typically 0.5-1.5% of revenue
- Project-based: Some technology budgeted per project; field technology; BIM/VDC; drone
- Equipment CapEx: Heavy equipment technology (GPS, machine control); 5-7 year depreciation
- Safety technology: Often budgeted separately; compliance-driven; insurance premium reduction justifies investment
- Preconstruction technology: Estimating software; bid management; annual subscription
- Innovation budget: Some large GCs have innovation teams; pilot projects; 1-2% of IT budget

**Regulatory**
- OSHA: Occupational safety; fall protection; silica; recordkeeping; inspections; penalties up to $156K per willful violation
- Building codes: IBC + local amendments; inspections; special inspections; occupancy permits
- Davis-Bacon: Prevailing wage on federal projects; certified payroll; compliance requirements
- EPA: Stormwater (SWPPP); dust control; noise; hazardous materials (asbestos, lead)
- State licensing: Contractor licensing varies by state; insurance requirements; bonding capacity
- Insurance: Workers' comp, general liability, builder's risk, professional liability; EMR impacts premiums
- Union agreements: Project labor agreements; jurisdictional rules; training requirements
- EMR (Experience Modification Rate): Below 1.0 = good safety record; impacts insurance premiums and bid competitiveness

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- RFI cycle time: Average 7-14 days per RFI; $1,000-5,000 per RFI in overhead; 1,000-5,000 RFIs per $100M project
- Schedule overruns: 70%+ of projects finish late; average delay 20-30%; liquidated damages $5-50K/day
- Cost overruns: Average project runs 10-20% over budget; rework costs 5-12% of total project cost
- Labor productivity: Construction productivity flat for 30 years; 50%+ of craft hours lost to waiting, rework, logistics
- Safety: 1,000+ construction fatalities per year in US; OSHA citations cost $15K-$156K; EMR impacts competitiveness
- Submittal delays: 500-2,000 submittals per $100M project; average review cycle 10-20 days; bottleneck is architect/engineer review
- Change order disputes: 10-20% of project value in change orders; disputes delay payment 60-90 days; litigation common
- Talent shortage: 650K+ worker shortage; aging workforce; competition for superintendents and project managers intense

**Buying Triggers**
- New project award: Full project management stack; field technology; safety technology
- Schedule delay on active project: Schedule recovery technology; coordination tools; resource management
- Safety incident: Safety technology investment; compliance upgrade; reporting improvement
- EMR increase: Safety program overhaul; wearable technology; real-time safety monitoring
- New VP Operations hire: Technology evaluation; platform standardization; 90-120 day review
- Procore/Autodesk evaluation: Platform decision triggers adjacent tool choices
- Subcontractor default on project: Subcontractor prequalification technology; risk management; financial monitoring
- Insurance premium increase: Safety technology; risk management; EMR improvement
- BIM mandate (owner or regulatory): VDC technology investment; model coordination; field layout
- Preconstruction efficiency: Estimating technology; bid management; historical cost data

**Channels**
- Industry events: AGC Annual Convention, NAHB International Builders' Show, World of Concrete, Procore Groundbreak
- Email: VP Operations, Project Executive, VP Preconstruction — must reference project type and peer GC references
- Trade media: Construction Dive, Engineering News-Record (ENR), Associated Builders and Contractors (ABC)
- LinkedIn: VP Operations, Director of BIM/VDC — project technology content performs
- Procore ecosystem: App marketplace; Procore-certified integrations; Groundbreak conference
- Insurance broker referrals: Safety technology recommendations; EMR improvement programs
- Subcontractor network: Sub recommendations influence GC technology choices (field-facing tools)

**Vocabulary**
- GMP, lump sum, cost-plus, CMAR, design-build, IPD, bridging

### 2.5 Architecture & Engineering (AEC)

**Market Shape**
- US AEC services market: ~$400B; 100K+ architecture firms; 60K+ engineering firms
- Market structure: Top 10 AEC firms (AECOM, Jacobs, Stantec, WSP, Fluor, HDR, HOK, Gensler, Perkins&Will, Arup) = 10% market share; highly fragmented
- Architecture: 100K+ firms; Gensler ($1.5B+ revenue) is largest; most firms <50 people; design-driven culture
- Engineering: 60K+ firms; structural, MEP, civil, environmental, transportation; technical-driven culture
- Growing segments: Sustainability/ESG consulting, resilience engineering, digital twin consulting, computational design

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Professional fees (primary — hourly or fixed fee), reimbursable expenses, design-build revenue
- Margins: Gross margin 40-60% (professional services); net margin 8-15%; utilization rate target 75-85%
- Fee structure: Fixed fee (70%+ of projects), hourly, GMP on design-build; fee typically 5-12% of construction cost
- Staffing: Project-based staffing; leverage model (1 principal : 2 PMs : 4-6 designers/engineers); utilization is king
- Key metric: Utilization rate, net revenue per employee, project profitability, win rate, client retention

**Tech Stack**
- Design: Revit (dominant for BIM), AutoCAD (2D), Rhino + Grasshopper (parametric), SketchUp (conceptual), ArchiCAD
- Analysis: ETABS, SAP2000, RAM (structural), IES VE, EnergyPlus (energy), Ladybug (environmental), Comsol (simulation)
- Visualization: Enscape, Lumion, V-Ray, Twinmotion, Unreal Engine (emerging)
- Project management: Deltek Ajera, Monograph, BQE Core, Harvest; smaller firms use Asana/Monday
- Collaboration: BIM 360, Trimble Connect, BIMcollab, Newforma
- Specifications: Deltek Specpoint, Masterspec, BSD SpecLink
- CA (Construction Administration): RPR (Field), PlanGrid, Procore (CA module)
- Financial: Deltek Vision/Vantagepoint (dominant), Sage Intacct, QuickBooks

**Decision-Making**
- Principal/Partner: Strategic technology decisions; design technology direction; budget approval

## 9. Compliance Considerations (Expanded)

### Real Estate Licensing
- Outreach cannot imply you're a licensed agent if you're not
- Cannot offer services that require a real estate license (negotiating, advising on transactions)
- State-specific advertising rules for real estate services
- Team and brokerage names may be regulated

### Fair Housing Act
- No discriminatory language in property-related outreach
- Cannot target or exclude based on race, color, religion, national origin, sex, familial status, disability
- Advertising must comply with Fair Housing Act (equal housing opportunity logo)
- AI-driven targeting must be audited for disparate impact

### RESPA (Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act)
- Kickback rules: Cannot pay referral fees for settlement services
- Affiliated business arrangements: Must disclose; restrictions on conditioning
- CFPB enforcement active; fines up to $25K per day

### TCPA
- Cold calling restrictions (DNC list); especially for FSBO leads
- SMS requires prior express consent; $1,500 per message for willful violation
- Auto-dialer restrictions; predictive dialer compliance

### Data Privacy
- CCPA/CPRA: Consumer data handling; right to opt-out; "do not sell"
- GDPR: EU consumer data; processing agreements; cookie consent
- State privacy laws: Growing patchwork; track via IAPP
- Lead data: Must be obtained lawfully; consent for marketing; retention limits

### Construction-Specific
- OSHA: Cannot promise elimination of safety risks; must be realistic about safety technology capabilities
- Prevailing wage: Davis-Bacon compliance on federal projects; technology must support certified payroll
- Lien law: Varies by state; technology for lien management must comply with state requirements

### LeadReach Agent Rules
- **NEVER** imply LeadReach is a licensed real estate broker
- **NEVER** use language that could violate Fair Housing Act
- **NEVER** pay referral fees that violate RESPA
- **NEVER** make automated calls without checking DNC and obtaining TCPA consent
- **ALWAYS** include proper disclaimers for real estate-related services
- **ALWAYS** verify state-specific requirements before outreach in new jurisdictions
