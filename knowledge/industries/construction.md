# Construction — B2B Lead Intelligence Guide

## 1. Industry Overview

Construction is a **$13 trillion+ global industry** and one of the **least digitized sectors on Earth** — McKinsey gives it a digitization score of 28/100, lower than agriculture and hunting. Productivity has **declined 1% per year for two decades** while every other industry has improved. The industry is defined by **thin margins** (2-5% net), **project-based business models** (every project is a startup), **acute labor shortages** (650K+ unfilled positions in the US alone), **fragmented supply chains** (50-100+ subcontractors on a major project), and **technology adoption that is accelerating but from a very low base**.

Key characteristics:
- **Project-based operations** — Each project has its own budget, team, schedule, and risk profile. Technology must work across projects, not just within one.
- **Thin margins** — 2-5% net profit means every dollar of technology spend must be justified against direct project cost or overhead reduction. ROI conversations are blunt.
- **Labor shortage is structural** — 40% of construction workers are over 50; fewer than 5% are under 25. Technology that replaces or augments labor has the strongest pull.
- **Risk-averse buyers** — Construction executives bet their companies on every project. They don't adopt unproven technology on a $100M project.
- **Field-first culture** — Technology that works in the trailer but not in the field fails. Mobile-first, offline-capable, simple UX wins.
- **Fragmented industry** — 900K+ construction firms in the US; 80% have fewer than 10 employees. The mid-market (50-500 employees) is the technology sweet spot.

The buying motion differs by sub-segment:
- **Selling to GCs/EPCs**: VP Operations / Project Executive-led, 3-9 month cycles. Committee includes VP Preconstruction, IT Director, and field operations. Procore or Autodesk is the system of record.
- **Selling to subcontractors**: Owner/VP Operations-led, 2-8 week cycles. Fast decisions, smaller ACV, high volume. Must demonstrate immediate field impact.
- **Selling to infrastructure contractors**: VP Operations / IT Director-led, 6-12 month cycles. Public procurement adds complexity. DBE and compliance requirements.
- **Selling to home builders**: VP Operations-led, 4-12 week cycles. Production building is repeatable; custom building is project-based.

LeadReach is best positioned for mid-market GCs ($50M-$2B revenue), specialty subcontractors with 50+ field workers, and ConTech vendors selling into the industry. Mega-EPCs (Bechtel, Fluor, Turner) require dedicated enterprise motions.

## 2. Sub-Segment Deep Dives

## 4. Buyer Personas

### VP Operations
- **At**: Mid-large GC or EPC firm
- **Cares about**: Project schedule, cost control, safety record, subcontractor performance, field productivity
- **Pain**: Schedule overruns, cost estimation inaccuracy, field communication gaps, document control chaos, safety compliance, workforce shortage
- **Outreach angle**: Project outcome improvement from peer GCs; schedule recovery case studies; safety improvement metrics
- **Channels**: Email, construction conferences (AGC, CMAA, DBIA), LinkedIn
- **What works**: Peer GC case studies with specific schedule and cost outcomes; Procore/Autodesk integration proof; field adoption evidence
- **What fails**: Office-only solutions; ignoring the field; not understanding GMP and cost-plus contract structures

### Project Executive
- **At**: GC or EPC firm
- **Cares about**: Project profitability, client relationships, team performance, risk management, win rate on bids
- **Pain**: Margin erosion from change orders, client communication gaps, subcontractor default risk, schedule delays, document disputes
- **Outreach angle**: Profitability improvement from peer projects; change order documentation; client communication tools
- **Channels**: Email, LinkedIn, direct phone
- **What works**: Profitability metrics from similar project types; specific change order reduction; risk management case studies
- **What fails**: Not understanding the project-as-startup model; generic business intelligence without construction context

### VP Preconstruction
- **At**: GC or EPC firm
- **Cares about**: Estimating accuracy, bid win rate, preconstruction schedule, design coordination, subcontractor selection
- **Pain**: Estimating inaccuracy (average 20% variance), bid preparation time, BIM coordination during precon, conceptual estimating, historical cost data access
- **Outreach angle**: Estimating accuracy improvement; bid preparation efficiency; historical cost data from peer firms
- **Channels**: Email, DBIA conference, LinkedIn
- **What works**: Estimating accuracy improvement with specific %; bid volume metrics; integration with estimating tools (WinEst, McCormick, Accubid)
- **What fails**: Not understanding the estimating workflow; proposing tools that don't integrate with Sage/Viewpoint accounting

### IT Director
- **At**: GC, EPC, or large subcontractor
- **Cares about**: System integration, data security, Procore/Autodesk ecosystem, mobile infrastructure, user adoption
- **Pain**: Procore/Autodesk integration complexity, field connectivity, data silos, cybersecurity, legacy accounting system maintenance, user adoption
- **Outreach angle**: Integration architecture; data unification; mobile-first field deployment; security posture
- **Channels**: Email, construction technology events, LinkedIn
- **What works**: Technical architecture depth; Procore/Autodesk API integration specifics; security certifications; deployment case studies
- **What fails**: Proposing rip-and-replace of Procore; ignoring offline field requirements; not understanding construction-specific security needs

## 7. Construction Vocabulary & Jargon

### Project Delivery
- **GMP** — Guaranteed Maximum Price (contract type where GC bears cost risk above cap)
- **Cost-plus** — Contract where owner reimburses costs plus a fee percentage
- **Lump sum** — Fixed-price contract
- **Design-Build** — Single entity provides design and construction
- **CM at-risk** — Construction Manager guarantees price, acts as GC
- **IPD** — Integrated Project Delivery (collaborative, shared risk/reward)
- **EPC** — Engineering, Procurement, Construction (turnkey delivery)

### Project Management
- **RFI** — Request for Information (clarification from architect/engineer)
- **Submittal** — Product data, shop drawings submitted for approval
- **Change order** — Modification to contract scope, price, or schedule
- **Punch list** — Outstanding items to complete before project closeout
- **Retainage** — Percentage of payment withheld until project completion (typically 5-10%)
- **Draw** — Payment request based on completed work
- **Schedule of values** — Breakdown of project cost by line item
- **Closeout** — Final project documentation and handover

### Field & Safety
- **Superintendent** — GC's on-site leader
- **Foreman** — Subcontractor's on-site leader
- **Journeyman** — Skilled trade worker (completed apprenticeship)
- **Rough-in** — Initial installation of MEP systems before walls close
- **Trim-out** — Final installation of fixtures and finishes
- **PPE** — Personal Protective Equipment
- **OSHA** — Occupational Safety and Health Administration
- **EMR** — Experience Modification Rate (safety metric; below 1.0 is good)
- **Toolbox talk** — Daily safety briefing

### BIM & Technology
- **BIM** — Building Information Modeling
- **VDC** — Virtual Design and Construction
- **Clash detection** — Identifying conflicts between building systems in BIM
- **LOD** — Level of Development (BIM detail level, 100-500)
- **Point cloud** — 3D data from laser scanning
- **Digital twin** — Virtual replica of physical asset
- **4D BIM** — BIM + schedule (time)
- **5D BIM** — BIM + schedule + cost
- **Parametric** — Model where changes propagate automatically

## 9. Outreach Best Practices & Sequencing

### Channel Sequencing for Construction

Construction buyers respond to **practical, outcome-specific messaging** and **peer proof**. Recommended sequence:

- **Day 0**: LinkedIn connection — reference a recent project award, ENR ranking, or community opening.
- **Day 3**: Cold email #1 — 120 words, lead with a project outcome (schedule improvement, cost reduction), no product pitch.
- **Day 7**: LinkedIn engagement — comment on a post about construction technology, labor, or project delivery.
- **Day 10**: Cold email #2 — case study from same project type (e.g., healthcare, education, industrial), mention Procore/Autodesk compatibility.
- **Day 14**: Share a relevant ENR article or Dodge data point about their market segment.
- **Day 17**: Cold call — reference the article share and project outcome data.
- **Day 21**: Cold email #3 — breakup email with specific metric ("GCs using LeadReach identify 35% more pre-qualified project opportunities").
- **Day 28**: Final touch — invite to a construction technology webinar or roundtable.

Expected meeting-booked rate: **1.5-2.5%** (higher for subcontractors, lower for large GCs).

Key elements:
- **Project-type specific** — Construction buyers think in project types (healthcare, education, industrial). Generic "construction" outreach fails.
- **Precon/BD focus** — This is where LeadReach adds the most value for GCs (finding projects before they hit Dodge).
- **Dodge Reports comparison** — Every GC uses Dodge; positioning against it is credible.
- **Soft CTA** — offer to share the framework before requesting a meeting

## 10. Regulatory & Compliance Notes

- **OSHA**: Occupational Safety and Health Administration — safety standards for all construction. Technology that improves safety compliance has a regulatory tailwind.
- **Davis-Bacon Act**: Prevailing wage requirements for federally funded construction. Subcontractor compliance is a major pain point.
- **DBE requirements**: Disadvantaged Business Enterprise participation requirements on federal projects. Compliance tracking tools are in demand.
- **Building codes**: IBC (International Building Code), local amendments. Code compliance tools and plan check automation.
- **Environmental regulations**: NEPA (National Environmental Policy Act), SWPPP (stormwater), ESA (Endangered Species Act). Environmental compliance tooling is growing.
- **Lien laws**: Mechanic's lien rights vary by state. Lien management and payment compliance tools.
- **Licensed contractor requirements**: State-by-state licensing. Not relevant to B2B prospecting directly, but understanding the regulatory landscape builds credibility.
- **Insurance requirements**: Workers' comp, general liability, professional liability. EMR (Experience Modification Rate) is a key safety metric that affects insurance premiums.
- **Union vs. non-union**: Project labor agreements (PLAs) and union requirements vary by market. Understanding this context matters for outreach.

LeadReach agents should NOT:
- Target individual construction workers for B2B products
- Make safety compliance claims that LeadReach cannot substantiate
- Ignore state-specific licensing and regulatory requirements
- Reference specific project bid amounts from non-public sources
- Propose technology that replaces safety-critical human judgment

## 13. Regional Variants

- **US — Sun Belt** (TX, FL, AZ, NV, NC): Fastest-growing construction markets. Residential and industrial focus. Lower union density. Technology adoption is higher due to workforce shortage.
- **US — Northeast** (NY, MA, PA): Union-heavy, complex regulatory environment, infrastructure renewal. Higher costs, more compliance tooling demand.
- **US — West Coast** (CA, WA, OR): Seismic requirements, environmental regulations, high labor costs. Green building (LEED) is standard. Technology adoption is highest.
- **US — Midwest** (IL, OH, MI, MN): Manufacturing construction boom (CHIPS Act, reshoring). Infrastructure renewal. Mixed union density.
- **UK**: Different contract structures (JCT, NEC), CDM regulations, BIM Level 2 mandate on public projects. Construction Playbook driving technology adoption.
- **EU**: Varies by country. Germany (slow tech adoption, strong union influence), Nordics (high tech adoption, BIM-native), Southern Europe (fragmented, lower tech adoption).
- **Middle East**: Mega-projects (NEOM, Dubai), international GCs, high technology expectations on showcase projects.
- **APAC**: Japan (aging workforce, robotics adoption), Australia (mining infrastructure, high costs), Singapore (DPR mandate for BIM).

## 15. Common Construction Prospecting Mistakes

- **Not knowing the project type** — Pitching healthcare construction tools to an industrial contractor signals you don't understand the market. Project types are worlds apart.
- **Ignoring the field** — Any tool that doesn't work on a construction site (dusty, noisy, no WiFi) will fail. If your messaging ignores field conditions, you lose credibility.
- **Not understanding contract types** — GMP, cost-plus, lump sum, and design-build have fundamentally different risk profiles and technology needs.
- **Confusing GC and subcontractor needs** — GCs manage projects; subcontractors execute work. Their technology needs are completely different.
- **Underestimating Procore's gravity** — 60%+ of ENR Top 400 GCs use Procore. Proposing to replace it is unrealistic; propose to integrate with it.
- **Not respecting the margin reality** — 2-5% net margins mean you must prove ROI quickly and bluntly. Soft ROI doesn't sell in construction.
- **Generic "construction" language** — "We help construction companies" means nothing. "We help healthcare GCs reduce precon research time by 60%" means everything.
- **Missing the precon window** — Preconstruction is where technology has the most impact and the most budget. Not targeting precon teams is a miss.
- **Ignoring union dynamics** — In union-heavy markets, labor technology has different requirements (prevailing wage, apprenticeship ratios, hall dispatch).
- **Not knowing ENR rankings** — ENR Top 400 is the industry's league table. Not referencing it signals outsider status.
