# Logistics & Supply Chain Industry — Prospecting Guide, Signals, and Buyer Personas

## 1. Industry Overview

The global logistics and supply chain industry is a **$10+ trillion market** that physically connects every other industry on the planet. It encompasses freight transportation (truck, rail, ocean, air, pipeline), warehousing and distribution, third-party logistics (3PL) and fourth-party logistics (4PL) services, last-mile delivery, cold chain, port and terminal operations, drayage, intermodal, and parcel delivery. The industry moves roughly 11 billion tons of freight annually in the United States alone and underpins every consumer good, industrial input, and agricultural commodity in modern commerce. It is structurally fragmented — the top 25 global 3PLs combined hold less than 30% market share — which creates a continuous prospecting surface of mid-market carriers, regional brokers, private fleets, and specialty operators.

Several macro forces are reshaping the industry simultaneously. **E-commerce saturation** has restructured demand toward parcel and last-mile, with Amazon Logistics alone now delivering over 5 billion parcels annually and forcing incumbents like UPS and FedEx to reprice accessorial fees. **Nearshoring and friendshoring** — accelerated by US-China decoupling, the USMCA, and post-COVID resilience mandates — is shifting freight flows toward Mexico, Vietnam, India, and Eastern Europe, creating greenfield opportunities for cross-border 3PLs and customs brokers. **Decarbonization regulation** — including IMO 2020 low-sulphur rules, the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), and California's Advanced Clean Fleets rule — is forcing carriers to invest in electric, hydrogen, and biofuel fleets. **Capacity cyclicality** (the freight recession of 2023-2024 wiped out 3,000+ US trucking bankruptcies, then rates spiked in 2024-2025 as capacity tightened) creates windows of intense need for cost-optimization and revenue-management tools.

The logistics buyer is **operational, ROI-obsessed, and intolerant of marketing fluff**. They run on razor-thin margins (typical US truckload carrier operating ratio is 90-95, meaning 5-10% operating margin), so any pitch must connect to either margin protection, revenue capture, or risk reduction. They trust peer references from operators far more than analyst reports, and they benchmark aggressively against their immediate competitors. Generic "AI for logistics" outreach fails; specific, citation-backed outreach tied to a recent earnings call, fleet expansion, or regulatory deadline wins.

## 2. Logistics Sub-Segments

Logistics is a federation of adjacent sub-segments, each with its own buyer, sales cycle, and budget source. Mis-segmenting a prospect is the single most common prospecting mistake — pitching a TMS to a 4PL, or a yard management system to a port operator, signals you don't understand the business.

### Third-Party Logistics (3PL)
Asset-light or hybrid operators providing outsourced logistics services: warehousing, transportation management, freight brokerage, customs brokerage. Examples: C.H. Robinson, XPO, J.B. Hunt, Expeditors, Kuehne+Nagel, DHL Supply Chain, GXO, RXO. The 3PL market is roughly $1.3 trillion globally and growing 6-8% annually as shippers continue outsourcing non-core logistics functions.
- **Buyer profile**: COO, VP Operations, VP Engineering/IT, Chief Commercial Officer (for shipper-facing tools). Procurement leads for tech vendor selection.
- **Sales cycle**: 60-180 days for SaaS/tech, 6-18 months for major WMS/TMS replacement.
- **Budget source**: IT budget (1-2% of revenue), Operations budget for opex tools.
- **Pain points**: Margin compression from carriers and shippers, capacity balancing, visibility across multi-modal moves, RFP response velocity, labor turnover in warehouses (often 30-40% annually).

### Fourth-Party Logistics (4PL / Lead Logistics Provider)
Asset-light integrators that orchestrate multiple 3PLs and provide consulting + technology + execution under one contract. Examples: DHL Supply Chain's 4PL practice, Kuehne+Nagel Lead Logistics, GEODIS Contract Logistics. 4PLs typically serve enterprise shippers with $100M+ annual freight spend.
- **Buyer profile**: VP Supply Chain Solutions, Managing Director Consulting, Head of Control Tower.
- **Sales cycle**: 6-18 months; relationship-driven, often started with consulting engagement.
- **Budget source**: Engagement margin from shipper clients; IT for the integration layer.

### Freight Forwarding
Non-vessel-owning common carriers (NVOCCs) and forwarders arranging ocean, air, and multimodal freight. Examples: Kuehne+Nagel, DSV, DB Schenker, Expeditors, Sinotrans, Dimerco. The global forwarding market is ~$250B and is being disrupted by digital forwarders (Flexport, Freightos, Zencargo) and online marketplaces (Freightos, INTTRA, Maersk Spot).
- **Buyer profile**: VP Operations, Head of Trade Lane, Head of Pricing, CIO.
- **Sales cycle**: 60-180 days for SaaS; major TMS replacement 9-18 months.
- **Budget source**: IT and Operations.
- **Pain points**: Rate volatility (ocean rates spiked 10x in COVID then collapsed 80% in 2023), customs and compliance automation, visibility for shipper customers, AMS/ISF filing accuracy.

### Last-Mile Delivery
Final-leg delivery to residential or business destinations, the fastest-growing segment due to e-commerce. Examples: Amazon Logistics, UPS, FedEx Ground, OnTrac, LaserShip, AxleHire, Veho. Sub-segment also includes gig-economy players (DoorDash Drive, Uber Freight's last-mile) and middle-mile specialists.
- **Buyer profile**: VP Last-Mile Operations, Director of Engineering, Head of Fleet.
- **Sales cycle**: 45-120 days for SaaS; capacity/driver onboarding is monthly.
- **Budget source**: Operations + Engineering.
- **Pain points**: Cost-per-delivery ($3-10 depending on density), failed first-attempt delivery (10-20%), driver recruitment and retention, route density optimization, returns (up to 30% in apparel e-commerce).

### Cold Chain Logistics
Temperature-controlled transportation and storage for pharma, food, and biologics. Examples: Lineage Logistics, Americold, Carrier Transicold, Knapp, FedEx Custom Critical, World Courier. The global cold chain market is ~$300B and growing 14%+ annually driven by biologics, frozen food, and direct-to-patient pharma.
- **Buyer profile**: VP Quality, Director of Cold Chain Operations, Head of Compliance, VP Engineering.
- **Sales cycle**: 90-180 days for SaaS; refrigeration/warehouse capex is 6-18 months.
- **Budget source**: Quality/Compliance + Operations + capex.
- **Pain points**: FDA FSMA Rule 204 traceability (enforcement starting 2026), GDP/GCP compliance for pharma, temperature excursion rates (typically 2-7% of shipments), energy intensity (cold warehouses use 3-4x more energy than ambient).

### Warehousing & Distribution
Operators of public, contract, and private warehouses. Examples: Prologis (REIT), Lineage Logistics, Americold, GXO, DHL Supply Chain, NFI, Weber Logistics. Global warehouse market is ~$400B and being transformed by automation (AutoStore, Symbotic, Geek+, Berkshire Grey).
- **Buyer profile**: VP Operations, Director of Engineering, Head of Automation, VP Real Estate (for site selection).
- **Sales cycle**: 60-180 days for WMS; 12-36 months for ASRS/robotics.
- **Budget source**: IT for WMS, capex for robotics/automation (often separate capital project budget).
- **Pain points**: Labor cost (40-60% of warehouse opex), throughput vs. peak (holiday, Double 11, Prime Day), slotting efficiency, returns processing, slot/zone automation ROI.

### Port Operations & Drayage
Terminal operators, port authorities, and the drayage carriers moving containers between port and rail/warehouse. Examples: Maersk APM Terminals, DP World, PSA International, Hutchison Ports, SSA Marine, Ports America. Drayage carriers are typically local/regional trucking companies.
- **Buyer profile**: Terminal Director, VP Operations, Chief Harbor Master, Head of IT/OT.
- **Sales cycle**: 6-24 months; complex procurement with port authority governance.
- **Budget source**: Port authority capital budget + operating budget.
- **Pain points**: Container dwell time, chassis availability, gate turn times, labor relations (ILWU, ILA), cybersecurity for OT/ICS systems, emissions from idling trucks.

### Intermodal / Rail Freight
Long-haul container movement via rail, often combined with truck drayage. Examples: BNSF, Union Pacific, CSX, Norfolk Southern, CN, CPKC, JB Hunt Intermodal, Schneider Intermodal.
- **Buyer profile**: VP Intermodal, Director of Network Planning, Head of Pricing.
- **Sales cycle**: 60-180 days for tech; multi-year service contracts.
- **Budget source**: Operations + Commercial.
- **Pain points**: Service reliability, container utilization, ramp congestion, crew availability, precision scheduled railroading (PSR) trade-offs.

### Parcel & Express
Small-package delivery dominated by UPS, FedEx, USPS, DHL, Amazon Logistics, and national postal operators (La Poste, Royal Mail, Japan Post, China Post, Australia Post). Also regional carriers (OnTrac, LSO, Spee-Dee) and consolidators (LSM, OSM, DHL eCommerce).
- **Buyer profile**: VP Network Planning, Director of Pricing, Head of Operations Engineering, CIO.
- **Sales cycle**: 60-180 days for tech; multi-year pricing agreements for shippers.
- **Budget source**: IT + Operations + Commercial pricing.

### Private Fleets & Shipper Logistics
Shippers running their own fleets (Walmart, PepsiCo, Kroger, Sysco, US Foods) or managing dedicated contract carriage. Often sophisticated operators who can be sold to as both buyers and prospects for benchmark services.
- **Buyer profile**: VP Transportation, Director of Fleet Operations, Head of Procurement (carrier side).
- **Sales cycle**: 60-180 days for tech; fleet capex is multi-year.

## 3. Firmographic Signals by Operator Size

### Owner-Operator / Single Truck (1-5 power units, <$2M revenue)
- **Signals**: Operating under their own authority or leased to a carrier; minimal IT footprint; QuickBooks for accounting; mobile phone as primary device.
- **Buying behavior**: Free tools (DAT One free tier), factoring services, fuel cards; will not engage with enterprise sales motions.
- **Budget availability**: <$5K/year for software; cash-flow constrained.
- **Outreach angle**: Skip unless you have a self-serve, sub-$100/month product. Owner-operator prospecting is a volume game with high churn.

### Small Fleet (6-50 power units, $2-15M revenue)
- **Signals**: Operating authority, modest back-office, may have a dispatcher or two, often using McLeod, TruckingOffice, or Axon for TMS.
- **Buying behavior**: Buys TMS, ELD, basic factoring; 30-90 day cycles; price-sensitive.
- **Budget availability**: $20K-150K/year for tech.
- **Outreach angle**: ROI on back-office automation, hours-of-service compliance, fuel savings, driver retention.

### Mid-Market Carrier or 3PL (50-500 power units or $15-200M revenue)
- **Signals**: Multi-terminal operations, formal safety director, IT manager, dedicated finance team; TMS in place (TMW, McLeod, EFS, MercuryGate).
- **Buying behavior**: Formal procurement, RFP for >$100K deals, 90-180 day cycles.
- **Budget availability**: $200K-2M/year tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Network optimization, driver productivity, margin per load, integration with existing TMS.

### Large Carrier / Enterprise 3PL (500+ power units or $200M+ revenue)
- **Signals**: Public company or PE-backed, formal procurement and IT security review, named CIO/CTO, multi-vendor TMS landscape.
- **Buying behavior**: 6-12 month cycles, RFP/RFI mandatory, security and compliance reviews (SOC 2, ISO 27001).
- **Budget availability**: $2M-50M+ annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference enterprise peers, specific cost-per-load or revenue-per-tractor outcomes, integration with SAP/Oracle.

### Global Integrators (DHL, FedEx, UPS, Maersk)
- **Signals**: Ticker symbol, 10-K filings, public earnings calls, global operations across 100+ countries.
- **Buying behavior**: Strategic vendor programs, 12-24 month cycles, vendor management office (VMO).
- **Outreach angle**: Tie to public earnings priorities, geopolitical expansion, ESG commitments.

## 4. Technographic Signals to Detect

The freight tech stack is the single best signal of buyer sophistication and unmet need. Detect these via BuiltWith, PublicWWW, LinkedIn job postings, and the company's careers page (job postings reveal stack faster than any other source).

### Core Systems Stack
- **TMS (Transportation Management System)**: MercuryGate, MercuryGate XI, TMW Suite, McLeod PowerSuite, Oracle OTM, SAP TM, Manhattan TMS, Blue Yonder TMS, Cerasis, Descartes Routing. New cloud-native TMS: Alpega, e2open, MercuryGate, Shipwell, Turvo, Rose Rocket, Truckin Digital.
- **WMS (Warehouse Management System)**: Manhattan Active WM, Blue Yonder WMS, SAP EWM, Oracle WMS, HighJump (Körber), Infor WMS, SnapFulfil, Softeon, Extensiv (3PL Warehouse Manager), Körber WMS.
- **OMS (Order Management System)**: Manhattan Active OM, IBM Sterling OMS, Salesforce OMS, Oracle OMS, Logiwa, ShipBob OMS.
- **ERP**: SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics 365, NetSuite, Infor, Epicor, Sage X3.
- **YMS (Yard Management)**: PINC, C3 Solutions, Blume YMS, Made4net.
- **OMS for parcel shipping**: ShipStation, EasyPost, ShipEngine (Stamps.com), ProShip, SendPro (Pitney Bowes), Asendia.

### Adjacency & Modernization Signals
- **Recently implemented Manhattan Active WM** → modernization wave; likely evaluating labor management, slotting, and automation within 12-18 months.
- **Recently switched from on-prem TMW to cloud Alpega or Turvo** → modernization; integration layer opportunity.
- **Posted a job for "Senior Data Engineer — Logistics"** → building internal analytics; may buy vs. build decision window for BI/reporting tools.
- **Posted a job for "Project44 Integration Lead"** → already invested in real-time visibility; ripe for adjacent use cases (predictive ETA, exceptions management).
- **Posted a job for "Symbotic Site Lead" or "AutoStore Integration Manager"** → robotics rollout; need WMS integration, peak planning, and ROI tracking.
- **Posted a job for "Head of Sustainability / ESG"** → carbon accounting and GLEC framework reporting needs.
- **EPA SmartWay Partner badge** → public sustainability commitment; opens carbon-tracking tool conversation.
- **Recently acquired by PE** (e.g., TPG, Apollo, Warburg, Bain Capital) → tech consolidation, value-creation playbook; 6-12 month window for transformation tools.

### Stack Maturity Heuristics
| Stack Pattern | What it signals | Best outreach angle |
|---|---|---|
| SAP TM + Oracle EBS + on-prem | Legacy enterprise, slow buyer | Migration story, integration, phased modernization |
| Manhattan Active WM + Salesforce + Snowflake + AWS | Modern enterprise, sophisticated buyer | Adjacent modules, data activation, AI use cases |
| MercuryGate + QuickBooks + Excel | Mid-market, ops-led buyer | ROI on back-office automation, scaling story |
| Turvo + Shipwell + Project44 + modern API stack | Digital-first 3PL, sophisticated buyer | Adjacent digital products, broker TMS |
| ELD-only, no TMS, paper BOLs | Small fleet, low maturity | Self-serve TMS / factoring / fuel card |
| Project44 + FourKites + Shippeo visibility stack | Enterprise shipper with multi-vendor visibility | Exception management, dwell analytics, carrier scorecarding |

## 5. Buyer Personas

### The VP Supply Chain (Shipper Side)
- **At**: Enterprise manufacturers, retailers, CPG, electronics — companies spending $50M-$1B+ annually on freight.
- **Cares about**: Total landed cost, service levels (on-time, in-full — OTIF), inventory turns, network resilience, sustainability Scope 3 reporting.
- **Pain**: Multi-tier visibility (tier-1 to tier-N suppliers), demand volatility, port congestion, supplier risk, freight rate negotiation.
- **Outreach angle**: OTIF benchmarks, landed cost reduction case studies, references from same-industry peers (e.g., peer CPG shipper reduced OTIF deduction penalties by $14M).
- **Channels**: LinkedIn, CSCMP Annual Conference, Gartner Supply Chain Symposium, peer roundtables.
- **What NOT to say**: "AI-powered supply chain" without specifying the use case and benchmark.

### The COO (3PL / Carrier)
- **At**: Mid-market to large 3PLs and asset-based carriers.
- **Cares about**: Operating ratio (OR), margin per load, asset utilization, driver productivity, safety scores, customer profitability.
- **Pain**: Carrier margin compression, shipper RFP velocity, capacity balancing, claims ratio, driver turnover (often 90%+ annually for OTR truckload).
- **Outreach angle**: OR improvement references, margin-per-load uplift, safety score (CSA) improvement tied to insurance savings.
- **Channels**: Email, ATA Management Conference, TIA Capital Ideas, peer COO network.

### The Director of Logistics / Transportation
- **At**: Shippers and 3PLs.
- **Cares about**: Carrier scorecard, lane rates, on-time pickup/delivery, freight claims, mode optimization, RFP execution.
- **Pain**: Manual tendering, carrier onboarding friction, freight bill audit accuracy, exception management, peak season capacity.
- **Outreach angle**: Specific KPI improvements (on-time pickup +3pp, claims ratio -20%), references from similar network size.
- **Channels**: LinkedIn, industry webinars, peer groups (Transportation Intermediaries Association).

### The Director of Procurement / Strategic Sourcing (Logistics)
- **At**: Shippers spending $100M+ on freight, also 3PLs buying carrier capacity.
- **Cares about**: Rate benchmarking, RFP cycle time, carrier diversification, contract compliance, fuel surcharge formula optimization.
- **Pain**: Lack of lane-level rate benchmarks (DAT, Truckstop, S&P Global Benchmarks are expensive), RFP response time, bid spreadsheet chaos.
- **Outreach angle**: Lane-level rate intelligence, automated RFP tooling, bid optimization case studies.

### The VP of Engineering / CIO (Logistics Tech)
- **At**: Mid-market and large 3PLs, freight tech companies, shipper logistics tech teams.
- **Cares about**: API-first architecture, integration scalability, observability, edge compute for IoT, AI/ML platform, security and compliance.
- **Pain**: Legacy TMS/WMS APIs, EDI translation overhead (X12, EDIFACT), real-time visibility data pipeline (Project44, FourKites, Shippeo), data warehouse unification.
- **Outreach angle**: Integration patterns, case studies (e.g., "how Maersk built a unified shipment data lake on Snowflake"), specific engineering KPIs (deploy frequency, p99 API latency).
- **Channels**: GitHub, engineering blogs, FreightWaves SmartSCM, Robin (logistics engineering community), Kubernetes Slack.

### The Director of Warehouse Operations / DC Manager
- **At**: 3PL warehouses, retailer DCs, manufacturer distribution centers.
- **Cares about**: Throughput per hour, units per labor hour (UPLH), order accuracy, dock-to-stock time, pick accuracy, returns processing time.
- **Pain**: Labor scheduling, peak-season surge, slotting inefficiency, RF scanner ergonomics, replenishment.
- **Outreach angle**: UPLH benchmarks, peak-season case studies, automation augmentation (not replacement).

### The Head of Sustainability / ESG
- **At**: Large shippers and 3PLs with public ESG commitments.
- **Cares about**: GLEC Framework adoption, Scope 3 Category 4 (upstream transportation) and Category 9 (downstream transportation) reporting, CDP score, SBTi validation.
- **Pain**: Carrier data quality (Smarter Freight Centre guidance, EPA SmartWay), book-and-claim accounting for sustainable aviation/marine fuels, EV/Hydrogen fleet transition ROI.
- **Outreach angle**: GLEC-aligned carbon calculation, references to peer CDP scores, EPA SmartWay partner onboarding.

## 7. Logistics Industry Vocabulary

Use the prospect's language. Logistics has deep jargon that signals insider status. Misuse any of these and you lose credibility instantly.

- **OR (Operating Ratio)** — Operating expenses / operating revenue. Lower is better; 95 means 5% operating margin.
- **OTIF (On-Time, In-Full)** — The shipper's master metric; typically 95% target.
- **OTD (On-Time Delivery)** / **OTP (On-Time Pickup)** — Carrier scorecard metrics.
- **Loaded/Empty miles** — Empty miles are deadhead; 15-25% empty is typical for truckload.
- **Bobtail** — Tractor without trailer. **Deadhead** — Truck driving empty to next load.
- **Drayage** — Short-haul port/rail terminal to warehouse.
- **Intermodal (IMDL)** — Container on rail + truck.
- **TEU / FEU** — Twenty-foot / Forty-foot Equivalent Unit (container volume).
- **LTL (Less-Than-Truckload)** — Multiple shippers' freight on one trailer (e.g., Old Dominion, XPO, Saia, Estes). Typically 150-15,000 lbs.
- **FTL / TL (Full Truckload)** — One shipper per trailer (e.g., J.B. Hunt, Schneider, Werner, Swift).
- **3PL / 4PL / LLP** — Third-Party Logistics / Fourth-Party / Lead Logistics Provider.
- **Bill of Lading (BOL)** — Legal shipping document. **MBOL** (Master BOL), **HBOL** (House BOL).
- **POD (Proof of Delivery)** — Required for invoice settlement.
- **Accessorial** — Extra fees (detention, lumper, layover, fuel surcharge).
- **Detention / Dwell** — Time a driver waits at shipper/receiver; billed after 2 hours typically.
- **Lumper** — Third-party loading/unloading service.
- **Capacity cycle** — Boom/bust freight market; 2-4 year cycle.
- **Spot rate vs. Contract rate** — Spot = one-off market rate; Contract = annual committed rate.
- **Tender** — Offer of a load to a carrier; **tender acceptance ratio** is a key shipper metric.
- **RFP / RFB (Request for Proposal / Bid)** — Annual shipper bid cycle, typically Aug-Nov for the following year.
- **ELD (Electronic Logging Device)** — Mandated HOS recording device since 2017.
- **HOS (Hours of Service)** — FMCSA driver hours rules; 11-hour driving, 14-hour window.
- **CSA scores** — FMCSA Compliance, Safety, Accountability scores; insurance pricing input.
- **GVWR / GCWR** — Gross Vehicle / Combination Weight Rating.
- **IMO 2020 / 2030** — International Maritime Organization sulphur and GHG rules.
- **CBP / ACE / HTS** — Customs and Border Protection / Automated Commercial Environment / Harmonized Tariff Schedule.
- **AMS / ISF / ENS** — Advance Manifest System / Importer Security Filing (10+2) / Entry Summary Declaration (EU).
- **VGM** — Verified Gross Mass (SOLAS requirement for ocean containers).
- **GLEC Framework** — Global Logistics Emissions Council methodology for Scope 3 logistics emissions.
- **SBTi** — Science Based Targets initiative; many shippers and carriers have validated targets.

Using these terms correctly signals you understand their world. Using them incorrectly (e.g., confusing LTL and FTL, or "deadhead" with "bobtail") signals you don't.

## 8. Common Logistics Prospecting Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Confusing Shipper with 3PL with Carrier
Pitching a TMS to a shipper that needs a TMS for parcel (ShipStation) vs. an LTL carrier that needs McLeod — these are different products, different buyers, different budgets. Always confirm segment first.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Capacity Cycle Context
Pitching "growth" tools to a carrier in a freight recession (like 2023-2024) fails; pitch cost-takeout. Pitching cost-takeout to a carrier in a capacity crunch (like 2024-2025 post-recession rebound) underperforms; pitch revenue capture and pricing optimization. Match the macro cycle.

### Mistake 3: Wrong Stakeholder
Emailing the CIO about a dock scheduling tool. Dock scheduling is bought by the DC Manager or VP Operations — not IT. Match the tool's value to the right functional owner.

### Mistake 4: Generic "AI for Logistics" Claims
"We use AI to optimize your supply chain" is meaningless. "We helped Estes Express reduce empty miles by 3.2% across 8,000 tractors using reinforcement learning on lane pairing — $14M annual savings" is meaningful. Always tie to a quantified outcome and named peer.

### Mistake 5: Ignoring Margin Reality
A truckload carrier with 95 OR (5% margin) cannot afford a $50K/year tool that doesn't return multiples. Always do the unit math before pitching: a tool that saves $0.01/mile across 100M annual miles = $1M savings, justifying a $200K annual cost.

### Mistake 6: Wrong Timing for RFP / Procurement Cycle
Most US shippers run their annual freight bid cycle Aug-Nov for the following year. Pitching a "new RFP optimization tool" in February misses the cycle by 6 months; pitch instead for "post-bid audit and spot market intelligence" in Q1.

### Mistake 7: Assuming All "Logistics" Companies Are the Same
A cold chain pharma 3PL is regulated under GDP and has a Director of Quality as a key buyer; a drayage carrier in Long Beach is buying chassis pools and ELDs. Don't spray generic messaging.

### Mistake 8: Forgetting the Driver / Operator Voice
Tools that increase driver friction (extra clicks, slower check-in) get abandoned within 30 days. Always cite driver-experience improvements alongside shipper/3PL ROI.

## 11. Qualification Criteria (BANT / MEDDIC for Logistics)

### Budget
- **Public/PE-backed carriers/3PLs** ($200M+ revenue): IT budget typically 1-2% of revenue; large enough for $200K-$2M annual deals.
- **Mid-market carriers/3PLs** ($15-200M revenue): Tech budget $200K-$2M; need ROI in 6-9 months.
- **Small fleets** ($2-15M): $20K-150K; self-serve or transactional.
- **Shippers spending $50M+ on freight**: Logistics tech budget typically 0.1-0.5% of freight spend.

### Authority
- **Decisions under $50K**: Director-level can approve.
- **$50K-500K**: VP-level, with Procurement review.
- **$500K+**: C-level + Procurement + IT Security + Legal.
- **Multi-year contracts**: Board / Investment Committee for >$5M.

### Need
- **Quantified pain**: OR below peer median; OTIF below 95%; driver turnover above 80%; manual tendering; visibility gaps.
- **Trigger**: Recent leadership change, M&A, capacity cycle pivot, regulatory deadline, ERP/TMS/WMS modernization.

### Timing
- **Best**: 90 days before annual RFP cycle (Aug-Oct for most US shippers); 60 days post-WMS/TMS go-live; 6 months post-PE acquisition; 90 days pre-regulatory deadline.
- **Worst**: Mid-RFP cycle; pre-earnings quiet period for public carriers.

### MEDDIC specific to logistics
- **Metrics**: OR, OTIF, OTD, empty miles %, claims ratio, CSA scores, dock-to-stock, UPLH, cost-per-delivery.
- **Economic Buyer**: COO or VP Supply Chain (carrier/3PL); VP Transportation or VP Supply Chain (shipper).
- **Decision Criteria**: ROI in <12 months, integration with existing TMS/WMS, peer references, data security, scalability.
- **Decision Process**: Pilot → IT security review → Procurement → Legal → contract → pilot → rollout.
- **Identify Pain**: Tie to public earnings commentary, peer benchmark gap, regulatory deadline.
- **Champion**: Director of Operations, Director of Engineering, or VP Operations; rarely the C-level.

## 12. Common Pitfalls Specific to Logistics

### Margin Compression Cycles
Logistics is structurally low-margin and cyclical. A carrier that was a great prospect in 2022 (capacity tight) may be in cost-cutting mode in 2024 (capacity loose). Always check current FreightWaves SONAR tender rejection index and DAT rate trends before pitching.

### Fuel Volatility
Fuel is 25-40% of carrier operating cost. Fuel price spikes compress margin unless fuel surcharge programs are well-designed. Tools that optimize fuel surcharge formulas or fuel card discounts are evergreen.

### Driver Shortage & Turnover
ATA estimates US driver shortage at 60,000-80,000+; turnover at large truckload carriers is 90%+ annually. Tools targeting driver retention (pay optimization, schedule design, equipment comfort) are evergreen; tools that increase driver friction are dead on arrival.

### Capacity Cycles (Boom/Bust)
Freight markets cycle every 2-4 years: tight capacity → rate spikes → carrier expansion → capacity loose → rate compression → carrier bankruptcies → tight capacity. Pitching must align with cycle phase: growth tools in tight, cost tools in loose.

### IMO 2020 / 2030 Compliance
IMO 2020 (low-sulphur fuel) cost ocean carriers $10B+ in compliance. IMO 2030 (GHG reduction) will require $1T+ investment in alt-fuel vessels. Ocean carrier prospects are deep in transition planning now.

### Port & Customs Disruption
West Coast port labor (ILWU) contracts, Red Sea / Suez disruption, Panama Canal drought — these events reshape freight flows in days. Prospecting in their aftermath (e.g., East Coast diversification, nearshoring to Mexico) is high-yield.

### Cybersecurity in OT/ICS
Maersk NotPetya ($300M loss), Forward Air ransomware,Expeditors cyber incident — logistics is a top-5 target. Security tools for OT/ICS (port cranes, warehouse robots, ELDs) are urgent.

### Long Procurement Cycles for Public Operators
UPS, FedEx, DHL, Maersk procurement runs 12-24 months with formal vendor management office. Don't expect a 30-day close; structure the deal for the long cycle with milestone-based SOW.

## 13. LeadReach Logistics Playbook Summary

1. **Identify sub-segment** (3PL, carrier, shipper, forwarder, last-mile, cold chain, warehouse, port, intermodal, parcel, private fleet) — determines buyer and budget.
2. **Determine operator size** (owner-op, small fleet, mid-market, enterprise, global integrator) — determines budget and cycle length.
3. **Detect tech stack** (TMS, WMS, OMS, ERP, visibility platform, ELD) — determines integration story and adjacency opportunities.
4. **Identify correct buyer persona** — match the tool to the functional owner (COO for ops, VP Supply Chain for shipper-side, Director of Logistics for tactical, CIO for integration).
5. **Scan for trigger events** — earnings calls, M&A, port expansions, regulatory deadlines, capacity cycle pivots, leadership changes, RFP season.
6. **Compose outreach** referencing specific trigger + specific persona pain + quantified peer outcome.
7. **Multi-touch sequence** (email + LinkedIn + phone + content) over 2-4 weeks; logistics buyers respond well to direct phone follow-up.
8. **Track funnel metrics** — delivery, open, reply, meeting-booked rates; adjust by sub-segment.
9. **Iterate** based on which sub-segments and trigger types convert best in your ICP.
10. **Avoid the cardinal sins** — don't confuse segments, don't ignore the capacity cycle, don't use generic "AI for logistics" claims, don't pitch to the wrong functional owner.
