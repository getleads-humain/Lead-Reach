# Insurance & InsurTech — B2B Lead Intelligence Guide

## 1. Industry Overview

Insurance is a **$7 trillion global market** that is undergoing its most significant technology transformation since the advent of actuarial science. The InsurTech segment alone is a $50B+ market growing at 25%+ CAGR, but it represents only a fraction of the technology spending by incumbent carriers, brokers, and MGAs. The industry's defining tension is between **centuries-old actuarial tradition and AI-driven risk assessment**, between **agent-based distribution and digital-first channels**, and between **regulatory conservatism and customer expectations shaped by consumer technology**.

Key characteristics:
- **Regulatory complexity** — Insurance is regulated at the state level in the US (50 state insurance departments); country-by-country internationally; every product, rate, and form must be filed and approved
- **Legacy core systems** — Most carriers run policy administration on 20-30 year-old mainframe systems (GUIDE, Duck Creek legacy, custom COBOL); modernization is a multi-year, multi-million dollar initiative
- **Data as competitive moat** — Actuaries have always been data scientists; AI/ML amplifies this advantage but requires modern data infrastructure
- **Distribution transformation** — Digital-first, embedded insurance, and direct-to-consumer channels are disrupting traditional agent/broker models
- **Claims as the moment of truth** — Claims experience drives retention; AI-powered claims processing is the #1 investment priority
- **Catastrophe risk acceleration** — Climate change is increasing CAT losses; risk modeling and reinsurance optimization are critical technology needs
- **Embedded insurance** — Insurance sold within non-insurance transactions (car rental, travel, e-commerce) is the fastest-growing distribution channel

## 2. Sub-Segment Deep Dives

### Core Insurance Platforms (PAS)
Policy Administration Systems are the ERP of insurance — managing products, rates, rules, and policy lifecycle. Key players: Guidewire (P&C market leader), Duck Creek (P&C), FAST (Verisk, P&C), Majesco (P&C and L&A), EIS Group (P&C), and FAST. Signals: core system modernization RFP, product launch velocity, and cloud migration mandates.

### Claims Management
Claims processing platforms from first notice of loss (FNOL) through settlement. Key players: Guidewire ClaimCenter, Duck Creek Claims, and Snapsheet (auto physical damage). AI-powered claims: Tractable (computer vision), Shift Technology (fraud detection), and Cognizant/EXL. Signals: claims severity increase, fraud spike, and customer satisfaction decline.

### Underwriting & Rating
AI-powered underwriting, risk scoring, and rating engines. Key players: Verisk (ISO), LexisNexis Risk Solutions, and emerging AI underwriting platforms (Cape Analytics, Arturo, Betterview). Signals: underwriting profitability decline, loss ratio deterioration, and AI underwriting pilot programs.

### Distribution & Agency Management
Platforms for agent/broker management, comparative rating, and digital distribution. Key players: Applied Systems (agency management), EZLynx (comparative rating), Vertafore (agency), and Bold Penguin (commercial quoting). Signals: distribution channel strategy shifts, agent productivity initiatives, and digital-first product launches.

### Reinsurance Technology
Platforms for reinsurance placement, claims, and analytics. Key players: Stone Ridge (Re), RenaissanceRe (DaVinci), and Tradex. Signals: treaty renewal cycles, CAT loss events, and capital management initiatives.

### Insurance Data & Analytics
Actuarial data, loss history, and risk modeling platforms. Key players: Verisk Analytics, Moody's RMS, CoreLogic, and Karen Clark & Company. Signals: CAT model refresh, loss estimation requirements, and risk appetite changes.

### Life & Annuity Platforms
L&A-specific policy administration, new business, and illustration platforms. Key players: FAST (Verisk), Majesco, EIS, and SE2 (now part of Vitech). Signals: product innovation cycles, regulatory changes (principle-based reserving), and demographic shifts.

### Parametric & Embedded Insurance
Trigger-based insurance products that pay automatically when predefined conditions are met. Key players: Descartes Underwriting, Raincoat, and Blink. Signals: embedded insurance partnerships, parametric product launches, and API-first distribution.

## 4. Buyer Personas

### The CIO / VP of Technology (Economic Buyer)
- **Role**: Sets technology strategy, manages core system decisions, controls IT budget
- **Reporting**: Reports to CEO or COO
- **Pain points**: Legacy core system limitations, cloud migration, integration complexity, cybersecurity, budget pressure
- **Buying trigger**: Core system end-of-life, competitive pressure, regulatory mandate, new CEO/COO mandate
- **What they care about**: System architecture, cloud readiness, integration ecosystem, vendor stability, implementation risk
- **How to reach**: IASA, InsureTech Connect, carrier CIO forums
- **Key metric**: IT spend as % of premium, system availability, implementation success rate

### The Chief Underwriting Officer (Economic Buyer — Underwriting)
- **Role**: Sets underwriting strategy, risk appetite, and pricing; evaluates underwriting technology
- **Reporting**: Reports to CEO or President
- **Pain points**: Loss ratio, underwriting profitability, speed-to-quote, data quality, talent shortage
- **Buying trigger**: Loss ratio deterioration, competitive pricing pressure, AI underwriting pilot, rate filing changes
- **What they care about**: Risk selection accuracy, pricing precision, underwriter productivity, regulatory compliance
- **How to reach**: RAA, PLUS, underwriting conferences
- **Key metric**: Loss ratio, combined ratio, quote-to-bind ratio

### The VP of Claims (Operational Buyer)
- **Role**: Manages claims operations, customer experience, and fraud detection
- **Reporting**: Reports to COO or CEO
- **Pain points**: Claims severity, fraud losses, customer satisfaction, adjuster productivity, litigation costs
- **Buying trigger**: Claims severity spike, fraud increase, customer satisfaction decline, CAT event
- **What they care about**: Claims cycle time, fraud detection accuracy, customer satisfaction, loss adjustment expenses
- **How to reach**: CLM conferences, CPCU Society, claims leadership forums
- **Key metric**: Claims cycle time, loss adjustment expense ratio, customer satisfaction (NPS)

### The Chief Distribution Officer / VP Agency (Buyer — Distribution)
- **Role**: Manages distribution strategy, agent relationships, and channel technology
- **Reporting**: Reports to CEO or COO
- **Pain points**: Agent productivity, commission management, digital channel growth, carrier-appointments
- **Buying trigger**: Distribution strategy shift, agent technology demands, digital-first product launch
- **What they care about**: Agent satisfaction, quote turnaround time, bind rate, channel economics
- **How to reach**: Big I conference, agency management system events, carrier distribution forums
- **Key metric**: Agent retention, premium per agent, quote-to-bind ratio, digital channel premium %

### The Chief Actuary (Influencer — Pricing & Risk)
- **Role**: Sets pricing strategy, reserves, and risk models; influences technology decisions
- **Reporting**: Reports to CEO or CFO
- **Pain points**: Pricing accuracy, rate adequacy, CAT modeling, data quality, regulatory rate approval
- **Buying trigger**: Rate inadequacy, CAT model refresh, loss trend changes, AI pricing pilot
- **What they care about**: Model accuracy, data quality, regulatory compliance, pricing sophistication
- **How to reach**: CAS conferences, actuarial societies, pricing forums
- **Key metric**: Rate adequacy, reserve accuracy, pricing model lift

## 5. Technographic Signals to Detect

### Insurance Stack Patterns by Maturity Level

**Legacy Carrier:**
- Mainframe-based PAS (COBOL, GUIDE, custom)
- On-premise data centers; limited cloud adoption
- Manual underwriting with spreadsheet rating
- Paper-based FNOL; adjuster-dependent claims
- Agent portal with limited self-service
- Batch-based reporting; limited real-time analytics

**Modernizing Carrier:**
- Guidewire or Duck Creek (on-premise or hosted)
- Hybrid cloud infrastructure
- Partial straight-through processing (STP) for simple products
- Digital FNOL with manual triage
- Agency management system integration
- Basic analytics and reporting dashboards

**Digital-Native / Technology-Forward Carrier:**
- Cloud-native PAS (EIS, Instanda, OneShield)
- AI-powered underwriting and fraud detection
- Full STP for standard risks
- Computer vision-powered claims (Tractable)
- API-first distribution (embedded insurance)
- Real-time analytics with actuarial data warehouse
- Parametric product capabilities

## 7. Insurance Industry Vocabulary & Jargon

- **P&C (Property & Casualty)** — Insurance covering property damage and liability
- **L&A (Life & Annuity)** — Life insurance and annuity products
- **DWP (Direct Written Premium)** — Total premium written by the carrier
- **Loss Ratio** — Incurred losses / Earned premium; key profitability metric
- **Combined Ratio** — Loss ratio + expense ratio; <100% = underwriting profit
- **PAS** — Policy Administration System; core insurance platform
- **FNOL** — First Notice of Loss; initial claim reporting
- **STP (Straight-Through Processing)** — Automated end-to-end processing without human intervention
- **CAT (Catastrophe)** — Large-scale natural disaster events
- **MGA (Managing General Agent)** — Specialized agent with underwriting authority
- **Treaty Reinsurance** — Automatic reinsurance coverage for a portfolio of risks
- **Admitted vs. Non-Admitted** — Licensed vs. surplus lines insurance
- **Rate Filing** — Regulatory submission for premium rate approval
- **Loss Adjustment Expense (LAE)** — Cost of investigating and settling claims
- **PML (Probable Maximum Loss)** — Expected maximum loss from a single event

## 9. Common Objections & Responses

### "Our core system has worked for 20 years — why change?"
**Response**: "Your current system has served you well, but it's limiting your ability to launch new products in weeks instead of months, offer digital-first experiences, or leverage AI for underwriting. We're not proposing a rip-and-replace — we can wrap your existing system with modern capabilities. [Reference carrier] kept their PAS and added our platform for [specific capability], reducing time-to-market from [X months] to [Y weeks]."

### "Insurance is regulated — we can't just implement new technology."
**Response**: "We understand regulatory requirements deeply. Our platform supports rate filing workflows, produces audit-ready documentation, and integrates with state filing systems. [Reference] operates in [X states] and passed regulatory examinations with our technology in place."

### "Our actuaries won't trust AI for pricing decisions."
**Response**: "Our AI doesn't replace actuarial judgment — it augments it with more granular risk signals and faster model iteration. Actuaries at [Reference carrier] use our platform to test rate hypotheses [X times faster] while maintaining full model transparency and regulatory documentation."

### "Claims adjusters are resistant to technology change."
**Response**: "Adjusters are our biggest advocates once they see the results. Our platform reduces manual data entry by [X%], provides AI-assisted damage assessment, and accelerates settlement — which means happier customers and less overtime. [Reference] saw [Y%] adjuster adoption in 60 days."

### "The implementation risk is too high — carrier transformations have high failure rates."
**Response**: "Legacy transformations do have high failure rates — which is why we've developed a phased approach that delivers value incrementally. [Reference] went live with our platform in [X months] for [first product line], then expanded to [Y additional lines] over [Z months]. No big-bang cutover, no weekend go-live risk."

### "We can't afford the investment in the current rate environment."
**Response**: "In a hard market, efficiency gains are even more valuable. Every point of combined ratio improvement is worth [$X million] to your bottom line. Our platform has helped [reference] improve their combined ratio by [Y points] through [underwriting accuracy, claims efficiency, and expense reduction]."

## Regulatory & Compliance Considerations

### US Insurance Regulation
- **McCarran-Ferguson Act** — State-level insurance regulation; federal deference
- **State insurance departments** — 50+ jurisdictions; rate and form filing requirements
- **NAIC Model Laws** — Standardized regulations adopted by states (with variation)
- **Risk-Based Capital (RBC)** — Capital adequacy requirements for carriers
- **ORSA (Own Risk and Solvency Assessment)** — Enterprise risk management requirements
- **Privacy laws** — GLBA for financial data; state privacy laws for consumer data
- **Unfair claims practices acts** — State-level claims handling requirements

### AI in Insurance Regulation
- **Colorado SB 205** — Insurance AI regulation requiring bias testing and consumer notification
- **NAIC AI model bulletin** — Guidance on AI use in underwriting and claims
- **NY DFS Circular Letter** — AI and external consumer data guidance
- **EU AI Act** — Insurance AI may be classified as high-risk
- **State anti-discrimination laws** — AI must not create unfair discrimination in insurance

### International
- **Solvency II (EU)** — Capital requirements and risk management for EU insurers
- **IAIS** — International Association of Insurance Supervisors; global standards
- **UK PRA/FCA** — Prudential Regulation Authority and Financial Conduct Authority
- **OSFI (Canada)** — Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions

## Regional Variations

### United States
- State-level regulation creates 50+ compliance jurisdictions
- Largest insurance market globally; most mature InsurTech ecosystem
- P&C dominates technology investment; L&A catching up
- Strong reinsurance market; Bermuda hub
- Growing AI regulation (Colorado, Connecticut, NAIC)

### Europe
- Solvency II framework; more centralized than US
- Stronger consumer protection; GDPR for policyholder data
- Embedded insurance growth driven by Open Insurance initiatives
- UK: Post-Brexit Solvency UK reform; FCA Consumer Duty
- EIOPA oversight for EU-wide consistency

### Asia-Pacific
- **China**: CIRC regulation; massive market; government-led InsurTech adoption
- **Japan**: FSA regulation; aging population driving health/life innovation
- **India**: IRDAI regulation; massive uninsured population; mobile-first distribution
- **Australia**: APRA regulation; natural disaster exposure; parametric growth
- **Southeast Asia**: Underpenetrated markets; mobile distribution opportunity
