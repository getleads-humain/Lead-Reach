# Media — B2B Lead Intelligence Guide

## 1. Industry Overview

Media is a **$2.5 trillion+ global industry** spanning publishing, broadcasting, streaming, advertising technology, social media, and the creator economy. The industry is defined by **hit-driven economics** (a tiny fraction of content generates the vast majority of revenue), **advertising cyclicality** (ad spend drops 10-20% in recessions and shifts toward performance channels), **platform power** (Google and Meta control 50%+ of global digital ad spend), the **creator economy disruption** (individual creators are becoming media companies), and **AI-driven content creation** that is accelerating production while creating existential questions about originality and copyright.

Key characteristics:
- **Hit-driven** — 5% of content generates 80%+ of revenue. This applies to articles, videos, podcasts, and streaming titles. The implication for B2B selling: media companies are always looking for the next hit, and tools that improve hit rate or reduce miss cost have strong appeal.
- **Advertising is cyclical** — Ad spend tracks GDP with a 1.2x multiplier. In downturns, brand advertising cuts first; performance advertising is more resilient. This matters for timing outreach.
- **Platform dependency** — Every media company depends on platforms (Google, Meta, Apple, Amazon, TikTok) for distribution. Platform policy changes can destroy business models overnight.
- **Talent-driven** — Key creators, editors, and showrunners have outsized influence. Retention and monetization of talent is a core challenge.
- **Measurement complexity** — Attribution, viewability, and identity resolution remain unsolved problems. Tools that improve measurement have high demand.
- **AI disruption is existential** — Generative AI threatens traditional content creation while enabling new formats. Media companies are both the most excited and most fearful buyers of AI technology.

The buying motion differs by sub-segment:
- **Selling to publishers**: CRO / VP Revenue-led, 2-6 month cycles. Revenue diversification (subscriptions, commerce, events) drives technology demand.
- **Selling to streaming platforms**: VP Product / VP Engineering-led, 3-9 month cycles. Scale, personalization, and content recommendation drive technology decisions.
- **Selling to creator economy companies**: CTO / VP Product-led, 2-8 week cycles. Speed-to-market and creator satisfaction are the currencies.
- **Selling to ad tech**: VP Product / CTO-led, 2-4 month cycles. Technical depth and integration speed matter most.

LeadReach is best positioned for digital publishers, mid-market streaming platforms, creator economy infrastructure, and ad tech companies. Tier-1 media conglomerates (Disney, Comcast, Warner Bros. Discovery) require dedicated enterprise motions.

## 4. Buyer Personas

### Chief Revenue Officer
- **At**: Digital publisher or media company
- **Cares about**: Total revenue, revenue per user (ARPU), subscription growth, ad yield, revenue diversification, LTV
- **Pain**: Programmatic CPM decline, subscription churn, cookie deprecation, dependence on platform traffic, advertiser concentration
- **Outreach angle**: Revenue diversification strategies; ARPU improvement from peer publishers; subscription and ad revenue optimization
- **Channels**: Email, Digiday events, AdMonsters, LinkedIn
- **What works**: Specific revenue impact metrics ("increased ARPU by 18% at [peer publisher]"); subscription + ad revenue case studies; first-party data monetization proof
- **What fails**: Not understanding the subscription vs. advertising revenue tradeoff; ignoring cookie deprecation impact; generic monetization language

### VP Product
- **At**: Streaming platform, social media company, or publisher
- **Cares about**: User engagement, retention, personalization, recommendation quality, feature velocity, platform performance
- **Pain**: Content recommendation accuracy, churn after content consumption, A/B testing velocity, feature prioritization, platform scalability, AI integration
- **Outreach angle**: Engagement and retention improvement from peer platforms; recommendation algorithm optimization; A/B testing frameworks
- **Channels**: LinkedIn, Product School events, Medium/Swegle, email
- **What works**: Specific engagement metrics ("improved 30-day retention by 12%"); technical depth on recommendation systems; peer platform case studies
- **What fails**: Marketing-heavy pitches; not understanding recommendation engine architecture; ignoring the engagement-retention relationship

### VP Ad Operations
- **At**: Publisher, streaming platform, or ad tech company
- **Cares about**: Ad yield, fill rate, viewability, brand safety, programmatic efficiency, header bidding optimization, CTV ad operations
- **Pain**: Cookie deprecation impact on targeting, CTV measurement complexity, supply path optimization, brand safety incidents, ad fraud, header bidding latency
- **Outreach angle**: Yield optimization metrics; identity resolution solutions; CTV ad operations efficiency from peer companies
- **Channels**: Email, AdMonsters, Programmatic I/O, LinkedIn
- **What works**: Specific yield improvement ("increased programmatic yield by 22%"); header bidding optimization proof; CTV measurement case studies
- **What fails**: Not understanding the buy-side/sell-side dynamic; ignoring identity resolution challenges; generic ad tech language

### Head of Creator Partnerships
- **At**: Creator economy platform or talent management company
- **Cares about**: Creator acquisition, retention, monetization, satisfaction, brand deal pipeline, community health
- **Pain**: Platform dependency risk, creator churn, brand deal pricing transparency, content repurposing workflow, audience data access, creator burnout
- **Outreach angle**: Creator retention improvement; brand deal volume growth; creator satisfaction metrics from peer platforms
- **Channels**: Email, VidCon, Creator Economy Live, LinkedIn
- **What works**: Creator-specific metrics ("reduced creator churn by 25%"); brand deal pipeline growth case studies; platform diversification strategies
- **What fails**: Not understanding creator economics; ignoring platform dependency anxiety; treating creators like employees

## 7. Media Vocabulary & Jargon

### Advertising & Monetization
- **CPM** — Cost Per Mille (cost per thousand impressions)
- **RPM** — Revenue Per Mille (publisher-side revenue metric)
- **ARPU** — Average Revenue Per User
- **LTV** — Lifetime Value (subscriber or user)
- **SAC** — Subscriber Acquisition Cost
- **Churn rate** — Percentage of subscribers who cancel per period
- **Fill rate** — Percentage of ad inventory filled with paid ads
- **Viewability** — Percentage of ads actually seen by users (IAB standard: 50% of pixels in view for 1 second)
- **Header bidding** — Technique allowing multiple demand sources to bid simultaneously
- **Prebid** — Open-source header bidding framework
- **Programmatic guaranteed** — Automated direct deal between buyer and publisher

### Content & Distribution
- **Paywall** — Gated access requiring subscription
- **Metered paywall** — Allows N free articles before requiring subscription
- **Dynamic paywall** — AI-driven paywall that adjusts based on user behavior
- **First-party data** — Data collected directly from users (increasingly valuable post-cookie)
- **Zero-party data** — Data voluntarily provided by users (preferences, interests)
- **Contextual targeting** — Ad targeting based on content context, not user data
- **Content recommendation** — Algorithmic suggestions for what to read/watch next
- **Engagement depth** — Measure of how deeply users interact with content

### Streaming
- **AVOD** — Ad-supported Video on Demand
- **SVOD** — Subscription Video on Demand
- **FAST** — Free Ad-Supported Streaming TV
- **OTT** — Over-The-Top (delivered via internet, not cable)
- **SSAI** — Server-Side Ad Insertion (ad stitching for seamless viewing)
- **CSAI** — Client-Side Ad Insertion (traditional ad delivery)
- **CDN** — Content Delivery Network
- **QoE** — Quality of Experience (streaming performance metric)
- **Bitrate** — Data rate of video stream (higher = better quality)
- **Windowing** — Content release strategy across platforms and time periods

### Creator & Social
- **UGC** — User-Generated Content
- **MCN** — Multi-Channel Network
- **FYP** — For You Page (TikTok algorithmic feed)
- **Engagement rate** — Interactions / Followers or Impressions
- **Creator economy** — Ecosystem of independent content creators
- **Brand deal** — Paid partnership between creator and brand
- **Affiliate** — Performance-based commission on referred sales

## 9. Outreach Best Practices & Sequencing

### Channel Sequencing for Media

Media buyers respond to **audience and revenue insights** before product pitches. Recommended sequence:

- **Day 0**: LinkedIn connection — reference a recent Digiday article, their company's content launch, or a revenue milestone.
- **Day 3**: Cold email #1 — 120 words, lead with a media-specific insight (CPM trend, subscription benchmark, cookie deprecation update), no product pitch.
- **Day 7**: LinkedIn engagement — comment on a post about media revenue, creator economy, or ad tech trends.
- **Day 10**: Cold email #2 — case study from same media sub-vertical (e.g., digital publisher, streaming), mention specific revenue or engagement metrics.
- **Day 14**: Share a Digiday, AdExchanger, or Nieman Lab article relevant to their business.
- **Day 17**: Cold call — reference the article share and the revenue insight.
- **Day 21**: Cold email #3 — breakup email with a specific metric ("publishers using LeadReach identify 40% more high-CPM advertiser opportunities").
- **Day 28**: Final touch — invite to a media industry roundtable or webinar.

Expected meeting-booked rate: **1.8-3.0%** (higher for ad tech and creator economy, lower for publishers with editorial gatekeepers).

## 10. Regulatory & Compliance Notes

- **GDPR**: Strict consent requirements for ad targeting and user tracking. Media companies serving EU audiences must comply. Cookie consent management is table stakes.
- **CCPA/CPRA**: California privacy regulations. Opt-out rights for data sale. Applies to media companies with CA users.
- **DSA (Digital Services Act)**: EU regulation requiring content moderation, transparency reporting, and risk assessment for large online platforms (VLOPs). Meta, Google, TikTok are designated VLOPs.
- **Online Safety Act (UK)**: Duty of care requirements for user-generated content platforms. Content moderation and reporting obligations.
- **COPPA**: Children's Online Privacy Protection Act — applies to platforms with users under 13 (YouTube Kids, TikTok).
- **Copyright / DMCA**: Content licensing and takedown requirements. AI-generated content raises new copyright questions.
- **FTC Endorsement Guidelines**: Disclosure requirements for sponsored content and creator partnerships. #ad compliance.
- **GARM (Global Alliance for Responsible Media)**: Industry framework for brand safety standards. Not law but widely adopted by major advertisers.
- **FCC regulations**: Apply to broadcast media. Less relevant for digital-first companies but relevant for streaming services with live content.
- **AI content regulations**: Emerging — EU AI Act, state-level AI regulations. Deepfake and AI-generated content disclosure requirements.

LeadReach agents should NOT:
- Target individual content consumers for B2B products (consumer privacy regulations)
- Access or store individual user viewing/reading data
- Make claims about ad performance that LeadReach cannot substantiate
- Facilitate undisclosed sponsored content or native advertising
- Ignore platform Terms of Service when scraping or researching

## 13. Regional Variants

- **US**: Largest media market ($800B+). Dominated by tech platforms (Google, Meta, Amazon) and traditional media conglomerates (Disney, Comcast, WBD). Programmatic advertising is mature. Subscription economy is well-established.
- **UK**: Strong publishing tradition (BBC, Financial Times, The Guardian). DSA and Online Safety Act driving compliance demand. CTV adoption is high.
- **EU**: Fragmented market with strong public broadcasters. DSA compliance is the primary technology driver. GDPR makes ad targeting harder. Regional publishers are underserved.
- **APAC**: China (closed ecosystem — WeChat, Douyin, Bilibili), Japan (LINE, NicoNico), India (Jio, Hotstar), Southeast Asia (diverse, mobile-first). Significant localization requirements.
- **Latin America**: Fast-growing digital advertising market. WhatsApp is a media platform. Streaming adoption is high (Globoplay, Paramount+). Mobile-first audience.
- **Middle East**: MBC Group, Shahid (streaming). High mobile penetration. Young demographic. Content localization demand.

See `regions/` for detailed regulatory playbooks.

## 15. Common Media Prospecting Mistakes

- **Not knowing the revenue model** — Pitching subscription tools to an ad-supported publisher (or vice versa) signals you don't understand the business. Revenue model determines everything.
- **Ignoring editorial culture** — Many media companies have strong editorial independence. Pitching "audience targeting" to an editor who values editorial judgment is insulting.
- **Confusing DSP and SSP** — These are fundamentally different businesses. Pitching a sell-side tool to a buy-side company (or vice versa) destroys credibility.
- **Not understanding cookie deprecation impact** — This is the #1 concern for digital publishers. Not referencing it signals you're out of touch.
- **Generic "media" language** — "We help media companies" means nothing. "We help digital publishers increase high-CPM advertiser pipeline by 40%" means everything.
- **Ignoring platform dependency** — Every media company is terrified of Google/Meta algorithm changes. Not acknowledging this anxiety is a miss.
- **Underestimating the creator shift** — The creator economy isn't a niche; it's the fastest-growing segment of media. Not mentioning it signals you don't understand the current landscape.
- **Missing the ARPU/CPM vocabulary** — If you don't speak in CPMs, ARPU, and LTV, media buyers won't take you seriously.
- **Not tracking Digiday/AdExchanger** — These are the industry's information gatekeepers. Not referencing their reporting signals outsider status.
- **Pitching to journalists** — For B2B sales, target the CRO, VP Ad Ops, or VP Product. Journalists don't buy business tools.
