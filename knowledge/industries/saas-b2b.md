# SaaS B2B — Industry Intelligence

## 1. Market Shape

SaaS B2B is the canonical LeadReach customer profile. The vertical is defined by **subscription recurring revenue**, **land-and-expand motion**, and **net retention as the dominant financial metric**. Public-market SaaS companies trade on a multiple of ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) that compresses when net retention drops below 100% and expands when it exceeds 120%.

The buying motion is consensus-driven. A typical SaaS deal at the SMB-to-mid-market tier involves 3–6 stakeholders; enterprise deals involve 8–12. The economic buyer is usually a VP or C-level executive, but the technical buyer is often a director-level engineering, ops, or rev-ops leader. Deals stall when the seller addresses only one of these two roles.

SaaS companies buy in three distinct waves:
1. **Seed → Series A**: tooling for the founding team (CRM, analytics, internal tools). Decisions made in days, by founders, on price + speed.
2. **Series B → C**: tooling to scale the GTM motion (sales engagement, lead scoring, intent data). Decisions made in weeks, by VPs of Sales/Marketing, on ROI proof.
3. **Series D+**: tooling to optimize and defend (data warehouses, observability, security). Decisions made in months, by CIO/CISO, on compliance + integration depth.

LeadReach is best positioned for wave 2 — companies that have product-market fit and need to scale their outbound motion.

## 5. Buying Triggers

- **Funding announcement** within last 90 days — highest-signal trigger.
- **Hiring SDR/BDR** roles on the company's careers page within last 30 days.
- **CRO / VP Sales hire** announced on LinkedIn within last 60 days.
- **New product launch** (press release or Product Hunt feature).
- **Leadership change** at the C-suite (CEO, CRO, CMO).
- **Recent funding round decline** (FinTech specifically — they need to do more with less).
- **Acquisition closed** — they will be integrating systems and re-evaluating vendors.
- **Public earnings call** mentioning "improve pipeline" or "sales efficiency".
- **G2 review velocity** — sudden spike in negative reviews of a competitor.

## 7. Channels & Sequencing

The most effective outbound motion for SaaS B2B is a **3-channel, 21-day sequence**:

- **Day 0**: LinkedIn connection request (no pitch) — 2-line personalized note.
- **Day 2**: Cold email #1 — 90 words, problem-led, soft CTA ("worth a 15-min call next week?").
- **Day 5**: LinkedIn engagement — like + comment on a recent post.
- **Day 7**: Cold email #2 — case study from their industry + vertical.
- **Day 10**: Cold call — reference the emails, ask for 15 minutes.
- **Day 14**: Cold email #3 — breakup email ("should I close your file?").
- **Day 18**: LinkedIn direct message — soft re-engagement.
- **Day 21: Final email** — value-add resource (no ask).

Sequence produces ~3% meeting-booked rate when the ICP is tight and the data is fresh. Below 1% means either the ICP is wrong or the data is stale.

## 8. Regulatory & Compliance Notes

- **GDPR (EU)**: Cold email requires either B2B legitimate interest (member state varies) or explicit consent. Germany is strictest; France mid; UK lenient post-Brexit. See `regions/eu-gdpr.md`.
- **CCPA/CPRA (California)**: Sale of personal data is restricted; "sharing" for cross-context behavioral advertising requires opt-out. B2B contact data is generally exempt but check with counsel.
- **CASL (Canada)**: Implied consent for B2B lasts 2 years from last interaction; explicit consent is preferred.
- **SOC 2 / ISO 27001**: Enterprise SaaS buyers will require a SOC 2 Type II report before signing. Have one ready or be prepared to walk.
- **DPA (Data Processing Addendum)**: Have a standard DPA template ready; enterprise buyers will redline it.

## 9. Regional Variants

- **US**: Fastest sales cycle, highest ACV, most competitive.
- **EU**: 30–50% longer cycle, lower ACV, privacy-first messaging required.
- **UK**: Between US and EU; GDPR applies post-Brexit (UK GDPR).
- **APAC (Singapore, Australia)**: Relationship-first; expect 2x cycle length vs US.
- **LATAM**: Price-sensitive; expect 40–60% lower ACV.

## 11. Competitive Landscape

LeadReach competes with — and complements — a layered stack:

- **Direct competitors**: Apollo, ZoomInfo, Cognism, Lusha, Lead411. These sell data + light sequencing. LeadReach differentiates on agent-driven research depth.
- **Adjacent tools (complement)**: Outreach, Salesloft, Lemlist, Reply.io. These execute sequences; LeadReach feeds them better targets.
- **CRM**: Salesforce, HubSpot. LeadReach integrates; does not compete.
- **Intent data**: Bombora, 6sense, G2 Buyer Intent. LeadReach consumes intent signals; does not produce them.
- **Conversational intelligence**: Gong, Chorus. LeadReach does not compete.

The key positioning line: **"LeadReach finds your next 100 customers before your competitors do — using agents that research like your best SDR, at 1000x the speed."**
