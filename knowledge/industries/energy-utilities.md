# Energy & Utilities Industry — Prospecting Guide, Signals, and Buyer Personas

## 1. Industry Overview

The global energy and utilities industry is a **$8+ trillion market** spanning upstream oil and gas exploration and production, midstream pipelines and terminals, downstream refining and petrochemicals, power generation (thermal, nuclear, hydro, wind, solar, geothermal, biomass), transmission and distribution (T&D) utilities, retail energy providers, and the rapidly emerging grid-edge and cleantech ecosystem. The industry produces ~620 quadrillion BTUs of primary energy annually, employs 30+ million people globally, and accounts for roughly 40% of global greenhouse gas emissions — making it simultaneously the most strategically important and most regulated industry on the planet.

Three macro forces are reshaping the industry simultaneously. **The energy transition** is real and accelerating: global clean energy investment exceeded $2 trillion in 2024 (twice total fossil fuel investment), with the IEA forecasting renewables will overtake coal as the largest source of electricity by early 2026. The Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) commits $369B+ to US clean energy over a decade, while the EU's REPowerEU plan and Green Deal commit €300B+, and China continues to dominate solar/wind/battery manufacturing at 70-85% global share. **Commodity volatility** continues to reshape strategies: oil prices swung from negative in April 2020 to $120+/bbl after the 2022 Russia-Ukraine invasion, then settled in the $70-90 range; European gas prices spiked 10x in 2022 (TTF hub) before normalizing; lithium carbonate prices spiked 5x in 2022 then collapsed 80% in 2023-2024. **Geopolitical fragmentation** — OPEC+ production cuts, US-China trade tensions, Middle East shipping disruption, Russian sanctions, and energy security concerns post-2022 — has made every energy buyer think about resilience, not just cost.

The energy buyer is **engineer-led, safety-obsessed, regulator-aware, and capex-cycle bound**. They run on decades-long asset lifecycles (a new LNG terminal takes 5-7 years and $10-20B; a new nuclear plant takes 10-15 years and $20-30B), so any pitch must account for project lifecycles, regulatory approvals, and capital allocation windows. They trust engineering firms (Bechtel, Fluor, McDermott, Worley) and Tier-1 service companies (Schlumberger, Halliburton, Baker Hughes, Weatherford) far more than analyst reports. Generic "AI for energy" outreach fails; specific, citation-backed outreach tied to a project announcement, FERC order, or IRA tax credit qualification wins.

The industry is also undergoing a digital transformation that is decades overdue. Operational technology (OT) and industrial control systems (ICS) that historically ran isolated SCADA networks are now being connected to IT and cloud for predictive maintenance, digital twins, and AI-driven optimization. Cybersecurity is a top-3 priority after Colonial Pipeline (2021), the Ukraine power grid attacks (2015, 2016), and the surge in ransomware targeting utilities. The IT/OT convergence creates new prospecting surface for vendors who understand both worlds and can speak fluently about NERC CIP, IEC 62443, ATEX/IECEx, and API standards.

## 2. Energy Sub-Segments

Energy is a federation of seven major sub-segments, each with distinct buyers, sales cycles, and budget sources. Mis-segmenting is the most common prospecting error — pitching a refining turnaround tool to a wind developer, or a smart metering platform to an upstream producer, signals you don't understand the industry.

### Upstream Oil & Gas (Exploration & Production)
Companies that explore for and produce oil and natural gas. Examples: ExxonMobil, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, Occidental, EOG Resources, Pioneer Natural Resources (now ExxonMobil), Devon Energy, Hess, Marathon Oil (now ConocoPhillips), Shell, BP, TotalEnergies, Eni, Equinor, Saudi Aramco, ADNOC, Petrobras, Reliance, ONGC. The global upstream market is ~$3.5T revenue, characterized by long asset lifecycles (10-30 year fields), volatile margins tied to commodity prices, and capital intensity ($5-50M per onshore well; $100M-$2B per offshore platform).
- **Buyer profile**: VP Operations, Director of Asset Integrity, Director of Drilling & Completions, Director of Reservoir Engineering, Head of Digital/IT, Head of HSE (Health, Safety, Environment).
- **Sales cycle**: 6-18 months for SaaS/tools; 12-36 months for major software platforms; multi-decade asset partnerships for major service contracts.
- **Budget source**: Operating budget (typically 30-50% of revenue for operating expenses) + capex (varies by cycle, $5-50M per well).
- **Pain points**: Reserve replacement ratios, decline curve management, methane emissions compliance (EPA Subpart W, OOOOb/c, EU Methane Regulation), flaring reduction, well productivity, drilling cost per foot, frac efficiency, asset integrity (corrosion, scaling), carbon capture tax credits (45Q).

### Midstream Oil & Gas (Pipelines, Terminals, Storage)
Companies transporting and storing crude oil, natural gas, NGLs, refined products, and CO2. Examples: Kinder Morgan, Energy Transfer, Enterprise Products, Williams, Plains All American, Enbridge, TC Energy, ONEOK, EQT Midstream, Energy Transfer, Magellan, Buckeye Partners, Colonial Pipeline. The US midstream market alone is ~$250B revenue and growing modestly with LNG export expansion and CO2 pipeline buildout for CCUS.
- **Buyer profile**: VP Operations, Director of Pipeline Integrity, Director of Operations Engineering, Head of HSE, Director of Commercial/Marketing, CIO.
- **Sales cycle**: 6-18 months for SaaS/tools; 12-36 months for major SCADA/leak detection replacements.
- **Budget source**: Operating budget + capex (for new pipeline builds, $1-10M/mile).
- **Pain points**: Pipeline integrity management (PHMSA Part 192/195), leak detection (API RP 1130, API RP 1175), in-line inspection (ILI) scheduling, methane emissions (EPA OOOOb/c), compressor station efficiency, cyber/OT security (TSA Security Directives after Colonial Pipeline), hydrostatic testing, regulatory reporting.

### Downstream Oil & Gas (Refining, Petrochemicals, Retail)
Companies processing crude into refined products and petrochemicals. Examples: ExxonMobil, Chevron, Shell, BP, Phillips 66, Valero, Marathon Petroleum, PBF Energy, HollyFrontier, TotalEnergies, Reliance (Jamnagar — world's largest refinery), Sinopec, Formosa Plastics, Dow, LyondellBasell, INEOS, Sabic. Global refining capacity is ~102 million bpd; petrochemicals are ~$600B market growing 4-5% annually.
- **Buyer profile**: Refinery Manager, VP Operations, Director of Process Engineering, Director of Reliability, Director of Turnarounds, Director of HSE, Head of Commercial/Trading.
- **Sales cycle**: 6-18 months for SaaS/tools; 12-36 months for major automation/control system replacements; turnarounds planned 18-36 months ahead.
- **Budget source**: Operating budget (refining margin-driven, typically $5-15/bbl) + capex (turnarounds $20-200M each; major upgrades $500M-$2B+).
- **Pain points**: Refinery turnaround planning and execution, energy intensity (refineries use 6-8% of their throughput as fuel), emissions compliance (EPA NSPS, EU BREF), carbon capture readiness, hydrogen production for decarbonization, biofuels co-processing (Renewable Diesel, SAF), reliability (mechanical availability, MTBF), process safety (API RP 754, PSV reliability).

### Power Generation (Thermal, Nuclear, Hydro, Renewables)
Companies producing electricity from any source. Includes investor-owned utilities (IOUs), independent power producers (IPPs), municipal utilities, cooperatives, and merchant generators. Examples: NextEra Energy, Duke Energy, Southern Company, Dominion, Vistra, NRG, Calpine, Berkshire Hathaway Energy, American Electric Power, Exelon, Southern Co, Engie, EDF, E.ON, RWE, Enel, Iberdrola, Ørsted, TEPCO, KEPCO, State Grid Corporation of China.
- **Buyer profile**: VP Operations, Director of Generation, Director of Asset Management, Director of Engineering, Director of Renewables Development, Head of Grid Operations, CIO/CTO.
- **Sales cycle**: 6-18 months for software/tools; 12-36 months for major SCADA/EMS replacements; renewable project development is 18-48 months from origination to commercial operation.
- **Budget source**: Operating budget (rate case-regulated for utilities) + capex (regulated utilities recover through rate base; merchant generators through project finance).
- **Pain points**: Plant availability/reliability (NERC GADS reporting), heat rate optimization, forced outage reduction, emissions compliance (EPA Clean Air Act, EU IED), renewable integration (intermittency forecasting, curtailment), grid stability (inverter-based resources, grid-forming inverters), nuclear license renewal, coal plant retirement economics, hydrogen co-firing, BESS (battery energy storage) integration.

### Transmission & Distribution (T&D) Utilities
Companies operating the high-voltage transmission and local distribution grids. Examples: AEP, PJM Interconnection (RTO), MISO, CAISO, ERCOT, ISO-NE, NYISO, SPP, National Grid (UK/US), SSE, Iberdrola (Avangrid), Enel, E.ON, TenneT, Amprion, RTE (France), TEPCO, KEPCO. The global T&D market is ~$400B annually and growing 6-8% with grid modernization, electrification, and renewable interconnection.
- **Buyer profile**: VP Operations, Director of Grid Operations / Control Center, Director of Asset Management, Director of System Planning, Director of Smart Grid, Head of Distribution Automation, CIO/CISO, Head of AMI/Smart Metering.
- **Sales cycle**: 6-24 months for SaaS/tools; 12-48 months for major system replacements; capital projects 5-15 years.
- **Budget source**: Rate base recovery (regulated utilities) + DOE grants (DOE GDO programs, IIJA grid funding) + capex plans (multi-year rate cases).
- **Pain points**: Grid modernization (FERC Order 881 ambient line ratings, FERC 2222 distributed energy resources, FERC 1920 transmission planning), grid resilience (wildfire, storms, physical security), NERC CIP compliance, DER integration (rooftop solar, EVs, batteries), distribution automation (FLISR — Fault Location, Isolation, and Service Restoration), Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) deployment, workforce transition (lineman shortage), cyber-OT convergence.

### Renewables (Solar, Wind, Hydro, Storage, Green Hydrogen)
Companies developing, owning, and operating renewable generation. Includes utility-scale developers (NextEra Energy Resources, Invenergy, Pattern Energy, EDF Renewables, Clearway Energy, AES Clean Energy, Brookfield Renewable, Iberdrola Renewables, Ørsted, Enel Green Power), residential/commercial solar (Sunrun, Sunnova, SunPower, Tesla Energy, SolarEdge, Enphase), and storage (Fluence, Tesla Megapack, BYD, CATL, Plus Power, Key Capture Energy, AES). Global renewable investment exceeded $2T in 2024 with the IEA forecasting 5,500 GW of new renewable capacity 2024-2030.
- **Buyer profile**: VP Project Development, Director of Operations (O&M), Director of Asset Management, Director of Origination/PPA, Director of Interconnection, Head of Engineering, CTO.
- **Sales cycle**: 60-180 days for SaaS/tools; renewable project development 18-48 months.
- **Budget source**: Project finance (tax equity, debt, sponsor equity) + IRA tax credits (ITC, PTC, 45Y, 48E, 45U, 45V for hydrogen, 45Q for CCUS) + operating budgets.
- **Pain points**: Project origination (interconnection queue wait times now 3-5 years in PJM, MISO, CAISO), permitting timelines, supply chain (solar module tariffs, polysilicon sourcing, wind turbine supply), curtailment, asset performance management (APM), battery dispatch optimization, IRA tax credit transferability, community engagement, O&M cost reduction.

### Grid Edge & Smart Metering (DERMS, VPPs, EV Charging)
Companies providing technology at the grid edge: Distributed Energy Resource Management Systems (DERMS), Virtual Power Plants (VPPs), EV charging networks, smart inverters, demand response aggregators. Examples: Sunrun (VPP), Tesla Energy (VPP, Powerwall), Stem Inc., AutoGrid (now Schneider), Spirae, Octopus Energy (Kraken), Ovo, Sense, Bidgely, Itron, Landis+Gyr, Aclara, Sensus (Xylem), Schneider EcoStruxure, GE Digital (now Black & Veatch partnership).
- **Buyer profile**: Director of DERMS / Smart Grid, Director of Demand Response, Director of EV Programs, Director of Customer Programs, Head of Innovation, CTO.
- **Sales cycle**: 6-18 months for SaaS; 12-36 months for utility pilots → full deployment.
- **Budget source**: Utility rate base + DOE grants (DOE GDO Grid Resilience Program, IIJA $65B for grid) + IRA direct pay for non-profits/co-ops.
- **Pain points**: DER integration (FERC 2222), demand flexibility programs, EV managed charging, grid-interactive efficient buildings (GEB), customer engagement, AMI data utilization (15-min interval data underutilized), grid services revenue stacking.

### Energy Trading & Retail Energy Providers
Companies trading wholesale energy and selling retail. Examples: Vitol, Trafigura, Gunvor, Mercuria, BP Trading, Shell Trading, Macquarie, Freepoint, NextEra Energy Marketing, NRG, Direct Energy, Octopus Energy, Tara Energy. Also includes exchanges (ICE, CME, EEX, Nodal Exchange) and data providers (S&P Global Platts, Argus, ICIS).
- **Buyer profile**: Head of Trading, Head of Risk, Head of Quant Research, Head of Origination, CIO/CTO, Head of Retail Operations.
- **Sales cycle**: 60-180 days for SaaS/tools.
- **Budget source**: Trading desk budget + IT + retail operations.

## 3. Firmographic Signals by Operator Size

### Independent Operator / Small E&P (1-50 wells, <$500M revenue)
- **Signals**: Single basin focus, small engineering team, often PE-backed, minimal IT footprint.
- **Buying behavior**: 30-90 day cycles, ROI in 6-9 months, less formal procurement.
- **Budget availability**: $50K-$2M annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Well productivity, drilling cost per foot, methane compliance (OOOOb/c).

### Mid-Market Operator (50-1,000 wells, $500M-$5B revenue)
- **Signals**: Multi-basin, formal engineering and operations team, dedicated IT/OT staff, formal HSE function. Examples: Civitas Resources, Permian Resources, Chord Energy, Comstock Resources, Matador, Range Resources.
- **Buying behavior**: 90-180 day cycles, RFP for >$250K, IT security review.
- **Budget availability**: $2M-$30M annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Network-wide production optimization, methane intensity reduction, asset integrity ROI.

### Major / Supermajor (1,000+ wells or $5B+ revenue, often $50B+)
- **Signals**: Ticker symbol, 10-K filings, multi-continent operations, formal procurement and IT security review (NSA-grade), named CIO/CTO, vendor management office. Examples: ExxonMobil, Chevron, Shell, BP, TotalEnergies, ConocoPhillips, Equinor.
- **Buying behavior**: 12-24 month cycles, RFP mandatory, vendor consolidation, master service agreements.
- **Budget availability**: $30M-$500M+ annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference peer supermajors, specific project outcomes (e.g., "Permian productivity +8% over 18 months"), global rollout capability.

### National Oil Companies (NOCs)
- **Signals**: State-owned, often >$50B revenue, strategic national asset, diplomatic/political dimensions. Examples: Saudi Aramco, ADNOC, QatarEnergy, Kuwait Petroleum, Petronas, Petrobras, Pemex, CNPC, Sinopec, Gazprom, Rosneft, Statoil/Equinor (now publicly traded).
- **Buying behavior**: 12-36 month cycles, often requires local partner or in-country value (ICV) requirements, diplomatic considerations.
- **Budget availability**: $100M-$1B+ annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Local partnership, in-country value alignment, technology transfer, sovereign capability building.

### Small Municipal / Cooperative Utility (<50K meters)
- **Signals**: Public power or member-owned, small IT staff, often part of joint action agency, NRECA member (co-ops) or APPA member (munis).
- **Buying behavior**: 60-180 day cycles, often buys through NRTC, NRTC Solutions, Touchstone Energy, statewide associations.
- **Budget availability**: $200K-$5M annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Cooperative contract listing (NRTC), peer co-op/muni references, NRECA TechAdvantage conference.

### Mid-Market IOU / Regional Utility (50K-2M meters, $500M-$10B revenue)
- **Signals**: Investor-owned, state PUC regulation, formal IT and OT, named CIO. Examples: PNM Resources, Avangrid, Evergy, PPL, FirstEnergy, NiSource, WEC Energy, Alliant Energy, IDACORP, Pinnacle West.
- **Buying behavior**: 6-18 month cycles, formal RFP, IT security + NERC CIP review, rate case recovery planning.
- **Budget availability**: $20M-$200M annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Rate case recovery strategy, peer utility references, NERC CIP compliance.

### Large IOU / Transmission Operator (2M+ meters, $10B+ revenue)
- **Signals**: Ticker symbol, 10-K filings, multi-state operations, FERC jurisdiction, RTO/ISO member, large IT/OT staff (500-5,000+). Examples: NextEra, Duke, Southern Co, Dominion, AEP, Exelon, Xcel, Edison International, PG&E, Sempra, Berkshire Hathaway Energy, National Grid, EDF, E.ON, RWE, Enel, Iberdrola.
- **Buying behavior**: 12-36 month cycles, RFP mandatory, vendor management office, multi-year master service agreements.
- **Budget availability**: $100M-$1B+ annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference peer IOUs and FERC/NERC regulatory drivers, large-scale integration, multi-state rollout.

### Renewable Developer (utility-scale, <1GW operating)
- **Signals**: IPP model, project finance, often PE-backed ( Brookfield, BlackRock, EQT, Macquarie, Capital Dynamics), IRA tax credit monetization.
- **Buying behavior**: 60-180 day cycles for SaaS/tools; project development 18-48 months.
- **Budget availability**: $1M-$30M annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Asset performance, curtailment reduction, IRA tax credit optimization, O&M cost reduction.

### Large Renewable Operator (1GW+ operating or in development)
- **Signals**: Public or PE-backed, multi-technology portfolio (solar + wind + storage), sophisticated O&M, asset management. Examples: NextEra Energy Resources, Invenergy, Brookfield Renewable, AES Clean Energy, Clearway, EDF Renewables, Ørsted, Iberdrola Renewables, Enel Green Power, Pattern, Apex Clean Energy.
- **Buying behavior**: 6-18 month cycles, RFP for >$500K, IT security review.
- **Budget availability**: $10M-$200M+ annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Portfolio-wide asset performance, predictive maintenance, dispatch optimization, IRA stack optimization (45Y/48E + 45Q + 45V).

## 5. Buyer Personas

### The VP Operations (E&P / Refining / Utility)
- **At**: Mid-market and large operators.
- **Cares about**: Asset availability, production / throughput targets, HSE performance (TRIR, environmental incidents), operating cost per barrel / per MWh, asset integrity, regulatory compliance.
- **Pain**: Aging workforce (50% of US oil & gas workers eligible to retire by 2030), unscheduled downtime (costs upstream $50M-$500M/year per major operator; refining $1-5M/day per unit), supply chain inflation, regulatory reporting burden, emissions compliance.
- **Outreach angle**: Specific availability/MTBF improvements, peer operator references with similar assets, quantified emissions compliance savings.
- **Channels**: SPE (Society of Petroleum Engineers), API conferences, NPRA (now AFPM) Annual Meeting, EEI (Edison Electric Institute) Annual Convention, peer VP Ops networks.
- **What NOT to say**: Generic "AI for energy" — be specific (e.g., "reduced offshore platform downtime 4.2% using vibration analytics on 1,200 rotating assets").

### The Chief Procurement Officer / VP Procurement
- **At**: Mid-market and large energy companies.
- **Cares about**: Strategic sourcing, supplier diversity (often required by PUC rate case), total cost of ownership, supplier risk, local content (in-country value for NOCs), ESG supplier scoring.
- **Pain**: Complex spend categories (well services, equipment, EPC contracts), supplier consolidation, geopolitical supply chain risk (Russian制裁, China tariffs), inflation pass-through negotiations.
- **Outreach angle**: Spend analytics, supplier risk scoring, peer procurement benchmarks, ICV/local content alignment.

### The Director of Asset Integrity / Reliability
- **At**: Midstream, refining, and offshore upstream operators.
- **Cares about**: Mechanical integrity (API 510, 570, 653, 580 RBI), corrosion management, inspection planning, RBI (Risk-Based Inspection), PSV/PRV reliability, fitness-for-service (API 579).
- **Pain**: Aging infrastructure, inspection backlogs, data fragmentation across inspections, regulatory reporting (PHMSA Part 192/195, OSHA PSM, EPA RMP), API RP 1175 leak detection program management.
- **Outreach angle**: RBI optimization case studies, inspection digitalization, peer operator integrity references, leak detection program KPIs.

### The Head of Renewables Development / VP Project Development
- **At**: IPPs, utility renewable subsidiaries, developer teams.
- **Cares about**: Project origination (PPAs, virtual PPAs), interconnection queue position, permitting timeline, EPC contract structure, financing close, IRA tax credit stack (ITC/PTC/45Y/48E + 45Q + domestic content + energy community adders), commercial operation date.
- **Pain**: Interconnection queue wait (PJM backlog now ~3,000 projects, average 4-5 year wait), permitting delays, supply chain (solar module tariffs, polysilicon China exposure, wind turbine OEM consolidation), tax credit monetization complexity, community opposition (NIMBY).
- **Outreach angle**: Project origination tools, interconnection strategy, IRA tax credit optimization case studies, peer developer references.
- **Channels**: RE+, ACP (American Clean Power) Conference, Solar Power International, RECharge, INFRA WEEK, REFF Wall Street.

### The Director of Grid Operations / Control Center Manager
- **At**: Transmission and distribution utilities, RTOs/ISOs.
- **Cares about**: System reliability (SAIDI, SAIFI, CAIDI), NERC compliance, balancing authority performance (CPS1, CPS2, BAAL, DCS), restoration time, situational awareness, control room ergonomics.
- **Pain**: Renewables integration (frequency response, voltage support), DER penetration (rooftop solar reverse flows), cyber-OT convergence, workforce attrition (operators retiring), storm response (wildfire PSPS, hurricane restoration).
- **Outreach angle**: ADMS modernization, situational awareness tools, peer utility reliability references, NERC compliance automation.

### The Director of Smart Grid / Distribution Automation
- **At**: Mid-market and large IOUs.
- **Cares about**: FLISR deployment, volt/VAR optimization, distribution system state estimation, hosting capacity analysis, DER integration, AMI analytics.
- **Pain**: Legacy SCADA limitations, GIS data quality, ADMS integration complexity, DER forecasting, customer engagement in DER programs.
- **Outreach angle**: FLISR case studies (peer utilities reduced SAIDI 20-40%), hosting capacity methodology, ADMS integration references.

### The Head of Grid Modernization / VP Strategy
- **At**: Mid-market and large IOUs.
- **Cares about**: Long-term grid investment plan ($1-20B over 5-10 years), regulatory strategy (rate case filings, performance-based ratemaking), technology roadmap, FERC/NERC regulatory engagement, public policy (state RPS, federal IIJA/IRA funding).
- **Pain**: Multi-decade capital planning under regulatory uncertainty, technology obsolescence, geopolitical supply chain risk, public scrutiny.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference peer utility capital plans, regulatory strategy, federal funding stacking (IIJA + IRA + DOE GDO grants).

### The VP Engineering / CTO (Energy Tech)
- **At**: Mid-market and large energy companies, energy tech startups.
- **Cares about**: IT/OT architecture, data platform (historian + cloud + data lake), API-first design, AI/ML deployment, cybersecurity (NERC CIP, IEC 62443), developer productivity.
- **Pain**: Legacy SCADA / DCS (Honeywell Experion, Yokogawa Centum, Emerson DeltaV), IT/OT airgap friction, data silos, vendor lock-in, OT cyber skills shortage.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference peer engineering organizations (e.g., "how Equinor built a Cognite-powered data lake for 30,000 assets"), specific engineering KPIs (deploy frequency, p99 API latency, OT detection MTTD).
- **Channels**: SPE Digital Energy, ENG, OT Cybersecurity conferences (SANS ICS, Dragos), GitHub, engineering blogs.

### The Head of Sustainability / Chief Sustainability Officer
- **At**: Large energy companies, utilities, refiners, midstream.
- **Cares about**: GHG emissions reporting (Scope 1, 2, 3), CDP score, SBTi target validation, TCFD/IFRS S2 disclosure, EU CSRD compliance, IRA / EU Taxonomy alignment, methane intensity (Oil & Gas Climate Initiative targets).
- **Pain**: Methane measurement and reporting (EPA Subpart W, EU Methane Regulation, OGMP 2.0), Scope 3 Category 11 (use of sold products) for upstream, carbon credit quality, transition plan credibility.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference peer sustainability reports (e.g., "Equinor's net-zero roadmap alignment"), CDP score improvement, OGMP 2.0 gold standard pathway, EU CBAM compliance.

### The Director of Turnarounds (Refining / Petrochemicals)
- **At**: Refining and petrochemical operators.
- **Cares about**: Turnaround scope development, schedule optimization, cost control (typical turnaround $20-200M), execution safety, downtime minimization (1 day of downtime = $1-5M revenue loss per refinery unit).
- **Pain**: Scope creep, schedule delays, contractor coordination (often 500-2,000+ contractors per turnaround), inspection findings during execution, materials availability.
- **Outreach angle**: Turnaround planning platform, peer refiner turnaround benchmarking, scope optimization case studies.

### The Head of Trading / Risk (Energy Trading)
- **At**: Energy trading desks (Vitol, Trafigura, BP, Shell, Mercuria, Macquarie, utility trading arms).
- **Cares about**: P&L volatility, Value-at-Risk (VaR), stress testing, counterparty credit risk, position limits, regulatory compliance (Dodd-Frank, REMIT, MAR), trade execution speed.
- **Pain**: Legacy CTRM (Commodity Trading & Risk Management) systems (Openlink, Allegro), market data integration, model risk management, regulatory reporting (REMIT, EMIR, Dodd-Frank), AI for price forecasting.
- **Outreach angle**: CTRM modernization, peer trading desk references, AI price forecasting case studies.

## 7. Energy Industry Vocabulary

- **Upstream / Midstream / Downstream** — E&P / pipelines / refining.
- **bpd / bbl** — Barrels per day / barrel (42 US gallons).
- **Mcf / MMcf / Bcf / Tcf** — Thousand / Million / Billion / Trillion cubic feet (natural gas).

- **ITC / PTC** — Investment Tax Credit / Production Tax Credit.

## 8. Common Energy Prospecting Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Confusing Upstream / Midstream / Downstream / Utility
Pitching a refining turnaround tool to an upstream producer, or a SCADA system to a retail energy provider. Always confirm segment first; the buyers, budgets, and cycles are completely different.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Commodity Cycle Context
Pitching "growth" tools to an E&P in a low-oil-price year (like 2020) fails; pitch cost takeout and resilience. Pitching cost takeout to an E&P in a high-price year (like 2022) underperforms; pitch capital efficiency and expansion. Match the macro cycle.

### Mistake 3: Wrong Stakeholder
Emailing the CEO about a SCADA tool. SCADA is bought by VP Operations or Director of Engineering/OT — not the C-suite. Match the tool to the functional owner.

### Mistake 4: Generic "AI for Energy" Claims
"We use AI to optimize your operations" is meaningless. "We helped [Peer Operator] reduce offshore platform downtime 4.2% using vibration analytics on 1,200 rotating assets — $38M annualized savings" is meaningful. Always tie to a quantified outcome and named peer.

### Mistake 5: Ignoring Margin Reality
A refining operator with $5/bbl margin cannot afford a $1M/year tool that doesn't return multiples. Always do the unit math: a tool that saves $0.10/bbl on 200,000 bpd = $7.3M annual savings, justifying a $2M annual cost.

### Mistake 6: Wrong Timing for Regulatory Cycles
Pitching FERC Order 881 compliance tools after the compliance deadline (April 2025) is too late; pitch during the implementation phase (2023-2025). Always map outreach to regulatory deadlines.

### Mistake 7: Forgetting IT/OT Cyber and Safety Constraints
Tools that violate airgaps, lack IEC 62443 certification, or don't meet NERC CIP requirements are dead on arrival. Always address OT cybersecurity upfront.

### Mistake 8: ATEX/IECEx Ignorance for Hazardous Areas
Pitching sensors or devices for refinery / offshore use without ATEX/IECEx certification is dead on arrival. Always address hazardous area certification (Zone 0/1/2, Class I Div 1/2) upfront.

### Mistake 9: Treating Renewables Like Traditional Utilities
Renewables developers have project-finance models, IRA tax credit stacks, and 25-30 year asset lifecycles with very different O&M dynamics than regulated utilities. Don't pitch utility rate-case recovery tools to a developer.

### Mistake 10: Underestimating Project Development Timelines
A wind or solar project takes 18-48 months from origination to commercial operation; a new transmission line takes 7-15 years; a new LNG terminal takes 5-7 years. Plan pipeline accordingly; don't expect 30-day closes.

### Mistake 11: Ignoring Geopolitical Dimensions
Pitching to NOCs requires local partner consideration, in-country value requirements, and diplomatic awareness. Pitching in sanctioned jurisdictions (Russia, Iran, Venezuela) is illegal under OFAC. Always check sanctions.

### Mistake 12: Overpromising on Decarbonization
Energy buyers are skeptical of net-zero claims. Reference peer credible roadmaps (e.g., Equinor, Ørsted, Iberdrola) and avoid "100% renewable" framing that doesn't account for intermittency, transmission, and system value.

## 12. Common Pitfalls Specific to Energy

### Commodity Volatility Cycles
Energy is structurally cyclical. An E&P that was a great prospect in 2022 (high oil prices) may be in cost-cutting mode in 2024 (lower prices). Always check current WTI/Brent/Henry Hub prices, Baker Hughes rig count, and BNEF renewable investment trends before pitching.

### Regulatory Complexity
Energy is the most regulated industry after finance. NERC CIP (cyber), FERC (wholesale markets), EPA (emissions), PHMSA (pipeline integrity), NRC (nuclear), state PUCs (rate cases) — each creates compliance-driven buying cycles. Pitching without regulatory awareness signals you don't understand the industry.

### Long Capex Cycles
A new LNG terminal takes 5-7 years; a new transmission line 7-15 years; a new nuclear plant 10-15 years; offshore wind 5-10 years. Plan pipeline accordingly; do not expect 30-day closes outside of SaaS SMB tools.

### Safety and Compliance Culture
Energy buyers are obsessed with safety (TRIR, process safety events, environmental incidents). Tools that compromise safety culture (e.g., replacing safety reviews with AI) are dead on arrival. Always cite safety case alignment (API RP 754, OSHA PSM, IEC 61508/61511 functional safety).

### ATEX / IECEx Hazardous Area Certification
Pitching sensors, devices, or computing equipment for refinery / offshore / pipeline use without ATEX (EU Directive 2014/34/EU) or IECEx certification is dead on arrival. Always address hazardous area certification (Zone 0/1/2, Class I Div 1/2, NEMA 7) upfront.

### IT/OT Cybersecurity Convergence
OT networks (SCADA, DCS, ICS) have different protocols (DNP3, Modbus, IEC 61850, OPC UA), different lifecycles (10-30 years vs IT's 3-5), and different security postures (airgaps vs connected). Pitching IT tools for OT use cases without IEC 62443 / NERC CIP / ISAsecure awareness is dead on arrival.

### Geopolitical and Sanctions Risk
Pitching to NOCs requires local partner consideration, in-country value (ICV) requirements, and diplomatic awareness. Pitching in sanctioned jurisdictions (Russia, Iran, Venezuela, Cuba, North Korea, Crimea, Donetsk, Luhansk) is illegal under OFAC. Always check sanctions before prospecting.

### Vendor Consolidation and Lock-In
Energy buyers fear vendor lock-in (especially after major SCADA/ERP migrations that took 5+ years). Always address data portability (OPC UA, OpenADR, IEEE 2030.5), API-first architecture, and exit clauses.

### Workforce Transition (Demographic Cliff)
50% of US oil & gas workers and 30-40% of utility lineworkers are eligible to retire by 2030. Tools targeting knowledge capture, workforce productivity, and AI-assisted operations are evergreen; tools that increase worker friction are dead on arrival.

### Climate Transition Strategy Mismatch
Pitching "growth" tools to a company in managed decline (e.g., coal-heavy utility, declining offshore basin) fails; pitch transition support. Pitching transition tools to a company in expansion mode underperforms; pitch growth and capital efficiency. Match the company's transition posture.

### Long Procurement Cycles for Public Operators
Supermajors, large IOUs, and NOCs have 12-36 month procurement cycles with formal vendor management offices (VMOs). Don't expect a 60-day close; structure the deal for the long cycle with milestone-based SOW and master service agreement (MSA).

### Interconnection Queue Reality
US interconnection queues (PJM, MISO, CAISO, ERCOT) now have 3-5 year wait times. Renewable developers cannot deploy capital until they have queue position. Tools targeting interconnection strategy, cost allocation, and queue optimization are high-value.

### Public Scrutiny and Reputation Risk
Energy companies face intense public and activist scrutiny (climate protests, shareholder resolutions, NGO campaigns). Tools that reduce emissions, improve safety, or support credible transition are welcome; tools that increase reputational risk are dead on arrival.

## 13. LeadReach Energy Playbook Summary

1. **Identify sub-segment** (upstream, midstream, downstream, power generation, T&D utility, renewables, grid edge, trading/retail) — determines buyer and budget source.
2. **Determine operator size and type** (small operator, mid-market, supermajor, NOC, co-op, muni, mid-market IOU, large IOU, RTO, renewable developer) — determines budget and cycle length.
3. **Detect tech stack** (ERP, SCADA, historian, APM, EMS, ADMS, AMI, DERMS, CTRM, APM) — determines integration story and adjacency opportunities.
4. **Identify correct buyer persona** — match the tool to the functional owner (VP Ops for operations tools, Director Asset Integrity for integrity, Head of Renewables Development for projects, Director Grid Operations for control center, CIO/CTO for IT/OT, Head of Sustainability for ESG).
5. **Scan for trigger events** — IRA tax credit awards, FERC orders, EPA regulations, project FIDs, M&A, leadership changes, cyber incidents, capacity cycle pivots, rate case filings, interconnection queue reforms.
6. **Compose outreach** referencing specific trigger + specific persona pain + quantified peer outcome + regulatory context.
7. **Multi-touch sequence** (email + LinkedIn + phone + conference meeting) over 4-12 weeks; energy buyers respond well to peer references and conference connections.
8. **Track funnel metrics** — delivery, open, reply, meeting-booked rates; adjust by sub-segment.
9. **Iterate** based on which sub-segments and trigger types convert best in your ICP.
10. **Avoid the cardinal sins** — don't confuse segments, don't ignore commodity cycles, don't use generic "AI for energy" claims, don't pitch to the wrong functional owner, don't ignore ATEX/IECEx, don't underestimate IT/OT cyber complexity, don't ignore geopolitical/sanctions risk, don't underestimate capex cycle length.
