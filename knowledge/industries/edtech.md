# EdTech & Education Technology — B2B Lead Intelligence Guide

## 1. Industry Overview

EdTech is a **$400B+ global market** spanning K-12, higher education, corporate learning, and workforce development. The post-pandemic era reshaped the industry: remote and hybrid learning became permanent, institutional IT budgets shifted from on-premise to cloud, and the "digital campus" became table stakes. The defining characteristics of B2B EdTech are **long, committee-driven procurement cycles** (6-18 months in higher ed), **compliance with FERPA, COPPA, and accessibility mandates** (ADA/Section 508/WCAG), **multi-stakeholder buying** (IT, faculty, administration, students), and **procurement gatekeepers** like state-level ESAs and cooperative purchasing agreements.

Key characteristics:
- **Committee-driven procurement** — Buying decisions in higher ed involve IT, academic leadership, faculty senate, procurement, and student government. K-12 involves district IT, curriculum directors, superintendents, and school boards
- **Regulatory-heavy environment** — FERPA (student data privacy), COPPA (children's online privacy), ADA/Section 508 (accessibility), state data-privacy laws (CA, CO, VA, CT), GDPR for international students
- **Seasonal budget cycles** — K-12 budgets tied to fiscal year (July-June in most states); higher ed aligned with academic calendar; corporate L&D tied to annual planning Q4-Q1
- **High switching costs** — LMS and SIS migrations are multi-year projects; data migration, faculty retraining, and integration with legacy systems create significant lock-in
- **Bifurcated buyer landscape** — Institutional buyers (schools, districts, universities) vs. corporate buyers (L&D departments, HR) vs. direct-to-learner (B2C/B2B2C hybrids)
- **Procurement vehicles** — E-rate, ESAs (Educational Service Agencies), cooperative contracts (NASPO, OMNIA Partners, TIPS/TAP), state-level adoption lists, and RFP-driven processes

The EdTech market has consolidated significantly since 2020. Major acquirers include PowerSchool, Renaissance Learning, Turnitin (Advance Publications), Instructure (Thoma Bravo), and Byju's (now restructuring). Venture funding dropped from $20B+ in 2021 to ~$3B in 2024-2025, shifting focus from growth-at-all-costs to sustainable unit economics and proven learning outcomes.

## 2. Sub-Segment Deep Dives

### Learning Management Systems (LMS)
The LMS market is dominated by Instructure (Canvas), Blackboard (Anthology), Moodle (open-source), and D2L (Brightspace). The market is mature ($12B+) but still sees disruption from AI-native platforms, microlearning tools, and skills-based learning systems. Key buying triggers include LMS migrations (Blackboard → Canvas remains common), LTI 1.3 integration requirements, and AI-assessment readiness. Corporate LMS is a separate market led by Cornerstone, SAP SuccessFactors Learning, and Docebo.

### Student Information Systems (SIS)
SIS is the ERP of education — managing enrollment, grading, transcripts, and compliance. PowerSchool dominates K-12 SIS; Ellucian (Banner), Workday Student, and Oracle PeopleSoft compete in higher ed. SIS replacements are massive, multi-year undertakings. Signals: institutions running on deprecated versions, merger/acquisition of colleges, new compliance mandates (e.g., FAFSA simplification), or integration with modern LMS/cloud platforms.

### Corporate Learning & Development (L&D)
The $380B corporate training market is shifting from compliance-driven course catalogs to skills-based, adaptive, and AI-powered learning. Key players: Cornerstone, Degreed, EdCast (Cornerstone), Docebo, 360Learning, and Articulate. Buying triggers: skills-gap analysis initiatives, leadership development programs, compliance training mandates, and LMS consolidation after M&A.

### Adaptive Learning & AI-Tutoring
AI-powered personalized learning platforms (Knewton, DreamBox, Carnegie Learning, Khan Academy's Khanmigo) represent the highest-growth sub-segment. These platforms use learning science, spaced repetition, and generative AI to personalize content. Buying triggers: learning-outcome accountability, AI-readiness mandates, and ROI pressure on remediation programs.

### Assessment & Proctoring
High-stakes assessment (ETS, Pearson VUE, Prometric) and online proctoring (Proctorio, ExamSoft, Respondus) surged during COVID and remain critical. AI-based proctoring faces significant pushback over bias and privacy. Buying signals: accreditation reviews, exam integrity incidents, hybrid exam requirements, and accessibility compliance.

### Student Success & Retention
Platforms like EAB, Civitas Learning, and Ivy.ai focus on predictive analytics for student retention, early-alert systems, and advising workflows. Buying triggers: declining retention rates, enrollment management pressure, and strategic plan mandates for graduation rates.

### Workforce Development & Credentialing
Credentialing platforms (Credly, Accredible), bootcamp operators (General Assembly, Springboard), and workforce-readiness platforms (Intersect by Handshake, Coursera for Business) bridge education and employment. Signals: apprenticeship program launches, skills-based hiring mandates, and employer-education partnerships.

### Test Prep & Admissions
Test preparation (Kaplan, Princeton Review, Manhattan Prep) and admissions platforms (Common App, Slate, Liaison International) serve K-12 and higher ed transitions. Signals: test-optional policy reversals, enrollment yield optimization, and international student recruitment.

## 4. Buyer Personas

### The CIO / VP of IT (Economic Buyer — Institutional)
- **Role**: Sets technology strategy, controls edtech budget, owns infrastructure decisions
- **Reporting**: Reports to President/Chancellor or Provost
- **Pain points**: Legacy system integration, cybersecurity compliance, budget constraints, faculty dissatisfaction with existing tools
- **Buying trigger**: System end-of-life, security breach, accreditation requirement, board mandate
- **What they care about**: Integration architecture (LTI, SIS APIs, SSO), security posture (SOC 2, FERPA compliance), total cost of ownership, vendor stability
- **How to reach**: Higher ed CIO conferences (EDUCAUSE), CIO roundtables, LinkedIn
- **Key metric**: IT spend per student FTE

### The Provost / VP Academic Affairs (Strategic Buyer)
- **Role**: Academic leadership; approves curriculum technology, faculty adoption mandates
- **Reporting**: Reports to President/Chancellor
- **Pain points**: Student retention, faculty satisfaction, learning outcomes, enrollment revenue
- **Buying trigger**: Retention rate decline, strategic plan refresh, accreditation visit, competitive pressure from peer institutions
- **What they care about**: Learning outcomes data, faculty adoption rates, student satisfaction scores, competitive differentiation
- **How to reach**: Academic leadership conferences, provost networks, direct outreach with outcomes data
- **Key metric**: Retention rate, graduation rate, enrollment yield

### The Director of L&D / Chief Learning Officer (Economic Buyer — Corporate)
- **Role**: Owns corporate learning strategy, training budget, and employee development programs
- **Reporting**: Reports to CHRO or CEO
- **Pain points**: Measuring training ROI, content relevance, engagement, skills-gap closure, compliance tracking
- **Buying trigger**: M&A integration, compliance mandate, skills transformation initiative, LMS consolidation
- **What they care about**: Skills taxonomy, learning analytics, content library breadth, integration with HCM, learner engagement metrics
- **How to reach**: ATD conference, CLO summits, LinkedIn, HR tech events
- **Key metric**: Training hours per employee, skills proficiency improvement, compliance completion rates

### The Instructional Designer / EdTech Director (Champion)
- **Role**: Day-to-day technology evaluator; designs learning experiences and evaluates tools
- **Reporting**: Reports to CIO, Provost, or L&D Director
- **Pain points**: Faculty resistance to new tools, LMS limitations, accessibility compliance, content migration
- **Buying trigger**: Faculty complaints, new program launches, accessibility audit, AI integration mandate
- **What they care about**: Ease of use for faculty, LTI compliance, content authoring tools, analytics dashboards, accessibility (VPAT/WCAG)
- **How to reach**: OLC Accelerate, EDUCAUSE, instructional design communities, Twitter/X edtech circles
- **Key metric**: Faculty adoption rate, support ticket volume

### The District Superintendent / School Board (Economic Buyer — K-12)
- **Role**: Final approval authority for district-wide edtech purchases
- **Reporting**: Elected or appointed; reports to community/taxpayers
- **Pain points**: Student achievement gaps, community trust, budget transparency, teacher retention
- **Buying trigger**: State mandate, test score decline, strategic plan, ESSER/Title funding availability
- **What they care about**: Evidence of efficacy, equity, parent communication, data privacy, cost per student
- **How to reach**: AASA conferences, state superintendent associations, school board meetings
- **Key metric**: Student achievement scores, per-pupil expenditure, teacher satisfaction

## 5. Technographic Signals to Detect

### EdTech Stack Patterns by Maturity Level

**Early-Stage / Resource-Constrained Institution:**
- Google Classroom or free-tier Moodle
- Paper-based or legacy SIS (e.g., Skyward on-prem)
- No SSO; manual account provisioning
- Minimal analytics; spreadsheet-based reporting
- No formal accessibility review process

**Growth-Stage / Mid-Market Institution:**
- Canvas or D2L Brightspace
- Modern SIS (PowerSchool, Ellucian Colleague)
- SSO via Azure AD or Google Workspace
- Basic analytics dashboard; early-alert system
- VPAT review for new purchases
- Zoom/Teams integrated for hybrid learning

**Enterprise / Technology-Forward Institution:**
- Canvas + specialized tools (adaptive learning, XR labs)
- Workday Student or Banner 9 cloud
- Full IAM (Okta, Azure AD) with role-based access
- Predictive analytics (Civitas, EAB)
- AI-powered chatbots and virtual assistants
- Comprehensive data warehouse (Snowflake/Databricks)
- API-first integration architecture

## 7. EdTech Industry Vocabulary & Jargon

### Core Metrics & Terms
- **FTE (Full-Time Equivalent)** — Standard enrollment measure; drives budget and licensing
- **IPEDS** — Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System; federal reporting
- **LTI (Learning Tools Interoperability)** — Standard for integrating tools with LMS (1.3/Advantage is current)
- **FERPA** — Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act; student data privacy law
- **COPPA** — Children's Online Privacy Protection Act; applies to K-12 tools for under-13
- **VPAT (Voluntary Product Accessibility Template)** — Accessibility compliance document (Section 508/WCAG)
- **E-rate** — FCC program providing discounts on telecommunications for schools/libraries
- **ESSA / ESSER** — Every Student Succeeds Act / Elementary and Secondary School Emergency Relief funds
- **Title I / Title IV** — Federal funding categories for disadvantaged students and student support
- **SIS** — Student Information System (enrollment, grades, transcripts)
- **LMS** — Learning Management System (course delivery, assignments, grading)
- **Competency-Based Education (CBE)** — Progress by mastery, not seat time
- **OER (Open Educational Resources)** — Free/open-licensed learning materials
- **Micro-credentials / Digital Badges** — Stackable, verifiable skill certifications
- **XAPI / Caliper** — Learning analytics data standards (successor to SCORM)

## 9. Common Objections & Responses

### "We just migrated to a new LMS/SIS two years ago."
**Response**: "Understood — migration fatigue is real. We're not proposing a rip-and-replace. Our platform integrates via LTI 1.3 with your existing LMS, adding [specific capability] without disrupting faculty workflows. Most of our higher-ed clients layer us on top of Canvas/Blackboard within weeks."

### "We don't have budget until the next fiscal year."
**Response**: "That's common in education — budget cycles are rigid. Let's work backwards from your fiscal year start: we can scope a pilot now using [Title IV/grant/operational] funds, have outcomes data ready for your budget committee, and position you for a full approval in the next cycle. We've done this with [reference institution]."

### "Our faculty won't adopt another tool."
**Response**: "Faculty adoption is the #1 risk, and we've designed specifically to minimize it. Our tool works inside the LMS they already use — no new login, no new workflow. [Institution X] saw 80% faculty adoption in the first semester because we don't ask them to change how they teach, we enhance what they already do."

### "We need to ensure FERPA/COPPA compliance."
**Response**: "Absolutely non-negotiable. We're FERPA-compliant by design — we sign institutional FERPA agreements, we don't use student data for advertising, and we provide a complete data-processing agreement. Our VPAT covers Section 508 and WCAG 2.1 AA. We can share our compliance documentation and introduce you to our compliance officer."

### "We can't justify the ROI — how do we measure learning outcomes?"
**Response**: "That's exactly the right question. We built our analytics to measure what matters: [specific outcome metrics]. [Institution Y] tracked a [X%] improvement in pass rates and a [Y%] reduction in DFW rates after implementing our platform. We'll help you design a study that your provost and board will find compelling."

### "We're waiting to see how AI in education shakes out."
**Response**: "Prudent approach — and that's why we've built AI features that augment rather than replace educators. Our AI assists with [specific task], gives faculty full control, and includes transparent citation and bias auditing. The institutions that pilot now will have data and institutional knowledge when AI standards solidify — those that wait will be playing catch-up."

## 10. Outreach Angles & Talk Tracks

### Angle 1: LMS Migration Concierge
**Target**: Institutions on Blackboard Original, legacy Moodle, or ANGEL
**Hook**: "Your LMS migration is a once-in-a-decade opportunity to rethink how technology supports learning outcomes. We help institutions like [reference] make Canvas/Brightspace even more powerful with [specific integration]."
**Timing**: After RFP announcement or migration decision

### Angle 2: Student Retention Crisis
**Target**: Institutions with >5% enrollment decline or <70% retention rates
**Hook**: "[Institution name]'s retention rate is [X%], and each percentage point represents [$Y] in tuition revenue. Our predictive analytics platform identifies at-risk students [Z weeks] earlier than traditional early-alert systems."
**Timing**: After IPEDS data release, accreditation visit announcement

### Angle 3: AI-Ready Campus
**Target**: Technology-forward institutions, R1 universities, progressive districts
**Hook**: "AI in education isn't about replacing faculty — it's about giving every student a personalized tutor and every instructor actionable insights. We've helped [reference] deploy AI-assisted learning that improved outcomes by [X%] while maintaining full faculty control."
**Timing**: After AI task force formation, strategic plan refresh

### Angle 4: Compliance Acceleration (FERPA/COPPA/ADA)
**Target**: Institutions facing compliance deadlines or audits
**Hook**: "Your upcoming [accreditation visit / accessibility audit / FERPA review] doesn't have to be a fire drill. Our platform automates [compliance task] and generates audit-ready documentation. [Reference institution] cut their compliance prep time by [X%]."
**Timing**: After audit announcement, regulatory change, ADA coordinator hire

### Angle 5: Corporate Skills Transformation
**Target**: Enterprise L&D departments in regulated or tech-adjacent industries
**Hook**: "The half-life of technical skills is now [2.5 years]. Your current LMS tracks completion, not capability. Our skills-based learning platform measures actual proficiency and maps it to your competency framework — so you can prove ROI to your CHRO."
**Timing**: After M&A, compliance mandate, skills-gap survey

### Angle 6: E-Rate and Federal Funding Maximizer
**Target**: K-12 districts eligible for E-rate, Title I, or ESSER funds
**Hook**: "Your district is eligible for [$X] in E-rate and Title IV funding that expires [date]. We've helped [Y districts] navigate the application process and deploy [technology] at [Z%] federal cost share. Let's make sure you're not leaving money on the table."
**Timing**: Before E-rate filing window (typically Q1), after grant announcements

### Angle 7: Equity and Access
**Target**: Urban districts, community colleges, HBCUs, MSIs
**Hook**: "At [institution type], technology should be an equalizer, not a barrier. Our platform is WCAG 2.1 AA compliant, works on low-bandwidth connections, supports [languages], and includes offline capabilities. [Reference institution] saw [X%] increase in completion rates among first-gen students."
**Timing**: After equity audit, strategic plan, enrollment decline in underserved populations

## Regulatory & Compliance Considerations

### FERPA (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act)
- Applies to all institutions receiving federal funding
- Governs collection, use, and disclosure of student education records
- Requires written consent for most disclosures; "school official" exception for vendors
- Vendors must sign FERPA-compliant data-processing agreements
- Annual notification and record-keeping requirements
- Enforcement: DOE Office of Federal Student Aid; penalties include loss of federal funding

### COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act)
- Applies to online services directed at children under 13
- Requires verifiable parental consent before data collection
- Limits data collection to what's reasonably necessary
- Schools can consent on behalf of parents in limited educational contexts
- FTC enforcement; penalties up to $50,120 per violation

### ADA / Section 508 / WCAG 2.1
- All EdTech products must be accessible to users with disabilities
- Section 508 applies to federal agencies and their vendors; many states extend to public institutions
- WCAG 2.1 AA is the de facto standard (DOJ 2024 rule)
- VPAT/ACR documentation required for procurement
- OCR (Office for Civil Rights) complaints and resolutions are public record

### State Data Privacy Laws
- **California (CCPA/CPRA)** — Applies to EdTech vendors serving CA students
- **Colorado (CPA)** — Consumer privacy with education-specific provisions
- **Virginia (VCDPA)** — Data protection with education data considerations
- **Connecticut, Utah, Iowa** — Growing patchwork of state student privacy laws
- **New York (Education Law §2-d)** — Strict student data privacy requirements
- **Texas (SB 820)** — District cybersecurity requirements

### International Considerations
- **GDPR** — Applies to institutions and vendors serving EU/EEA students
- **UK Data Protection Act 2018** — Post-Brexit UK GDPR
- **Australia (Privacy Act)** — Australian student data protections
- **Brazil (LGPD)** — Brazilian data protection law

## Regional Variations

### United States
- Largest single EdTech market; $100B+ spend
- Highly decentralized K-12 (13,000+ districts); more centralized higher ed
- Federal funding (Title I, E-rate, Pell) creates buying cycles
- State-level adoption lists and procurement policies vary widely
- Growing state privacy legislation patchwork

### European Union
- GDPR compliance is non-negotiable for any institution serving EU students
- Public procurement rules (open competition, transparency) extend sales cycles
- Digital Education Action Plan drives technology adoption
- Smaller institutional budgets; more emphasis on open-source (Moodle dominant)
- Multiple languages and localization requirements

### United Kingdom
- Post-Brexit regulatory framework (UK GDPR + DfE guidance)
- Multi-academy trusts (MATs) are key buyers — consolidation of purchasing
- Ofsted inspections drive compliance and technology adoption
- EdTech Strategy (2019) and subsequent updates shape priorities
- Jisc plays a key role as sector body and procurement facilitator

### Asia-Pacific (India, China, Australia, Japan)
- India: World's largest EdTech market by user volume; NEP 2020 drives digitization
- China: Regulatory crackdown on for-profit tutoring (2021); government-led digital campus initiatives
- Australia: Strong higher ed sector; international student focus; ASQA compliance
- Japan: GIGA School Program (1 device per student); conservative procurement
- Southeast Asia: Rapid digitization; mobile-first; language diversity

### Latin America
- Brazil: Largest LATAM market; BNCC curriculum standards; growing startup ecosystem
- Mexico: Telesecundaria and remote learning infrastructure gaps
- Government-led digitization programs create buying windows
- Mobile-first student population; offline capabilities valued
- Currency volatility affects vendor pricing strategies

### Middle East & Africa
- UAE/Saudi Arabia: Massive investment in smart campuses and education cities
- NEOM, KAUST, MBZUAI — flagship technology-forward institutions
- Africa: Mobile-first; offline capabilities critical; UNESCO-driven initiatives
- World Bank and development bank funding creates procurement windows
