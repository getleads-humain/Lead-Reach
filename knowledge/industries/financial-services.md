# Financial Services Industry — Prospecting Guide

## 1. Industry Overview

Financial services is a **$22 trillion global industry** spanning banking, insurance, investment management, payments, and fintech. It is the **most heavily regulated industry on earth** — every product, vendor, and process is subject to oversight by SEC, FINRA, OCC, FDIC, CFPB (US), FCA (UK), BaFin (Germany), MAS (Singapore), and dozens of others.

Key characteristics:
- **Regulated everywhere** — Multiple regulators per country; cross-border adds complexity
- **Risk-averse** — Failed products can cost billions in fines (Wells Fargo $3B, Goldman Sachs $5B+)
- **Long sales cycles** — 12-24 months for enterprise bank deals; 6-18 months for insurance
- **Security-obsessed** — SOC 2 Type II minimum; ISO 27001; sometimes FedRAMP
- **Procurement-heavy** — RFPs, vendor risk assessments, security questionnaires (Sig.io, OneTrust)
- **Talent-poor** — Bank IT teams are stretched thin; modernization backlog is huge
- **Data-rich** — More transaction data than any industry; AI/ML opportunity is massive

### 2.1 Banking (Retail & Commercial)

**Market Shape**
- Global banking assets: ~$190T; US banks alone hold ~$23T in assets
- Market structure: 4,000+ US banks; top 4 (JPMorgan, BofA, Wells Fargo, Citi) hold 40%+ of US deposits
- Community banks: 4,500+ in US (declining from 8,000 in 2010); credit unions: 5,000+
- Digital-only banks: Chime (14M users), Revolut (35M+ users), Monzo, N26, Nubank (90M+ users in LATAM)
- Revenue pressure: Net interest margin compression (3.3% → 2.8% over 5 years); fee income under regulatory pressure

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Net interest income (60-70%), fee income (20-25%), trading/investment income (10-15%)
- Margins: Net interest margin 2.5-3.5%; ROE 8-12% (below cost of equity for many); efficiency ratio 55-65% (target <50%)
- Deposits: Core deposits are lifeblood; cost of deposits 0.1-0.5% (vs. 4-5% Fed funds rate in 2025)
- Loan portfolio: Commercial (40%), residential mortgage (25%), consumer (20%), other (15%)
- Key metric: Efficiency ratio, ROE, net interest margin, loan loss provision, deposit growth

**Tech Stack**
- Core banking: FIS (30% market share), Fiserv (25%), Jack Henry (community banks/credit unions), Temenos (international), Mambu (cloud-native)
- Digital banking: Q2 (digital banking platform), Alkami (digital banking), NCR Digital
- CRM: Salesforce Financial Services Cloud (dominant), Microsoft Dynamics
- Payments: Fedwire, ACH, RTP (Real-Time Payments), FedNow (launched 2023), SWIFT (cross-border)
- Lending: nCino (loan origination), Blend (digital lending), Roostify
- Risk/compliance: Verafin (BSA/AML), Actimize (NICE), SAS Anti-Money Laundering
- Data: Teradata, Snowflake, Databricks — banks are heavy data warehouse users

**Decision-Making**
- CIO/CTO: Technology strategy; core system decisions; cloud migration
- Head of Digital: Digital banking platform; mobile app; CX technology
- Head of Retail Banking: Branch technology; ATM strategy; teller systems
- Head of Commercial Banking: Commercial lending tech; treasury management
- CFO: Budget approval; cost optimization; ROI requirements
- Board/risk committee: Vendor risk; cyber risk; regulatory compliance
- Procurement: Vendor management; security assessment; contract negotiation (3-6 month process)

**Budget Cycles**
- Annual budget: Q3-Q4 planning for following year; board approval in December
- CapEx vs OpEx: Banks prefer CapEx for infrastructure; shifting to OpEx via cloud
- Regulatory project budget: Often separate; mandated spend (CCAR, AML, CECL) is non-discretionary
- Innovation budget: Typically 1-3% of IT budget; separate from "keep the lights on" spend
- Multi-year commitments: Banks prefer 3-5 year contracts with annual review options

**Regulatory**
- OCC (Office of the Comptroller of the Currency): National bank charter; examinations; enforcement
- FDIC: Deposit insurance; bank examinations; resolution authority
- Federal Reserve: Bank holding company supervision; stress tests (CCAR/DFAST); monetary policy
- CFPB: Consumer protection; fair lending; UDAAP (Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices)
- SEC/FINRA: Broker-dealer regulation; investment advisory; securities trading
- State regulators: Dual banking system; state-chartered banks regulated by state banking departments
- BSA/AML: Bank Secrecy Act; anti-money laundering; SAR (Suspicious Activity Report) filing
- OFAC: Sanctions compliance; prohibited party screening
- GLBA: Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act; data sharing restrictions; privacy notices
- SOX: Sarbanes-Oxley; internal controls; auditor independence
- CCAR/DFAST: Stress testing; capital planning; annual submission required for banks >$100B assets
- CECL: Current Expected Credit Losses; loan loss reserve methodology
- New York DFS 500: Cybersecurity requirements; incident notification; risk assessment

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- Legacy core systems: 70% of bank IT spend is "keep the lights on"; core modernization takes 5-10 years and $100M-$500M
- Branch traffic decline: Branch transactions down 40-50% since 2019; but branch costs remain fixed ($1-2M/year per branch)
- Fraud losses: US card fraud $12B+ annually; ACH fraud growing 25% YoY; authorized push payment fraud emerging
- Regulatory burden: Compliance spend $30B+ annually for US banks; growing 5-8% per year
- Talent shortage: 30% of bank IT workforce eligible for retirement in 5 years; competition with fintech for talent
- Digital adoption gap: Mobile banking adoption 65% but mobile loan application rate only 15%; digital drop-off high
- Cyber threats: Bank cyber incidents up 30% YoY; ransomware targeting financial institutions; cost per breach $5.9M

**Buying Triggers**
- Regulatory change (new CFPB rule, Fed guidance): Mandatory compliance projects
- Consent order / enforcement action: Mandatory remediation; timeline-driven; budget unlocked
- M&A announcement: System consolidation; data migration; integration projects
- Earnings call priorities: "Digital transformation," "efficiency ratio improvement," "deposit growth"
- New CIO/CTO hire: Modernization agenda; typically evaluates stack within 90-120 days
- Core conversion announcement: 12-24 month project; adjacent tool purchases follow
- Branch closure program: Digital banking investment; cost optimization
- Partnership with fintech: API integration; open banking infrastructure
- Cyber incident (theirs or peer): Security investment; incident response capability

**Channels**
- Executive events: Money20/20, Finovate, BAI Global Banking Conference, ABA Annual Convention
- Email: Primary cold channel; must be peer-referenced; CIO/CTO response rate 1-3%
- Advisory firms: Gartner Financial Services, Celent, Aite-Novarica — significant influence on bank buying
- Trade media: American Banker, Banking Dive, The Financial Brand
- LinkedIn: CIO, Head of Digital, CTO — thought leadership performs well
- Industry associations: ABA, ICBA (community banks), CUNA (credit unions)
- Peer networks: CIO roundtables; bank technology forums

**Vocabulary**
- NII (Net Interest Income), NIM (Net Interest Margin), ROE, ROA, efficiency ratio, cost-to-income ratio
- Core banking, core conversion, core modernization, legacy migration, rip-and-replace vs. wrap-and-renew
- Deposits, loan-to-deposit ratio, cost of deposits, deposit beta, core vs. non-core deposits
- CCAR, DFAST, stress testing, capital adequacy, CET1 ratio, Tier 1 capital
- BSA/AML, KYC, CDD (Customer Due Diligence), EDD (Enhanced Due Diligence), SAR, CTR
- OFAC, sanctions screening, prohibited party, PEP (Politically Exposed Person)
- CECL, ALLL (Allowance for Loan and Lease Losses), provision, charge-off, recovery
- RTP, FedNow, ACH, wire, SWIFT, correspondent banking, Nostro/Vostro
- Digital banking, mobile banking, online banking, omnichannel banking, branch transformation
- API banking, open banking, BaaS (Banking-as-a-Service), embedded finance

### 2.2 Fintech

**Market Shape**
- Global fintech market: ~$300B revenue (2025); $50B+ VC funding annually
- Sub-sectors: Payments (35%), lending (20%), wealth (15%), neobanking (15%), B2B/infra (15%)
- Market structure: 10,000+ fintechs globally; ~500 at scale ($100M+ revenue); high mortality rate
- Public fintechs: Block (Square), PayPal, Stripe (private, $65B valuation), SoFi, Affirm, Robinhood
- Consolidation: 300+ M&A deals per year; banks acquiring fintechs; fintech-fintech mergers

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Transaction fees (payments), interest income (lending), SaaS subscriptions (B2B), interchange (cards), spread (neobanks)
- Margins: Payments (gross 20-40%), lending (gross 50-70%), SaaS (gross 70-85%), interchange (gross 15-30%)
- Unit economics: CAC $20-200 per user; LTV $50-500 per user; payback period 6-18 months
- Growth model: Land with free/low-cost product; monetize through premium, credit, or interchange
- Key metric: MAU/DAU, revenue per user, take rate, authorization rate, default rate (lending)

**Tech Stack**
- Cloud: AWS (dominant), GCP, Azure — fintech is cloud-native by definition
- Payments: Stripe (most common), Adyen, Checkout.com, Wise (cross-border)
- Banking infrastructure: Plaid (account connectivity), Treasury Prime (BaaS), Synctera, Unit
- Identity/KYC: Jumio, Onfido (Entrust), Socure, Alloy, Persona
- Data: Snowflake, BigQuery, dbt, Fivetran, Segment
- Infrastructure: Kubernetes, Terraform, Datadog, PagerDuty, LaunchDarkly
- Lending: Blend, nCino, custom (most build their own underwriting engine)
- Banking: Mambu (core), Column (banking infrastructure), Lead Bank (BaaS sponsor)

**Decision-Making**
- CTO/VP Engineering: Technology architecture; build vs. buy decisions; vendor selection
- VP Product: Feature roadmap; user experience; integration decisions
- Head of Compliance/Chief Compliance Officer: Regulatory requirements; vendor compliance review
- CEO: Strategic direction; partnership decisions; M&A
- Board/investors: Growth metrics pressure; unit economics scrutiny; IPO readiness

**Budget Cycles**
- Rolling budgets: Fintech budgets are more fluid; tied to funding rounds and growth targets
- Post-funding spend: Series A → 12-month runway allocation; Series B → 18-24 month planning
- Product roadmap: Monthly/quarterly OKRs; agile sprint planning
- Compliance budget: Often reactive; scales with regulatory scrutiny
- Conference budget: Significant; fintech event presence is a recruiting and sales channel

**Regulatory**
- Charter-dependent: Bank charter (OCC/state) vs. fintech charter vs. no charter (partner bank model)
- State money transmitter licenses: Required in 49 states for payments; expensive and time-consuming ($500K-$2M to obtain all)
- CFPB: UDAAP authority; scrutiny of fintech lending practices; "junk fees" focus
- SEC: If offering investment products; Reg D, Reg A+, Reg CF exemptions
- SOC 2 Type II: Expected by enterprise customers; audit cost $50K-$200K annually
- State lending laws: Rate caps (36% in many states); usury laws; licensing requirements
- GDPR/CCPA: Data processing requirements; cross-border data transfer restrictions
- ESG/regulatory: Consumer protection focus; fair lending scrutiny; algorithmic bias regulation emerging

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- Compliance cost: $2-10M annually for compliance team + technology + licensing at growth stage
- Fraud: Authorization fraud 0.06-0.1% of transaction volume; friendly fraud growing 25% YoY
- Unit economics: CAC rising 30-50% YoY in saturated markets; LTV/CAC under pressure
- Talent: Engineering talent $150-300K fully loaded; fintech competes with big tech for AI/ML talent
- Bank partner dependency: BaaS model relies on sponsor banks; sponsor bank failures create existential risk
- Regulatory uncertainty: CFPB rulemaking pace creates compliance whiplash; enforcement-first approach
- Scale challenges: Systems built for 100K users break at 1M; re-architecture needed; 6-12 month projects

**Buying Triggers**
- Funding round: Scaling budget; infrastructure investment; compliance maturity needed
- New product launch (e.g., neobank launching credit): New compliance requirements; new technology needs
- SOC 2 audit: Pre-audit gap analysis; technology investment to close gaps
- Regulatory exam/enforcement: Mandatory remediation; compliance technology investment
- Scale bottleneck: System performance degradation at scale; architecture investment needed
- Partnership with bank/financial institution: Integration requirements; security/compliance upgrade
- IPO preparation: SOX compliance; audit readiness; enterprise-grade systems required
- Competitor launch: Feature parity pressure; speed-to-market investment

**Channels**
- Engineering blogs: Stripe, Plaid, Brex — engineers read these before engaging sales
- Developer conferences: Strange Loop, QCon, KubeCon — fintech engineering presence
- Fintech events: Money20/20, Finovate, LendIt, Tearsheet conferences
- LinkedIn: CTO, VP Engineering, Head of Product — technical content performs well
- Twitter/X: Fintech Twitter community; founders and investors active
- Slack/Discord communities: Fintech Devs, various company-run communities
- YC/network: Portfolio company referrals; accelerator network

**Vocabulary**
- MAU, DAU, ARPU, take rate, interchange, issuer processor, acquirer processor
- BaaS (Banking-as-a-Service), embedded finance, sponsor bank, partner bank
- KYC, CDD, EDD, AML, SAR, CTR — compliance acronym fluency is table stakes
- API-first, developer experience, SDK, sandbox, webhook, idempotency
- Lending: underwriting, credit decision, scorecard, default rate, charge-off, recovery
- Payments: authorization, settlement, clearing, reconciliation, refund, chargeback, representment
- Neobank: challenger bank, digital bank, high-yield savings, early direct deposit, overdraft-free
- Unit economics: CAC, LTV, LTV/CAC, payback period, contribution margin, burn rate, runway

### 2.3 Insurance (P&C, Life, Reinsurance)

**Market Shape**
- Global insurance premiums: ~$7T (2025); US ~$3T; P&C ~$2.5T global, Life ~$3T global
- Market structure: Top 10 US P&C insurers = 50% market share; top 10 life = 45%; long tail of mutuals and specialists
- Reinsurance: Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re, SCOR — $700B+ premium
- Insurtech: ~3,000 companies; ~$10B VC funding annually; Lemonade, Hippo, Root (public, struggling)
- Market dynamics: Hard market (rising rates) in P&C since 2020; social inflation driving liability costs; climate risk escalating

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Premium income (primary), investment income (20-30% of total revenue for life), fee income
- Margins: Combined ratio (P&C) target <100% (underwriting profit); investment yield 4-5%; total ROE 8-12%
- Underwriting: Risk selection, pricing, portfolio management; actuarial science core discipline
- Distribution: Independent agents (60% of P&C), direct (20%), captive agents (15%), brokers (5%); shifting toward direct
- Key metric: Combined ratio, loss ratio, expense ratio, retention rate, policy growth, investment yield

**Tech Stack**
- Policy admin: Guidewire (dominant in P&C — 300+ carriers), Duck Creek, Majesco, FAST (Verisk — life)
- Claims: Guidewire ClaimCenter, Duck Creek Claims, Friss (fraud), Tractable (AI claims)
- Underwriting: Verisk (ISO), LexisNexis Risk Solutions, Risk Management Solutions (RMS — Moody's)
- Distribution: Applied Systems (agency management), EZLynx (rating), ITC (agency marketing)
- Reinsurance: Sapiens, Reinsurance Systems, custom actuarial models
- Data/analytics: SAS, Verisk, ISO, CLUEDIN, Snowflake
- CRM: Salesforce Financial Services Cloud, Microsoft Dynamics
- Customer engagement: Five9 (call center), Sprinklr (CX), custom mobile apps

**Decision-Making**
- CIO/CTO: Technology strategy; core system decisions; cloud migration
- Head of Claims: Claims technology; fraud detection; customer experience
- Head of Underwriting: Pricing technology; risk assessment tools; underwriting workflow
- Chief Actuary: Pricing models; reserving; capital modeling
- CEO/COO: Strategic direction; digital transformation; distribution strategy
- Board/audit committee: Risk management; cyber risk; regulatory compliance
- Agent advisory councils: Independent agents influence technology decisions (especially distribution tech)

**Budget Cycles**
- Annual budget: Q3-Q4 planning; board approval December-January
- Actuarial cycle: Rate filing preparation (6-12 months before implementation); technology supports rate changes
- Claim season: Natural disaster season (June-November hurricane) drives claims technology demand
- Regulatory filings: Annual statement preparation (March); technology supports regulatory reporting
- Multi-year programs: Core system replacements (3-7 year programs); major CapEx commitment

**Regulatory**
- NAIC (National Association of Insurance Commissioners): Model laws; accreditation; solvency oversight
- State insurance departments: 50 state regulators; rate filing requirements; market conduct examinations
- McCarran-Ferguson Act: State primacy in insurance regulation (with limited federal role)
- Dodd-Frank/FIO: Federal Insurance Office; systemic risk monitoring; limited direct authority
- Risk-based capital (RBC): NAIC capital requirement; annual filing; action levels trigger regulatory intervention
- ORSA (Own Risk and Solvency Assessment): Enterprise risk management; annual self-assessment required
- GDPR/privacy: Consumer data handling; consent requirements; cross-border data transfer
- AI/ML regulation: Colorado AI insurance law (2024); NAIC model bulletin on AI; growing scrutiny of algorithmic underwriting
- Climate risk: NAIC climate disclosure requirements; TCFD alignment; growing regulatory focus

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- Legacy policy admin: 60%+ of P&C insurers still on legacy systems (25+ years old); replacement cost $50M-$300M
- Claims leakage: 5-10% of claims paid are overpayments; $30B+ annually in P&C leakage
- Claims fraud: P&C fraud $45B+ annually (10% of total P&C losses); detection rates only 5-10%
- Combined ratio pressure: Industry combined ratio 95-100% (barely profitable underwriting); social inflation driving 5-8% annual loss cost increases
- Distribution costs: Agent commissions 10-20% of premium; shifting to direct but customer acquisition expensive ($200-500 per policy)
- Talent shortage: 50% of actuarial workforce approaching retirement; data science talent competes with tech
- Catastrophe losses: $100B+ annually in global insured catastrophe losses; rising 5-8% per year due to climate change
- Customer experience: NPS for insurance typically 15-30 (lowest of any financial service); claims experience is make-or-break

**Buying Triggers**
- Catastrophe event: Claims surge; technology needs for CAT modeling, claims automation, fraud detection
- Rate filing denial/reduction: Need for better pricing models; more granular risk assessment
- Legacy system failure: Outage or data breach triggers modernization discussion
- New CIO hire: Modernization agenda; cloud-first strategy; digital transformation
- Regulatory change (NAIC model law, state requirement): Compliance project budget unlocked
- M&A: System consolidation; data migration; integration of disparate policy admin systems
- Insurtech competition: Lemonade, Hippo pressure on customer experience; digital response needed
- Earnings pressure: Combined ratio improvement target; expense ratio reduction; technology as lever
- Agent feedback: Independent agent advisory council recommendations; distribution technology investment

**Channels**
- Industry events: ITC Vegas (Insurtech Connect), ACORD Annual Conference, RIMS, LOMA
- Email: Primary cold channel for CIO/VP; peer references mandatory
- Advisory firms: Celent, Aite-Novarica, Gartner Insurance — significant influence
- Trade media: Insurance Journal, Carrier Management, Insurance Thought Leadership
- LinkedIn: CIO, Head of Claims, Chief Actuary — content about claims automation and AI performs well
- Actuarial organizations: CAS (Casualty Actuarial Society), SOA (Society of Actuaries)
- Agent networks: Independent agent associations; agency management system influence

**Vocabulary**
- Combined ratio, loss ratio, expense ratio, underwriting profit, investment income
- Premium, rate, rate filing, loss cost, trend factor, credibility
- Policy, endorsement, rider, deductible, limit, sublimit, aggregate
- Claim, first notice of loss (FNOL), adjuster, reserve, subrogation, salvage
- Leakage, fraud detection, SIU (Special Investigations Unit), anti-fraud
- Retention, renewal, lapse rate, policy lifetime, churn
- Reinsurance, treaty, facultative, ceding commission, retrocession
- Cat modeling, PML (Probable Maximum Loss), AAL (Average Annual Loss), return period
- RBC, ORSA, statutory accounting, SAP vs GAAP, surplus, admitted vs non-admitted
- Underwriting, risk selection, pricing, segmentation, class plan, territory

### 2.4 Wealth & Asset Management

**Market Shape**
- Global AUM: ~$120T (2025); US ~$50T; growing 8-10% per year (market-dependent)
- Market structure: Top 10 asset managers hold 30% of global AUM (BlackRock $10T, Vanguard $8T, Fidelity $4.5T, State Street $4T)
- Wealth management: ~400K financial advisors in US; wirehouses (Morgan Stanley, Merrill, Wells Fargo) vs. RIAs (17,000+ firms) vs. independents
- Alternative assets: PE $8T, hedge funds $5T, real assets $3T — growing share of allocations
- RIA growth: Fastest-growing segment; 17,000+ SEC-registered RIAs; average AUM $400M

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Management fees (0.5-2% of AUM), performance fees (hedge funds, PE — 20% of profits above hurdle), transaction fees, advisory fees
- Margins: Asset management gross margin 60-80%; wealth management EBITDA margin 25-35%
- Fee compression: Average equity fund fee 0.44% (down from 0.99% in 2000); index/ETF pressure
- Distribution: Institutional (60%), wealth advisory (25%), direct-to-consumer (15%)
- Key metric: AUM growth, net flows, fee rate, client retention, advisor productivity (revenue per advisor)

**Tech Stack**
- Portfolio management: Bloomberg Terminal (front office), BlackRock Aladdin (risk/institutional), Charles River IMS, SimCorp Dimension (Europe)
- Order management: Charles River, Bloomberg TOMS, Eze (hedge funds)
- CRM: Salesforce Financial Services Cloud (dominant), Redtail (RIA), Wealthbox (RIA)
- Financial planning: eMoney Advisor (Fidelity), MoneyGuidePro (Envestnet), RightCapital
- Custody/integration: Fidelity, Schwab, Pershing, BNY Mellon — API connectivity critical
- Reporting: Addepar (multi-asset reporting), Black Diamond (SS&C), Orion
- Compliance: Smarsh (communications archiving), Lexicon (surveillance), ComplySci
- Research: Bloomberg, FactSet, Refinitiv (LSEG), Capital IQ (S&P)

**Decision-Making**
- CIO/CTO: Technology strategy; front-office systems; data infrastructure
- COO/Head of Operations: Middle/back-office technology; operational efficiency; vendor management
- Chief Compliance Officer: Regulatory technology; surveillance; archiving
- Head of Wealth Management: Advisor technology stack; client experience; practice management
- Portfolio Manager: Front-office tools; research access; execution quality
- CEO: Strategic direction; digital transformation; M&A integration
- Investment committee: Risk systems; compliance technology (indirect influence)

**Budget Cycles**
- Annual budget: Q4 planning for following year; aligned with fiscal year (often calendar)
- Front-office budget: Portfolio managers have significant influence; premium tools justified by alpha generation
- Compliance budget: Often reactive; scales with regulatory scrutiny and AUM growth
- Technology refresh: 3-5 year cycles for core systems; annual upgrades for front-office tools
- Advisory firm budget: Small RIAs ($1-5M revenue) spend 3-5% on technology; larger firms 5-8%

**Regulatory**
- SEC: Investment Advisers Act of 1940; fiduciary duty; Form ADV; examinations
- FINRA: Broker-dealer regulation; suitability; best execution; communications compliance
- ERISA: Employee Retirement Income Security Act; fiduciary rules for retirement assets
- DOL fiduciary rule: Expanding fiduciary definition; impact on IRA rollover recommendations
- MiFID II (EU): Best execution; research unbundling; transaction reporting
- GDPR: Client data handling; cross-border data transfer; right to erasure
- AML/KYC: Customer Identification Program; SAR filing; beneficial ownership
- Tax reporting: Cost basis tracking; wash sale compliance; 1099 reporting
- Marketing rule (SEC 2022): New rule for investment adviser advertisements; testimonials now permitted with conditions
- ESG regulation: SEC climate disclosure; EU SFDR; greenwashing scrutiny

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- Fee compression: Average fee 0.44% (down from 0.99%); revenue flat despite AUM growth; must scale operations without proportional cost increase
- Operational complexity: Multi-custodian, multi-asset, multi-jurisdiction — reconciliation takes 40-60% of operations team time
- Compliance burden: SEC exam cycle 3-5 years; exam preparation costs $200K-$1M per cycle; deficiency rate 40%+
- Technology fragmentation: Average wealth firm uses 12-20 tools that don't integrate; data silos; manual reconciliation
- Advisor productivity: Average advisor manages $100-150M AUM; top performers $300-500M; technology is the multiplier
- Client expectations: Digital-first experience expected; 70% of heirs change advisor after inheritance; tech-enabled CX is retention
- Alternative assets: Operational complexity of PE, hedge funds, crypto; valuation challenges; reporting gaps
- Cyber risk: $5.9M average cost per financial services breach; wire fraud schemes targeting advisory firms

**Buying Triggers**
- SEC examination announcement: Compliance technology investment; documentation remediation
- AUM growth milestone: Operations scalability; technology upgrade needed
- New CTO/COO hire: Technology modernization agenda
- Custodian change (e.g., Schwab/TD Ameritrade integration): Technology migration required
- M&A (firm acquisition): System consolidation; data migration; integration projects
- Client demand for digital portal: Client experience investment; reporting technology
- ESG reporting requirement: Data infrastructure; ESG scoring integration; compliance tools
- Fee compression pressure: Operational efficiency investment; automation; advisor productivity tools
- Regulatory change (marketing rule, fiduciary): Compliance technology update; process changes

**Channels**
- Industry events: WealthManagement.com Conference, Schwab Impact, Orion Ascent, eMoney Summit
- Email: Primary cold channel; must reference specific AUM range and peer firms
- Advisory firms: Gartner Wealth Management, Aite-Novarica; influence on enterprise buying
- Trade media: WealthManagement.com, RIABiz, Financial Planning, InvestmentNews
- LinkedIn: CIO, COO, Head of Wealth — content about operational efficiency and fee compression
- Custodian referrals: Schwab, Fidelity, Pershing recommend technology partners to advisors
- T3 conferences: Technology hub for wealth management; advisors evaluate tools here

**Vocabulary**
- AUM, net flows, gross flows, redemptions, fee rate, basis points (bps)
- Fiduciary, suitability, best execution, duty of care, duty of loyalty
- Front office, middle office, back office, STP (Straight-Through Processing)

### 2.5 Payments

**Market Shape**
- Global payments revenue: ~$2.5T (2025); growing 6-8% per year
- Card networks: Visa (60% global card volume), Mastercard (30%), Amex/Discover/JCB (10%)
- Processors: Stripe, Adyen, Fiserv (First Data), FIS (Worldpay), Global Payments (TSYS), Checkout.com
- PSPs: Worldpay, Checkout.com, Braintree (PayPal), CyberSource (Visa)
- BNPL: Affirm, Klarna, Afterpay (Block); under regulatory scrutiny but growing 20%+ per year
- Real-time payments: RTP (US), FedNow (US, launched 2023), UPI (India — 12B+ monthly transactions), Pix (Brazil)

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Interchange fees (0.5-3% of transaction), processing fees ($0.10-0.30 per transaction), subscription fees, FX markup, value-added services
- Margins: Interchange (gross 70-90%), processing (gross 30-50%), FX (gross 50-80%)
- Take rate: Varies by segment — SMB 2.5-3.5%, enterprise 1.5-2.5%, marketplace 2-4%
- Key metric: Payment volume (TPV), authorization rate, chargeback rate, active merchants, revenue per merchant
- Flywheel: More payment methods → more merchants → more volume → better rates → more payment methods

**Tech Stack**
- Processing: Stripe (API-first), Adyen (enterprise), Checkout.com (enterprise), Fiserv (legacy enterprise)
- Fraud: Sift, Kount (Equifax), Signifyd, Riskified, Forter
- Authentication: 3D Secure 2.0, FIDO, biometrics
- Orchestration: Spreedly, Pagos, Primer (payments orchestration layer)
- Reconciliation: ReconArt, BlackLine, custom
- FX: Wise (TransferWise), Currencycloud, custom
- Infrastructure: Cloud-native (AWS/GCP), microservices, event-driven architecture

**Decision-Making**
- CTO/VP Engineering: Payment architecture; processor selection; fraud technology
- Head of Payments: Payment strategy; method expansion; authorization optimization
- VP Product: Checkout experience; payment method roadmap; conversion optimization
- CFO: Processing cost optimization; FX management; reconciliation

## 7. Compliance Considerations for Outreach

### FINRA Rules
- Outreach to broker-dealers must comply with communications rules (FINRA Rule 2210)
- Must not make false or misleading claims about financial products
- Testimonial/recommendation rules apply to any client references

### TCPA
- Auto-dialers and SMS require prior express consent
- $1,500 per message for willful violations
- Financial services cold calling has additional state-level restrictions

### GLBA
- Financial institution data sharing restrictions
- Privacy notices required; opt-out rights
- LeadReach must not share financial prospect data with third parties

### Cross-Border
- EU: GDPR applies; data processing agreements required; standard contractual clauses for data transfer
- UK: UK GDPR + FCA conduct rules for financial promotions
- Singapore: MAS Technology Risk Management guidelines; PDPA
- Different rules for EU vs US vs APAC prospects — always check jurisdiction

### LeadReach Agent Rules
- **NEVER** send cold emails to consumer-facing banking customers (only B2B contacts)
- **NEVER** make automated calls without consent (TCPA)
- **NEVER** store financial account numbers (GLBA)
- **NEVER** claim FINRA/SEC registration that doesn't exist
- **ALWAYS** include proper disclaimers if referencing financial products
- **ALWAYS** verify the contact is a business/professional contact, not a consumer

## 8. Common Financial Services Prospecting Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | What To Do Instead |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Wrong regulator | Pitching US bank with EU compliance language | Know their charter type and primary regulator |
| No peer references | Banks/insurers won't engage without them | Always include 1-2 peer institution references |
| Ignoring the core system | Every solution must integrate with FIS/Fiserv/Jack Henry | Reference core system integration in first touch |
| Underestimating security review | 3-6 months for security review is normal | Proactively send SOC 2 / ISO 27001 documentation |
| Consumer vs. commercial confusion | Different regulations; targeting retail customers is illegal | Target B2B contacts only; verify role |
| Not knowing the charter type | Bank vs. credit union vs. fintech = different rules | Research charter type before outreach |
| Generic "digital transformation" | Every vendor says this; meaningless | Reference specific workflow: "loan origination," "claims automation" |
| Wrong buying committee | Pitching CMO on core banking technology | Map the buying committee for each sub-segment |
| Ignoring procurement | Banks have formal procurement; going around it kills deals | Offer to engage procurement early; provide security documentation |
| No compliance language | Financial buyers filter for compliance signals | Mention SOC 2, ISO, FedRAMP, PCI as relevant |
