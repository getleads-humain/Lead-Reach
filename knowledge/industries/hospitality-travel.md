# Hospitality & Travel Industry — Prospecting Guide, Signals, and Buyer Personas

## 1. Industry Overview

The global Hospitality & Travel industry is a **$5.4T+ aggregate market** spanning hotels ($600B+), short-term rentals ($100B+), food service ($4T+), online travel ($1T+ gross bookings), corporate travel ($$1.4T spend), cruise ($30B), casinos ($260B+), and travel technology. It is the largest services industry on earth by employment, with restaurants alone employing 15M+ in the US and hotels adding another 2M+. The industry was the most disrupted by COVID-19 — global travel spend fell 73% in 2020 — and is still mid-recovery: US hotel RevPAR exceeded 2019 levels only in 2023, and international arrivals to Asia-Pacific remained below pre-pandemic through 2024. The post-COVID reset has accelerated technology adoption by 5-7 years, according to Skift Research, with cloud PMS, contactless check-in, and revenue management systems moving from nice-to-have to table stakes.

Three structural forces define prospecting in this industry. First, **franchise vs. corporate decision rights** — Marriott, Hilton, IHG, and Hyatt own fewer than 1% of their properties; the rest are franchised or managed. Selling to "Marriott" often means selling to 5,000+ individual franchisees through the brand's approved vendor list — a 12-24 month process — OR selling to a top-50 multi-unit franchisee (Sunstone, Apple Hospitality, Service Properties, Ashford) that can deploy across 50-200 properties. Second, **margin compression and labor shortage** — restaurant margins hover at 3-9%, hotels at 20-35% EBITDA but with high operating leverage; labor costs are up 25%+ post-COVID, and any tool that reduces labor hours or improves yield per labor dollar gets executive attention. Third, **digital distribution dominance by OTAs** — Booking, Expedia, and Airbnb take 12-25% commission, and direct-booking tools that shift share to brand.com are an evergreen pitch.

The hospitality buyer is **operational, hands-on, data-driven at the unit level, and intensely focused on weekly metrics** (RevPAR, ADR, occupancy, food cost %, labor cost %). Outreach must speak the language of weekly P&L and unit economics. Generic "AI-powered growth" pitches fall flat; "we reduced labor cost 1.2% at comparable McDonald's franchisees" wins meetings. Sales cycles range from 14 days (single-unit restaurant operators) to 24 months (multi-brand, multi-property franchisee deployments). Decision-making authority is often surprisingly concentrated — a General Manager or franchisee owner can often greenlight $50K-$200K in annual spend without escalation.

## 2. Sub-Segments

Hospitality & Travel is the broadest industry in this guide. Identify the sub-segment first; everything else flows from that.

### Luxury & Upper-Upscale Hotels
Four Seasons, Mandarin Oriental, Ritz-Carlton (Marriott), St. Regis (Marriott), Waldorf Astoria (Hilton), Park Hyatt, Peninsula, Aman, Belmond (LVMH), Auberge Resorts. ADR $500-$2,500+. Targets: ultra-high-net-worth travelers, corporate execs, group business.

**Buyer profile**: General Manager, Regional VP, Director of Sales & Marketing, Director of Revenue, F&B Director, Spa Director, CIO (brand-level).
**Sales cycle**: 30-90 days for single-property tech; 6-12 months for brand-level.
**Budget source**: Property operating budget (P&L); brand capex for brand-wide tech.

### Midscale & Upscale Hotels
Marriott (Courtyard, Fairfield), Hilton (Hampton, Hilton Garden Inn), Hyatt (Hyatt Place, Hyatt House), IHG (Holiday Inn, Holiday Inn Express), Accor (Mercure, Novotel), Wyndham (Super 8, Days Inn), Choice (Comfort, Quality). The bulk of US hotel rooms.

**Buyer profile**: GM (single property), Regional Director (multi-property), VP Operations (multi-unit franchisee), CIO (brand-level).
**Sales cycle**: 30-90 days for unit-level; 6-18 months for brand-level.
**Budget source**: Property P&L; brand tech fund; franchisee co-op.

### Economy & Extended Stay
Motel 6, Red Roof Inn, Extended Stay America, WoodSpring Suites, Value Place, InTown Suites. ADR $40-$90. Targets: budget travelers, construction crews, transient workers, relocating families.

**Buyer profile**: GM, Regional Manager, VP Operations (chain-owned), Owner-Operator (franchised).
**Sales cycle**: 30-60 days.
**Budget source**: Operating budget; owner capital.

### Short-Term Rentals & Alternative Accommodation
Airbnb (5M+ hosts, 7.7M listings), Vrbo (Expedia), Vacasa, Sonder, AvantStay, Casago, Evolve, TurnKey. ~$100B+ global GMV; growing 15-20% annually.

**Buyer profile**: Founder/CEO (most are VC-backed), Head of Operations, Head of Revenue, Head of Supply, Head of Technology.
**Sales cycle**: 30-120 days.
**Budget source**: VC funding + operating revenue; technology is core to the model.

### Restaurants — QSR (Quick Service)
McDonald's, Burger King, Wendy's, Taco Bell (Yum), KFC (Yum), Subway, Chick-fil-A, Domino's, Pizza Hut, Dunkin', Starbucks, Tim Hortons, Panera. ~$380B US QSR market.

**Buyer profile**: Franchisee Owner (single to 50+ units), Director of Operations (multi-unit), VP Marketing (brand), CIO/CTO (brand).
**Sales cycle**: 14-60 days for unit-level; 6-18 months for brand-level.
**Budget source**: Franchisee operating budget; brand tech fund; franchisee co-op (NFA, NFCF).

### Restaurants — Fast Casual & Casual Dining
Chipotle, Sweetgreen, Cava, Panera, Shake Shack, Five Guys, MOD Pizza (casual); Darden (Olive Garden, LongHorn, Bahama Breeze, Yard House, Capital Grille, Ruth's Chris), Texas Roadhouse, Bloomin' (Outback), Brinker (Chili's), Cracker Barrel, Applebee's (casual dining).

**Buyer profile**: CEO, COO, VP Operations, CIO, VP Marketing, VP Culinary, Director of Real Estate.
**Sales cycle**: 60-180 days for brand-level; 14-60 days for unit-level.
**Budget source**: Corporate operating budget; capex for equipment/renovation.

### Restaurants — Fine Dining & Independent
Independent operators, small groups (Atlas Restaurant Group, Noble 33, STARR Restaurants, Lettuce Entertain You, Hillstone), Michelin-starred restaurants.

**Buyer profile**: Owner-Operator, Executive Chef, GM, Beverage Director.
**Sales cycle**: 14-30 days; fast, decisive buyers.
**Budget source**: Operating budget; owner capital.

### Online Travel Agencies (OTAs) & Metasearch
Booking Holdings (Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, Kayak, OpenTable), Expedia Group (Expedia, Hotels.com, Vrbo, Trivago), Trip.com Group, Airbnb, MakeMyTrip, Despegar, Hopper, Google Travel/Hotels.

**Buyer profile**: VP Product, VP Engineering, VP Supply, VP Revenue, Head of Partner Services, CMO.
**Sales cycle**: 60-180 days; sophisticated buyers.
**Budget source**: Operating budget; technology is the core product.

### Corporate Travel & TMCs
American Express Global Business Travel (GBT), CWT, BCD Travel, TripActions (Navan), SAP Concur, TravelPerk, corporate travel managers at Fortune 500. ~$1.4T global corporate travel spend.

**Buyer profile**: Travel Manager, Procurement Lead, CFO (cost containment), CHRO (employee experience).
**Sales cycle**: 90-180 days; often formal RFP.
**Budget source**: Travel budget (TMCs); corporate procurement.

### Cruise Lines
Carnival Corporation (Carnival, Princess, Holland America, Cunard, Costa, AIDA), Royal Caribbean Group (RCL, Celebrity, Silversea), Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings (NCL, Oceania, Regent), MSC Cruises, Disney Cruise Line, Virgin Voyages. ~$30B+ global revenue.

**Buyer profile**: VP Operations, VP Guest Experience, VP Revenue, CIO, VP Onboard Revenue.
**Sales cycle**: 90-365 days.
**Budget source**: Operating budget; capex for newbuilds and renovations.

### Casinos & Gaming
Las Vegas Sands, MGM Resorts International, Caesars Entertainment, Wynn, Penn Entertainment, Boyd Gaming, Galaxy, Melco. Combined casino/hotel $260B+ global market.

**Buyer profile**: General Manager, VP Casino Operations, VP Player Development, CIO, VP Marketing.
**Sales cycle**: 90-180 days.
**Budget source**: Property P&L; capex for property renovation.

### Travel Tech / PMS / CRS / RMS
Property Management Systems (Opera Cloud / Oracle, Mews, Cloudbeds, Stayntouch, Apaleo, RMS Cloud), Central Reservation Systems (SynXis/Sabre, TravelClick/Amadeus, IDeaS RMS, Duetto RMS, Cendyn CRS, Pegasus), GDS (Sabre, Amadeus, Travelport), payment (Shift4, Adyen, FreedomPay), guest experience (CANARY, StayMyWay, Akia), marketing (Cendyn, Revinate, TravelClick).

**Buyer profile**: CIO, VP IT, Director of Revenue, VP Digital, Director of Distribution.
**Sales cycle**: 90-365 days; integration-heavy.
**Budget source**: IT budget; brand-mandated at franchisee level.

## 5. Buyer Personas

### The General Manager (Hotel)
- **At**: Any hotel, 100-room select-service to 1,000-room luxury
- **Cares about**: GOP (gross operating profit), guest satisfaction scores (Medallia, TrustYou), team retention, brand inspection scores, RevPAR vs. comp set
- **Pain**: Labor costs, OTA commissions, online reviews, brand-mandated tech rollouts, capex requests getting denied
- **Outreach angle**: GOP impact, comp set benchmarks, brand-mandate compliance, labor savings
- **Channels**: Direct email, AAHOA, AHLA, brand conferences (Marriott GM Summit, Hilton GM Conference)
- **What NOT to say**: "Strategic transformation" — GMs think weekly P&L, not strategic transformation

### The Regional VP / Multi-Unit Franchisee
- **At**: Multi-unit operators (Apple Hospitality, Sunstone, top 50 franchisees)
- **Cares about**: Portfolio RevPAR, asset turnover, brand-mix optimization, capex allocation, exit valuations
- **Pain**: Inconsistent unit performance, GM turnover, brand-mandated capex without ROI clarity, labor cost variance
- **Outreach angle**: Portfolio-wide benchmarks, GM enablement tools, capex ROI studies
- **Channels**: Direct email, AAHOA, ALIS (Americas Lodging Investment Summit), regional brand meetings
- **What NOT to say**: Single-unit case studies; multi-unit buyers need portfolio evidence

### The Director of Revenue Management (Hotel)
- **At**: Any hotel with RevMgmt function; brand-level at Director, VP Revenue at corporate
- **Cares about**: ADR, RevPAR, occupancy, channel mix, competitive set indexing, forecast accuracy
- **Pain**: Manual rate shopping, OTA commission creep, group vs. transient displacement, RMS limitations, weekend compression
- **Outreach angle**: RMS integration, channel manager automation, group displacement analytics, peer benchmarks
- **Channels**: HSMAI (Hospitality Sales & Marketing Association International), LinkedIn, RMS vendor user groups
- **What NOT to say**: "AI-driven pricing" without specifying model, training data, and override workflow

### The F&B Director / Restaurant Operator
- **At**: Hotels (F&B Director), restaurants (Director of Operations, Owner, Executive Chef)
- **Cares about**: Food cost %, labor cost %, ticket average, table turns, menu engineering, guest satisfaction
- **Pain**: Commodity cost volatility, labor scheduling, kitchen tech integration, allergen tracking, third-party delivery economics
- **Outreach angle**: Food cost reduction, labor scheduling optimization, menu engineering, third-party delivery margin recovery
- **Channels**: NRA (National Restaurant Association), CoStar, LinkedIn, foodservice trade shows (NRA Show, IFMA)
- **What NOT to say**: "Reduce food waste" without quantifying — operators have heard it all

### The CIO / VP IT (Brand or Multi-Unit)
- **At**: Major brands, top-50 franchisees, large restaurant chains
- **Cares about**: Tech stack consolidation, integration architecture, cybersecurity, PCI compliance, vendor management
- **Pain**: Legacy on-prem Opera/Simphony, brand-mandated cloud migration, integration spaghetti, security audit findings
- **Outreach angle**: Integration architecture, security posture, peer brand migrations, vendor consolidation
- **Channels**: HITEC (Hospitality Industry Technology Exposition & Conference), LinkedIn, vendor user conferences
- **What NOT to say**: "Rip and replace" — CIOs want coexistence, migration paths, and integration stories

### The VP Marketing / CMO (Brand)
- **At**: Major brands, OTAs, restaurant chains
- **Cares about**: Direct booking share, loyalty enrollment, NPS, paid media efficiency, brand health
- **Pain**: OTA commission creep, paid media inflation, loyalty program fatigue, social platform algorithm changes
- **Outreach angle**: Direct booking growth case studies, loyalty engagement benchmarks, paid media efficiency peers
- **Channels**: HSMAI Marketing Strategy Conference, DMA, LinkedIn, peer CMO networks
- **What NOT to say**: "Engagement" — CMOs are measured on bookings and revenue, not engagement

### The COO / VP Operations (Restaurant Brand)
- **At**: Multi-unit restaurant brands
- **Cares about**: Unit economics, same-store sales, new unit growth, food cost %, labor cost %, throughput
- **Pain**: Labor cost inflation, commodity volatility, throughput in drive-thru, manager turnover, brand consistency
- **Outreach angle**: Unit economics case studies, throughput benchmarks (cf. McDonald's drive-thru times), labor cost peers
- **Channels**: Restaurant Leadership Conference, LinkedIn, peer COO networks
- **What NOT to say**: "Customer experience" — COOs think unit economics

## 7. Qualification Criteria (BANT / MEDDIC for Hospitality)

Hospitality deals die most often on **Authority** (franchise vs. brand vs. franchisee decision rights) and **Budget** (margin compression + capex cycles). Apply the following framework rigorously.

### BANT
- **Budget**: Single hotel property IT budget $50K-$500K/yr; top-50 franchisee $5M-$50M; brand corporate $50M-$500M. Single restaurant $30K-$200K IT/yr; multi-unit franchisee $1M-$20M; brand corporate $20M-$300M. Disqualify early if revenue band doesn't support your price.
- **Authority**: Hotel GM can typically approve $25K-$100K/yr; RVP up to $500K; brand CIO up to $5M; >$5M needs C-suite + brand President. Restaurant Owner-Operator approves alone up to $50K; multi-unit VP up to $500K; brand >$1M needs C-suite. Franchisees: CEO decides alone up to $1M typically.
- **Need**: Triggered by specific event (new opening, conversion, GM change, brand mandate, RevPAR inflection, post-COVID recovery). No trigger = no urgency.
- **Timing**: Hotels buy Q1 (post-budgeting) and Q3 (pre-holiday); restaurants buy Q1 (post-busy season) and Q3 (pre-holiday); cruise/casino aligned to fiscal year. Brand-level decisions align to brand-specific budgeting cycle.

### MEDDIC
- **Metrics**: RevPAR, ADR, occupancy, GOP, food cost %, labor cost %, direct booking %, loyalty enrollment, NPS, throughput. Tie offering to one of these.
- **Economic Buyer**: Brand: CIO (tech), CMO (marketing), COO (operations). Hotel: GM (property), RVP (multi-property), Owner (franchisee). Restaurant: CEO/Owner (operator), COO (brand), CIO (brand tech).
- **Decision Criteria**: Brand-mandated tech often pre-determines vendor list. RFP-driven for brand-level; informal for unit-level. PCI compliance + SOC 2 are table stakes. References from peer brands/franchisees critical.
- **Decision Process**: Single-property 14-60 days; multi-property 60-180 days; brand-level 6-18 months; franchisee rollouts 90-365 days. Procurement review for >$500K.
- **Identify Pain**: Specific and recent — "Your [Comp set] RevPAR outpaced you by [X%] last quarter; peer properties deployed [tool] to [Y] effect."
- **Champion**: Director of Revenue, GM, or VP Operations with P&L credibility. For brand deals: VP-level exec with multi-property influence.

## 8. Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Confusing Brand vs. Franchisee Decision Rights
Selling to "Marriott" doesn't mean selling to 8,700 Marriott properties — it means getting on Marriott's approved vendor list (12-24 month process) OR selling to one of 5,000+ Marriott franchisees directly (some of whom manage 100+ properties). Determine which path you're on first.

### Pitfall 2: Ignoring Property-Level P&L Reality
Hotel GMs live and die by weekly GOP. Tools that show ROI in 6-12 months win; tools that show ROI in 36 months die. Restaurant operators live and die by food cost % and labor cost % — pitch in those terms.

### Pitfall 3: Underestimating Labor Sensitivity
Post-COVID, labor is the #1 topic in hospitality. Any tool that touches scheduling, training, or labor optimization must address labor cost % explicitly. Don't pitch "employee engagement" — pitch labor cost reduction.

### Pitfall 4: Misreading Seasonality
Hospitality is highly seasonal. Pitching a new tool to a hotel GM during peak season (e.g., ski season in Aspen, summer in Myrtle Beach) means no decision until off-season. Time outreach to industry cycles.

### Pitfall 5: ADR / RevPAR Sensitivity
Hotel operators will scrutinize any tool's impact on RevPAR with forensic intensity. Case studies must show controlled comparisons (comp set vs. control group), not vanity metrics. Same for restaurants on same-store sales.

### Pitfall 6: OTA Commission Wars
Direct booking pitch is evergreen but oversaturated. Differentiate with specific evidence (comp set direct share %, peer brand results). Generic "shift to direct" pitches get ignored.

### Pitfall 7: Brand Mandate Friction
If a brand mandates Oracle Opera Cloud, franchisees must comply — but they often resist. Tools that ease brand-mandate migration (migration services, training, add-ons) are highly attractive to franchisees; tools that compete with brand-mandated tech are radioactive.

### Pitfall 8: High Turnover
Hospitality has 70-80% annual turnover in line roles and 30-40% in management. Don't build pipeline around a single GM or Director — they may not be in role when deal closes. Track role + brand + comp set.

### Pitfall 9: Wrong Stakeholder Map
Emailing a GM about a CRS (brand-level decision), or a brand CIO about a single-property POS upgrade, signals you don't understand the industry. Match tool to function and decision level.

### Pitfall 10: PCI / Compliance Underestimation
Any tool touching payment data triggers PCI-DSS review. Any tool touching guest PII triggers privacy review (GDPR in EU, CCPA in CA). Build compliance package before first call.

## 9. Industry Vocabulary

- **RevPAR** — Revenue per Available Room (ADR × Occupancy; the master hotel metric)
- **ADR** — Average Daily Rate
- **GOP** — Gross Operating Profit
- **GOPPAR** — Gross Operating Profit per Available Room
- **TRevPAR** — Total Revenue per Available Room (including F&B, spa, etc.)
- **Comp set** — Competitive set (benchmark hotels)
- **Occupancy** — % of rooms sold
- **STR** — Smith Travel Research (now CoStar) — weekly hotel data
- **PMS** — Property Management System (Opera Cloud, Mews, Cloudbeds)
- **CRS** — Central Reservation System (SynXis, TravelClick, Cendyn)
- **RMS** — Revenue Management System (IDeaS, Duetto)
- **GDS** — Global Distribution System (Sabre, Amadeus, Travelport)
- **OTA** — Online Travel Agency (Booking, Expedia, Trip.com)
- **BAR** — Best Available Rate
- **Channel manager** — Software distributing inventory across OTAs
- **Direct booking share** — % of bookings via brand.com (vs. OTA)
- **Loyalty share** — % of bookings from loyalty members
- **Capex / Opex** — Capital / Operating Expenditure
- **Brand standard** — Mandated specifications for franchised properties
- **PIP** — Property Improvement Plan (brand-mandated renovation)
- **Reflag / Conversion** — Changing brand affiliation
- **Newbuild** — Ground-up construction
- **Pre-opening** — Period before hotel opening
- **QSR / FCR / Casual / Fine dining** — Restaurant segments
- **Same-store sales / Comp sales** — YoY sales at units open 13+ months
- **Ticket average** — Average check per guest
- **Table turns** — Number of seatings per table per meal period
- **Throughput** — Cars per hour (QSR drive-thru)
- **Food cost %** — Food cost as % of food sales (target 28-32%)
- **Labor cost %** — Labor cost as % of sales (target 25-35%)
- **Prime cost** — Food cost + Labor cost (target 55-65%)
- **Franchisee / Franchisor** — Operator / Brand owner
- **Multi-unit franchisee** — Operator with 2+ units
- **TMC** — Travel Management Company (GBT, CWT, BCD, Navan)
- **Bleisure** — Business + leisure travel
- **Bleisure traveler** — Combining business trip with leisure extension
- **GDS code** — Booking code in Global Distribution System
- **Corporate negotiated rate** — Discounted rate for corporate accounts
- **Consortium rate** — Rate for travel agency consortium (Virtuoso, Signature)
- **Package rate** — Bundled rate (room + F&B / spa / activities)
- **Wholesaler rate** — Net rate to wholesalers (Hotelbeds, WebBeds)
- **Pre-paid vs. Post-paid** — Payment timing
- **Front desk / Back office** — Operational vs. administrative areas
- **RevMgr / DOSM** — Revenue Manager / Director of Sales & Marketing
- **GM / AGM** — General Manager / Assistant General Manager
- **RVP / SVP** — Regional / Senior Vice President

## 11. LeadReach Hospitality & Travel Playbook Summary

1. **Sub-segment first**: Luxury / Midscale / Economy / STR / QSR / Casual / Fine Dining / OTA / Corporate Travel / Cruise / Casino / Travel Tech — each has different buyers, cycles, budgets.
2. **Map the buying center**: Brand (CIO/CMO/COO) for brand-wide; Franchisee (Owner/RVP) for multi-property; GM for single-property; Restaurant Owner-Operator for single-unit. Understand franchise vs. corporate decision rights.
3. **Detect the trigger**: RevPAR inflection, ADR change, brand conversion, renovation, new opening, GM change, brand-mandated tech, post-COVID recovery, OTA commission battles, loyalty refresh, M&A, restaurant menu refresh, IPO filing.
4. **Validate budget via revenue band**: Single hotel $50K-$500K IT/yr; top-50 franchisee $5M-$50M; brand $50M-$500M. Single restaurant $30K-$200K; multi-unit $1M-$20M; brand $20M-$300M. Disqualify early.
5. **Build the champion**: Director of Revenue, GM, or VP Operations with P&L credibility. For brand deals: VP-level exec with multi-property influence.
6. **Lead with research, not pitch**: reference specific comp set RevPAR, brand conversion announcements, peer franchisee moves.
7. **Plan for unit economics**: Tools that show ROI in 6-12 months win; tools that show ROI in 36+ months die. Speak RevPAR, GOP, food cost %, labor cost %.
8. **Respect seasonality**: Time outreach to industry cycles (off-season for GMs; Q1/Q3 for brand decisions).
9. **Multi-touch sequence** (email + LinkedIn + in-person at ALIS/HITEC/NRA Show/AAHOA) over 3-9 months.
10. **Track by role + brand + comp set, not person**: Hospitality turnover is high; build pipeline around stable entities.
11. **Iterate by sub-segment**: Hotel brand vs. franchisee vs. restaurant brand vs. OTA conversion rates differ by 5-10x.
