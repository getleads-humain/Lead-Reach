# Education & EdTech Industry — Prospecting Guide, Signals, and Buyer Personas

## 1. Industry Overview

Education is a **$7+ trillion global industry** spanning K-12 (primary and secondary), higher education, vocational and trade schools, corporate learning and development (L&D), test preparation, language learning, and professional certification. The EdTech sub-segment — software and digital content sold into education — is roughly $340B+ globally by 2030 (up from $140B in 2023), growing 13-16% annually as digital adoption deepens in classrooms, campuses, and corporate learning functions. The United States alone spends ~$1.4 trillion annually on education across federal, state, local, and private sources, with the Department of Education budget exceeding $250B and K-12 per-pupil spending averaging $14,000-$25,000 depending on state.

The industry is structurally unique in three respects that fundamentally shape prospecting. **First, the buyer is rarely the user**: in K-12, district administrators and school boards buy tools that teachers and students use; in higher ed, Provosts and Deans buy tools that faculty and students use; in corporate L&D, HR leaders buy tools that employees use. **Second, the budget cycle is seasonal and political**: most US K-12 districts budget on a July-June fiscal year with procurement windows concentrated in spring (March-May) and early fall (Sept-Nov), while higher ed runs on academic calendars with fall and spring decision cycles. **Third, the funding sources are pluralistic**: K-12 districts blend local property taxes, state aid, and federal Title funds (I, II, III, IV) under ESSA; higher ed blends tuition, endowment payout, federal grants (NSF, NIH, Department of Ed), and state appropriations; corporate L&D is funded through HR or talent budgets that ebb and flow with the labor market.

The education buyer is **mission-driven, risk-averse, and committee-oriented**. They cite peer districts and case studies far more than analyst reports. They fear student data privacy scandals (FERPA, COPPA, state laws like California's Student Online Personal Information Protection Act). They face skeptical buyers: teachers resist new tools, faculty senates vote on platform adoption, and corporate learners drop out of mandatory training at 30-50% rates. Generic outreach fails; specific, peer-referenced, funding-aware outreach wins.

The post-COVID landscape has accelerated and reshaped the industry. Remote learning forced 1.5B students globally onto digital platforms in 2020-2021, pulling forward 5-7 years of adoption in 18 months. The 2022-2024 hangover brought budget pressure (ESERF/ESSER federal COVID funding expired in September 2024, removing $190B of US K-12 stimulus), enrollment declines (US college enrollment is down 8% since 2018, with demographic cliff approaching), and a wave of EdTech consolidation (Byju's collapse, 2U bankruptcy, Coursera/Udemy stock down 70%+ from peaks). At the same time, generative AI is reshaping content creation, tutoring, and assessment — with both enormous opportunity and significant faculty/board resistance. Prospecting must navigate these cross-currents carefully.

## 2. Education Sub-Segments

Education is not one industry — it is at least seven distinct sub-segments with different buyers, sales cycles, and budget sources. Mis-segmenting is the most common prospecting error: pitching a corporate L&D tool to a K-12 district, or a Title I reading intervention to a corporate L&D leader, fails immediately.

### K-12 (Public School Districts)
Public school districts serving ~50M students in the US across ~13,000 districts, 100,000+ schools, and 3.5M teachers. The largest districts (NYC DOE, LAUSD, Chicago Public Schools, Miami-Dade, Clark County) have IT budgets in the $100M-$500M range, while small rural districts may have <$100K for tech.
- **Buyer profile**: Superintendent, CTO/CIO (district technology director), Director of Curriculum & Instruction, Director of Federal Programs (Title I), Director of Special Education, school principals.
- **Sales cycle**: 90-180 days for individual school purchases; 6-18 months for district-wide adoptions; RFP mandatory for >$50K-$100K (varies by state).
- **Budget source**: District general fund, federal Title funds (I, II, III, IV under ESSA), IDEA special ed, state categorical programs, E-rate (telecom subsidy), PTA/parent groups.
- **Pain points**: Post-ESSER budget cliff (federal COVID funding expired Sept 2024), literacy and math score declines (NAEP scores at 20-year lows), chronic absenteeism (post-COVID rates doubled to 20-30%), teacher burnout and turnover, cyberattacks on schools (60%+ of US districts reported ransomware incidents in 2023), parent transparency demands.

### K-12 (Private, Charter, Parochial)
~35,000 private schools serving 5M+ US students, plus ~8,000 charter schools serving 3.7M students. Charter schools in particular are growth-oriented and tech-forward; many operate under charter management organizations (CMOs) like KIPP, Success Academy, IDEA Public Schools, Uncommon Schools.
- **Buyer profile**: Executive Director / CEO (charter), Head of School (private), Director of Operations, Director of Curriculum.
- **Sales cycle**: 60-120 days; less bureaucratic than traditional public districts.
- **Budget source**: Per-pupil allocation (charter) or tuition + fundraising (private); less reliant on Title funds.
- **Pain points**: Growth and scaling, standardizing across campuses, marketing/recruitment, accountability under state charter authorizer.

### Higher Education
4,000+ US degree-granting institutions: ~1,600 public, ~1,700 private non-profit, ~700 private for-profit. Global higher ed is ~25,000 institutions. Sizes range from 200-student liberal arts colleges to 100,000+ student public research universities (e.g., Texas A&M, UCF, Arizona State).
- **Buyer profile**: Provost / VP Academic Affairs, CIO/CISO, Dean of [School/College], Director of Center for Teaching & Learning, Registrar, VP Enrollment Management, VP Student Affairs, Director of Online Programs.
- **Sales cycle**: 6-18 months typical; enterprise SIS/LMS replacements 18-36 months; faculty governance adds months.
- **Budget source**: Operating budget (tuition + state appropriation + endowment payout), federal research grants (NSF, NIH, DoD, DOE), Title III/V (HSIs, HBCUs, tribal colleges), HEERF-style stimulus.
- **Pain points**: Enrollment cliff (demographic decline starting 2025), tuition discounting pressure, online program manager (OPM) renegotiation (US DoE's 2024 OPM rule unwinding revenue-share), AI policy and academic integrity, faculty workload, financial sustainability (50%+ of small private colleges are at financial risk).

### Vocational / Trade / Workforce Education
Community colleges (1,000+ in US serving 11M students), trade schools, apprenticeship programs, workforce development boards. Also includes coding bootcamps (General Assembly, Springboard, Bloom Institute of Technology), healthcare training (Pima Medical, Concorde), and skilled trades (Lincoln Tech, Universal Technical Institute).
- **Buyer profile**: VP Workforce Development, Dean of Career & Technical Education (CTE), Director of Apprenticeships, Executive Director of Workforce Board.
- **Sales cycle**: 90-180 days; ties to workforce board grant cycles.
- **Budget source**: Perkins V (federal CTE funding), WIOA (Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act), state workforce funds, employer-sponsored training contracts.
- **Pain points**: Employer alignment, job placement metrics (gainful employment regulations), completion rates, stackable credentials, credit for prior learning.

### Corporate Learning & Development (L&D)
Enterprise L&D budgets total ~$370B globally (US ~$165B), averaging $1,000-$1,500 per learner per year. Includes onboarding, leadership development, compliance training, technical upskilling, sales enablement, and DEI training.
- **Buyer profile**: Chief Learning Officer (CLO), VP Talent / Head of L&D, Director of Learning Technologies, Director of Leadership Development, Chief Diversity Officer (for DEI-specific tools).
- **Sales cycle**: 60-180 days for SaaS tools; multi-year for enterprise LMS replacements.
- **Budget source**: HR / Talent budget; sometimes separate L&D budget or IT budget for LMS infrastructure.
- **Pain points**: Engagement (completion rates <30% for voluntary content), ROI measurement (linking training to business outcomes), manager capability gaps, AI skills gap (60% of L&D leaders cite AI upskilling as #1 priority for 2025-2026), hybrid/remote learning design, compliance tracking.

### Test Preparation & Admissions
SAT/ACT prep (Kaplan, Princeton Review, Manhattan Prep), GRE/GMAT/LSAT/MCAT prep, language proficiency (TOEFL, IELTS), college admissions counseling, graduate school admissions. Also tutoring marketplaces (Wyzant, Varsity Tutors, Wonderskills) and AI tutors (Khan Academy's Khanmigo).
- **Buyer profile**: Founder/CEO (consumer-facing), Director of Partnerships (B2B with schools/districts), Director of Marketing.
- **Sales cycle**: 30-90 days for consumer products; 60-180 days for B2B school/district partnerships.
- **Budget source**: Consumer subscription revenue, parent-paid services, school/district contracts, Title I funding (when sold into schools).

### Language Learning
Duolingo ($6B+ market cap), Babbel, Rosetta Stone (IXL Learning), Busuu (Chegg), Memrise, EF Education First, Berlitz, Pearson English. Also corporate language training (GoFluent, Speexx) and bilingual education (K-12 dual-language programs).
- **Buyer profile**: Founder/CEO (consumer), Director of Product, Head of Growth, VP Learning Content; corporate buyers (Director of L&D, Head of Global Mobility).
- **Sales cycle**: 30-60 days for SaaS; consumer self-serve.
- **Budget source**: Subscription revenue, ad revenue (Duolingo), corporate L&D budget.

### Professional Certification & Continuing Education
Professional associations and certifying bodies (PMI, ISACA, (ISC)², SHRM, HRCI, AICPA, AMA, ABA), continuing medical education (CME), continuing legal education (CLE), continuing professional education (CPE), and corporate credential programs (Coursera, Udemy Business, LinkedIn Learning, Pluralsight, O'Reilly).
- **Buyer profile**: CEO/Executive Director (association), Director of Credentialing, Director of Education/Content; corporate buyers (Director of L&D, Director of Sales Enablement).
- **Sales cycle**: 90-180 days for association partnerships; 30-90 days for corporate.
- **Budget source**: Association membership dues, certification exam fees, corporate L&D budget.

## 3. Firmographic Signals by Education Segment

### Small / Rural K-12 District (1-5K students, <$20M budget)
- **Signals**: Single IT director or shared BOCES/county service; 1-2 school buildings; Google Workspace dominant; free/freemium tool adoption.
- **Buying behavior**: Buys through cooperative contracts ( Equalis Group, AEPA, OMNIA Partners); 60-90 day cycles.
- **Budget availability**: $50K-500K annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Cooperative contract listing, peer rural district references, free trial with simple onboarding.

### Mid-Market K-12 District (5K-50K students, $20M-$300M budget)
- **Signals**: Dedicated IT department, CTO/CIO, formal RFP process, multiple Title directors, large special ed population. Examples: Wake County (NC), Gwinnett County (GA), Fairfax County (VA), Montgomery County (MD).
- **Buying behavior**: RFP for >$50K-$100K, 6-12 month cycles, board approval required for major purchases.
- **Budget availability**: $1M-$20M annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Title funding alignment, peer district references (similar size), ESSA evidence tier, student data privacy (Student Data Privacy Consortium / DPA signing).

### Large Urban K-12 District (50K+ students, $300M+ budget)
- **Signals**: CIO with IT staff of 50-300, formal procurement office, multi-year tech plans, equity-focused board. Examples: NYC DOE, LAUSD, Chicago, Miami-Dade, Houston, Clark County, Hillsborough, Broward.
- **Buying behavior**: 12-24 month cycles, formal RFP/RFI, vendor management office, IT security review, equity audit.
- **Budget availability**: $20M-$200M+ annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Equity outcomes with disaggregated data, peer large-urban district case studies (Council of Great City Schools members), MWBE certification, multi-year contract flexibility.

### Small Liberal Arts College / Private College (1K-5K students)
- **Signals**: Provost-led academic tech decisions, small IT staff, high-touch faculty culture, financial pressure. Examples: Pomona, Williams, Amherst, Bowdoin, Wellesley, Middlebury.
- **Buying behavior**: 6-12 month cycles, faculty governance approval required.
- **Budget availability**: $1M-$10M annual tech budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Faculty adoption support, peer college references, integrated learning experiences.

### Public Research University (15K-60K+ students)
- **Signals**: R1/R2 Carnegie classification, large research portfolio, decentralized IT (central IT + college IT), complex governance. Examples: Ohio State, Michigan, Berkeley, UCLA, Texas A&M, UCF, Arizona State, Wisconsin.
- **Buying behavior**: 12-36 month cycles for enterprise systems, RFP mandatory, IT security + accessibility review, faculty senate consultation.
- **Budget availability**: $20M-$200M+ IT budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference peer AAU institutions, integration with Banner/Workday/Canvas, accessibility (VPAT/WCAG 2.1 AA compliance), research computing alignment.

### Regional Public University / Community College (5K-30K students)
- **Signals**: Access mission, workforce alignment, transfer pathways, financial aid dependence, growing online programs. Examples: Cal States, SUNYs, CUNYs, Florida State System, community colleges (Miami Dade, Valencia, Lone Star, Ivy Tech, Maricopa).
- **Buying behavior**: 6-18 month cycles, RFP, strong interest in cost optimization and student success outcomes.
- **Budget availability**: $5M-$50M IT budget.
- **Outreach angle**: Student success metrics (retention, completion, transfer), Title III/V funding for HSIs/MSIs, workforce alignment, peer institution references.

### Corporate L&D (Enterprise: 5,000+ employees)
- **Signals**: CLO/Head of L&D on LinkedIn, learning tech stack (Cornerstone, Docebo, SAP SuccessFactors, Workday Learning, Degreed, EdCast), talent development function, succession planning.
- **Buying behavior**: 60-180 days for SaaS, RFP for >$250K, 12-24 months for LMS replacement.
- **Budget availability**: $500K-$10M+ annual L&D budget (varies widely by industry).
- **Outreach angle**: Skills taxonomy, manager effectiveness, AI upskilling (top 2025-2026 priority), peer company case studies, measurable behavior change.

### EdTech Startup (Series A-C, $1M-$30M revenue)
- **Signals**: VC-backed, founder-led, growing ARR, hiring GTM team, often on Salesforce/HubSpot + Snowflake + modern stack.
- **Buying behavior**: 30-90 day cycles, founder/CEO involved, less formal procurement.
- **Budget availability**: $100K-$2M annual tooling budget.

## 5. Buyer Personas

### The Superintendent
- **At**: K-12 public school districts; appointed by elected school board.
- **Cares about**: Student achievement (state test scores, NAEP, graduation rates), equity (closing achievement gaps), community relations, board politics, bond elections, safety, teacher retention.
- **Pain**: ESSER fiscal cliff (post-2024), state accountability ratings, chronic absenteeism, parent advocacy groups, social media controversies, school board polarization.
- **Outreach angle**: References to peer districts with similar demographics, ESSA evidence tiers (Tier 1 = strong evidence), state-specific accountability alignment, board presentation support.
- **Channels**: AASA (School Superintendents Association) National Conference, District Administration magazine, LinkedIn, peer superintendent networks.
- **What NOT to say**: Generic "improve student outcomes" — be specific (e.g., "reduced chronic absenteeism 12pp at [Peer District]").

### The District CIO / CTO
- **At**: K-12 districts with 5K+ students; larger districts have dedicated CIO.
- **Cares about**: Cybersecurity (60%+ districts hit by ransomware), network infrastructure (E-rate funded), interoperability (Ed-Fi, OneRoster), data privacy (FERPA, state laws), budget efficiency, teacher ease of use.
- **Pain**: Tool sprawl (avg district uses 1,000-2,000 digital tools), SSO rostering burden, vendor security review backlog, aging infrastructure, procurement complexity.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference Student Data Privacy Consortium (SDPC) DPA, Ed-Fi certification, E-rate category eligibility, peer CIO references (CoSN), cybersecurity case studies.
- **Channels**: CoSN Annual Conference, ISTE, LinkedIn, state CIO networks.
- **What NOT to say**: "AI-powered learning" without addressing privacy and security.

### The Director of Curriculum & Instruction
- **At**: K-12 districts, sometimes also at large charter networks.
- **Cares about**: Standards alignment (Common Core, state standards, NGSS), curriculum adoption cycles (typically 6-7 years per subject), ESSA evidence tiers, teacher buy-in, professional development.
- **Pain**: Curriculum adoption politicization (book bans, CRT controversies), teacher workload, intervention for struggling students, professional learning time scarcity.
- **Outreach angle**: ESSA evidence tier alignment, peer district adoption case studies, teacher PD support.
- **Channels**: ASCD Annual Conference, Learning Forward, state curriculum associations.

### The Director of Federal Programs / Title I
- **At**: K-12 districts receiving federal Title funds (~95% of US districts).
- **Cares about**: Title I, II, III, IV compliance; ESSA plan alignment; parent involvement requirements; equity allocations; supplement-not-supplant rules.
- **Pain**: ESSER fiscal cliff (Sept 2024 deadline), monitoring visits from state departments, documentation burden, schoolwide vs. targeted assistance program design.
- **Outreach angle**: Specific Title funding eligibility (e.g., "Title I, Part A schoolwide eligible"), ESSA evidence tier, supplement-not-supplant compliance, peer district references.
- **Channels**: National Association of Federal Education Program Administrators (NAFEPA), state Title I conferences.

### The Director of Special Education
- **At**: K-12 districts (every district has one; required under IDEA).
- **Cares about**: IEP compliance, due process, transition planning,assistive technology, Medicaid reimbursement for school-based services, staff caseloads.
- **Pain**: Special ed teacher shortage, IEP paperwork (estimated 20+ hours/week per teacher), Medicaid billing complexity, parent advocacy, post-Endrew F. rigor requirements.
- **Outreach angle**: IEP workflow automation, Medicaid billing accuracy, peer district references, Endrew F. compliance.

### The Provost / VP Academic Affairs (Higher Ed)
- **At**: All higher ed institutions.
- **Cares about**: Academic program viability, faculty retention and recruitment, accreditation (regional accreditors, programmatic accreditors), enrollment management partnership, online program strategy, AI policy.
- **Pain**: Faculty governance friction, enrollment cliff pressure, program budget cuts, faculty AI anxiety, online program manager (OPM) renegotiation under 2024 DoE rule change.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference peer AAU/AASCU/APLU institution outcomes, OPM-to-in-house transition case studies, faculty adoption playbook.
- **Channels**: ACE (American Council on Education), AAC&U, state higher ed executive associations.

### The Dean of [School/College]
- **At**: Higher ed institutions with multiple schools (e.g., Dean of Business, Engineering, Nursing).
- **Cares about**: Program ranking, alumni outcomes, employer partnerships, research funding (especially in professional schools), faculty hiring, building campaigns.
- **Pain**: Accreditation (AACSB for business, ABET for engineering, CCNE for nursing), declining enrollment in some disciplines, online competition, employer alignment.
- **Outreach angle**: Specific accreditor alignment, peer school rankings reference, employer-credible credentials.

### The VP Enrollment Management (Higher Ed)
- **At**: All higher ed institutions; critical in enrollment-challenged regions.
- **Cares about**: Yield rate, financial aid leveraging (need vs. merit), net tuition revenue, demographic trends, international enrollment, transfer pathways, FAFSA completion rates.
- **Pain**: FAFSA simplification chaos (2024-25 cycle debacle), demographic cliff (2025+), tuition discount rate escalation, international student policy uncertainty.
- **Outreach angle**: FAFSA completion tooling, predictive yield modeling, peer institution references, demographic-aware outreach.

### The Higher Ed CIO / CISO
- **At**: Higher ed institutions of all sizes; R1s have CISO reporting structure.
- **Cares about**: Cybersecurity (high education is #1 targeted sector by ransomware), research computing (HPC, GPU clusters for AI research), identity (Shibboleth/InCommon), accessibility compliance, IT cost recovery, cloud strategy.
- **Pain**: Legacy ERP migrations (Banner/Workday/PeopleSoft), accessibility OCR complaints, ransomware, research cyber compliance (NIST 800-171 for DoD research, CMMC), faculty demands for AI infrastructure.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures in higher ed, NIST CSF maturity, peer CISO references (HECVAT, REN-ISAC), shared services.

### The Chief Learning Officer / VP Talent (Corporate L&D)
- **At**: F500 and large enterprise; 5,000+ employees typically.
- **Cares about**: Skills gap closure (especially AI/digital), leadership pipeline, employee engagement, retention, manager capability, DEI outcomes, learning ROI / business impact.
- **Pain**: Engagement (voluntary learning completion <30%), manager skills gap, AI upskilling mandate (CEO/CFO pressure), ROI measurement, content sprawl across LXP/LMS.
- **Outreach angle**: Reference peer company CLO priorities, AI upskilling case studies, measurable behavior change (not vanity metrics).
- **Channels**: ATD International Conference & Expo, LinkedIn Learning blog, Chief Learning Officer magazine, Training Industry Conference.
- **What NOT to say**: "1,000+ courses" — they have too much content already. Talk curation, skills graphs, and outcome measurement.

### The Head of Talent Development / Director of L&D
- **At**: Mid-market and enterprise.
- **Cares about**: Onboarding speed, manager development, compliance training completion, leadership bench, sales enablement.
- **Pain**: Limited budget, vendor consolidation pressure, manager resistance, hybrid/remote learning design.
- **Outreach angle**: Peer references, manager development case studies, vendor consolidation support.

## 7. Education Industry Vocabulary

Use the prospect's language. Education has rich jargon that signals insider status. Misuse any of these and you lose credibility instantly.

- **ESSA** (Every Student Succeeds Act) — Federal K-12 law (2015 replacement for NCLB); sets Title I-V rules.
- **Title I, II, III, IV** — Federal K-12 funding streams: I (low-income), II (teacher quality), III (English learners), IV (student support / well-rounded).
- **IDEA** (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act) — Federal special ed law; mandates FAPE in LRE.
- **IEP / 504 Plan** — Individualized Education Program (special ed) / 504 Plan (disability accommodation).
- **MTSS** (Multi-Tiered System of Supports) — Tiered intervention framework (academic + behavioral).
- **RTI** (Response to Intervention) — Tiered academic intervention; subset of MTSS.
- **PBIS** (Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports) — Tiered behavioral framework.
- **ESSA Evidence Tiers** — I (strong), II (moderate), III (promising), IV (demonstrates rationale).
- **FAPE** (Free Appropriate Public Education) — IDEA mandate.
- **LRE** (Least Restrictive Environment) — IDEA mandate.
- **Endrew F.** — Supreme Court case (2017) raising IEP substantive rigor standard.
- **FERPA** (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act) — Federal student records privacy law.
- **COPPA** (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act) — FTC rule for under-13 data collection.
- **CIPA** (Children's Internet Protection Act) — E-rate eligibility requirement for filtering.

## 8. Common Education Prospecting Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Wrong Procurement Window
Pitching "new curriculum adoption" in March to a district that adopted in June; pitching "summer melt intervention" in October. Education is calendar-driven; missing the window means waiting a full year. Always map outreach to the relevant academic/fiscal calendar.

### Mistake 2: Wrong Stakeholder
Emailing the Superintendent about a teacher PD tool — Superintendents delegate to Director of Curriculum. Emailing the Dean about an LMS — Deans defer to CIO + Provost. Match the tool to the right functional owner.

### Mistake 3: Ignoring Funding Source
A "great for Title I" tool pitched without ESSA evidence tier alignment fails immediately. A "great for special ed" tool pitched without IDEA citation fails. Always align outreach to the specific funding stream and its requirements.

### Mistake 4: Generic "Engagement" Claims
"Increases student engagement" is meaningless. "Reduced chronic absenteeism 12pp at [Peer District] over 18 months using early warning data + parent notification" is meaningful. Always tie to a quantified outcome and named peer.

### Mistake 5: Ignoring FERPA / COPPA / State Privacy Laws
Pitching an AI tool that "trains on student data" without addressing FERPA, COPPA, and state laws (e.g., California SOPIPA, Colorado, Connecticut) is dead on arrival. Always address privacy upfront with the SDPC DPA signing.

### Mistake 6: Underestimating Committee Buying
Education is committee buying: teacher council, parent council, school board, IT security, accessibility, equity review. Sales cycles of 6-18 months are normal. Single-stakeholder pitches fail.

### Mistake 7: Pitching "Disruption" to Risk-Averse Buyers
Education buyers fear disruption; they seek stability, evidence, peer references, and gradual adoption. "Disruptive" framing repels them; "evidence-based, peer-adopted, gradual rollout" framing wins.

### Mistake 8: Forgetting the Teacher / Faculty Voice
Tools that add teacher workload without clear payoff get abandoned within weeks. Always cite teacher-experience improvements alongside administrator ROI.

### Mistake 9: Treating Corporate L&D Like K-12
Corporate L&D buyers care about business outcomes (retention, productivity, sales quota attainment); K-12 buyers care about student outcomes (test scores, graduation, equity). Don't pitch corporate tools with K-12 language.

### Mistake 10: Missing Seasonal Buying Windows
Most US K-12 districts budget for the following year in spring (March-May RFPs, summer board approval, fall deployment). Most corporate L&D budgets refresh in Q4 for the following calendar year. Pitching in the wrong season wastes cycles.

## 11. Qualification Criteria (BANT / MEDDIC for Education)

### Budget
- **Large K-12 districts** ($300M+ budget): $20M+ IT/edtech budget; $50K-$2M deal sizes possible.
- **Mid-market K-12** ($20-300M budget): $1M-$20M tech budget; $20K-$500K deal sizes.
- **Small K-12** (<$20M budget): $50K-$2M tech budget; <$100K deal sizes, often through cooperatives.
- **R1 universities**: $20M-$200M+ IT budget.
- **Mid-size universities**: $5M-$50M IT budget.
- **Small colleges**: $1M-$10M IT budget; under severe financial pressure.
- **Corporate L&D (F500)**: $5M-$50M+ L&D budget; deal sizes $50K-$5M.
- **Corporate L&D (mid-market)**: $500K-$5M L&D budget.

### Authority
- **Decisions under $25K**: Principal / Director-level.
- **$25K-$250K**: Director / Dean / Assistant Superintendent, with Procurement review.
- **$250K-$1M**: Superintendent / Provost / VP-level, with cabinet review.
- **$1M+**: Board / Board of Trustees / Board of Regents approval; multi-year contract.

### Need
- **Quantified pain**: State accountability rating below target; chronic absenteeism above 15%; NAEP score decline; enrollment decline; FAFSA completion below 50%; LMS contract expiring in 12 months; AI policy gap; accessibility OCR complaint.
- **Trigger**: New leadership, ESSER sunset, OPM renegotiation, cyberattack, accreditation visit, FAFSA cycle, state takeover.

### Timing
- **Best K-12**: Spring (Mar-May) RFP window for following year; fall (Sept-Nov) budget cycle; summer (June-Aug) for late-cycle approvals.
- **Best Higher Ed**: Fall (Sept-Nov) budget cycle for following FY; spring (Mar-May) for fall deployment.
- **Best Corporate L&D**: Q4 (Oct-Dec) for following CY budget; Q1 (Jan-Mar) for new budget deployment.
- **Worst**: June-Aug (vacation season); Dec (holidays); mid-RFP cycle; pre-accreditation visit (6 months).

### MEDDIC specific to education
- **Metrics**: State test scores, NAEP, graduation rate, chronic absenteeism, FAFSA completion, enrollment yield, retention rate, completion rate, learning completion %, manager capability scores.
- **Economic Buyer**: Superintendent / Provost / CLO; Board approval for major deals.
- **Decision Criteria**: ESSA evidence tier (K-12), accreditation alignment (higher ed), peer references, data privacy (SDPC DPA), accessibility (VPAT), integration (Ed-Fi, OneRoster), price (often ≤1% of per-pupil spending), equity.
- **Decision Process**: Committee (teachers, parents, IT, equity) → Procurement → RFP → Board approval → contract → pilot → rollout.
- **Identify Pain**: Tie to public accountability data (state report card, IPEDS, OCR resolution, ESSA report).
- **Champion**: Director-level (Curriculum, Federal Programs, Online Programs, Talent Development); rarely the Superintendent/Provost/CLO.

## 12. Common Pitfalls Specific to Education

### Long Sales Cycles
Education sales cycles are 6-18 months for district/university-wide adoptions, 12-36 months for enterprise system replacements. Plan pipeline accordingly; do not expect 30-day closes outside of corporate L&D SMB.

### Committee Buying
Decisions involve teacher councils, parent councils, school boards, IT security, accessibility, and equity reviewers. Single-stakeholder pitches fail; always map the full committee and provide materials for each.

### Skeptical Buyers
Educators are skeptical of vendor claims after decades of failed reform. Reference peer institutions and quantified outcomes; cite independent research (WestEd, SRI, J-PAL, What Works Clearinghouse) where possible.

### ESSER Fiscal Cliff
ESSER funds ($190B US K-12) had a September 2024 obligation deadline. Many districts now face budget cuts of 5-15%. Pitch cost optimization, sustainable interventions, and alternative funding alignment.

### FERPA / COPPA / State Privacy Laws
Pitching AI tools without addressing FERPA (K-12), COPPA (under-13), and state privacy laws (CA SOPIPA, CO, CT, NY Ed Law 2-D, IL BIPA) is dead on arrival. Always have SDPC DPA, data processing agreement, and clear retention/deletion policies.

### Accessibility Compliance (WCAG 2.1 AA / Section 508)
OCR resolution agreements and ADA lawsuits have surged (e.g., lawsuits against universities and edtech vendors). Always provide VPAT, accessibility roadmap, and references to peer institutions' accessibility audits.

### Political Polarization
K-12 districts face politicized school boards, book ban controversies, CRT debates, and parent advocacy groups. Avoid politically charged framing; emphasize evidence, equity, local control, and parent transparency.

### Faculty Governance (Higher Ed)
Faculty senates can vote down tool adoptions; shared governance is real. Always build faculty champion pathways, cite peer institutions, and provide faculty professional development support.

### Demographic Cliff (Higher Ed)
US high school graduate count peaks ~2025 then declines through 2040. Colleges in Northeast, Midwest face existential enrollment pressure. Position tools as enrollment/retention/completion solutions, not "growth."

### OPM Rule Change (2024)
July 2024 Department of Education rule unwinds the bundled services exception for OPMs, requiring universities to in-house or unbundle online program operations. Massive renegotiation opportunity 2024-2026.

### AI Anxiety and Resistance
Faculty and teachers fear AI (cheating, job displacement). Tools must address academic integrity, faculty empowerment, and student-centered use cases. "AI replaces teachers" framing kills deals; "AI augments teachers" framing wins.

### Vendor Lock-In Concerns
Education buyers fear lock-in (especially after LMS/SIS migrations that took 3+ years). Always address data portability, Ed-Fi/OneRoster compliance, and exit clauses.

## 13. LeadReach Education Playbook Summary

1. **Identify sub-segment** (K-12 public, K-12 private/charter, higher ed, vocational/workforce, corporate L&D, test prep, language, professional cert) — determines buyer and budget source.
2. **Determine institution size and type** (district size, university Carnegie class, corporate L&D scale) — determines budget and cycle length.
3. **Detect tech stack** (LMS, SIS, ERP, LXP, identity, accessibility, analytics) — determines integration story and adjacency opportunities.
4. **Identify correct buyer persona** — match the tool to the functional owner (Superintendent, CIO, Director of Curriculum, Director of Federal Programs, Provost, Dean, VP Enrollment, CIO/CISO, CLO, Director of L&D).
5. **Scan for trigger events** — ESSER sunset, FAFSA cycle, new leadership, M&A, cyberattacks, accreditation visits, OPM renegotiation, state takeovers, AI task force formation.
6. **Compose outreach** referencing specific trigger + specific persona pain + quantified peer outcome + funding source alignment.
7. **Multi-touch sequence** (email + LinkedIn + direct mail + in-person at conference) over 4-8 weeks; education buyers respond well to peer references and conference connections.
8. **Track funnel metrics** — delivery, open, reply, meeting-booked rates; adjust by sub-segment.
9. **Iterate** based on which sub-segments, trigger types, and funding streams convert best in your ICP.
10. **Avoid the cardinal sins** — don't ignore seasonal windows, don't pitch wrong stakeholders, don't skip FERPA/accessibility, don't use generic "engagement" claims, don't underestimate committee buying, don't treat corporate L&D like K-12.
