# Healthcare & Life Sciences — Prospecting Guide

## 1. Industry Overview

Healthcare and life sciences is a **$4 trillion global industry** with strict regulation (HIPAA in US, GDPR health data in EU, PMDA in Japan, NMPA in China). The industry is dominated by a few large buyers (hospitals, pharma) and a fragmented supplier base (medical device makers, biotech, digital health).

Key characteristics:
- **Heavily regulated** — HIPAA, HITECH, FDA, EMA, plus state-level rules and international equivalents
- **Long clinical cycles** — Drug development 7-10 years; device approval 1-3 years; clinical evidence is currency
- **Privacy-first** — Personal health information (PHI) requires special handling; HIPAA violation penalties up to $2.1M per incident category
- **Multi-stakeholder** — Physician, hospital admin, payer, regulator, patient — each with different incentives
- **Slow tech adoption** — Hospitals especially; legacy EHR systems dominate; change management is massive
- **Outcome-driven** — Clinical evidence matters more than marketing; ROI must be clinically validated
- **Interoperability challenges** — FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources) adoption growing but far from universal

### 2.1 Hospitals & Health Systems

**Market Shape**
- US hospital market: ~$1.3T; ~6,000 hospitals; 900,000+ staffed beds
- Market structure: 60% non-profit, 20% for-profit, 20% government; consolidation accelerating (50+ mergers/year)
- Top systems: HCA Healthcare (185 hospitals), CommonSpirit Health (142), Ascension (139), Kaiser Permanente (40)
- Rural hospital crisis: 150+ rural hospitals closed since 2010; 600+ at risk of closure
- Ambulatory shift: 60%+ of hospital revenue now from outpatient services; inpatient declining

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Patient services (85%), government payments (Medicare 40%, Medicaid 18%), commercial insurance (35%), other (7%)
- Margins: Operating margin 2-4% (non-profit); for-profit 5-8%; negative margins for 50%+ of rural hospitals
- Revenue cycle: Charge capture → coding → billing → collections; denial rate 10-15%; average days in A/R 45-55
- Key metric: Occupancy rate, average length of stay (ALOS), case mix index (CMI), revenue per adjusted discharge
- Value-based care: Shifting from fee-for-service to value-based; 30%+ of Medicare payments now in VBC models

**Tech Stack**
- EHR: Epic (dominant in large systems, 39% market share), Cerner/Oracle Health (23%), Athenahealth (ambulatory), Meditech, Allscripts
- Revenue cycle: Epic Resolute, Cerner RevElate, Waystar, Experian Health, AKASA
- CRM: Salesforce Health Cloud, Epic MyChart (patient portal), Press Ganey (patient experience)
- Imaging: PACS (Fujifilm, GE Healthcare, Philips), DICOM standard, AI radiology tools
- Clinical decision support: UpToDate (Wolters Kluwer), Micromedex (Merative), IBM Watson Health (divested)
- Telehealth: Teladoc, Amwell, Doxy.me, Epic Telehealth module
- Population health: Epic Healthy Planet, Cerner HealtheIntent, Arcadia, HealthEC
- Workforce: UKG (Kronos), HealthStream, API Healthcare

**Decision-Making**
- CMIO/CNIO: Clinical technology; EHR optimization; clinical decision support; physician adoption
- CIO: IT strategy; infrastructure; security; vendor management
- CFO: Revenue cycle technology; cost optimization; ROI requirements
- CMO/Chief Clinical Officer: Clinical quality technology; patient safety; clinical workflows
- VP Supply Chain: Medical device and pharmaceutical procurement; GPO relationships
- Board: Technology investment approval for >$1M projects; risk oversight
- Physician champions: Department chairs and clinical informaticists influence adoption; without them, implementations fail

**Budget Cycles**
- Annual budget: July-September planning for following fiscal year (many health systems have July-June fiscal year)
- Capital budget: Separate from operating; CapEx for EHR, imaging, construction; 5-year depreciation
- Operating budget: SaaS, staffing, supplies; annual renewal cycle
- Meaningful Use/CMS incentives: Technology adoption tied to CMS quality programs; incentive-driven investment
- Year-end spending: Q4 (April-June for July fiscal year) budget utilization; good time for proposals

**Regulatory**
- HIPAA: Privacy Rule, Security Rule, Breach Notification Rule; penalties $100-$50K per violation, up to $2.1M per category per year
- HITECH: Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health Act; EHR adoption incentives; strengthened HIPAA enforcement
- CMS Conditions of Participation: Minimum requirements for Medicare participation; impact technology requirements
- Joint Commission: Accreditation standards; technology impacts on patient safety; survey preparation
- 21st Century Cures Act: Information blocking rules; interoperability requirements; patient data access
- ONC Health IT Certification: EHR certification requirements; API standards (FHIR)
- State licensing: Certificate of Need (CON) in some states; facility licensing; telehealth licensing
- OIG/anti-kickback: Stark Law, Anti-Kickback Statute; vendor relationships must be structured carefully
- No Surprises Act: Surprise billing protections; good-faith estimate requirements; impact on revenue cycle

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- EHR dissatisfaction: 70%+ of physicians report burnout; EHR cited as #1 contributor; 16 minutes of EHR work per patient encounter
- Revenue leakage: Denial rate 10-15% represents $5-10M per hospital; under-coding estimated at 3-5% of revenue
- Staffing shortage: Nursing vacancy rate 10-15%; traveling nurse costs 3-5x staff nurse; $24B+ annual spending on travel nurses
- Cybersecurity: Healthcare breaches cost $10.9M per incident (highest of any industry); 725+ healthcare breaches in 2024
- Interoperability: Only 30% of hospitals can electronically exchange clinical data seamlessly; fax remains dominant communication method
- Patient experience: HCAHPS scores impact Medicare reimbursement; low scores = financial penalty
- Cost pressure: Operating expenses rising 5-7% per year; reimbursement flat or declining; margin compression

**Buying Triggers**
- EHR migration/modification: 12-24 month project; adjacent tools follow
- Hospital M&A: System consolidation; EHR standardization; vendor rationalization
- New CMIO/CIO hire: Modernization agenda; 90-120 day evaluation period
- CMS rule change: Quality program updates; technology compliance required
- Joint Commission survey: Preparation drives technology investment
- Cybersecurity incident (theirs or peer): Security investment; incident response capability
- Nursing shortage crisis: Workforce technology; scheduling optimization; virtual nursing
- Revenue cycle performance decline: Denial management; coding automation; RCM technology
- Value-based care contract: Population health technology; analytics; care coordination tools

**Channels**
- Industry events: HIMSS Global Health Conference, CHIME Fall Forum, Becker's Healthcare, AHIMA
- Email: Primary cold channel for CIO/CMIO; peer references mandatory; response rate 1-3%
- Advisory firms: Gartner Healthcare, KLAS Research, Advisory Board — significant influence on hospital buying
- Trade media: Becker's Hospital Review, Modern Healthcare, Fierce Healthcare, Healthcare IT News
- LinkedIn: CIO, CMIO, VP Clinical Informatics — thought leadership content performs well
- CHIME: College of Healthcare Information Management Executives; CIO network; highly influential
- GPO introductions: Group Purchasing Organizations (Vizient, Premier, HealthTrust) recommend technology vendors

**Vocabulary**
- EHR/EMR, PHR (Personal Health Record), HIE (Health Information Exchange), HIX (Health Insurance Exchange)
- Inpatient, outpatient, ambulatory, ED (Emergency Department), OR (Operating Room), ICU, med-surg
- ALOS (Average Length of Stay), CMI (Case Mix Index), occupancy rate, throughput, boarding
- Revenue cycle, charge capture, coding (ICD-10, CPT, HCPCS), billing, denial rate, A/R days
- Fee-for-service, value-based care, capitation, bundled payment, shared savings, quality measures
- HCAHPS, patient experience, Press Ganey, NPS, patient satisfaction, engagement
- FHIR, HL7, DICOM, CCD (Continuity of Care Document), ADT (Admit-Discharge-Transfer)
- Clinical decision support (CDS), order sets, alerts, best practice advisories (BPAs)
- Interoperability, information blocking, ONC, TEHCA (Trusted Exchange Framework)
- CMIO, CNIO, CIO, CMO, CNO (Chief Nursing Officer), CISO, CFO

### 2.2 Pharma & Biotech

**Market Shape**
- Global pharma market: ~$1.6T (2025); US ~$600B (largest single market); biologics growing 12% per year
- Market structure: Big pharma (Pfizer, J&J, Roche, Novartis, Merck); mid-cap biotech ($5-50B market cap); small biotech (thousands)
- R&D productivity: Cost to develop one new drug: $2.6B (Tufts CSDD); 90% failure rate from Phase 1 to approval
- Pipeline: ~20,000 active clinical trials globally; ~6,000 in Phase 3; oncology dominates (30% of all trials)
- Generics/biosimilars: $200B+ patent cliff 2025-2030; biosimilar market growing 25% per year

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Drug sales (primary), licensing revenue, milestone payments (biotech), government contracts
- Margins: Gross margin 75-85% (branded pharma); net margin 15-25% (big pharma); biotech often pre-revenue for 10+ years
- Blockbuster model: Top 10 drugs generate $100B+ revenue; Keytruda ($25B), Ozempic/Wegovy ($18B+)
- Pipeline risk: Revenue highly concentrated; single drug can represent 20-30% of company revenue; patent cliff = revenue cliff
- Key metric: Pipeline value, probability of success (PoS), time-to-peak-sales, patent life remaining

**Tech Stack**
- Clinical: Veeva Vault (clinical, regulatory, quality), Medidata Rave (EDC), Oracle InForm, Bio-Optronics CTMS
- R&D: Schrödinger (drug design), Recursion (AI drug discovery), Insilico Medicine, Benchling (lab informatics)
- Safety/pharmacovigilance: ArisGlobal, Oracle Argus, Veeva Vault Safety
- CRM: Veeva CRM (dominant — 80%+ of pharma), Salesforce Health Cloud
- Data/analytics: Snowflake, Databricks, Palantir Foundry, Saama (clinical analytics)
- Regulatory: Veeva Vault RIM, IQVIA, MasterControl
- Manufacturing: Siemens Opcenter, Werum PAS-X, AVEVA
- Commercial: IQVIA (data), DRG (Clarivate), Definitive Healthcare (prescriber data)

**Decision-Making**
- VP R&D / Head of Drug Discovery: R&D technology; drug discovery platforms; lab informatics
- Head of Clinical Operations: Clinical trial technology; site management; patient recruitment
- Chief Medical Officer: Clinical strategy; safety systems; medical affairs technology
- VP Regulatory Affairs: Regulatory technology; submission management; compliance
- VP Commercial: Sales force technology; marketing analytics; market access
- CIO/CTO: Enterprise IT strategy; cloud migration; data platform
- Procurement: Vendor management; contract negotiation; risk assessment

**Budget Cycles**
- Annual budget: Q4 planning for following year; R&D budget typically 15-25% of revenue (big pharma)
- Clinical trial budgets: Per-trial budgeting; Phase 3 trial cost $50-300M; technology is 3-5% of trial cost
- Biotech runway: Budget tied to funding runway; post-funding deployment over 12-24 months
- Manufacturing CapEx: Facility investment 2-5 year planning; technology tied to facility construction
- Regulatory milestone payments: Technology investment triggered by regulatory milestones (IND, NDA filing)

**Regulatory**
- FDA (US): IND, NDA, BLA, ANDA submissions; 21 CFR Part 11 (electronic records); REMS requirements
- EMA (EU): Centralized/decentralized procedures; Clinical Trials Regulation (CTR); ATMP regulation
- ICH: International Council for Harmonisation; GxP guidelines (GCP, GMP, GLP); E6(R2) Good Clinical Practice
- HIPAA: PHI in clinical trials; de-identification requirements; Business Associate Agreements required
- 21 CFR Part 11: Electronic records and signatures; audit trail requirements; system validation
- EU Clinical Trials Regulation (CTR): CTIS (Clinical Trials Information System); single submission; transparency requirements
- Sunshine Act (Open Payments): Reporting payments to physicians; vendor interactions tracked
- Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCSA): Track and trace; serialization; verification systems
- Pediatric Research Equity Act (PREA): Pediatric study requirements; impact on development timeline
- Orphan Drug Act: Incentives for rare disease drugs; 7-year market exclusivity; tax credits

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- Clinical trial delays: Average Phase 3 trial delayed 6-12 months; cost per day $30-60K; patient recruitment is #1 bottleneck
- R&D productivity: $2.6B per approved drug; 90% failure rate; AI/ML seen as key to improving odds
- Drug safety: Pharmacovigilance costs $50-100M per drug per year; manual case processing 40,000+ cases/year for large pharma
- Patent cliff: $200B+ revenue at risk 2025-2030; biosimilar competition eroding branded revenue
- Data fragmentation: Clinical data in 15-20 systems; trial master file scattered; analytics require massive data integration
- Regulatory complexity: 50+ country submissions per drug; format variations; constantly evolving requirements
- Manufacturing: Biologics manufacturing yield 50-70%; batch failures cost $5-50M; supply chain for cell/gene therapies extremely complex
- Commercial access: Payer restrictions growing; prior authorization burden; 30%+ of scripts require PA

**Buying Triggers**
- New drug approval: Commercial launch infrastructure; sales force technology; market access analytics
- Phase 3 trial initiation: Clinical technology; site management; patient recruitment platforms
- FDA warning letter / 483: Remediation technology; quality system investment; CAPA management
- Patent expiry approaching: Pipeline acceleration; lifecycle management; biosimilar defense
- M&A: System consolidation; data migration; integration of clinical platforms
- New VP Clinical Operations hire: Clinical technology evaluation; process modernization
- Clinical trial failure: Pipeline reassessment; technology for better trial design; real-world evidence
- Regulatory return/refuse to file: Submission process improvement; regulatory technology investment
- ESG/clinical trial diversity mandate: Diversity recruitment technology; real-world evidence platforms

**Channels**
- Industry events: BIO International, DIA Global Annual Meeting, SCOPE (clinical operations), Phocuswright
- Email: Primary cold channel for VP+; peer references from pharma are gold; response rate 1-3%
- Advisory firms: Gartner Life Sciences, IDC Health Insights, Accenture Life Sciences
- Trade media: Endpoints News, STAT News, BioPharma Dive, Fierce Pharma, Pink Sheet
- LinkedIn: VP Clinical Operations, VP Regulatory, CIO — content about trial efficiency performs
- Conference networking: BIO one-on-one partnering; DIA sessions; private dinners
- KOL networks: Key Opinion Leaders influence technology adoption (indirectly)

**Vocabulary**
- IND, NDA, BLA, ANDA, 505(b)(2), ODD (Orphan Drug Designation), BTD (Breakthrough Therapy Designation)
- Phase 1/2/3/4, clinical trial, pivotal trial, registrational study, post-marketing study
- MoA (Mechanism of Action), target, biomarker, companion diagnostic, precision medicine
- GMP, GCP, GLP, GVP (Good Pharmacovigilance Practice), GDP (Good Distribution Practice)
- CRO (Contract Research Organization), CDMO (Contract Development and Manufacturing Organization), SMO

- Market access, formulary, prior authorization, step therapy, copay assistance, REMS
- Patent cliff, biosimilar, reference product, interchangeability, exclusivity

### 2.3 Medical Devices

**Market Shape**
- Global medical device market: ~$600B (2025); US ~$200B; growing 5-6% per year
- Market structure: Top 10 (Medtronic, J&J, Abbott, Boston Scientific, Stryker, Becton Dickinson, Edwards, Zimmer, Siemens Healthineers, Philips) = 40% market share
- Segments: Cardiovascular (25%), orthopedic (20%), diagnostic imaging (15%), surgical (15%), diabetes/monitoring (10%), other (15%)
- Software-as-a-Medical-Device (SaMD): Fastest-growing segment; AI/ML-enabled devices 600+ FDA clearances
- EU MDR transition: Massive disruption; many devices being withdrawn from EU market due to compliance costs

**Business Model Anatomy**
- Revenue: Device sales (primary), consumables/implants (recurring 40-60% of revenue), service contracts, software subscriptions
- Margins: Gross margin 65-80%; net margin 15-25%; implant/consumable revenue is high-margin recurring
- Sales model: Direct sales force (large devices); distributor model (smaller devices); hybrid; physician training is key part of sale
- Regulatory pathway: 510(k) (substantial equivalence, 90 days, 85% of devices), PMA (premarket approval, 6-12 months, Class III), De Novo
- Key metric: Revenue per product line, regulatory clearance timeline, post-market complaint rate, recall rate

**Tech Stack**
- PLM: Windchill (PTC), Teamcenter (Siemens), Agile PLM (Oracle)
- QMS: MasterControl, Greenlight Guru, Veeva QualityDocs, Sparta Systems TrackWise
- Design: SolidWorks, AutoCAD, MATLAB, Simulink, COMSOL (simulation)
- Regulatory: Veeva Vault RIM, RIMSmart, IQVIA RIM, MasterControl
- Manufacturing: Siemens Opcenter, AVEVA, Rockwell FactoryTalk
- Post-market: Veeva Vault Safety, IQVIA, MAUDE database analysis
- CRM: Veeva CRM, Salesforce Health Cloud
- AI/ML development: Python, TensorFlow, PyTorch, MONAI (medical imaging AI)

**Decision-Making**
- VP R&D: Design and development technology; simulation; prototyping
- VP Regulatory Affairs: Regulatory submission technology; compliance; clearance strategy
- VP Quality: QMS; CAPA management; audit preparation; supplier quality
- VP Operations/Supply Chain: Manufacturing technology; supply chain visibility; serialization
- CIO/CTO: Enterprise IT; cloud strategy; cybersecurity (especially for connected devices)
- VP Commercial: Sales technology; CRM; marketing; KOL engagement

**Budget Cycles**
- Annual budget: Q4 planning; R&D typically 8-12% of revenue
- Product development cycle: Budget tied to product lifecycle; 2-5 year development; technology investment phases
- Regulatory milestone: Budget allocated per submission; 510(k) ($50-100K), PMA ($1-5M) technology support
- Post-market surveillance: Ongoing budget; complaint handling; vigilance reporting
- Quality improvement: Often reactive to audit findings; CAPA-driven investment

**Regulatory**
- FDA 510(k): Substantial equivalence to predicate device; 90-day review; ~85% of device clearances
- FDA PMA: Premarket approval for Class III devices; clinical data required; 6-12 month review; advisory panel
- FDA De Novo: Novel low-to-moderate risk devices; creates new classification
- EU MDR (Medical Device Regulation): Replaced MDD/IVDD; stricter clinical evidence requirements; Notified Body bottleneck; implementation deadline 2027-2028 (extended)
- EU IVDR (In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation): Even stricter; Notified Body capacity crisis; many IVDs being withdrawn
- ISO 13485: Quality management system standard; required for EU CE marking; FDA recognizes
- ISO 14971: Risk management standard; required for medical device development
- IEC 62304: Software lifecycle processes for medical device software
- UDI (Unique Device Identification): FDA and EU requirements; tracking and traceability
- Cybersecurity: FDA premarket cybersecurity guidance; IEC 62443; SBOM (Software Bill of Materials) requirements
- Post-market surveillance: FDA MDR (Medical Device Reports); EU vigilance; PSUR/PMS reports
- IMDRF: International Medical Device Regulators Forum; harmonization efforts

**Pain Points (Quantified)**
- EU MDR compliance: 70%+ of device manufacturers say MDR is their #1 challenge; recertification cost $500K-$5M per device family; Notified Body backlog 12-18 months
- 510(k) clearance timeline: Average 177 days (up from 120 days in 2019); each month of delay = $1-5M in delayed revenue
- Post-market complaints: Average device company processes 5,000-50,000 complaints/year; manual complaint handling 4-8 hours per complaint
- Recall costs: Average medical device recall costs $10-50M; FDA recalls 1,500+ per year; Class I recalls growing
- Quality system burden: CAPA cycle time 90-180 days average; audit findings 10-30 per audit; resource-intensive
- Connected device cybersecurity: 60%+ of medical devices in hospitals have known vulnerabilities; FDA requiring SBOM and patch plans
- SaMD classification uncertainty: AI/ML device regulation evolving rapidly; Predetermined Change Control Plan (PCCP) framework new
- Supply chain: Single-source components common; lead times 6-12 months; raw material quality variability

**Buying Triggers**
- 510(k)/PMA submission preparation: Regulatory technology; document management; submission assembly
- EU MDR recertification deadline: Quality system upgrade; clinical evidence management; Notified Body submission
- FDA warning letter / 483 observation: Remediation; QMS upgrade; CAPA management improvement
- New product development: Design control technology; simulation; prototyping
- Competitor recall: Quality investment; post-market surveillance upgrade; risk management
- Connected device launch: Cybersecurity framework; SBOM management; post-market monitoring
- M&A: QMS harmonization; product portfolio rationalization; data migration
- New VP Quality or VP Regulatory hire: System modernization; 90-120 day evaluation
- SaMD/AI device development: IEC 62304 tools; ML ops for medical devices; clinical validation support

**Channels**
- Industry events: MD&M West, RAPS (Regulatory Affairs Professionals Society), AdvaMed, BIOMEDevice
- Email: VP Regulatory, VP Quality, VP R&D — peer references from device makers are essential
- Advisory firms: Gartner Life Sciences, KLAS Research, L.E.K. Consulting
- Trade media: MedTech Dive, Medical Device & Diagnostic Industry (MDDI), RAPS Regulatory Focus
- LinkedIn: VP Regulatory, VP Quality — EU MDR and FDA content performs well
- Notified Body events: TÜV SÜD, BSI, DNV events — indirect influence on buying decisions
- Industry associations: AdvaMed, MDMA (Medical Device Manufacturers Association), RAPS

**Vocabulary**
- 510(k), PMA, De Novo, HDE (Humanitarian Device Exemption), Breakthrough Device Designation
- Class I, II, III; substantial equivalence, predicate device, special controls
- CE marking, Notified Body, Declaration of Conformity, EC Certificate, UDI
- EU MDR, IVDR, MDD (legacy), AIMDD (legacy); Annex IX/XI classification
- Design control, DHF (Design History File), DMR (Device Master Record), DHR (Device History Record)
- QMS, ISO 13485, ISO 14971, IEC 62304, IEC 62366 (usability)
- CAPA, NCR (Non-Conformance Report), complaint, MDR (Medical Device Report), vigilance

**Regulatory**

- HIPAA: Business Associate Agreements; PHI handling; breach notification; Security Rule compliance
- HITECH: Meaningful Use / Promoting Interoperability; patient data access; API requirements

- User engagement: 90-day retention for health apps is 10-20% (vs. 40%+ for fitness apps); engagement is existential
- Regulatory uncertainty: FDA SaMD framework evolving; AI/ML regulation unclear; state-level restrictions multiplying
- EHR integration: Epic/Cerner APIs limited; integration takes 6-12 months per health system; FHIR not fully realized
- Trust deficit: 80% of digital health companies fail within 5 years; buyers (providers, payers) skeptical

### 2.5 Payers (Health Insurance)

**Market Shape**
- US health insurance market: ~$1.3T in premiums; growing 5-7% per year
- Market structure: Top 5 (UnitedHealth, Elevance/Anthem, Cigna, Humana, CVS/Aetna) = 45% market share; Blue Cross Blue Shield = 30%+ in many states
- Government programs: Medicare Advantage ($450B+); Medicaid managed care ($400B+); growing 8-10% per year

## 5. HIPAA Compliance for Outreach (Deep Dive)

### Permitted Activities
- Collecting **business contact information** (name, title, work email, work phone)
- Storing company information about healthcare organizations
- Outreach to healthcare professionals about B2B products/services
- Referencing publicly available clinical trial data (ClinicalTrials.gov)
- Using de-identified data for benchmarking and case studies

### Prohibited Activities
- Collecting or storing any **Protected Health Information** (patient data, diagnoses, treatment info)
- Outreach to **patients** (B2C healthcare outreach is heavily regulated)
- Storing information about an individual's **personal medical history**
- Sending **unsecured** communications about PHI
- Using patient testimonials without proper authorization
- Accessing EHR systems or patient portals for prospecting intelligence

### Required Safeguards
- All healthcare prospect data must be stored encrypted (AES-256 minimum)
- Access logs for healthcare prospect data (who accessed, when, why)
- BAA (Business Associate Agreement) required if any PHI could be incidentally accessed
- Minimum necessary standard: only collect data needed for outreach purpose
- Breach notification: If healthcare prospect data is breached, must notify within 60 days
- Training: All LeadReach agents handling healthcare prospects must complete HIPAA training

### State-Level Health Privacy (Beyond HIPAA)
- **Washington My Health My Data Act (MHMD)**: Broadest state health privacy law; applies to entities outside HIPAA; consumer health data includes reproductive health, gender-affirming care, mental health; private right of action
- **Connecticut**: Similar to MHMD; health data consent requirements
- **Nevada**: Health data opt-out requirements
- **Texas**: Health data consent and opt-out provisions
- **California (CCPA/CPRA)**: Health information as "sensitive personal information"; right to limit use

### FDA Advertising & Promotion Rules
- **NEVER** claim FDA endorsement or clearance for LeadReach or client products
- **NEVER** make clinical claims about products unless verified FDA-cleared indications
- **ALWAYS** include fair balance if referencing prescription drug benefits
- **ALWAYS** verify FDA clearance/approval status before referencing in outreach
