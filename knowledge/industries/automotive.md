# Automotive & Mobility — B2B Lead Intelligence Guide

## 1. Industry Overview

Automotive and mobility is a **$4 trillion global industry** undergoing its most fundamental transformation since the invention of the assembly line. The convergence of electrification, software-defined vehicles, autonomous driving, and shared mobility is reshaping every aspect of the value chain. The defining shift: **vehicles are becoming rolling computers** — the average new car contains 100M+ lines of code, and software will represent 50%+ of vehicle value by 2030 (up from 10% in 2015).

Key characteristics:
- **Software-defined vehicles (SDV)** — Over-the-air updates, cloud-connected services, and feature-on-demand business models
- **Electrification imperative** — EV adoption accelerating globally; ICE phase-out dates set by 15+ countries
- **Autonomous driving progression** — L2+ widely deployed; L3 emerging; L4 limited deployment in geo-fenced areas
- **Supply chain transformation** — Semiconductor shortages exposed fragility; vertical integration accelerating
- **Regulatory complexity** — Safety (NHTSA, UNECE), emissions (EPA, Euro 7), data privacy (GDPR, CCPA), and AI regulation
- **New entrant disruption** — Tesla, Rivian, Lucid, NIO, and tech companies (Waymo, Apple, Baidu) challenging incumbents
- **Mobility-as-a-Service (MaaS)** — Car-sharing, ride-hailing, and subscription models redefining ownership

## 2. Sub-Segment Deep Dives

### Automotive Software & SDV Platforms
Software platforms for in-vehicle infotainment (IVI), advanced driver assistance (ADAS), and vehicle operating systems. Key players: BlackBerry QNX, Wind River, Automotive Grade Linux (AGL), Android Automotive, and Apex.AI. Signals: SDV platform selection, OTA update strategy, and new model development.

### Connected Vehicle & V2X
Vehicle-to-everything communication, telematics, and connected car platforms. Key players: Harman (Samsung), Airbiquity, Wireless Car (Volvo), and Covari. Signals: connected services launch, V2X pilot, and data monetization strategy.

### EV Technology & Charging Infrastructure
Battery management systems (BMS), charging management, and grid integration. Key players: ChargePoint, EVgo, Electrify America, ABB, and Driivz. Signals: EV model launch, charging network expansion, and fleet electrification.

### Autonomous Driving & ADAS
LIDAR, radar, perception, and decision-making platforms for autonomous vehicles. Key players: Waymo, Cruise (GM), Tesla FSD, Mobileye (Intel), and Aurora. LIDAR: Luminar, Innoviz, Valeo. Signals: autonomous pilot program, ADAS feature launch, and regulatory approval.

### Fleet Management & Telematics
Fleet tracking, driver behavior, maintenance, and fuel/energy management. Key players: Samsara, Geotab, Fleetio, and Motive. Signals: fleet electrification, ELD compliance, and fleet optimization initiative.

### Manufacturing & Supply Chain
Smart manufacturing, digital twin, and supply chain visibility for automotive OEMs and Tier 1 suppliers. Key players: Siemens (Opcenter), SAP, PTC, and Resilinc. Signals: production ramp, supply chain disruption, and digital factory initiative.

### Dealership & Retail Technology
CRM, DMS (Dealer Management System), and digital retailing platforms. Key players: CDK Global, Reynolds and Reynolds, Cox Automotive, and Tekion. Signals: DMS migration, digital retailing mandate, and EV sales preparation.

### Mobility Services
Ride-hailing, car-sharing, and subscription platforms. Key players: Uber, Lyft, Zipcar, Turo, and Fair. Signals: MaaS pilot program, corporate mobility budget, and urban mobility partnership.

## 4. Buyer Personas

### The CTO / VP Software Engineering (Economic Buyer — SDV)
- **Role**: Sets software architecture for vehicle platforms; selects SDV platforms
- **Reporting**: Reports to CEO
- **Pain points**: Software complexity, OTA reliability, cybersecurity, talent acquisition, time-to-market
- **Buying trigger**: New vehicle platform development, SDV strategy, OTA failure, cybersecurity incident
- **What they care about**: Architecture flexibility, OTA capability, safety certification (ISO 26262), developer ecosystem
- **How to reach**: SAE WCX, Automobility LA, LinkedIn
- **Key metric**: Software development velocity, OTA success rate, cybersecurity posture

### The VP of Electrification / EV Platform (Economic Buyer — EV)
- **Role**: Manages EV platform development, battery strategy, and charging integration
- **Reporting**: Reports to CEO or COO
- **Pain points**: Battery cost, charging infrastructure, range anxiety, thermal management, grid integration
- **Buying trigger**: EV model launch, battery supplier change, charging network partnership, fleet electrification mandate
- **What they care about**: Battery performance, charging speed, total cost of ownership, grid integration
- **How to reach**: EVS conference, Battery Show, automaker EV events
- **Key metric**: Battery cost per kWh, charging time, range, EV margin

### The VP of Fleet Operations (Buyer — Fleet)
- **Role**: Manages fleet acquisition, operations, maintenance, and total cost of ownership
- **Reporting**: Reports to COO or CFO
- **Pain points**: Fuel costs, maintenance, driver safety, compliance, fleet electrification complexity
- **Buying trigger**: Fleet electrification commitment, ELD compliance, TCO optimization, safety incident
- **What they care about**: Total cost of ownership, driver safety, compliance, fuel/energy management
- **How to reach**: NAFA, AFLA, fleet management conferences
- **Key metric**: TCO per vehicle, fuel cost per mile, safety incident rate, utilization rate

### The VP of Autonomous Driving (Economic Buyer — AV)
- **Role**: Manages autonomous driving development, validation, and deployment
- **Reporting**: Reports to CEO
- **Pain points**: Perception accuracy, simulation at scale, regulatory approval, edge cases, validation cost
- **Buying trigger**: AV pilot program, regulatory framework, L3 feature launch, simulation bottleneck
- **What they care about**: Perception accuracy, simulation fidelity, validation methodology, regulatory compliance
- **How to reach**: AV conferences (Autonomous Vehicle Symposium, CES), SAE
- **Key metric**: Disengagement rate, simulation miles, validation coverage, regulatory milestones

## 5. Technographic Signals to Detect

### Automotive Stack Patterns by Maturity Level

**Legacy Vehicle Platform:**
- Proprietary ECU architecture with distributed software
- No OTA capability; dealer-only updates
- Basic telematics (emergency calling)
- ICE-only powertrain
- Manual manufacturing processes
- Siloed engineering tools

**Connected Vehicle Platform:**
- Domain controller architecture with central compute
- Limited OTA capability (infotainment only)
- Comprehensive telematics and connected services
- Hybrid ICE/EV powertrain
- Digital manufacturing (MES, PLM)
- ADAS L2 features (lane keep, adaptive cruise)

**Software-Defined Vehicle:**
- Central compute with zonal architecture
- Full OTA capability (infotainment + ADAS + powertrain)
- Cloud-native vehicle platform with edge computing
- EV-native or EV-dominant powertrain
- Digital twin for manufacturing and operations
- ADAS L2+ to L3 features
- Data monetization platform
- V2X communication capability

## 7. Automotive Industry Vocabulary & Jargon

- **SDV (Software-Defined Vehicle)** — Vehicle whose features are primarily delivered through software
- **OTA (Over-the-Air)** — Remote software updates to vehicle systems
- **ADAS** — Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (L0-L5 automation)
- **ECU (Electronic Control Unit)** — Individual computer controlling vehicle subsystems
- **BMS (Battery Management System)** — Battery monitoring and control for EVs
- **V2X** — Vehicle-to-Everything communication (V2V, V2I, V2P, V2N)
- **UNECE WP.29** — UN vehicle regulation framework (R155 cybersecurity, R156 OTA, R157 ALKS)
- **ISO 26262** — Functional safety standard for automotive electronics
- **Homologation** — Regulatory approval for vehicle type in specific markets
- **DMS (Dealer Management System)** — Software for automotive dealership operations
- **ELD (Electronic Logging Device)** — Mandated driver hours tracking (FMCSA)
- **TCO (Total Cost of Ownership)** — Full lifecycle cost including acquisition, operation, disposal
- **SoC (State of Charge)** — Battery charge level in EVs
- **NCAP** — New Car Assessment Program; vehicle safety ratings

## 9. Common Objections & Responses

### "Vehicle software must be safety-certified — we can't use new vendors."
**Response**: "Our platform is developed to ISO 26262 ASIL-D standards and pre-certified for automotive safety. We provide full safety documentation and work with your homologation team. [Reference OEM] achieved [safety certification] with our platform in [X months]."

### "Our current OTA works for infotainment — we don't need more."
**Response**: "Infotainment OTA is table stakes. The real value is OTA for ADAS, powertrain, and EV battery management — which requires a fundamentally different architecture. [Reference OEM] reduced recall costs by [$X million/year] by extending OTA to safety-critical systems."

### "EV charging infrastructure is too fragmented."
**Response**: "That's exactly the problem our platform solves — we aggregate [X charging networks] into a single driver experience and backend management platform. [Reference fleet] manages [Y charging locations] across [Z networks] with one platform."

### "Fleet electrification is too complex — range, charging, cost."
**Response**: "Our platform simplifies fleet electrification with route-optimized charging, total cost of ownership modeling, and grid integration. [Reference fleet] electrified [X vehicles] and reduced fuel costs by [Y%] while maintaining 100% operational readiness."

### "Autonomous driving is still years away — we can wait."
**Response**: "L2+ features are generating revenue today, and L3 is regulatory-approved in multiple markets. The technology choices you make now for L2+ determine your L3/L4 trajectory. [Reference OEM] launched L3 using the same platform they deployed for L2+."

## Regional Variations

### United States
- Second-largest automotive market; EV adoption accelerating
- Three US OEMs (GM, Ford, Stellantis) + Tesla, Rivian, Lucid
- Federal EV incentives (IRA); state ZEV mandates
- NHTSA regulatory framework; no UNECE type approval
- Strong fleet market; ELD mandate driving telematics adoption

### Europe
- Largest automotive market by production; strong regulatory push
- EU 2035 ICE phase-out; CO2 fleet targets driving EV
- UNECE type approval; stricter regulation than US
- Strong OEM ecosystem (VW, Stellantis, Mercedes, BMW, Renault)
- Growing EV charging infrastructure mandates

### China
- World's largest automotive market; EV leadership
- Strong government direction (NEV mandates, smart city integration)
- Domestic OEMs (BYD, NIO, Xpeng, Li Auto) + JVs
- Unique connected services ecosystem (Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent)
- Data localization requirements for vehicle data

### Asia-Pacific (Non-China)
- **Japan**: Toyota, Honda, Nissan; hybrid leadership; hydrogen exploration
- **South Korea**: Hyundai/Kia; EV and hydrogen; government tech support
- **India**: Fastest-growing market; affordability-driven; 2-wheel EV leadership
- **Southeast Asia**: Motorcycle-dominant; emerging EV adoption; ASEAN NCAP
