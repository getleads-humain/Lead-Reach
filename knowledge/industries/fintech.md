# FinTech & Financial Services — Prospecting Guide

## 1. Industry Overview

Financial services is a **$22 trillion global industry** spanning banking, insurance, investment management, payments, and fintech. FinTech — the technology-driven segment — is a high-velocity, high-trust, heavily regulated vertical within the broader financial services landscape. The defining characteristics are: **regulatory overhead** (KYC/AML, PCI-DSS, SOC 2, regional banking licenses), **long sales cycles** for enterprise deals (6-18 months), **trust as the primary currency**, and **a bifurcated buyer landscape** between regulated financial institutions (banks, credit unions) and non-regulated FinTechs (payments infrastructure, BNPL, embedded finance).

Key characteristics:
- **Regulated everywhere** — Multiple regulators per country; cross-border adds complexity. Every product, vendor, and process is subject to oversight by SEC, FINRA, OCC, FDIC, CFPB (US), FCA (UK), BaFin (Germany), MAS (Singapore), and dozens of others
- **Risk-averse** — Failed products can cost billions in fines
- **Security-obsessed** — SOC 2 Type II minimum; ISO 27001; sometimes FedRAMP
- **Procurement-heavy** — RFPs, vendor risk assessments, security questionnaires
- **Talent-poor** — Bank IT teams are stretched thin; modernization backlog is huge
- **Long clinical cycles** — Drug development 7-10 years; device approval 1-3 years (for pharma/biotech sub-segments)

The buying motion differs dramatically by sub-segment:
- **Selling to banks**: Procurement-driven, RFP-heavy, 9-18 month cycles. Decisions made by committees that include risk, compliance, IT, and a line-of-business sponsor. Trust signals (SOC 2, ISO 27001, prior bank references) gate the conversation.
- **Selling to FinTech startups**: Founder/CTO-driven, 2-8 week cycles, evaluation-led. Decisions made by a small team that values speed-to-integration and pricing transparency.
- **Selling to mid-market merchants**: Ops/Finance-driven, 4-12 week cycles. ROI-led; the conversation is about cost takeout and reconciliation efficiency.

LeadReach is best positioned for the FinTech startup and mid-market merchant segments. Selling to Tier-1 banks requires dedicated enterprise sales motions that LeadReach's agent-driven outbound model does not displace. Mid-tier banks and credit unions are accessible.

## 2. Sub-Segment Deep Dives

### Banking (Retail & Commercial)

- **Market shape**: Core banking is dominated by FIS, Fiserv, and Jack Henry (US), Temenos (international), Finacle/Infosys (Asia). Digital transformation is a $50B+ annual spend category. Community banks and credit unions are underserved and accessible for LeadReach.
- **Buyer**: Head of Digital, CIO, Head of Retail Banking, Head of Commercial Banking
- **Cycle**: 12-24 months for enterprise bank; 4-9 months for mid-tier/community bank
- **Pain**: Legacy core systems (Jack Henry, FIS, Fiserv), digital transformation, regulatory reporting, talent shortage, board pressure on digital
- **Tools**: Core banking (FIS, Fiserv, Jack Henry, Temenos), CRM (Salesforce Financial Services Cloud), digital banking platforms (Q2, Alkami)
- **Decision-making structure**: Committee-driven — risk, compliance, IT, and line-of-business sponsor. CIO signs off on technology; CCO has veto on compliance; CFO on budget.
- **Buying triggers**: Regulatory consent order; digital transformation initiative; new CIO/CTO hire; core system migration; branch closure announcement; M&A requiring system consolidation.
- **Vocabulary & jargon**: Core banking system, payment rails (ACH, wire, RTP, FedNow, SEPA), authorization rate, chargeback, NPS, CCAR/DFAST, NII (net interest income), CET1 ratio, LTV/DTI, deposit flight, treasury management.

### FinTech (Payments, Neobanks, Lending, B2B)

- **Sub-types**: Payments (Stripe, Square), Neobanks (Chime, Revolut), Lending (SoFi, LendingClub), Wealth (Betterment, Wealthfront), B2B (Brex, Ramp)
- **Buyer**: CTO, VP Engineering, Head of Product
- **Cycle**: 1-6 months (faster than traditional finance)
- **Pain**: Scaling, compliance, fraud, customer acquisition cost, building vs. buying
- **Tools**: Cloud-native (AWS, GCP), microservices, modern stack
- **Decision-making structure**: Founder/CTO-driven at early stage; VP Engineering at growth stage; CFO increasingly involved post-Series B. Small buying committees (2-4 people) at early stage; formal procurement above 200 employees.
- **Buying triggers**: Series B+ funding; new product launch (especially compliance/risk products); geographic expansion; partnership with Tier-1 bank; SOC 2 Type II attestation published; hiring compliance/risk roles; CRO or COO hire.
- **Vocabulary & jargon**: API-first, SDK, sandbox, KYC/AML, BSA, OFAC, PCI DSS, payment rails, authorization rate, chargeback, NPS, MRR/ARR, take rate, GMV, attach rate, embedded finance, banking-as-a-service.

## 4. Buyer Personas

### The Bank CIO
- **At**: Mid-large bank ($10B+ assets) or community bank/credit union
- **Cares about**: Modernization, cost reduction, regulatory compliance, vendor risk
- **Pain**: Legacy systems, talent shortage, board pressure on digital
- **Outreach angle**: Specific bank case studies (peer references are gold), regulatory compliance, integration with their core
- **Channels**: Email, executive events (Money20/20, Finovate), peer CIO networks
- **What works**: Hard references from similar-size banks; ROI backed by independent analyst reports
- **What fails**: "Disrupt your bank" messaging — they don't want disruption, they want evolution

### The Fintech CTO
- **At**: VC-backed fintech (Series A+)
- **Cares about**: Velocity, scalability, security posture, compliance
- **Pain**: Building vs. buying, vendor sprawl, audit prep, scaling team
- **Outreach angle**: Developer experience, integration speed, compliance out-of-the-box
- **Channels**: LinkedIn, Twitter/X, engineering blogs, fintech-specific events
- **What works**: Technical depth, references from peer fintechs, security/compliance details
- **What fails**: Sales-y pitches, missing technical specs

### The Chief Compliance Officer
- **At**: Any regulated financial institution
- **Cares about**: Regulatory exams, audit findings, AML/KYC, consumer protection
- **Pain**: Regulatory change management, audit prep, vendor risk
- **Outreach angle**: Specific regulation expertise, exam preparation, automated compliance
- **Channels**: Email, compliance officer networks, regulatory technology events
- **Objection patterns**: "We have an in-house compliance team" (disintermediation fear) — "Your compliance team should be reviewing decisions, not gathering data. We give them the data so they review faster."

### The Head of Claims (Insurance)
- **At**: P&C insurer
- **Cares about**: Cycle time, leakage, customer satisfaction, fraud detection
- **Pain**: Manual processes, fraud losses, customer churn post-claim
- **Outreach angle**: Specific claims KPIs, fraud reduction %, CX improvement
- **Channels**: Email, insurance events (ITC Vegas, ACORD)
- **What works**: Concrete case studies (e.g., "reduced claim cycle time by 40% at [peer insurer]")

### The VP Finance / CFO (FinTech)
- **At**: Series B+ fintech
- **Cares about**: Cash management, compliance costs, unit economics, audit readiness
- **Pain**: Manual financial close, regulatory reporting, FX exposure, vendor sprawl
- **Outreach angle**: ROI calculators, compliance automation, audit-ready reporting
- **Channels**: Email, CFO networks

### The Head of Payments / VP Engineering (Payments)
- **At**: Payments company or FinTech with payment operations
- **Cares about**: Authorization rates, fraud prevention, latency, compliance, scale
- **Pain**: Integration complexity, cross-border compliance, vendor sprawl, authorization rate optimization
- **Outreach angle**: Technical depth, integration benchmarks, fraud reduction case studies
- **Channels**: LinkedIn, technical conferences, engineering blogs

## 9. Outreach Best Practices & Sequencing

### Channel Sequencing for FinTech

FinTech outbound requires more **trust-building touches** than SaaS. Recommended sequence:

- **Day 0**: LinkedIn connection — reference a recent funding or product announcement.
- **Day 3**: Cold email #1 — 110 words, lead with a regulatory or operational insight, no product pitch.
- **Day 7**: LinkedIn engagement — thoughtful comment on a recent post.
- **Day 10**: Cold email #2 — case study from same sub-vertical, mention SOC 2 + ISO 27001.
- **Day 14**: Direct mail — physical book or report (high-trust verticals respond well).
- **Day 17**: Cold call — reference the mail and the emails.
- **Day 21**: Cold email #3 — breakup email.
- **Day 28**: Final email — invite to a private roundtable / webinar.

Expected meeting-booked rate: **1.5-2.5%** (lower than SaaS due to longer cycle and trust requirements).

### Bank Outreach Template
```
Subject: peer reference - [specific bank pain point]

Hi [Name],

Noticed [their bank] recently announced [trigger event]. We work with peer 
institutions like [peer bank 1] and [peer bank 2] on [specific problem].

At [peer bank], we achieved [specific outcome] in [timeframe] with [ROI]. 
I can share the case study if relevant.

Worth a 20-minute conversation? Happy to send the case study first if 
you prefer to review internally.

Best,
[Name]
```

Key elements:
- **Peer references** are mandatory — without them, no bank will engage
- **Specific outcome** with numbers
- **Soft CTA** — don't push for meeting immediately; offer to share info first
- **Compliance-aware** language (mention SOC 2, audit-ready)

### Fintech Outreach

More direct than bank outreach. Fintech buyers respond to:
- **Technical depth** — show you understand their stack
- **Velocity** — fast integration, fast time-to-value
- **Developer experience** — SDKs, API docs, sandboxes
- **References from peer fintechs** — but peer set is different (Series A-C, not banks)

## 10. Regulatory & Compliance Notes

- **PCI-DSS**: Required if handling card data. Most FinTechs are PCI-DSS Level 1.
- **SOC 2 Type II**: Required for any FinTech buyer. LeadReach must have its own SOC 2 Type II report.
- **ISO 27001**: Common in EU FinTechs; provides a baseline for information security.
- **KYC/AML**: FinTechs handling customer funds have KYC/AML obligations. Their vendors must demonstrate compliance with BSA/AML rules (US), 4AMLD/5AMLD (EU), or local equivalents.
- **GDPR**: Strict for EU FinTechs. Consent management, DSAR handling, and data residency are gating issues.
- **CCPA/CPRA**: California FinTechs and those serving CA residents.
- **PSD2 / Open Banking (EU/UK)**: Relevant for payments and account-information service providers.
- **Bank Secrecy Act (US)**: Required for money services businesses.
- **State money transmitter licenses (US)**: Required for payments companies operating in most states.
- **OCC / FINRA / SEC**: Regulated institutions have additional vendor risk requirements.
- **FINRA**: Regulates broker-dealer communications — outreach to financial advisors has specific rules.
- **TCPA**: Telephone Consumer Protection Act — auto-dialers and SMS need consent.
- **GLBA**: Financial institution data sharing restrictions.
- **State-level**: New York DFS 500 (cybersecurity), California CCPA, etc.
- **Cross-border**: Different rules for EU vs US vs APAC prospects.

LeadReach agents should NOT:
- Send cold emails to consumer-facing banking customers (only B2B contacts)
- Make automated calls without consent (TCPA)
- Store financial account numbers (GLBA)
- Claim FINRA/SEC registration that doesn't exist
- Mention specific patient data (for healthcare-adjacent financial services)

## Regional Variants

- **US**: Largest market, most fragmented regulation (state-by-state for money transmission). OCC, FDIC, CFPB, SEC, FINRA oversight.
- **EU (PSD2)**: Open-banking native; cross-border payments are easier than US. DORA and NIS2 adding compliance requirements. MiCA for crypto-assets.
- **UK (FCA)**: Post-Brexit but largely aligned with EU; fastest EU market for FinTech sales.
- **Singapore (MAS)**: Sandbox-first regulator; friendly to FinTech pilots.
- **Australia (APRA/ASIC)**: Mature FinTech market; long sales cycles.
- **Brazil (PIX)**: Real-time payments dominance; FinTech ecosystem growing rapidly.
- **India (RBI / UPI)**: Massive volume but price-sensitive; payments infrastructure market.
