# SaaS Industry — Prospecting Guide, Signals, and Buyer Personas

## 1. Industry Overview

SaaS (Software-as-a-Service) is a $250B+ global industry growing 15-20% annually. It is characterized by:
- **Subscription revenue model** — Annual contracts (ARR) or monthly (MRR); LTV/CAC ratio is the master metric
- **Cloud-native delivery** — Multi-tenant architecture; deployment in hours not months
- **Product-led growth (PLG)** — Many SaaS companies grow through free trials / freemium
- **Vertical SaaS explosion** — Industry-specific SaaS (e.g., Procore for construction, Veeva for pharma) growing faster than horizontal
- **API-first** — Modern SaaS exposes APIs; integrations are a competitive moat

The SaaS buyer is **sophisticated, evidence-driven, and skeptical of marketing**. They benchmark everything, read G2 reviews before demos, and want to see ROI calculations. Generic outreach fails; specific, data-driven outreach wins.

## 2. SaaS Sub-Segments

SaaS is not one industry — it's a dozen sub-segments with different dynamics. Identify which sub-segment your prospect belongs to:

### Horizontal SaaS (Cross-Industry)
Tools used across industries: CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot), HRIS (BambooHR, Rippling), ATS (Greenhouse, Lever), accounting (QuickBooks, NetSuite), communications (Slack, Zoom).

**Buyer profile**: Director of [function] or VP [function]. CRO for sales tools, CHRO for HR tools, CFO for finance tools.
**Sales cycle**: 30-90 days for SMB, 90-180 days for mid-market, 6-12 months for enterprise.
**Budget source**: Functional budget (Sales, HR, Finance) — not IT.

### Vertical SaaS (Industry-Specific)
Tools built for one industry: Procore (construction), Veeva (pharma), nCino (banking), Toast (restaurants), Guideline (retirement plans).

**Buyer profile**: Industry-specific titles (e.g., "Construction Project Manager", "Pharma R&D Director"). Less sophisticated about SaaS, more sophisticated about their industry.
**Sales cycle**: 60-180 days. Vertical buyers value references from their industry above all.
**Budget source**: Functional budget within the industry (e.g., R&D budget for pharma tools).

### Developer Tools / DevTools
Tools for software engineers: GitHub, GitLab, Datadog, Sentry, Vercel, Stripe (developer-first payments).

**Buyer profile**: Engineering Manager, VP Engineering, CTO. Sometimes individual contributor (bottom-up adoption).
**Sales cycle**: 0-30 days for PLG, 60-180 days for enterprise.
**Budget source**: Engineering budget. ICs can expense up to ~$1K/mo without approval.

### Infrastructure SaaS
Cloud infrastructure: AWS, GCP, Azure, Snowflake, Databricks, Confluent.

**Buyer profile**: VP Engineering, CTO, Head of Platform/Infrastructure.
**Sales cycle**: 90-365 days. Significant evaluation.
**Budget source**: Engineering budget, sometimes separate infrastructure budget.

### MarTech (Marketing Technology)
Marketing tools: Marketo, HubSpot, Mailchimp, Segment, Customer.io, Amplitude.

**Buyer profile**: VP Marketing, CMO, Head of Growth, Head of Demand Gen.
**Sales cycle**: 60-120 days.
**Budget source**: Marketing budget.

### SalesTech
Sales tools: Outreach, Salesloft, Apollo, Gong, Chorus, Clari.

**Buyer profile**: VP Sales, CRO, Head of Sales Ops, RevOps.
**Sales cycle**: 60-120 days.
**Budget source**: Sales budget.

### FinTech SaaS
Financial software: Stripe, Plaid, Brex, Mercury, Ramp, Carta.

**Buyer profile**: CFO, VP Finance, Head of FP&A, Controller.
**Sales cycle**: 90-180 days (compliance review).
**Budget source**: Finance budget.

### HRTech
HR software: BambooHR, Rippling, Gusto, Lattice, 15Five.

**Buyer profile**: CHRO, VP People, Head of HR Ops, Head of L&D.
**Sales cycle**: 60-180 days.
**Budget source**: HR budget.

### SecTech (Security)
Security tools: CrowdStrike, Okta, Auth0, Snyk, Lacework, Wiz.

**Buyer profile**: CISO, VP Security, Head of AppSec.
**Sales cycle**: 90-180 days (security review of security tools is rigorous).
**Budget source**: Security budget (often separate from IT).

## 3. Firmographic Signals by SaaS Stage

### Pre-Seed / Seed (1-10 FTE, $0-3M raised)
- **Signals**: Building MVP, hiring first engineers, no revenue or <$1M ARR
- **Buying behavior**: Free tools (GitHub, Notion free tier), low-priced SaaS ($10-50/mo), expense out of pocket
- **Budget availability**: $0 for new vendors; building everything in-house
- **Outreach angle**: Skip unless you have a free tier; otherwise wait for Series A

### Series A (10-50 FTE, $3-15M raised)
- **Signals**: Product-market fit achieved, first $1-5M ARR, hiring sales team
- **Buying behavior**: Start paying for tools ($100-1000/mo range), evaluate but buy quickly
- **Budget availability**: $50K-500K annual tooling budget
- **Outreach angle**: Position as enabling scale; reference similar Series A companies

### Series B-C (50-300 FTE, $15-100M raised)
- **Signals**: $5-50M ARR, hiring across functions, expanding geographically
- **Buying behavior**: Formal evaluation, multiple stakeholders, 60-120 day cycles
- **Budget availability**: $500K-5M annual tooling budget
- **Outreach angle**: Scale, efficiency, integration with existing stack

### Series D+ / Late Stage (300-2000 FTE, $100M+ raised)
- **Signals**: $50M+ ARR, preparing for IPO or major acquisition, international expansion
- **Buying behavior**: Procurement-led, RFP process, 6-12 month cycles
- **Budget availability**: $5M+ annual tooling budget
- **Outreach angle**: Enterprise-grade, compliance, references from Fortune 500

### Public SaaS (2000+ FTE)
- **Signals**: Ticker symbol, 10-K filings, earnings calls, public product launches
- **Buying behavior**: RFP, vendor management, 6-12 month cycles, legal review
- **Budget availability**: $10M+ annual tooling budget
- **Outreach angle**: Specific outcomes tied to public metrics; reference earnings call priorities

## 4. Technographic Signals to Detect

For SaaS prospects, the tech stack is the **strongest signal of need**. Use PublicWWW / BuiltWith to detect:

### The SaaS Stack Patterns
- **Modern PLG SaaS**: Stripe + Intercom + Customer.io + Segment + Amplitude + Vercel + Linear → buyer is sophisticated, values developer experience
- **Enterprise SaaS**: Salesforce + Marketo + Adobe Analytics + AWS + ServiceNow → buyer is procurement-led, values integrations and stability
- **Legacy / On-prem heavy**: Microsoft stack (Dynamics, SharePoint, IIS) + Oracle + SAP → slow buyer, needs migration story
- **Modern DevTools-first**: GitHub + Vercel + Linear + Slack + Notion → engineering-led buyer, values speed

### Tech Stack Migration Signals
- **Recently added Salesforce** → migrating from spreadsheets or smaller CRM; sales scaling phase
- **Recently added HubSpot** → marketing function professionalizing
- **Recently added Segment** → data infrastructure investment, likely buying more analytics tools
- **Recently added Snowflake/Databricks** → data warehouse investment, likely buying BI tools
- **Recently added Okta** → SSO rollout, growing security maturity
- **Recently removed a tool** → opportunity for replacement

## 5. SaaS Buyer Personas

### The CRO (Chief Revenue Officer)
- **At**: Series B+ SaaS, $10M+ ARR
- **Cares about**: Sales efficiency, ramp time, quota attainment, forecasting accuracy, revenue retention
- **Pain**: SDR ramp time, forecast accuracy, sales-marketing alignment, churn
- **Outreach angle**: Reference revenue metrics, sales efficiency benchmarks, similar-CRO case studies
- **Channels**: LinkedIn, email, peer events (SaaStr, Pavilion)
- **What NOT to say**: Anything about "growth hacking" or "viral loops"

### The VP Engineering / CTO
- **At**: Any SaaS with engineering team
- **Cares about**: Velocity, reliability, developer experience, technical debt, hiring
- **Pain**: Code review bottlenecks, on-call burnout, integration complexity, slow CI/CD
- **Outreach angle**: Technical depth, benchmarks (deploy frequency, MTTR), specific engineering metrics
- **Channels**: GitHub, Twitter/X, engineering blogs, conferences (QCon, Strange Loop)
- **What NOT to say**: Generic "10x productivity" claims without evidence

### The Head of RevOps
- **At**: Series A+ SaaS, $5M+ ARR
- **Cares about**: Process consistency, data hygiene, forecasting, territory planning, compensation plans
- **Pain**: CRM hygiene, manual reporting, broken integrations, slow deal velocity
- **Outreach angle**: Process automation, data quality, similar-RevOps case studies
- **Channels**: LinkedIn (very active), RevOps Co-op community, Pavilion
- **What NOT to say**: "AI-powered" without specifying how

### The CFO
- **At**: Series B+ SaaS, $10M+ ARR (or earlier if VC-backed with board oversight)
- **Cares about**: Cash burn, runway, unit economics, gross margin, deferred revenue
- **Pain**: Manual financial close, ASC 606 compliance, FX exposure, board reporting
- **Outreach angle**: Financial metrics, compliance, automation, similar-ARR benchmarks
- **Channels**: Email, peer CFO networks, CFO-specific events
- **What NOT to say**: ROI claims without backing math

### The Head of People / CHRO
- **At**: Series A+ SaaS, 50+ FTE
- **Cares about**: Retention, performance management, DEI, comp benchmarking, remote-first operations
- **Pain**: Manual HR processes, manager training gaps, comp band inconsistencies, performance review cycles
- **Outreach angle**: Retention metrics, manager effectiveness, similar-headcount benchmarks
- **Channels**: LinkedIn, People Ops communities (Rogue HR, People Geeks)
- **What NOT to say**: Anything implying their team is replaceable with software

## 6. Trigger Events Specific to SaaS

In addition to the general trigger events catalog, watch for SaaS-specific triggers:

- **New ARR milestone announced** (e.g., "$10M ARR" blog post) → scaling phase, buying infrastructure
- **Open role for specific function** (e.g., first Head of Sales Ops) → function being professionalized, buying tools
- **New product launch** on Product Hunt → post-launch evaluation, complementary tools
- **Acquisition of smaller SaaS** → integrating stacks, may displace tools
- **SOC 2 / ISO 27001 announcement** → compliance maturity, may buy GRC tools
- **Listed on a marketplace** (Salesforce AppExchange, HubSpot Marketplace) → integration plays
- **Featured in Gartner Magic Quadrant** → recognition moment, may expand spend
- **Down round announcement** → cost optimization mode
- **Public earnings call** with specific guidance → reference the guidance

## 7. SaaS Industry Vocabulary

Use the prospect's language. SaaS has specific jargon that signals insider status:

- **ARR / MRR** — Annual/Monthly Recurring Revenue
- **NRR / GRR** — Net/Gross Revenue Retention
- **LTV / CAC** — Lifetime Value / Customer Acquisition Cost
- **CAC payback** — Months to recover customer acquisition cost
- **Burn multiple** — Net burn / Net new ARR (efficiency metric)
- **Rule of 40** — Growth rate + profit margin ≥ 40%
- **Magic number** — (Q4 ARR - Q1 ARR) / Q1 Sales+Marketing spend
- **PLG** — Product-Led Growth
- **SaaS flywheel** — Self-reinforcing growth loops
- **NPS / CSAT** — Customer satisfaction metrics
- **MQL / SQL / SAL** — Marketing/Service/Sales Accepted Lead
- **AE / SDR / SE** — Account Executive / Sales Dev Rep / Sales Engineer
- **POC** — Proof of Concept (vs. trial)
- **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)** — How SaaS companies are valued
- **ARR multiple** — Valuation / ARR (currently 6-12× for growth SaaS, was 20-30× in 2021)

Using these terms correctly signals you understand their world. Using them incorrectly signals you don't.

## 8. Common SaaS Outreach Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Pitching "Growth" to a Mature SaaS
A Series D SaaS at $100M ARR doesn't want "growth" — they want efficiency, retention, expansion. Tailor the angle to the stage.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring the Stack
Pitching your marketing automation tool to a company that just installed Marketo 30 days ago. They're not buying another tool in that category for 12+ months. Read the signals.

### Mistake 3: Wrong Stakeholder
Emailing the CTO about a sales tool. SaaS has clear functional budget owners — match the tool to the function.

### Mistake 4: Generic "10x" Claims
"We help SaaS companies 10x their growth" is meaningless. "We helped Lattice reduce SDR ramp time from 6 months to 8 weeks, saving $1.2M in onboarding costs across 40 hires" is meaningful.

### Mistake 5: Ignoring Funding Reality
A pre-seed SaaS cannot afford your $5K/mo tool. Either pitch to a later-stage prospect, or have a free / startup tier.

## 9. SaaS-Specific Data Sources

In addition to the standard sources, use these for SaaS prospecting:

- **Crunchbase** — Funding rounds, investors, acquisitions
- **PitchBook** — Deeper financial data (paid)
- **SaaStr** — Community, benchmarks, anonymized data
- **ChartMogul** — SaaS metrics benchmarks
- **KeyBanc SaaS Survey** — Quarterly benchmarks
- **OpenView SaaS Benchmarks** — Free benchmark report
- **G2 / TrustRadius** — Software reviews (reveals current stack and pain points)
- **Product Hunt** — Recent launches
- **Gartner / Forrester reports** — Market landscape, vendor positioning
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator** — Filter by SaaS company, function, seniority
- **GitHub** — Trending repos for DevTools prospects
- **Hacker News** — "Who is hiring" threads reveal SaaS companies scaling

## 10. LeadReach SaaS Playbook Summary

1. **Identify sub-segment** (horizontal, vertical, DevTools, etc.) — determines buyer and angle
2. **Determine stage** (funding round, ARR) — determines budget and cycle length
3. **Detect tech stack** — determines integration story and adjacency opportunities
4. **Identify correct buyer persona** — match the tool to the function
5. **Scan for trigger events** — funding, hires, product launches, earnings
6. **Compose outreach** referencing specific trigger + specific persona pain
7. **Multi-touch sequence** (email + LinkedIn + content) over 2-3 weeks
8. **Track funnel metrics** — delivery, open, reply, meeting-booked rates
9. **Iterate** based on what's working
