# Nigeria — B2B Lead Intelligence & Compliance Guide

## Regional Overview

Nigeria is Africa's largest economy (~$470B GDP, 2025) and most populous nation (230M), with the continent's largest tech ecosystem. Lagos has emerged as Africa's premier startup hub, producing 7+ unicorns (Flutterwave, Paystack, OPay, Chipper Cash, Interswitch, Andela, Moniepoint) and attracting $2B+ in VC funding in 2023 alone. The B2B SaaS market is estimated at ~$800M (2025), growing at 25–30% annually, driven by fintech innovation, mandatory digital tax compliance, and a massive SME sector (40M+ MSMEs).

Nigeria's B2B landscape is defined by its mobile-first infrastructure (mobile internet exceeds fixed-line by 20:1), its youthful population (median age 18), and the dominance of the informal sector (estimated 65% of GDP). The formal B2B market is concentrated in Lagos (financial services, tech, oil & gas), Abuja (government, defense), and Port Harcourt (oil & gas). The fintech sector is the crown jewel — Nigeria's instant payment system (NIP) processed ~$3T in 2024, and the Central Bank of Nigeria's regulatory sandbox has attracted global attention.

However, Nigeria also presents significant challenges: macroeconomic volatility (NGN depreciation, inflation ~30%), unreliable infrastructure (power grid instability, internet outages), complex and sometimes inconsistent regulation, and security concerns in certain regions. For LeadReach, Nigeria is a high-risk, high-reward market requiring deep local understanding, mobile-first product design, and NDPA compliance.

**Key facts**:
- **GDP**: ~$470B USD (2025); largest in Africa
- **Population**: ~230M; median age 18; ~60% under 25
- **Currency**: NGN (Nigerian Naira); highly volatile (~1,500–1,800 NGN per USD in 2025)
- **Government**: Federal presidential republic; 36 states + FCT Abuja; bicameral National Assembly
- **Languages**: English (official); Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo (major); 500+ languages; Pidgin English (widely spoken)
- **Time zone**: WAT (UTC+1); no daylight saving
- **Internet penetration**: ~36%; mobile ~85%; mobile-first market
- **Business centers**: Lagos (finance, fintech, tech, oil & gas HQs), Abuja (government, defense, construction), Port Harcourt (oil & gas operations), Ibadan (agri-tech), Kano (manufacturing, trade)

## Business Registration Infrastructure

### CAC — Corporate Affairs Commission

- **CAC** (`cac.gov.ng`): Nigeria's company registry. All businesses must register. The RC (Registration Certificate) number is the primary identifier.
- **Company types**: Private Limited Company (Ltd), Public Limited Company (Plc), Limited Liability Partnership (LLP), Company Limited by Guarantee (CLG).
- **Access**: Online search available at `search.cac.gov.ng` but often unreliable. Commercial aggregators (Proshare, Nairametrics) provide better data.
- **BNR (Business Registration Number)**: Increasingly used as a unified identifier.

### FIRS — Federal Inland Revenue Service

- **TIN (Tax Identification Number)**: Issued by FIRS for tax purposes. Required for all business transactions.
- **VAT registration**: Required for businesses with >NGN 25M annual turnover.

### NIPC — Nigerian Investment Promotion Commission

- **Foreign investment registration**: Required for companies with foreign participation.

## Regulatory & Compliance Framework

### NDPA — Nigeria Data Protection Act (2023)

Replaced the 2019 NDPR with a comprehensive GDPR-modeled law.

- **Scope**: All processing of personal data in Nigeria or of Nigerian data subjects. Extraterritorial application.
- **Legal bases**: Consent, contractual necessity, legal obligation, legitimate interest, vital interests, public interest. Legitimate interest allows B2B marketing with appropriate safeguards.
- **Consent**: Must be free, specific, informed, and unambiguous. For sensitive data, must be explicit.
- **Data subject rights**: Access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection.
- **DPO**: Required for data controllers processing data of >100 subjects or conducting high-risk processing.
- **Cross-border transfer**: Requires adequacy or appropriate safeguards; no adequacy determinations issued.
- **Enforcement**: NDPC (Nigeria Data Protection Commission); fines up to 2% of annual gross revenue or NGN 10M (~$12K), whichever is greater.
- **Breach notification**: Must notify NDPC within 72 hours.

### NITDA — National Information Technology Development Agency

- Oversees IT development and has issued supplementary data protection regulations.

## Cultural Business Norms

### Communication Style

- **Relationship-driven and energetic**: Nigerian business culture is vibrant, outgoing, and relationship-first. Personal trust is prerequisite for business. Cold outreach works but warm introductions improve results by 3–5x.
- **Hierarchical**: Titles (Chief, Alhaji, Dr., Oga) are important. Address seniors with respect. The "Oga at the top" culture means decisions flow from senior leadership.
- **Owambe culture**: Celebration and social events (owambe — lavish parties) are central to relationship-building. Attendance at social events builds trust faster than formal meetings.
- **Nollywood references**: Cultural references to Nigerian entertainment (Nollywood, Afrobeats — Burna Boy, Wizkid, Davido) are effective icebreakers.
- **Punctuality**: "African time" — 30–60 minutes late is common. But corporate Lagos (especially VI, Ikoyi, and Lekki) is more punctual.
- **Small talk**: Essential — family, football (Super Eagles), food (jollof rice debates — always claim Nigerian jollof is the best) are safe topics.

### Meeting Etiquette

- **Greetings**: Handshake is standard; close contacts may offer a back-slap or embrace. "You are welcome" is a common Nigerian greeting (meaning "welcome," not "you're welcome").
- **Dress code**: Nigerian formal business attire is elaborate — traditional attire (agbada, isiagu) is common and respected. For virtual meetings, smart casual is acceptable.
- **Prayer**: Meetings often open with a prayer, especially in the south. Participate respectfully regardless of personal beliefs.
- **Business meals**: "Small chops" (appetizers) and pepper soup are standard meeting fare. Refusing food can be seen as disrespectful.

### Decision-Making Speed

- **Moderate for Africa**: 2–6 weeks for SaaS mid-market; 3–6 months for enterprise; 1–3 weeks for startups.
- **Founder/CEO-driven in startups**: Lagos startups are fast-moving — decisions often made in a single meeting.
- **Government procurement**: Extremely slow (12–24 months) due to BPP (Bureau of Public Procurement) requirements.
- **Currency risk awareness**: NGN volatility means Nigerian buyers may delay procurement decisions during currency instability.

## Language & Localization

- **English is the business language** — Nigeria's primary advantage for US/UK vendors. Pidgin English is widely spoken but not used in formal B2B.
- **Mobile-first**: All outreach and landing pages must be mobile-optimized. Lightweight formats (no heavy images) for low-bandwidth environments.
- **Nigerian English**: Unique conventions — "flash" means to call and hang up (expecting a callback), "send" often means "transfer money."

| Channel | Permission Model | Best Practice | Restrictions |

| **Email (B2B)** | NDPA legitimate interest or consent | English; warm, personal tone; include opt-out | NDPA requires legitimate interest assessment for B2B outreach |

### Channel Mix Recommendations

- **Fintech/SaaS**: Email → WhatsApp → Phone → Events (7-10 touches, 30 days)
- **Enterprise/Oil & Gas**: Event → Email → Phone → Long-term relationship (30-60 days)
- **SMEs**: WhatsApp → Email → Phone (5-7 touches, 21 days)

## ICP Considerations by Sector

### Fintech & Digital Banking

- **Company size**: Startups (20–500), banks (2,000–30,000), microfinance banks (50–500), fintechs (50–2,000)
- **Decision-making**: CBN (Central Bank of Nigeria) regulated; compliance-driven; formal procurement in banks
- **Key signals**: CBN fintech licensing rounds, NIP transaction volume growth, open banking regulation, Paystack/Flutterwave API adoption
- **Revenue benchmarks**: Access Bank ~$3B; GTCO ~$2B; fintechs $1M–$500M; OPay ~$2B valuation
- **Special considerations**: CBN regulation is the primary driver; any solution must demonstrate regulatory compliance; NIP integration is table stakes

### Oil & Gas

- **Company size**: NNPC (10,000+), IOCs (5,000–20,000), indigenous producers (100–2,000), service companies (500–5,000)
- **Decision-making**: Extremely hierarchical; procurement committees; NCD (Nigerian Content Division) requirements mandate local content
- **Key signals**: NCD compliance deadlines, marginal field awards, NNPC JV call announcements, DPR (Department of Petroleum Resources) licensing
- **Revenue benchmarks**: NNPC ~$20B; IOCs $5B–$50B; service companies $100M–$5B
- **Special considerations**: Nigerian Content Act requires significant local content; security considerations in Niger Delta; long procurement cycles

### Telecommunications

- **Company size**: MNOs (1,000–5,000), ISPs (50–500), infrastructure companies (100–2,000)
- **Decision-making**: NCC (Nigerian Communications Commission) regulated; technology-driven procurement
- **Key signals**: NCC licensing rounds, 5G rollout milestones, NIN-SIM linkage compliance, broadband penetration targets
- **Revenue benchmarks**: MTN Nigeria ~$3B; Airtel Nigeria ~$1.5B; ISPs $10M–$100M

## Outreach Compliance Checklist

### Pre-Outreach

- [ ] Verify company via CAC search (active RC number, directors)
- [ ] Document NDPA legal basis (legitimate interest or consent)
- [ ] Prepare English-language outreach (Nigerian English conventions)
- [ ] Include opt-out and sender identification in every email template
- [ ] Verify no prior opt-out from the prospect
- [ ] Ensure mobile-optimized email templates (lightweight, no heavy images)

### During Outreach

- [ ] Use Nigerian English conventions (not British or American English)
- [ ] Include sender identification in every email
- [ ] Include one-click opt-out mechanism
- [ ] Honor opt-out within 48 hours
- [ ] For phone: expect and engage in small talk; identify self and company
- [ ] Respect local business hours (8am–5pm WAT)
- [ ] Be aware of potential infrastructure disruptions (power, internet)

### Post-Outreach

- [ ] Process all opt-out requests within 48 hours
- [ ] Maintain NDPA records for 5+ years
- [ ] Monitor for NDPA data subject requests (30 days under NDPA)
- [ ] Report breaches to NDPC within 72 hours
- [ ] Annual review of NDPA compliance documentation
