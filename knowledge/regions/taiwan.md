# Taiwan — B2B Lead Intelligence & Compliance Guide

## Regional Overview

Taiwan is the world's 21st-largest economy (~$800B GDP, 2025) and arguably the most strategically important B2B technology market globally, thanks to its dominance in semiconductor manufacturing. TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company) alone fabricates ~90% of the world's most advanced chips, and Taiwan's semiconductor ecosystem (TSMC, MediaTek, ASE Group, Foxconn, Delta Electronics) represents a $200B+ industry that is the linchpin of global technology supply chains. Beyond semiconductors, Taiwan has strong positions in ICT hardware (Acer, Asus, Gigabyte), precision machinery, and a growing SaaS/fintech sector.

With a population of ~23.5M, Taiwan is a relatively small but extremely wealthy and technologically sophisticated market. Internet penetration exceeds 92%, smartphone penetration exceeds 90%, and the country has one of Asia's highest broadband speeds. Taiwan's B2B SaaS market is estimated at ~$1.5B (2025), growing at 15–20% annually. The government's "Asian Silicon Valley" initiative and the "5+2 Industrial Innovation" plan have driven digital transformation, particularly in smart manufacturing, medtech, and green energy.

Taiwan's geopolitical situation creates unique B2B dynamics: the threat of Chinese military action has accelerated "de-risking" strategies among multinational companies, driving diversification of semiconductor supply chains and creating new B2B opportunities in resilience, cybersecurity, and supply chain visibility. For LeadReach, Taiwan is a tier-1 strategic market for semiconductor, cybersecurity, and smart manufacturing solutions, with significant localization requirements (Mandarin Chinese is essential, business culture is distinct from mainland China).

**Key facts**:
- **GDP**: ~$800B USD (2025); 21st largest globally
- **Population**: ~23.5M; aging population (median age 43)
- **Currency**: TWD (New Taiwan Dollar); ~31–33 TWD per USD
- **Government**: Semi-presidential republic; unicameral Legislative Yuan
- **Languages**: Mandarin Chinese (official); Taiwanese Hokkien (~70% spoken); Hakka (~12%); indigenous languages
- **Time zone**: CST (UTC+8); same as Beijing, Singapore, Hong Kong
- **Internet penetration**: ~92%; mobile penetration ~90%
- **Business centers**: Taipei (finance, SaaS, corporate HQs), Hsinchu (semiconductors, R&D — "Taiwan's Silicon Valley"), Taichung (precision machinery), Tainan (TSMC fabs, manufacturing), Kaohsiung (manufacturing, maritime)

## Business Registration Infrastructure

### MOEA — Ministry of Economic Affairs

- **Commerce Department** (`moea.gov.tw`): Taiwan's primary corporate registry. All companies must register with the MOEA. The unified business number (統一編號 — Tǒngyī Biānhào, commonly "統編" / Tǒngbiān) is the 8-digit universal business identifier.
- **Company types**: Company Limited (股份有限公司 — Gǔfèn Yǒuxiàn Gōngsī, most common), Limited Company (有限公司 — Yǒuxiàn Gōngsī), Unlimited Company, Partnership.
- **Access**: Searchable online at the MOEA Commercial Registration portal. Basic data (name, unified number, address, representative, capital) is free; detailed financials require paid access.

### Ministry of Finance — Tax Administration

- **Unified Business Number (統編)**: Also serves as the tax identification number. Used for all business transactions, invoicing, and tax filing.
- **e-Invoice (電子發票)**: Mandatory for most businesses since 2021. B2B e-invoices (B2B統一發票) must be issued electronically and uploaded to the Ministry of Finance platform.

### TWSE / TPEx — Taiwan Stock Exchange / Taipei Exchange

- **TWSE** (`twse.com.tw`): Lists ~1,000 companies on the main board. Comprehensive financial data available.
- **TPEx (GreTai Securities Market)** (`tpex.org.tw`): Lists ~800 companies on the over-the-counter market. Essential for mid-cap and tech companies.
- **MOPS (Market Observation Post System)** (`mops.twse.com.tw`): The electronic filing system for all listed company disclosures. Equivalent to EDINET (Japan) or SEDAR+ (Canada). Free access to financial statements, annual reports, and material disclosures.

## Regulatory & Compliance Framework

### PDPA — Personal Data Protection Act (個人資料保護法, 2010, amended 2015, 2023)

Taiwan's PDPA was significantly amended in 2023 to strengthen data subject rights and enforcement mechanisms.

- **Scope**: Applies to all processing of personal data by government agencies and non-government entities in Taiwan. Extraterritorial application — applies to foreign entities processing Taiwanese residents' personal data.
- **Definition of personal data**: Any information that can directly or indirectly identify a natural person. "Sensitive personal data" (health, genetics, sex life, criminal records, religion, political opinions) has enhanced protections.
- **Legal bases for processing**: (1) Legal obligation, (2) Contractual necessity, (3) Public interest, (4) Legitimate interest (with conditions), (5) Consent, (6) Academic research, (7) Employment. The 2023 amendment clarified that legitimate interest requires balancing the data controller's interest against the data subject's rights.
- **Consent**: Must be informed, specific, and freely given. For sensitive personal data, must be explicit and written. Can be withdrawn at any time.
- **Data subject rights**: Access, duplication, supplementation, correction, cessation of processing/erasure, objection to processing. Must respond within 15 days.
- **Cross-border transfer**: Restricted — requires: (1) consent, (2) contractual necessity, (3) public interest, or (4) the recipient country has adequate protection. No formal adequacy determinations issued, but the EU-Taiwan relationship is evolving (Taiwan is not recognized as a sovereign state by most countries, complicating formal adequacy agreements).
- **DPO**: Not explicitly required under PDPA, but recommended for organizations processing large volumes of personal data.
- **Enforcement**: The 2023 amendment created the Personal Data Protection Committee (preliminary body for a future Personal Data Protection Board). Fines up to NTD 5M (~$160K) per violation for non-government entities. Criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment for unauthorized collection or use.
- **Breach notification**: Must notify the competent authority and data subjects "promptly" after becoming aware of a breach. The 2023 amendment is expected to introduce specific timeframes.

### B2B Marketing Specifics

- **Cold email**: The PDPA does not explicitly prohibit B2B cold email. Legitimate interest may apply for B2B marketing if the email is relevant to the recipient's professional role and includes an opt-out mechanism. However, consent is the safest basis.
- **Opt-out**: Must be easy and honored promptly. Best practice: within 48 hours.
- **Telemarketing**: No formal DNC registry for B2B. The Communications Act regulates telecom marketing but enforcement is limited.

## Cultural Business Norms

### Communication Style

- **More direct than Japan, more formal than mainland China**: Taiwanese business communication falls between Japanese indirectness and mainland Chinese directness. Clarity is valued but delivered with courtesy.
- **Guanxi (關係 — relationships)**: Like mainland China, relationships are essential for business. However, Taiwanese guanxi is less transactional and more relationship-oriented than in mainland China.
- **Mianzi (面子 — face)**: Face-saving is important. Public criticism, direct refusals, and aggressive negotiation are counterproductive.
- **Mandarin with Taiwanese characteristics**: Taiwanese Mandarin uses traditional Chinese characters (繁體字 — Fántǐzì), not simplified (简体字 — Jiǎntǐzì). Using simplified characters in Taiwan is a serious cultural error — it implies mainland Chinese political alignment.

### Meeting Etiquette

- **Greetings**: Handshake is standard. Two-handed exchange of business cards is expected.
- **Business cards**: Exchanged at the start of meetings. Titles are important. Receive with both hands and study the card.
- **Gift-giving**: Modest gifts are welcome. Avoid clocks (鐘 — zhōng, sounds like "end/death"), umbrellas (傘 — sǎn, sounds like "scattering"), and anything in sets of four (四 — sì, sounds like "death").
- **Food**: Dining is a critical relationship-building activity. Night markets are a cultural touchstone — references to local food culture are appreciated.

### Decision-Making Speed

- **Moderate**: 2–4 months for SaaS mid-market; 4–8 months for enterprise; 1–2 months for startups.
- **Consensus-driven**: Decisions typically require alignment across multiple stakeholders, but final authority rests with senior leadership.
- **Semiconductor ecosystem**: Selling into TSMC's supply chain requires vendor qualification processes that can take 6–18 months.

## Language & Localization

### Mandarin Chinese Requirements

- **Official language**: Mandarin Chinese (Traditional Characters). This is non-negotiable for most B2B outreach.
- **Written script**: Traditional Chinese (繁體字) ONLY. Do NOT use Simplified Chinese (简体字) — this is a critical cultural and political distinction.
- **English proficiency**: Moderate (ranked ~45th on EPI). English is common in the semiconductor and tech sectors, especially in Hsinchu and Taipei, but less common in traditional industries.
- **Taiwanese Hokkien**: Spoken by ~70% of the population but not used in formal written business communications. Some familiarity with common Hokkien phrases builds rapport.

### Localization Imperatives

- **Traditional Chinese outreach is required** for most B2B contexts. English is acceptable for tech/semiconductor sectors.
- **Cultural sensitivity**: Never use Simplified Chinese. Never refer to Taiwan as a "province of China" or use mainland Chinese terminology.
- **Local references**: Taiwanese case studies and references are essential. References from mainland China may be counterproductive due to political sensitivities.

### Tax Rates

- **Corporate income tax**: 20% (standard); 18% for companies with taxable income ≤NTD 500K
- **VAT (營業稅)**: 5% standard
- **Business tax**: 1% for small businesses; 5% for general businesses
- **Withholding tax**: 20% on payments to foreign entities (reduced by tax treaties)

## Outreach Compliance Checklist

### Pre-Outreach

- [ ] Verify company exists and is active via MOEA or Unified Business Number lookup
- [ ] Determine PDPA legal basis: legitimate interest or consent
- [ ] Document the legal basis assessment
- [ ] Prepare Traditional Chinese outreach materials (NEVER Simplified Chinese)
- [ ] Include opt-out mechanism in every email template
- [ ] Include sender identification: company name, unified business number, address, contact information

### During Outreach

- [ ] Use Traditional Chinese (English acceptable for tech/semiconductor sectors)
- [ ] Use appropriate formality (titles and surname)
- [ ] Include sender identification in every email
- [ ] Include easy opt-out mechanism
- [ ] Honor opt-out requests within 48 hours
- [ ] For phone: identify yourself and company
- [ ] Respect local business hours (9am–6pm CST)

### Post-Outreach

- [ ] Process all opt-out requests within 48 hours
- [ ] Maintain PDPA records for 5+ years
- [ ] Monitor for PDPA data subject requests — respond within 15 days
- [ ] Report data breaches to competent authority promptly
- [ ] Annual review of PDPA compliance documentation
