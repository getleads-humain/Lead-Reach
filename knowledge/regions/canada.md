# Canada — Regional Playbook

## 1. Market Shape

Canada is the second-largest B2B outbound market in North America, with a sophisticated but **smaller and more concentrated buyer ecosystem** than the US. The defining characteristics are: **CASL (Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation)** — one of the strictest B2B anti-spam laws in the world, requiring express or implied consent for most commercial electronic messages; **PIPEDA** (Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act) as the federal privacy baseline; **Quebec Law 25** adding stricter consent and breach notification requirements; and **bilingual requirements** for federal regulated industries and Quebec.

Sales cycles are 20-40% longer than the US for SaaS B2B (45-120 days typical). ACVs are 15-25% lower than the US for the same buyer profile. The buyer ecosystem is concentrated in three hubs: Toronto (financial services, SaaS), Montreal (AI, gaming, life sciences), and Vancouver (gaming, DevTools, clean tech). Ottawa (gov-tech) and Calgary (energy tech) are secondary hubs.

LeadReach treats Canada as a tier-1 expansion market — easier to enter than EU (no GDPR), more regulated than US (CASL), and high cultural fit with US GTM playbooks with modest adaptation.

## 2. Federal Regulations

### CASL — Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (2014)

CASL is materially stricter than CAN-SPAM. Key requirements:

- **Consent required** — either express (opt-in) or implied (existing business relationship).
- **B2B implied consent** — permitted for 2 years after last purchase, inquiry, or business interaction; expires after 6 months of no contact if no purchase.
- **Identification** — sender name, physical address, and either phone, email, or URL.
- **Unsubscribe mechanism** — must be free, easy, and honored within 10 business days.
- **No false or misleading subject lines / sender info.**

Penalties: up to **CAD $10 million** per violation for businesses; personal liability for directors and officers. CRTC enforcement is active.

> **LeadReach recommendation**: Treat all Canadian B2B outreach as if express consent is required. Use a soft opt-in (download, demo request, content unlock) before cold email. Implied consent is fragile and high-risk at scale.

### PIPEDA (Federal Privacy Law)

PIPEDA governs collection, use, and disclosure of personal information in commercial activities. Key requirements:

- **Consent** — meaningful, valid, documented.
- **Purpose limitation** — only use data for the stated purpose.
- **Access and correction** — individuals can request their data and corrections.
- **Breach notification** — mandatory notification to OPC and affected individuals for "real risk of significant harm" breaches.
- **Accountability** — designate a privacy officer.

### Quebec Law 25 (2021–2023 phased)

Quebec's privacy law is stricter than PIPEDA and applies to any business operating in Quebec or handling Quebec residents' data. Key differences:

- **Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA)** required for any new project involving personal information.
- **Privacy officer** must be designated and named publicly.
- **Confidentiality / non-disclosure** of personal information explicitly required.
- **Breach notification** to CAI (Commission d'accès à l'information) within "reasonable time."
- **Consent** must be explicit, free, informed, and revocable.

### Provincial Privacy Laws (Other)

- **Alberta PIPA** — similar to PIPEDA, slightly less strict.
- **British Columbia PIPA** — similar to PIPEDA.
- **PHIPA (Ontario health)** — governs personal health information in Ontario.

## 3. Outreach Channels & Preferences

| Channel | Permission Required | Best Practice |
|---------|---------------------|---------------|
| Email (B2B) | CASL — implied OK for 2yr; express safer | Always include unsubscribe + sender address |
| Phone (B2B) | National DNCL (with B2B exemption nuances) | Scrub against DNCL quarterly; honor internal DNC |
| LinkedIn DM | ToS compliance; CASL applies if commercial | Connection-first, then pitch after 1-2 exchanges |
| SMS (B2B) | Express consent required under CASL | Avoid for cold; use for appointment reminders only |
| Direct mail | No federal restriction | Effective for executive-tier outreach; high cost |

## 4. Language & Localization

- **Quebec**: Bill 96 (2022) amends the Charter of the French Language. Commercial communications to Quebec residents (including contracts, websites, marketing materials) must be available in French. English is permitted alongside, but French must be at least equivalent in prominence.
- **Federal regulated industries** (banking, telecom, transportation): bilingual required.
- **Rest of Canada**: English-only is acceptable for B2B; French is a positive differentiator.

## 7. Buying Triggers (Canada-Specific)

- **FedDev / IRAP / SR&ED funding** announced — Canadian government funding is a strong capital signal.
- **MaRS Discovery District / Communitech acceleration** — Toronto / Waterloo startup signals.
- **Hiring in Toronto / Montreal / Vancouver** on LinkedIn — expansion signal.
- **Recent SOC 2 / ISO 27001** — enterprise readiness.
- **PCI / financial services compliance** (OSFI guidelines) — bank procurement.

## 10. Compliance Notes

- **CASL compliance** is the single biggest difference from US outbound. Plan for double-opt-in on any cold list.
- **PIPEDA + Quebec Law 25** — be ready to produce a privacy policy and PIA on request.
- **Data residency** — AWS ca-central-1 (Toronto) and Azure Canada Central (Toronto) are the standard. Sovereign cloud (Microsoft Cloud for Sovereignty) for government.
- **Bilingual (French)** — required for Quebec commercial communications under Bill 96.
- **Indigenous business considerations** — for resource-sector deals, NRCAN Indigenous participation requirements may apply.
