# DACH (Germany, Austria, Switzerland) — Regional Playbook

## 1. Market Shape

DACH (Germany, Austria, Switzerland) is the largest B2B market in the EU by revenue, characterized by **risk-averse buyers**, **strict privacy enforcement**, **strong preference for local language and local presence**, and **long sales cycles (90-180 days for mid-market, 6-12 months for enterprise)**. The defining characteristic is **relationship-driven procurement** — German-speaking buyers expect in-person meetings, technical due diligence, and references from peers in their industry.

The three DACH markets are distinct:
- **Germany** — largest, most regulated, slowest decisions. Strong manufacturing, automotive, financial services, and Mittelstand (SME) base.
- **Austria** — smaller, more flexible, often used as a DACH entry point. Vienna is a financial services hub.
- **Switzerland** — non-EU but GDPR-aligned. Highest ACVs in DACH. Pharma, finance, and high-tech concentration in Zurich/Geneva.

LeadReach treats DACH as a tier-1 EU expansion market — high ACVs compensate for longer cycles, and German-language capability is essential for credibility.

## 2. Regulations

### GDPR (EU) — Applies to Germany & Austria

GDPR is the baseline privacy law for Germany and Austria. Key requirements:

- **Lawful basis** — consent, contract, legitimate interest, etc. B2B cold email generally falls under legitimate interest but requires careful balancing test (Legitimate Interest Assessment, LIA).
- **Data Subject Rights (DSARs)** — access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection. Must respond within 30 days.
- **DPA (Data Processing Agreement)** — required Article 28 contract with any vendor processing personal data on your behalf.
- **Breach notification** — 72 hours to supervisory authority.
- **Records of processing activities (RoPA)** — Article 30 documentation.

Penalties: up to **€20M or 4% of global annual revenue**, whichever is higher.

### German Federal Data Protection Act (BDSG)

BDSG supplements GDPR with stricter German-specific provisions:

- **Employee data protection** — stricter consent requirements for employee monitoring.
- **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** mandatory for any company with >20 employees regularly processing personal data (lower threshold than GDPR).
- **Works Councils (Betriebsrat)** must approve any tool that affects employee behavior or performance — including CRM, SDR tooling, surveillance.

### Swiss FADP (Revised 2023)

Switzerland's Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP) was revised in 2023 to align with GDPR. Key requirements:

- **Cross-border data transfer** — adequacy decision or safeguards required.
- **Swiss DPO** recommended for any company >50 employees.
- **Breach notification** — to FDPIC for high-risk breaches.

### Telekommunikation-Telemedien-Datenschutz-Gesetz (TTDSG, Germany)

TTDSG governs electronic communications metadata. Relevant for any vendor tracking email opens, click patterns, or device fingerprints. **Tracking pixels in cold email are illegal without consent** in Germany.

## 3. Outreach Channels & Preferences

| Channel | Permission Required | Best Practice |
|---------|---------------------|---------------|
| Email (B2B) | Legitimate interest + LIA documented | No tracking pixels without consent; include Impressum |
| Phone (B2B) | Generally permitted for B2B | Always offer opt-out; respect Robinson list |
| LinkedIn DM | GDPR + ToS compliance | German-language preferred; relationship-first |
| Direct mail | No federal restriction | Effective for executive outreach; high quality |
| In-person events | Industry events dominate | Hannover Messe, CeBIT (defunct), IAA, DMEXCO |

> **Impressum requirement**: German law (Telemediengesetz) requires an "Impressum" (legal notice) on all commercial communications including emails and websites. Must include: full legal name of company, address, contact info, register number, VAT ID. Omitting is a cease-and-desist risk (Abmahnung).

## 4. Language & Localization

- **Germany**: German is the dominant business language outside of international tech companies. English-only outreach to mid-market German companies will underperform by 3-5x.
- **Austria**: German (Austrian variant). Vienna business culture is more international than the rest of Austria.
- **Switzerland**: German (Swiss Standard German for written; Swiss German dialect for spoken). French in western Switzerland (Geneva, Lausanne); Italian in Ticino. English widely spoken in Zurich tech/finance.

> **LeadReach recommendation**: For DACH, Bard agent composes German-language outreach by default. Switch to English only when prospect's LinkedIn profile or company website indicates international team.

## 7. Buying Triggers (DACH-Specific)

- **Recent EU funding** (Horizon Europe, EIC) — strong capital signal.
- **Recent ISO 27001 / TISAX certification** — auto industry procurement prerequisite.
- **Hiring in Berlin / Munich / Zurich** on LinkedIn — expansion signal.
- **BaFin / FINMA announcement** (financial services) — regulatory-driven procurement.
- **Trade fair participation** (Hannover Messe, DMEXCO, IAA Mobility) — relationship opportunity.
- **Press release on digital transformation initiative** — budget unlocked.

## 10. Compliance Notes

- **GDPR + BDSG + TTDSG** triple-compliance is the baseline for Germany.
- **DPO designate** mandatory for companies >20 employees processing personal data.
- **Works Council approval** — common blocker for any tool affecting employees.
- **TISAX** (automotive industry) — required for any vendor touching automotive supplier data.
- **BaFin** (financial services Germany) — supervisory requirements for IT vendors.
- **FINMA** (financial services Switzerland) — equivalent Swiss regulator.
- **Impressum** on all commercial communications — non-negotiable.
- **No tracking pixels** in cold email without consent (TTDSG).
- **Schrems II** — for any data flowing to US, Standard Contractual Clauses + Transfer Impact Assessment required.
