# India — B2B Prospecting Regional Guide

## 1. Country Overview

India is the world's fifth-largest economy (~$3.7T GDP), the fastest-growing major economy (6–7% real GDP growth), and the most populous nation on earth (1.45B people, having surpassed China in 2023). It is also the world's most B2B-fragmented market: over 60M MSMEs (Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises) account for ~30% of GDP, ~48% of exports, and the overwhelming majority of B2B prospecting targets. The corporate layer above MSMEs comprises ~1.7M active companies registered with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA), ~13,000 active LLPs, and a long tail of partnerships, proprietorships, and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) that fall outside the MCA register. Prospecting infrastructure is mixed — MCA21 is the canonical source for registered companies, GSTIN is the canonical tax identifier, and Udyam is the MSME self-registration portal — but the data is fragmented, often outdated, and frequently complemented by commercial aggregators (Zauba, Tofler, IndiaMart).

The cultural landscape is the most complex of any major B2B market: 22 scheduled languages, ~1,600 mother tongues, 28 states and 8 union territories each with distinct business norms, and four major regional blocs (North, South, West, East) that differ as sharply as European nations. English is the operating language of Indian B2B at the corporate and tech layer; Hindi dominates the north and centre at the SME layer; regional languages (Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Gujarati, Bengali, Punjabi) dominate regional SME trade. Mobile-first is not a buzzword in India — it is the reality: WhatsApp is the dominant B2B channel for SME suppliers, SMS remains effective (subject to DLT compliance), and cold email is increasingly crowded. The post-2023 regulatory shift, anchored by the Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA), has tightened consent rules and created new compliance obligations that any foreign prospecting operation must respect.

**Key facts**:
- **GDP**: ~$3.7T (2024), growth 6–7%/year
- **Population**: ~1.45B
- **Currency**: INR (₹); ~83 ₹/USD (2024)
- **Government**: Federal parliamentary republic; 28 states, 8 union territories
- **Languages**: 22 scheduled languages (Hindi, English, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Gujarati, Bengali, Punjabi, etc.); English is the subsidiary official language and lingua franca of corporate India
- **Time zone**: IST (UTC+5:30) — single zone nationwide
- **Internet penetration**: ~52% (but ~800M mobile broadband users)
- **Business centres**: Mumbai (finance, Bollywood), Bengaluru (IT, startups), Delhi-NCR (govt, services, manufacturing), Hyderabad (pharma, IT), Chennai (auto, manufacturing), Pune (auto, IT), Kolkata (industrial, services), Ahmedabad (textiles, chemicals), Gurugram (BPO, Fortune 500 India HQs)

## 2. Regulatory & Compliance Framework

### Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA) 2023
The DPDPA is India's first comprehensive personal data protection law, passed in August 2023 and phased into enforcement through 2024–2026. It applies to processing of **personal data** of Indian residents (data principals) by any entity, domestic or foreign, that processes such data within India or offers goods/services to data principals in India. Key obligations relevant to B2B prospecting:

- **Consent**: Personal data processing requires **free, specific, informed, and unambiguous consent** via a clear affirmative action. A Data Principal can withdraw consent at any time with equivalent ease.
- **Purpose limitation**: Data collected for one purpose cannot be repurposed without fresh consent.
- **Data minimisation**: Collect only what is necessary for the stated purpose.
- **Notice**: A clear, multi-lingual notice must be issued at or before collection, in English or any language in the 8th Schedule of the Constitution (22 languages).
- **Data Principal rights**: Access, correction, erasure, grievance redressal, nomination.
- **Significant Data Fiduciaries (SDFs)**: Volume/sensitivity thresholds trigger additional obligations — DPO appointment, DPIA, audits.
- **Cross-border transfer**: Permitted to all countries except those notified by the Central Government as restricted (a "negative list" approach, less restrictive than EU GDPR adequacy).
- **Penalties**: Up to **₹250 crore (~$30M) per instance** for significant breaches.

DPDPA does not yet have full Rules notified (as of mid-2026); until then, the **Information Technology Act 2000** and the **IT (Reasonable Security Practices) Rules 2011** remain operative alongside DPDPA. B2B marketing is permitted but consent for personal data of decision-makers (mobile, email) must be obtained or lawfully processed under a legitimate use exception — which is narrower than EU GDPR's legitimate interest.

### IT Act 2000 & Intermediary Guidelines
The IT Act 2000 governs electronic communications and digital signatures. The 2021 Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code impose due diligence obligations on intermediaries (including messaging platforms and B2B marketplaces). The IT Rules 2011 require "reasonable security practices" for handling "sensitive personal data or information" (SPDI) — passwords, financial data, biometric data, etc.

### TRAI Regulations & DLT for SMS
The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) regulates commercial communications through the **Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations (TCCCPR) 2018**, operationalised via the **Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT) platform**. Every commercial sender — including B2B — must:
1. Register as a **Principal Entity (PE)** on the DLT platform of any Indian telco (Airtel, Jio, Vi, BSNL, MTNL)
2. Register sender IDs (6-char alphanumeric for SMS)
3. Register message templates (every SMS content must match an approved template)
4. Use only scrubbed and consent-registered contacts
5. Honor customer preference (fully blocked, partially blocked categories)

Non-DLT-compliant SMS is blocked at the telco level; violations attract penalties of up to ₹5,000 per message in egregious cases. WhatsApp Business API messages require opt-in through Meta's approved flow; promotional WhatsApp to non-opted-in numbers is prohibited and gets numbers banned quickly.

### Goods & Services Tax (GST)
GST is the unified indirect tax (1 July 2017) that replaced dozens of central and state taxes. Every business with turnover > ₹40 lakh (goods) or ₹20 lakh (services) must register and obtain a **GSTIN** — a 15-digit state-wise identifier. GSTIN is the de facto verification number for B2B prospecting: it confirms a business is registered, active, and tax-compliant. The GST portal (`gst.gov.in`) allows verification; `services.gst.gov.in` enables public search by GSTIN. GST returns (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B) are monthly and contain trading counterparty data — but these are not public.

### Other Applicable Regulations
- **Companies Act 2013**: Governs incorporation, governance, filings, audits; enforced by MCA
- **Competition Act 2002**: CCI approval for M&A above thresholds
- **FEMA 1999**: Foreign exchange rules; FDI sectoral caps matter for prospecting foreign-invested firms
- **Consumer Protection Act 2019**: Unfair trade practices; applies to B2B misleading claims
- **SPD Rules**: For payments data (PCI DSS-equivalent obligations)

## 3. Business Registration & Identification

### MCA21 (Ministry of Corporate Affairs)
**Website**: `mca.gov.in`
**Portal**: `www.mca.gov.in/mcafoportal` (public company search via "MCA Services → Check Company Name")
**Data**: Basic company information is free; full company filings (via "View Company Master Data" + "View Index of Charges") are paid through the MCA21 portal.

MCA21 is the canonical registry for Indian companies and LLPs. It maintains:
- **Corporate Identification Number (CIN)** — 21-character alphanumeric: e.g., `U72200KA2000PTC027831` (Wipro Technologies). Format: `U` (industry code) + 5-digit NIC + 4-char state code + 4-char year + `PTC`/`PLC`/`LLP` etc. + 6-digit serial
- **LLP Identification Number (LLPIN)** — similar format for LLPs
- **Company/LLP name** (registered)
- **Registered office** (state, ROC)
- **Date of incorporation**
- **Company status** — `Active`, `Dormant under section 455`, `Strike Off`, `Amalgamated`, `Liquidated`
- **Authorised & paid-up capital**
- **List of directors / partners** (with DIN — Director Identification Number)
- **Filing history** — annual returns (MGT-7), financial statements (AOC-4), director changes (DIR-12)
- **Charges** — registered charges against the company's assets

### ROC (Registrar of Companies)
Each state has one or more ROCs under the jurisdiction of the Regional Director. Maharashtra has ROC Mumbai, ROC Pune; Karnataka has ROC Bangalore; Tamil Nadu has ROC Chennai; etc. ROCs handle incorporation, filings, and strike-off actions.

### GSTIN (GST Identification Number)
**Portal**: `gst.gov.in` → Services → Search Taxpayer → GSTIN

The GSTIN is a 15-character identifier: first 2 digits = state code, next 10 = PAN, 13th = entity serial within PAN, 14th = `Z`, 15th = check digit. Public search reveals: legal name, trade name, business constitution, date of registration, status (active/suspended/cancelled), centre jurisdiction, state jurisdiction. This is the second-most-used identifier for B2B verification in India (after PAN).

### PAN / TAN
- **PAN (Permanent Account Number)** — 10-char alphanumeric tax ID issued by Income Tax Department; required for any taxable entity. Format: 5 letters + 4 digits + 1 letter (e.g., `AABCU9603K`). The 4th character indicates entity type (C=Company, P=Person, H=HUF, F=Firm, A=AOP, T=Trust). PAN is private but widely used as identity proof.
- **TAN (Tax Deduction Account Number)** — 10-char alphanumeric for TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) compliance.

### Udyam (MSME Registration)
**Portal**: `udyamregistration.gov.in`
Udyam is the simplified MSME self-registration portal launched July 2020, replacing the older Udyog Aadhaar. Registration is free, paperless, and based on Aadhaar + PAN + business details. Udyam provides a registration number (`UDYAM-XX-00-0000000`) and classifies the enterprise as Micro (investment < ₹1 crore, turnover < ₹5 crore), Small (< ₹10 crore / < ₹50 crore), or Medium (< ₹50 crore / < ₹250 crore). The Udyam database is searchable publicly for verified MSMEs.

### Common Entity Types
- **Private Limited Company (Pvt Ltd)** — most common for VC-backed startups, ~1.5M active; min 2 shareholders, 2 directors
- **Public Limited Company (Ltd)** — listed or unlisted; ~10,000 active; min 7 shareholders
- **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** — ~250K active; preferred for professional services
- **One Person Company (OPC)** — single-shareholder Pvt Ltd
- **Section 8 Company** — non-profit
- **Partnership Firm** — registered under Partnership Act 1932; not on MCA
- **Sole Proprietorship** — informal; not on MCA; common for SME trade
- **Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)** — tax-status entity for family businesses

### South India Tech Belt
The Bengaluru–Chennai–Hyderabad triangle is India's deepest tech cluster, accounting for ~50% of national IT-services exports and the majority of SaaS unicorn births (Freshworks, Zoho, Chargebee, Postman, Hasura, BrowserStack). Bengaluru alone hosts 40+ active tech parks (Electronic City, Whitefield, Manyata Tech Park, Bagmane) and the highest concentration of VC-funded startups in India. Cultural norms are more formal and English-led than in the north; hierarchy within IT services majors (Infosys, Wipro, TCS) is well-defined and procurement-led.

### Mumbai–Gujarat Financial & Industrial Belt
Mumbai anchors the financial system (BSE, NSE, RBI, SEBI, all major banks' HQs); Gujarat adds the industrial base (refining, chemicals, pharma, textiles). Prospecting into this belt requires fluency in the Tata/Reliance/Adani ecosystem, the Bajaj/Mahindra group structures, and the family-office investment patterns that drive much of the SME M&A activity.

### Delhi-NCR Service Economy
Delhi-NCR is the centre of India's BPO/KPO industry, the India HQs of most Fortune 500 companies (Gurugram), the D2C and consumer-internet cluster (Zomato, Paytm, PolicyBazaar, MakeMyTrip, Yatra), and the gateway to government procurement. The proximity to the central government and the Lodhi Road/Central Secretariat ecosystem makes it indispensable for any B2B targeting public sector buyers.

## 5. Dominant Industries

### IT Services & IT-Enabled Services (IT/ITeS)
IT services are India's signature export industry — ~$250B in annual revenue, ~$190B in exports, ~5M direct employment. The "WIT" trio (Wipro, Infosys, TCS) plus HCL, Tech Mahindra, Cognizant India, and Capgemini India anchor the sector. Specialisations span application development, infrastructure management, BPM (business process management), engineering R&D, and digital transformation. **NASSCOM** (`nasscom.in`) is the trade body; its member directory is the canonical industry list. The IT services procurement model is gatekeeper-heavy (CIO office, vendor management office) and relationship-driven — first meetings almost always warm-introduced.

### Pharmaceutical & Life Sciences
India is the "pharmacy of the world" — third-largest by volume, 13th by value, supplying ~20% of global generic medicines and ~60% of global vaccine demand. Major players: Sun Pharma, Dr Reddy's, Cipla, Lupin, Aurobindo, Zydus Cadila, Glenmark, Biocon, Bharat Biotech, Serum Institute of India (world's largest vaccine maker by dose). Hub cities: Hyderabad (bulk drugs, vaccines), Ahmedabad (formulations), Mumbai (HQs), Visakhapatnam (API). The **Pharmexcil** (Pharmaceuticals Export Promotion Council) and **BDMA** (Bulk Drug Manufacturers Association) maintain member directories. The regulator is **CDSCO** under the Ministry of Health; **Indian Pharmacopoeia** sets quality standards.

### Automotive
India is the world's third-largest auto market (passenger vehicles + two-wheelers + commercial vehicles) and the largest two-wheeler market. Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors, Mahindra & Mahindra, Hyundai India, Bajaj Auto, Hero MotoCorp, TVS, Eicher (Royal Enfield), Ashok Leyland. Auto-component ecosystem (Bosch India, Motherson Sumi, Bharat Forge, Minda) is large, export-oriented, and concentrated in Pune, Chennai, Gurugram-Manesar, and Sanand (Gujarat). The **ACMA** (Automotive Component Manufacturers Association) directory is a core prospecting resource.

### FinTech
India's FinTech adoption rate (~87%) is the world's highest, anchored by **UPI** (Unified Payments Interface — ~12B monthly transactions, ~$2T monthly value) built by NPCI. Key players: Paytm, Razorpay, Pine Labs, PhonePe, Cred, BharatPe, Groww, Zerodha, PolicyBazaar, Slice. The regulator **RBI** (Reserve Bank of India) maintains the public list of authorised payment system operators; **SEBI** registers market intermediaries. Targeting FinTech requires understanding the PPI (prepaid instruments), Payment Aggregator, and Payment Gateway licence categories.

### Telecom
India has ~1.1B wireless subscribers and one of the world's lowest ARPUs (~₹150/month). The duopoly of Reliance Jio (~470M subs) and Bharti Airtel (~380M subs), with Vi (Vodafone Idea) as the challenger, defines the sector. The Department of Telecom (DoT) and TRAI are joint regulators; **COAI** (Cellular Operators Association of India) is the trade body. Telecom equipment manufacturing has accelerated under the PLI (Production-Linked Incentive) scheme — Jio Platforms, Tejas Networks, HFCL.

### E-Commerce & Consumer Internet
Flipkart (Walmart-owned), Amazon India, Meesho, Snapdeal, Nykaa, Myntra (Flipkart-owned), BigBasket (Tata-owned). The D2C wave (Mamaearth, Boat, Sugar Cosmetics, Wakefit, Lenskart) is concentrated in Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Gurugram. **IAMAI** (Internet and Mobile Association of India) publishes reports and member directories.

### Manufacturing & Textiles
India is a global manufacturing hub for textiles (Tirupur, Surat, Coimbatore), gems & jewellery (Surat diamonds), leather (Kanpur, Chennai), and increasingly electronics (Noida, Sri City, Hosur). The **Make in India** initiative and PLI schemes have driven manufacturing FDI; the **FICCI** and **CII** (Confederation of Indian Industry) maintain extensive member directories.

### Agriculture & Food Processing
India is the world's largest producer of milk, pulses, jute, and the second-largest of rice, wheat, sugar, fruit, vegetables. The **APEDA** (Agricultural & Processed Food Products Export Development Authority) registers exporters; **MPEDA** for marine products. Food processing is a high-growth segment, with government push via the Pradhan Mantri Kisan SAMPADA Yojana.

## 7. Cultural Norms for B2B

### Relationship-Driven, Hierarchy-Conscious
Indian B2B culture is **relationship-driven and hierarchy-conscious** in ways Westerners often underestimate. The Indian buyer evaluates the vendor as a person before evaluating the product as a solution; first impressions, family background (in informal contexts), alma mater (IIT/IIM is a strong tie), and caste/community networks (declining but present) all shape trust. Hierarchy matters: the senior person in the room is the decision-maker, even if a junior person is doing the talking; addressing the junior while ignoring the senior is a serious error. Decisions typically route through 3–5 stakeholders — initiator, evaluator, technical validator, budget holder, signer — and the cycle is slow by US standards (4–12 weeks for a mid-sized SaaS deal).

### Festival Calendar
The Indian festival calendar is the single biggest scheduling constraint on B2B prospecting. Major festivals that effectively shut down business for a week or more:
- **Diwali** (Oct/Nov, dates vary) — biggest festival; 5–7 day slowdown; gifting is expected for active accounts
- **Holi** (March) — 2–3 day slowdown
- **Eid al-Fitr & Eid al-Adha** — 2–3 day slowdown each, regional
- **Durga Puja / Navratri** (Sep/Oct) — major in East India; ~7 day slowdown in Kolkata
- **Pongal / Makar Sankranti** (Jan 14) — major in South India
- **Onam** (Aug/Sep) — major in Kerala
- **Christmas** (Dec 25) — national holiday; week-long slowdown in Christian-majority NE and Goa
- **Independence Day** (Aug 15), **Republic Day** (Jan 26), **Gandhi Jayanti** (Oct 2) — national holidays

Avoid important outreach in the week before/after Diwali (typically late October–early November). Plan gifting for active accounts at Diwali — sweets, dry fruits, branded merchandise are standard.

### Multi-Lingual & Regional Sensitivity
India has 22 scheduled languages and ~1,600 mother tongues. English is the lingua franca at the corporate and tech layer; Hindi dominates north and central India; the four South Indian states (Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala) are predominantly non-Hindi and have strong linguistic pride. Sending Hindi-language outreach to a Chennai-based prospect is a relationship-ending mistake. Match the regional language where the prospect is in SME trade — Tamil for SMEs in Chennai, Marathi for Pune SMEs, Gujarati for Ahmedabad SMEs. At the corporate/enterprise layer, English is universal and regional-language outreach is not expected.

### Time, Punctuality, and "IST"
"Indian Stretchable Time" is a real phenomenon — meetings often start 10–15 minutes late, especially in informal contexts; senior figures may arrive significantly later. However, foreign vendors are expected to be punctual; arriving late to a meeting with an Indian buyer signals disrespect. Schedule meetings in the late morning (10:30–12:00) or late afternoon (15:00–17:00); avoid the 13:00–14:30 lunch window. Tea (chai) is served at most meetings; accepting a cup is a small trust signal.

### Personalisation & Family
Indian prospects expect a higher degree of personal warmth than Western counterparts — inquiries about family (at the right rapport level), acknowledgement of festivals, and recognition of career milestones all build trust. However, do not pry into caste, religion, or spousal details; these are off-limits even at rapport. Use of "Sir" / "Madam" / "Ji" (Hindi honorific suffix) is appropriate with senior figures until invited to use first names.

## 8. Outreach Patterns — Channels, Scripts, Do's & Don'ts

### Channel Priority for India B2B
1. **WhatsApp** — dominant for SME suppliers; first-touch only after opt-in via WhatsApp Business API
2. **Phone (cold call)** — effective; IST business hours 10:00–18:00; mobile-first
3. **Email** — primary for enterprise/corporate layer; less effective for SME
4. **LinkedIn** — strong for IT services, FinTech, startup ecosystem; less for traditional manufacturing
5. **SMS** — DLT-compliant only; works for transactional/promotional SME outreach
6. **In-person events** — high-yield; Indian trade events have high decision-maker density
7. **Direct mail** — declining but memorable for tier-1 ABM

### DLT-Compliant SMS — The Mandatory Workflow
Any commercial SMS to Indian numbers must follow this workflow:
1. Register as **Principal Entity** on the DLT platform of at least one telco (Airtel, Jio, Vi, BSNL, MTNL)
2. Obtain a 6-character **Sender ID** (e.g., `LEADRC`)
3. Register every message **template** on DLT (with content + variables)
4. Scrub recipient list against customer preference registry
5. Send via a DLT-connected SMS gateway (MSG91, Karix, Kaleyra, Gupshup, ValueFirst)
6. Honor DND (Do Not Disturb) — fully-blocked users get zero promotional SMS

Promotional SMS uses 6-char numeric sender IDs and is restricted to 9:00–21:00 IST. Transactional SMS (order updates, OTPs) can use alphanumeric sender IDs and is unrestricted. Violations get the sender ID blocked.

### WhatsApp Business — Opt-In Required
WhatsApp Business API permits promotional messaging only to opted-in users. Opt-in must be collected through a first-party flow (form, checkbox, IVR), and the first 24 hours after a user-initiated message are the "session window" — free-form content allowed. After 24 hours, only pre-approved **template messages** can be sent. WhatsApp to non-opted-in numbers gets the business number banned quickly; Meta has zero tolerance for spam.

### Email Outreach Tone for India
Indian B2B email tone is **warmer and more formal than US B2B**:
- Use "Dear [First name]" or "Dear Mr/Ms [Surname]" — not "Hi [First name]" for senior figures
- Acknowledge the prospect's company and role explicitly in sentence 1
- Lead with social proof (an Indian peer reference) — Indian buyers are highly peer-influenced
- Be specific on outcome (₹ savings, % improvement, hours saved) — Indian buyers are price-sensitive
- Use British English spelling (organisation, optimise) — Indian English follows British norms
- Avoid US idioms ("circle back," "double-click on that") — they read as foreign

### Send Times (IST)
- **Best days**: Tuesday–Friday (Monday is recovery; Friday afternoon is dead)
- **Best email times**: 10:30–12:00 IST (pre-lunch), 14:30–16:30 IST (post-lunch revival)
- **Best call times**: 11:00–12:30 IST, 15:00–17:00 IST
- **Avoid**: Before 9:30, after 18:30 (family time), weekends, festivals

## 9. Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: DLT Non-Compliance
Sending promotional SMS without DLT registration is blocked at the telco level and attracts TRAI penalties. Foreign senders routing SMS via offshore gateways are also subject to DLT — TRAI has enforcement cooperation with international carriers. Always register as Principal Entity on at least one Indian telco DLT platform before any SMS campaign.

### Pitfall 2: WhatsApp Spam
Sending promotional WhatsApp to non-opted-in numbers gets business numbers banned. Meta's enforcement is automated and ruthless. Build opt-in through first-party forms, IVR, or QR codes before any WhatsApp outreach.

### Pitfall 3: DPDPA Consent Mismanagement
The DPDPA's consent standard is "free, specific, informed, and unambiguous" — pre-checked boxes and bundled consent do not qualify. Buying contact lists without proof of source consent is high-risk under DPDPA; the maximum penalty is ₹250 crore per instance. Build consent-first data pipelines.

### Pitfall 4: Multi-State Compliance
India's states have their own GST administrations, labour laws, and (in some cases) data protection rules (e.g., Karnataka's data centre policy, Telangana's tech rules). A "national" prospecting campaign must respect state-specific public holidays (Maharashtra Day on 1 May, Karnataka Rajyotsava on 1 Nov, etc.) and state-language preferences.

### Pitfall 5: Fragmented Data
Indian corporate data is fragmented across MCA21, GST, Udyam, DLT, RBI, SEBI, and dozens of state portals. No single source is comprehensive; commercial aggregators (Tofler, Zauba, Tracxn) stitch these together but have gaps. Always cross-verify a prospect across at least two sources before contracting.

### Pitfall 6: Treating India as One Market
A Chennai SME, a Mumbai family-run business, and a Bengaluru VC-backed startup are three different prospecting problems with different languages, decision-making styles, and tech adoption. Segment by region, language, and company stage — "India" is too coarse.

### Pitfall 7: Festival Blindness
Major Indian festivals (Diwali, Holi, Durga Puja, Eid) shut down B2B activity for days at a time. Planning a campaign launch during Diwali week or scheduling a critical call during Durga Puja is a planning failure. Maintain a festival calendar alongside the national holiday calendar.

### Pitfall 8: Over-Reliance on Cold Email
Cold email works at the corporate/tech layer but is increasingly crowded and frequently spam-filtered by Indian enterprise IT (especially the Tata, Reliance, Infosys, Wipro mail systems). SME trade is on WhatsApp and phone; cold email to a Coimbatore textiles manufacturer is largely wasted. Match channel to segment.

### Pitfall 9: Hindi Default in Outreach
Defaulting to Hindi for "Indian" outreach is a common error that damages response rates in South India, Northeast India, and parts of West Bengal. English is the safest default at the corporate layer; regional language at the SME layer requires explicit regional targeting.
