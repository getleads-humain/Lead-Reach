# Customer Success Methodology — Health Scoring, Retention & Expansion

## 1. Executive Summary

Customer success is the post-sale discipline of ensuring customers achieve their desired outcomes while using your product, thereby driving retention, expansion, and advocacy. In B2B SaaS, where 70-80% of revenue comes from existing customers, customer success is not a support function — it is a revenue function.

The economics are stark: acquiring a new customer costs 5-7x more than retaining an existing one. A 5% increase in retention increases profitability by 25-95% (Bain & Company). Net Revenue Retention (NRR) above 110% means the company grows even without new customers. Customer success is the engine that drives these economics.

This methodology defines LeadReach's approach to customer success: how health is measured, how churn is predicted and prevented, how expansion is identified and executed, and how the LeadReach agent ecosystem provides the intelligence and automation that makes customer success proactive rather than reactive.

The core principle: **customer success begins before the contract is signed**. The handoff from sales to CS, the expectations set during the sales process, and the success criteria defined at close determine whether the customer will achieve value. Customer success that starts at onboarding is already behind.

## 2. Core Concepts and Definitions

### Customer Success
The organizational function responsible for ensuring customers achieve their desired outcomes through their interactions with the company. Customer success owns retention, expansion, and advocacy for the installed base.

### Net Revenue Retention (NRR)
The percentage of recurring revenue retained from existing customers over a period, including expansion, contraction, and churn. Formula: `(Starting ARR + Expansion ARR - Contraction ARR - Churn ARR) / Starting ARR`. NRR >100% means the customer base is growing organically.

### Health Score
A composite metric (0-100) that predicts the likelihood of a customer renewing and expanding. Health scores combine product usage data, engagement signals, support interactions, and relationship quality indicators.

### Churn
The loss of a customer (logo churn) or the loss of revenue from an existing customer (revenue churn). Churn is the negative outcome that customer success exists to prevent.

### Expansion
Revenue growth from existing customers through upsell (more of the same product), cross-sell (additional products), or seat expansion (more users). Expansion is the positive outcome that customer success drives.

### Time-to-Value (TTV)
The elapsed time from contract signature to the customer achieving their first measurable business outcome. TTV is the single most important predictor of retention — customers who achieve value quickly renew at significantly higher rates.

### Customer Lifecycle Stage
The stage of the customer's relationship with the company: Onboarding → Adoption → Value Realization → Growth → Renewal → Advocacy.

### Success Plan
A documented agreement between the CS team and the customer defining goals, success criteria, milestones, and the path to achieving them. Success plans are living documents that evolve as the customer's needs change.

### QBR (Quarterly Business Review)
A structured meeting between the CS team and customer stakeholders to review progress against the success plan, demonstrate ROI, and identify expansion opportunities.

### Risk Signal
Any indicator that a customer may churn, contract, or fail to achieve value. Risk signals include declining product usage, champion departure, support escalation patterns, and executive disengagement.

### Expansion Signal
Any indicator that a customer may be ready to buy more — increased usage approaching limits, new department inquiries, positive feedback, or budget cycle timing.

## 3. Methodology Overview — The Customer Lifecycle

Customer success follows a structured lifecycle with specific objectives, actions, and metrics at each stage:

```
ONBOARD → ADOPT → REALIZE → GROW → RENEW → ADVOCATE
```

### Stage 1: Onboard (0-30 days)
**Objective**: Get the customer live and using the product for their first use case.
**Key actions**: Kickoff call, implementation planning, technical setup, initial training, first-value milestone.
**Key metrics**: Time to first login, time to first value milestone, onboarding completion rate.

### Stage 2: Adopt (30-90 days)
**Objective**: Drive broad adoption across the customer's team; establish regular usage patterns.
**Key actions**: Usage monitoring, training reinforcement, feature discovery, stakeholder expansion, workflow optimization.
**Key metrics**: DAU/MAU ratio, feature adoption breadth, training completion, number of active users vs. contracted seats.

### Stage 3: Value Realization (90-180 days)
**Objective**: The customer achieves the business outcomes they purchased for; ROI is demonstrated.
**Key actions**: ROI measurement, success plan milestone tracking, executive engagement, business review preparation.
**Key metrics**: Success plan milestone completion, customer-stated ROI, NPS/CSAT scores, executive engagement level.

### Stage 4: Growth (180-365 days)
**Objective**: Identify and execute expansion opportunities — more seats, new use cases, additional products.
**Key actions**: Usage analysis for expansion signals, cross-sell/upsell recommendations, executive relationship building, multi-department engagement.
**Key metrics**: Expansion pipeline created, expansion revenue closed, seat utilization rate, multi-department adoption.

### Stage 5: Renewal (90 days before renewal)
**Objective**: Secure renewal at or above current contract value; address any risk factors.
**Key actions**: Renewal preparation, executive engagement, ROI documentation, risk mitigation, negotiation support.
**Key metrics**: Renewal rate, gross revenue retention, renewal cycle time, early renewal rate.

### Stage 6: Advocate (ongoing)
**Objective**: Convert satisfied customers into advocates who provide references, case studies, and referrals.
**Key actions**: Reference program, case study development, referral program, community engagement, product feedback.
**Key metrics**: Reference participation rate, case studies produced, referrals generated, community activity.

## 4. Detailed Process Steps

### Step 1: Sales-to-CS Handoff

The handoff is the most critical moment in the customer lifecycle. A bad handoff creates a bad customer experience and sets the stage for churn.

**1.1 Pre-close preparation**
Before the deal closes, the AE documents:
- Customer's business goals and expected outcomes
- Success criteria (how the customer will measure success)
- Key stakeholders and their roles
- Competitive alternatives considered and why they chose us
- Any commitments made during the sales process
- Risks and concerns raised during evaluation

**1.2 Handoff meeting (within 48 hours of close)**
The AE, CSM, and (ideally) the customer participate in a structured handoff:
- AE introduces the CSM to the customer
- AE presents the account context to the CSM
- CSM confirms understanding and proposes initial success plan
- Customer validates goals, timeline, and success criteria
- Next steps and kickoff date are agreed

**1.3 Handoff quality measurement**
- CSM rates the handoff quality (1-5) based on completeness of information
- Customer confirms they feel well-supported in the transition
- Time from close to kickoff meeting (target: <5 business days)

### Step 3: Implement Health Scoring

Health scoring provides an early warning system for churn risk and expansion opportunity:

**3.1 Health score components**
```typescript
interface HealthScoreInputs {
  // Product usage (0-30 points)
  daumaRatio: number;            // Daily/Monthly active users ratio
  featureAdoptionBreadth: number; // % of key features used
  usageTrend: 'growing' | 'stable' | 'declining';
  seatUtilization: number;       // Active users / Contracted seats

  // Engagement (0-25 points)
  meetingCadence: number;        // Meetings with CSM in last 90 days
  executiveEngagement: boolean;  // Executive sponsor engaged in last 90 days
  qbrAttendance: number;        // Stakeholder attendance at last QBR
  supportTicketSentiment: number; // CSAT on support interactions

  // Relationship (0-25 points)
  championStatus: 'active' | 'at-risk' | 'departed';
  multiThreaded: boolean;        // 3+ stakeholders engaged
  npsScore: number | null;       // Latest NPS response
  csmRelationshipQuality: number; // CSM's assessment (1-5)

  // Business (0-20 points)
  roiDocumented: boolean;        // Has the customer documented ROI?
  successPlanOnTrack: boolean;   // Are milestones being met?
  contractValue: 'growing' | 'stable' | 'at-risk';
  renewalTimelineRisk: 'none' | 'moderate' | 'high';
}

function calculateHealthScore(inputs: HealthScoreInputs): number {
  let score = 0;

  // Product usage
  score += Math.min(inputs.daumaRatio * 30, 10);
  score += inputs.featureAdoptionBreadth * 10;
  if (inputs.usageTrend === 'growing') score += 5;
  else if (inputs.usageTrend === 'declining') score -= 5;
  score += Math.min(inputs.seatUtilization * 5, 5);

  // Engagement
  score += Math.min(inputs.meetingCadence * 5, 10);
  if (inputs.executiveEngagement) score += 5;
  score += inputs.qbrAttendance * 3;
  score += inputs.supportTicketSentiment * 2;

  // Relationship
  if (inputs.championStatus === 'active') score += 10;
  else if (inputs.championStatus === 'departed') score -= 15;
  if (inputs.multiThreaded) score += 5;
  if (inputs.npsScore && inputs.npsScore >= 8) score += 5;
  else if (inputs.npsScore && inputs.npsScore <= 6) score -= 5;
  score += inputs.csmRelationshipQuality * 2;

  // Business
  if (inputs.roiDocumented) score += 8;
  if (inputs.successPlanOnTrack) score += 7;
  if (inputs.contractValue === 'growing') score += 5;
  else if (inputs.contractValue === 'at-risk') score -= 5;
  if (inputs.renewalTimelineRisk === 'high') score -= 5;

  return Math.max(0, Math.min(100, score));
}
```

**3.2 Health score bands**
- **80-100 (Healthy)**: Customer is on track, engaged, and likely to renew and expand
- **60-79 (Monitor)**: Customer is stable but showing early warning signs; increase touchpoints
- **40-59 (At Risk)**: Customer is disengaging or struggling; intervention required immediately
- **0-39 (Critical)**: Customer is at imminent churn risk; executive escalation required

**3.3 Health score governance**
- Calculated weekly for all accounts
- Reviewed by CS management weekly for At Risk and Critical accounts
- Triggers automated actions: Critical → executive outreach, At Risk → CSM intervention plan
- Trend analysis: declining health over 3+ weeks triggers escalation regardless of absolute score

### Step 4: Execute the QBR Process

QBRs are the cadence that keeps the customer relationship on track:

**4.1 QBR preparation (1 week before)**
- CSM pulls health score and trend analysis (Sage)
- CSM documents progress against success plan milestones
- CSM prepares ROI data and usage analytics
- CSM identifies expansion opportunities and risks
- Bard generates the QBR presentation draft

**4.2 QBR execution (45-60 minutes)**
1. **Review** (10 min): Progress against success plan milestones
2. **Results** (15 min): ROI demonstrated, usage analytics, value delivered
3. **Challenges** (10 min): Open issues, support tickets, adoption gaps
4. **Roadmap** (10 min): Upcoming product features relevant to customer goals
5. **Growth** (10 min): Expansion opportunities, new use cases, additional stakeholders
6. **Action items** (5 min): Commitments from both sides with deadlines

**4.3 QBR follow-up (within 24 hours)**
- Send meeting notes and action items
- Update success plan with new milestones
- Log expansion opportunities in CRM
- Escalate any critical risks to CS leadership

### Step 5: Churn Prevention

Churn prevention is a systematic process, not a last-minute rescue attempt:

**5.1 Early warning signals (detect 90+ days before renewal)**
- Product usage declining for 3+ consecutive weeks
- Champion departure or role change
- Executive sponsor disengagement (no meeting in 90+ days)
- Support ticket volume spike or CSAT decline
- Contracted seats underutilized (<50% seat utilization)
- Competitor evaluation signals (intent data, direct mentions)
- Budget or leadership changes at the customer

**5.2 Intervention protocol**
| Signal | Owner | Action | Timeline |
|--------|-------|--------|----------|
| Usage decline | CSM | Usage analysis, training offer, feature recommendation | Within 3 days |
| Champion departure | CSM + AE | Identify new champion, executive outreach | Within 5 days |
| Executive disengagement | CS Manager | Executive-to-executive outreach | Within 1 week |
| Support escalation | CSM + Support | Root cause analysis, remediation plan | Within 2 days |
| Competitor evaluation | CSM + AE | Competitive displacement play, ROI reinforcement | Within 3 days |

**5.3 Save playbook**
When a customer provides notice of non-renewal or expresses intent to churn:
1. **Acknowledge and understand**: Don't argue — listen to the reasons
2. **Quantify the loss**: What value will they lose by leaving?
3. **Address the root cause**: Is it product, service, price, or relationship?
4. **Propose a path forward**: Specific, actionable remediation plan with timeline
5. **Escalate appropriately**: If the CSM can't resolve, involve CS leadership or the CRO
6. **Document everything**: Win or lose, the lessons feed back into the system

### Step 6: Expansion Execution

Expansion is the growth engine of customer success:

**6.1 Expansion signal detection**
- Seat utilization >85% (room to grow)
- New department inquiring about the product
- Feature requests that map to premium tiers
- Customer achieving success milestones (they're ready for more)
- Positive NPS or CSAT signals
- Budget cycle timing (customer has money to spend)

**6.2 Expansion motion types**
| Motion | Trigger | Owner | Process |
|--------|---------|-------|---------|
| Seat expansion | Utilization >85% | CSM → AE | Usage review, proposal for additional seats |
| Upsell | Feature gap identified | CSM → AE | Feature demo, ROI for premium tier |
| Cross-sell | New department interest | CSM → AE | Multi-department intro, separate evaluation |
| Contract extension | Strong health + upcoming renewal | CSM | Multi-year proposal with discount |

**6.3 Expansion playbook**
1. Identify the expansion signal (Echo alerts, usage analysis)
2. Validate with the customer (is there genuine need and budget?)
3. Introduce the AE for commercial conversation (CSM stays involved)
4. Support the evaluation (CSM provides proof points, usage data, success stories)
5. Close the expansion deal (AE negotiates, CSM ensures continuity)

## 7. Best Practices

### Practice 1: Start Customer Success Before the Deal Closes
The best time to ensure customer success is during the sales process. AEs who set realistic expectations, define clear success criteria, and document the customer's goals create the conditions for retention. Overselling to close the deal creates churn 12 months later.

### Practice 2: Measure and Reduce Time-to-Value
TTV is the strongest predictor of retention. Customers who achieve their first value milestone within 30 days renew at 2x the rate of those who take 90+ days. Optimize onboarding ruthlessly to get customers to value as fast as possible.

### Practice 3: Multi-Thread the Customer Relationship
Just as in sales, single-threaded customer relationships are fragile. When the champion leaves, the CSM loses their primary contact. By building relationships with 3+ stakeholders from day one, the relationship survives personnel changes.

### Practice 4: Be Proactive, Not Reactive
Don't wait for the customer to call with a problem. Monitor health scores weekly, detect risk signals early, and intervene before the customer decides to leave. The cost of saving an at-risk customer is 1/10th the cost of replacing a churned one.

### Practice 5: Document and Prove ROI Continuously
Customers renew when they can demonstrate value to their organization. Don't wait until renewal time to calculate ROI — prove it at every QBR, in every success plan milestone, and in every executive conversation. ROI that isn't documented doesn't exist.

### Practice 6: Separate CS from Support
Customer success (strategic, proactive, growth-oriented) is fundamentally different from customer support (tactical, reactive, issue-resolution). Combining them creates a support function that can't focus on strategy. Counter: Separate teams, separate metrics, separate career paths.

### Practice 7: Use Health Scores as a Starting Point, Not a Conclusion
A health score of 75 doesn't tell you what to do — it tells you where to look. Always validate health scores with direct customer conversations. A customer with a 90 health score who just lost their champion may be at higher risk than the score suggests.

### Practice 8: Create a Customer Advisory Board
Engage your best customers in product development through a formal advisory board. This deepens relationships, provides product feedback, and creates advocates who feel invested in your success. Advisory board members renew at near-100% rates.

## 8. Common Mistakes and Anti-Patterns

### Anti-Pattern 1: Reactive Customer Success
CSMs who only engage when the customer reaches out or when the renewal is approaching. This is customer support, not customer success. Counter: Establish a proactive engagement cadence (weekly check-ins during onboarding, bi-weekly during adoption, monthly during growth, quarterly QBRs).

### Anti-Pattern 2: Health Score Theater
Building elaborate health scores that nobody acts on. Health scores that trigger no automated actions, no escalation, and no intervention are dashboards, not decision tools. Counter: Every health score band must have an associated action protocol.

### Anti-Pattern 3: Success Plans as Shelfware
Creating success plans at kickoff and never updating them. Success plans that aren't reviewed at every QBR and updated with new milestones are useless documents. Counter: Success plans are living documents. Require CSMs to update them weekly and review them at every QBR.

### Anti-Pattern 4: Over-Focusing on At-Risk Accounts
Spending 80% of CS time on the bottom 20% of accounts (at-risk) while neglecting the top 20% (high-health, high-expansion potential). This optimizes for retention at the expense of growth. Counter: Allocate time proportionally — 40% at-risk intervention, 40% healthy account growth, 20% operational.

### Anti-Pattern 5: Ignoring Product Usage Data
Making health assessments based on relationship quality alone ("They seem happy!") without looking at actual product usage data. Counter: Product usage is the most objective health indicator. Always cross-reference subjective assessments with usage analytics.

### Anti-Pattern 6: Renewal Surprises
Discovering at renewal time that the customer is considering leaving. If churn risk is a surprise at renewal, the health scoring and engagement cadence have failed. Counter: Every renewal should have a 90-day, 60-day, and 30-day checkpoint. At-risk accounts should be identified 180+ days before renewal.

### Anti-Pattern 7: No Expansion Motion
CS that focuses exclusively on retention without driving expansion. This leaves growth on the table and fails to deepen the customer relationship (expansion correlates with retention). Counter: Train CSMs to identify and qualify expansion opportunities; create a clear handoff process from CSM to AE for commercial conversations.

## 10. Integration with Other Methodologies

### Pipeline Management
Pipeline management and customer success share the CRM as the source of truth. Closed-won opportunities in the sales pipeline become customer records in the CS system. RevOps ensures the data flows correctly between these views.

### Deal Velocity Optimization
Faster deal velocity sometimes leads to poorly qualified customers who churn quickly. CS feedback on customer quality should inform ICP refinement and velocity optimization targets. Velocity without quality is a churn factory.

### Account-Based Marketing
ABM doesn't end at closed-won. Existing customers should be targeted with expansion campaigns (cross-sell, upsell, advocacy). ABM for expansion uses the same account intelligence and personalization principles as new-logo ABM.

### Sales Enablement
Sales enablement must include CS-specific content: success stories, ROI models, reference programs, and handoff templates. The sales team needs to understand what makes a successful customer so they can sell to the right prospects.

### Revenue Operations
RevOps provides the data infrastructure, metric definitions, and cross-functional alignment that CS depends on. NRR, GRR, and expansion revenue are RevOps metrics that CS drives. RevOps ensures the systems, data, and processes are in place for CS to operate effectively.

### Competitive Intelligence
Competitive intelligence informs both retention (understanding why customers evaluate competitors) and expansion (positioning upgrades against competitor weaknesses). CS should be briefed on competitive threats and armed with displacement-proof messaging.

### Data Enrichment
Forge's enrichment capabilities keep customer records current — especially organizational changes, new stakeholders, and technographic shifts. Stale customer data leads to missed expansion opportunities and failed risk detection.

## 11. Case Study Examples

### Case Study 1: SaaS — Time-to-Value Optimization
A project management SaaS company had an average TTV of 87 days and a first-year churn rate of 22%. Diagnosis: Onboarding was ad hoc — each CSM had a different approach, and customers often got stuck on configuration. Intervention: Standardized 14-day onboarding playbook with daily milestones; automated setup wizard for common configurations; mandatory value milestone check at Day 14. Results: TTV decreased to 34 days; first-year churn dropped to 11%; NRR improved from 102% to 116%.

### Case Study 2: Cybersecurity — Champion Departure Recovery
A cybersecurity company's customer (ACV: $280K) lost their CISO champion 60 days before renewal. The health score dropped from 82 to 51. Intervention: Echo detected the LinkedIn job change within 48 hours; CSM immediately engaged the VP of Security (known contact) and the new CISO (introduced by Scout); AE provided competitive context; QBR was accelerated to demonstrate ROI to the new CISO. Results: Renewal closed on time at 105% of current contract (small expansion included).

### Case Study 3: Data Platform — Health Score-Driven Expansion
A data analytics company analyzed health scores against expansion outcomes and discovered that customers with health scores >80 had a 4.2x higher expansion rate than those with scores 60-79. Intervention: CSMs were trained to focus expansion conversations on high-health accounts; expansion pipeline from existing customers increased 65% in one quarter. The insight: healthy customers want to do more with you — they just need to be asked.

### Case Study 4: MarTech — Save Playbook
A marketing technology company with 15% annual churn implemented a systematic save playbook. When a customer gave notice, the protocol was: (1) CSM acknowledges and listens, (2) root cause analysis within 48 hours, (3) remediation plan presented within 5 business days, (4) executive engagement if the issue is strategic. Results: 30% of notices-to-churn were converted to renewals, reducing net churn from 15% to 10.5%. Each saved customer represented $45K average ACV.

## 13. References and Further Reading

1. **"Customer Success"** by Nick Mehta, Dan Steinman, and Lincoln Murphy — The definitive guide to customer success strategy, operations, and metrics
2. **"The Customer Success Economy"** by Nick Mehta — How customer success drives the subscription economy and SaaS business models
3. **"The Effortless Experience"** by Matthew Dixon — Research on what actually drives customer loyalty (hint: it's not delight, it's reducing effort)
4. **"Competing on Customer Experience"** by John Gladow — Framework for designing and measuring customer experience across the lifecycle
5. **"Measured"** by John Warrillow — How to build a business that can run without you, including customer success systems
6. **Gainsight Pulse Benchmark Reports** — Annual data on NRR, health scoring, and customer success operational benchmarks
7. **"The Challenger Customer"** by Dixon and Adamson — Multi-stakeholder engagement strategies that apply to customer success as well as sales
8. **Bain & Company NPS Research** — The foundational research on Net Promoter Score and its correlation with growth
9. **"Subscription Economy"** by Tien Tzuo — How subscription business models make customer success a revenue function, not a cost center
10. **"Monetizing Innovation"** by Madhavan Ramanujam and Georg Tacke — How pricing and packaging decisions impact customer success and expansion revenue
