# Territory Planning Guide

Territory planning is the strategic allocation of accounts and geographic regions to sales representatives to maximize coverage and minimize overlap. Effective territory planning balances four factors: market potential (total addressable accounts by segment), rep capacity (number of accounts a rep can effectively manage), historical performance (which territories have produced the most revenue), and equity (fair distribution of opportunity across the team). Modern territory planning uses data-driven scoring models that assign each account a potential score based on firmographics (company size, industry, technology stack), intent signals (buying activity), and relationship signals (existing engagement, referrals). The most common territory models include: geographic territories (simple but may create unequal opportunity), named-account territories (best for enterprise with concentrated accounts), industry-vertical territories (enables deep domain expertise), and hybrid models (combining elements based on go-to-market strategy). Territory planning should be reviewed quarterly and rebalanced annually, as market conditions, rep performance, and company strategy evolve.

## Why Territory Planning Matters

Territory planning is one of the highest-leverage activities in sales operations. A well-designed territory plan directly impacts quota attainment distribution, rep retention, customer coverage, and ultimately revenue predictability. Research from the Sales Management Association shows that organizations with formalized territory planning processes achieve 15-25% higher quota attainment rates compared to those with informal or ad-hoc allocation methods. The impact is compounding: territories that balance opportunity fairly reduce rep turnover (which costs 1.5-2x OTE to replace), improve customer experience (through consistent coverage), and increase pipeline generation (by ensuring no high-potential accounts fall through the cracks).

Poor territory planning, on the other hand, creates a cascade of dysfunction. Reps with low-potential territories become demoralized and disengage, leading to attrition. Reps with over-concentrated territories may hit quota but leave significant revenue on the table due to capacity constraints. Overlap between territories creates internal competition that damages team cohesion and confuses prospects. Whitespace territories — accounts that belong to no one — represent the single largest source of missed revenue in most organizations, typically accounting for 20-35% of total addressable market potential that goes completely uncovered.

The financial impact of territory misalignment is measurable. A typical enterprise B2B company with 50 AEs carrying $1M quotas and average territory potential variance of 40% will see a 10-15% revenue gap between optimally balanced and poorly balanced territories. This translates to $5-7.5M in unrealized revenue annually — not from rep skill gaps, but purely from structural allocation inefficiency. Territory planning is therefore not merely an administrative exercise; it is a strategic revenue lever that demands the same rigor as compensation design or forecast methodology.

## Territory Planning Fundamentals

### The Four Balancing Factors

Every territory plan must balance four interdependent factors. Understanding the tension between these factors is essential to creating territories that are both equitable and revenue-maximizing:

| Factor | Description | Measurement | Common Mistake |
|--------|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Market Potential** | Total revenue opportunity within the territory | TAM × win rate × average deal size | Overestimating potential based on company count alone |
| **Rep Capacity** | Number of accounts a rep can effectively work | Accounts × touch frequency × time per touch | Ignoring account complexity when setting capacity |
| **Historical Performance** | Past revenue production from the territory | Trailing 12-month revenue, pipeline generated | Assuming past = future without market change analysis |
| **Equity** | Fair distribution of opportunity across reps | Quota attainment standard deviation, potential-to-quota ratio | Confusing equal account count with equal opportunity |

Market potential must be measured in dollar terms, not account counts. A territory with 200 small accounts may have less potential than one with 20 enterprise accounts. The most reliable potential estimation uses a weighted scoring model:

**Potential Score = (Firmographic Score × 0.35) + (Intent Score × 0.25) + (Technographic Score × 0.20) + (Relationship Score × 0.15) + (Engagement Score × 0.05)**

Each component is scored on a 1-100 scale and weighted according to its predictive power for your specific market. The weights should be calibrated using historical win/loss data — if intent signals are highly predictive for your product, increase that weight; if technographics are less relevant, decrease it.

Rep capacity is often underestimated in its complexity. A rep handling 50 enterprise accounts with 90-day sales cycles has fundamentally different capacity than one handling 300 SMB accounts with 14-day cycles. Capacity modeling must account for: active pipeline management (number of deals in each stage), prospecting time (new logo development), customer management (expansion and retention), administrative overhead (CRM updates, forecasting, team meetings), and skill development (training, coaching). A practical capacity model assigns time budgets to each activity category and derives the maximum account load from available selling hours.

### Territory Design Principles

Effective territories share several design principles that should guide every planning decision:

1. **Exclusivity**: Every account belongs to exactly one territory and one rep. Dual ownership creates confusion, competition, and accountability gaps. If multiple reps need to engage an account (e.g., overlay specialists), the primary rep retains ownership and the overlay is managed through a collaboration model with clear credit assignment.

2. **Balance**: Territory potential should be within ±15% of the average. Greater variance creates systemic unfairness that no amount of individual effort can overcome. Balance is measured in potential dollars, not account count.

3. **Contiguity**: Geographic territories should be geographically connected. Fragmented territories increase travel time, reduce face-to-face meeting density, and make it harder for reps to develop regional expertise and referral networks.

4. **Strategic Alignment**: Territory design should reflect go-to-market strategy. If the company is pivoting to enterprise, territories should be restructured to concentrate enterprise accounts. If vertical expertise is a competitive advantage, territories should align to industry verticals.

5. **Stability**: Territories should change as infrequently as possible. Reps build relationships, domain knowledge, and pipeline over months and years. Frequent reassignment destroys this accumulated investment. Annual rebalancing with mid-year micro-adjustments is the recommended cadence.

6. **Scalability**: The territory model should accommodate growth. As the company adds reps, territories should split cleanly without wholesale restructuring. This requires planning for 1.5-2x headcount growth in the model design.

## Territory Models Compared

### Geographic Territory Model

The geographic model assigns reps based on physical location — states, regions, countries, or postal codes. This is the simplest model to implement and understand, making it the most common starting point for growing organizations.

**Advantages:**
- Simple to define and communicate
- Clear ownership boundaries with no overlap
- Enables regional expertise and local relationship building
- Facilitates in-person meeting efficiency
- Easy to administer and audit

**Disadvantages:**
- Creates unequal opportunity (Silicon Valley vs. rural Midwest)
- Doesn't account for vertical expertise requirements
- May concentrate or disperse key accounts suboptimally
- Doesn't align with remote-first selling models
- Requires frequent rebalancing as population and industry shift

**Best For:** Organizations with field-heavy sales models, localized products, or regulatory-bound markets. Also appropriate for early-stage companies that need a simple model to get started.

**Implementation Guide:**
1. Map all accounts to geographic regions using postal codes
2. Calculate total potential within each region
3. Combine or split regions to achieve ±15% potential balance
4. Assign reps based on existing relationships where possible
5. Create a "house" territory for unassigned accounts and inbound leads
6. Document territory boundaries in CRM with automated routing rules
7. Establish a quarterly review cadence to assess balance

### Named-Account Territory Model

The named-account model assigns specific accounts to individual reps, regardless of geography. This is the gold standard for enterprise sales where individual account relationships are the primary value driver.

**Advantages:**
- Maximum flexibility in account allocation
- Enables matching rep expertise to account needs
- Eliminates geographic disparity in opportunity
- Supports strategic account planning at the individual level
- Best model for account-based selling motions

**Disadvantages:**
- Complex to administer (requires ongoing account-level management)
- Can create perceived unfairness if allocation isn't transparent
- Doesn't handle inbound leads naturally (need routing rules)
- Requires robust account scoring to ensure fair potential distribution
- More susceptible to "cherry-picking" during assignment

**Best For:** Enterprise sales organizations with high-value accounts, long sales cycles, and account-based marketing programs. Most effective when average deal size exceeds $50K ACV.

**Implementation Guide:**
1. Score every account in the TAM using the potential scoring model
2. Rank accounts from highest to lowest potential
3. Use a "snake draft" allocation: Round 1, Rep A gets #1, Rep B gets #2, etc.; Round 2, reverse order
4. Verify each rep's total potential is within ±15% of average
5. Adjust individual account assignments to fine-tune balance
6. Create tiered coverage models: Tier 1 (quarterly touch), Tier 2 (semi-annual), Tier 3 (annual)
7. Document account assignments in CRM with ownership rules
8. Establish overlay models for specialized selling (technical, vertical)

### Industry-Vertical Territory Model

The vertical model assigns reps based on industry or vertical, enabling deep domain expertise. This model recognizes that selling to healthcare requires different knowledge, language, and relationships than selling to financial services.

**Advantages:**
- Develops deep domain expertise that accelerates trust
- Enables industry-specific value propositions and references
- Supports vertical-focused marketing and ABM campaigns
- Creates natural communities of practice among reps
- Aligns with customer buying behavior (industry-specific budgets, cycles)

**Disadvantages:**
- Can create coverage gaps in under-assigned verticals
- May concentrate accounts geographically (all fintech in NYC)
- Rep career development limited to single vertical
- Requires sufficient TAM within each vertical to support full-time reps
- Vertical definitions can be ambiguous (is "technology" one vertical or ten?)

**Best For:** Organizations selling into vertical-specific markets, products with strong industry differentiation, and companies where domain expertise is a competitive moat. Minimum 3-5 reps per vertical to justify the model.

**Implementation Guide:**
1. Define vertical taxonomy using NAICS or SIC codes as a base
2. Calculate TAM within each vertical
3. Consolidate small verticals and split large ones to create balanced groups
4. Assign reps to verticals based on existing expertise and interest
5. Create cross-vertical overlay for accounts that span industries
6. Develop vertical-specific playbooks, content, and reference stories
7. Build vertical-specific reporting in CRM and analytics tools

### Hybrid Territory Model

The hybrid model combines elements of the above models, typically using geography as the primary dimension with vertical overlays, or verticals as the primary dimension with geographic sub-territories. Most mature organizations end up with some form of hybrid model.

**Common Hybrid Configurations:**

| Primary Dimension | Secondary Dimension | Overlay | Example |
|-------------------|---------------------|---------|---------|
| Geography | Named accounts within region | Vertical specialists | East Region AE with Healthcare overlay |
| Vertical | Geographic sub-territories | Enterprise specialists | FinTech AE covering East Coast |
| Named accounts | Geographic proximity | Technical overlay | Enterprise AE with Solutions Engineer |
| Geography | Account tier within region | Channel partners | Mid-Market AE with partner co-sell |

**Implementation Guide:**
1. Determine primary dimension based on go-to-market strategy
2. Design primary territories using the appropriate model above
3. Identify where overlays or secondary dimensions add value
4. Define clear rules of engagement for overlay interactions
5. Create credit and compensation rules for multi-rep involvement
6. Build CRM routing logic that handles primary and overlay assignments
7. Document the complete model in a territory playbook
8. Train the team on rules of engagement and escalation paths

## Account Scoring & Potential Modeling

### Building the Account Scoring Model

The account scoring model is the engine that drives data-driven territory planning. Without accurate scoring, territory assignment devolves into political negotiation. The scoring model should be objective, transparent, and continuously refined based on actual performance data.

**Step 1: Define Scoring Dimensions**

Select 5-8 dimensions that predict buying propensity for your product. Common dimensions include:

| Dimension | Data Source | Scoring Method | Weight Range |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|--------------|
| Company Revenue | Data provider (ZoomInfo, Apollo) | Tiered bands | 20-30% |
| Employee Count | Data provider, LinkedIn | Tiered bands | 15-25% |
| Industry Vertical | NAICS/SIC codes | Match to ICP | 10-20% |
| Technology Stack | Technographics (BuiltWith, HG Insights) | Presence of key tech | 10-20% |
| Buying Intent | Intent data (6sense, Bombora) | Signal frequency & recency | 10-25% |
| Funding/Financial | Crunchbase, PitchBook | Recency and amount | 5-15% |
| Existing Relationship | CRM data | Engagement depth | 5-15% |
| Geographic Fit | Postal code mapping | Proximity, regulatory | 5-10% |

**Step 2: Calibrate Weights Using Historical Data**

Use your win/loss data from the past 12-24 months to determine which dimensions most strongly predict closed-won outcomes. Run a logistic regression or random forest model with closed-won as the dependent variable and dimension scores as predictors. The relative importance of each predictor informs weight allocation.

**Step 3: Validate Against Held-Out Data**

Hold back 20% of historical deals as a validation set. Score those accounts using the model and compare predicted probability to actual win rate. Adjust weights and scoring thresholds until the model achieves at least 70% accuracy on the validation set (i.e., accounts scored in the top quartile win at a rate 2x+ higher than bottom quartile accounts).

**Step 4: Implement in CRM and Analytics**

Deploy the scoring model as an automated field in your CRM. The score should update monthly based on refreshed data inputs. Create dashboards that show score distribution by territory, rep, and segment to support planning decisions.

### Potential Calculation Methods

**Method 1: Top-Down (Market Sizing)**

```
Territory Potential = (# of ICP-fit accounts) × (Average ACV) × (Historical Win Rate)
```

Example: A territory contains 45 ICP-fit accounts, average ACV is $85,000, and historical win rate for ICP accounts is 22%.
Potential = 45 × $85,000 × 0.22 = $841,500

**Method 2: Bottom-Up (Account-Level Scoring)**

```
Account Potential = (Potential Score / 100) × (Maximum Estimated Deal Value)
Territory Potential = Sum of Account Potentials across all accounts in territory
```

This method is more granular but requires individual account-level estimates of maximum deal value, which can be derived from similar-account analysis.

**Method 3: Spend-Based (Wallet Share)**

```
Territory Potential = Sum of (Category Spend × Addressable Percentage) for each account
```

This method uses third-party spend estimates (e.g., from D&B or industry reports) to estimate total addressable spending in your category for each account. Most accurate for mature categories with reliable spend data.

### Potential-to-Quota Ratio

The potential-to-quota ratio (P:Q) is the single most important metric in territory evaluation. It measures whether a territory has enough opportunity to support the assigned quota:

| P:Q Ratio | Interpretation | Action |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| < 1.5x | Under-resourced territory — rep cannot achieve quota even with perfect execution | Rebalance: add accounts or reduce quota |
| 1.5x - 2.0x | Minimum viable territory — achievable but requires high win rate | Monitor closely; consider marginal adjustments |
| 2.0x - 3.0x | Healthy territory — good balance of challenge and achievability | Target range for most territories |
| 3.0x - 4.0x | Over-resourced territory — rep may under-penetrate and still hit quota | Consider splitting or adding quota |
| > 4.0x | Severely over-resourced — significant revenue left on table | Split territory into two |

The target P:Q ratio varies by sales motion: transactional SMB sales may target 2.5-3.5x (higher volume needed), while enterprise sales may target 1.8-2.5x (fewer, larger deals). The key is consistency — all territories should fall within a narrow P:Q range to ensure equitable opportunity.

## Territory Capacity Modeling

### Understanding Rep Capacity

Rep capacity is not simply "how many accounts can a rep call on." It's a function of the total available selling hours, the time required per account engagement, and the distribution of those hours across different activities. Getting capacity wrong leads to either under-coverage (too many accounts per rep) or under-utilization (too few accounts per rep).

**Annual Selling Hours Calculation:**

| Component | Hours/Year | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Total working days | 260 | 52 weeks × 5 days |
| Less: PTO | -15 days | Average vacation + personal |
| Less: Holidays | -10 days | Company holidays |
| Less: Training/Enablement | -5 days | Quarterly training, onboarding |
| Less: Team Meetings | -5 days | Weekly team, QBRs |
| Less: Admin/CRM | -10 days | Forecasting, reporting, updates |
| Available selling days | 215 | |
| Selling hours per day | 5.5 | After internal meetings, email |
| **Total selling hours/year** | **1,183** | |

**Time Allocation by Activity:**

| Activity | % of Selling Hours | Hours/Year | Notes |
|----------|-------------------|-----------|-------|
| Prospecting (new logo) | 25-30% | 296-355 | Cold outreach, research, networking |
| Active deal management | 35-45% | 414-532 | Meetings, proposals, negotiations |
| Account management (expansion) | 15-20% | 177-237 | QBRs, upsell identification |
| Customer success collaboration | 5-10% | 59-118 | Handoffs, escalation support |
| Strategic account planning | 5-10% | 59-118 | Long-term account strategy |

### Account Load Calculations

**Enterprise Account Load (deal size > $100K, cycle > 6 months):**

- Active pipeline: 8-12 deals
- Prospecting pipeline: 15-25 opportunities
- Named accounts: 25-40 accounts
- Customer accounts (expansion): 10-20 accounts
- **Total account load: 50-80 accounts**

**Mid-Market Account Load (deal size $25-100K, cycle 3-6 months):**

- Active pipeline: 12-20 deals
- Prospecting pipeline: 30-50 opportunities
- Named accounts: 60-100 accounts
- Customer accounts (expansion): 15-30 accounts
- **Total account load: 100-150 accounts**

**SMB Account Load (deal size < $25K, cycle < 3 months):**

- Active pipeline: 20-35 deals
- Prospecting pipeline: 50-100 opportunities
- Named accounts: 150-300 accounts
- Customer accounts (expansion): 30-50 accounts
- **Total account load: 200-400 accounts**

These are guidelines, not rules. The actual capacity for any individual rep depends on their selling velocity (how quickly they move deals through stages), their efficiency (how much time per activity), and their skill level (tenured reps handle 20-30% more accounts than ramping reps).

### Capacity Planning Formula

```
Optimal Account Load = (Available Selling Hours) / (Avg Hours per Account per Year)

Where:
Avg Hours per Account per Year = (Prospecting Hours + Meeting Hours + Follow-up Hours + Admin Hours) per Account
```

For enterprise accounts: ~25-35 hours per account per year
For mid-market accounts: ~10-18 hours per account per year
For SMB accounts: ~3-8 hours per account per year

## Territory Assignment Process

### Annual Territory Planning Cycle

Territory planning follows a structured annual cycle with quarterly check-ins:

**Q4 (October-December): Annual Planning**
1. Collect market data: refreshed TAM, account scores, intent signals
2. Analyze prior year performance: attainment by territory, pipeline generation, win rates
3. Identify rebalancing needs: over/under-resourced territories, whitespace gaps
4. Design new territory structure: scoring, allocation, balance verification
5. Present proposed plan to sales leadership for review
6. Communicate changes to the team before the new year
7. Update CRM routing, assignments, and reporting

**Q1 (January-March): Implementation & Ramp**
1. Execute territory assignments in CRM
2. Brief reps on their new territories with account-level data
3. Begin account planning for top-tier accounts
4. Monitor early pipeline generation by territory
5. Make micro-adjustments for edge cases

**Q2-Q3 (April-September): Quarterly Review**
1. Review territory balance metrics each quarter
2. Identify emerging imbalances (rapid market growth, rep departures)
3. Make targeted adjustments (account swaps, not wholesale changes)
4. Document any changes and update CRM
5. Prepare data inputs for next annual planning cycle

### The Snake Draft Allocation Method

The snake draft is the most equitable method for allocating named accounts across a team. It works like a fantasy sports draft:

**Round 1 (Top Potential Accounts):**
- Rep 1 gets Account #1 (highest potential)
- Rep 2 gets Account #2
- Rep 3 gets Account #3
- ...continuing through all reps

**Round 2:**
- Last rep in Round 1 picks first (reverse order)
- Continues back up to Rep 1

**Subsequent Rounds:**
- Continue alternating direction each round

After the draft, calculate total potential per rep. If variance exceeds ±15%, make strategic account swaps to bring all territories into balance. Document the rationale for any post-draft adjustments to maintain transparency.

### Territory Assignment Decision Framework

| Situation | Assignment Approach | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------|
| New rep joining team | Assign under-performing territory + growth accounts | Fresh perspective on stalled territory; growth accounts for early wins |
| Rep departing | Temporary "house" assignment; reassign in next cycle | Avoid disruption; use interim coverage |
| Major account at risk | Assign to strongest relationship-builder | Retention priority overrides balance |
| New market segment | Assign to rep with relevant domain expertise | Expertise accelerates market entry |
| Acquired company accounts | Merge into existing territories by geography/vertical | Minimize disruption; maintain existing relationships |
| Strategic partnership accounts | Create overlay model; primary rep + partner lead | Dual coverage with clear roles |

## Whitespace Analysis

### What Is Whitespace?

Whitespace refers to accounts or segments that have no assigned coverage — they belong to no territory and receive no proactive outreach. Whitespace is the silent revenue killer in most organizations because nobody is responsible for it and nobody is measured on it.

**Types of Whitespace:**

| Type | Description | Size | Revenue Impact |
|------|-------------|------|----------------|
| Geographic Whitespace | Regions with no assigned rep | Common in early-stage companies | High — entire regions uncovered |
| Segment Whitespace | ICP segments not assigned to any territory | Occurs when ICP evolves | Medium-High — known buyers with no outreach |
| Account Whitespace | Individual accounts within territories not being worked | Very common — reps focus on top accounts | Medium — "long tail" of unworked accounts |
| Persona Whitespace | Buying personas not being engaged within accounts | Often overlooked | Low-Medium — incomplete engagement |
| Vertical Whitespace | Industry verticals without dedicated coverage | Depends on vertical strategy | Variable — can be very high in key verticals |

### Whitespace Identification Process

1. **Account Inventory**: Export all accounts from CRM and data provider. Tag each as assigned or unassigned.

2. **ICP Overlay**: Score every unassigned account against your ICP criteria. Rank by potential score.

3. **Coverage Analysis**: For assigned accounts, analyze activity levels. Accounts with no activity in 90+ days are effectively whitespace even if nominally assigned.

4. **Gap Quantification**: Calculate total potential in whitespace accounts. This is your uncovered revenue opportunity.

5. **Prioritization**: Rank whitespace opportunities by potential score and ease of coverage (can existing reps absorb them, or do you need new hires?).

6. **Action Plan**: Assign high-potential whitespace to existing territories (if capacity allows) or create a business case for new headcount to cover it.

### Whitespace Coverage Strategies

**Strategy 1: Territory Expansion** — Add whitespace accounts to existing territories that have capacity. Risk: may overload already-stretched reps. Mitigation: only add to territories with P:Q ratio > 3.0x.

**Strategy 2: New Territory Creation** — Create a new territory from whitespace accounts and hire a new rep. Risk: new territory may not have enough potential to support a full quota. Mitigation: combine whitespace with adjacent low-potential territories.

**Strategy 3: Overlay Coverage** — Assign whitespace to a specialized overlay rep (e.g., an SDR team or business development rep). Risk: overlay may not have full account ownership authority. Mitigation: define clear ownership and credit rules.

**Strategy 4: Digital-First Coverage** — Use automated outreach (LeadReach Bard sequences, marketing automation) for lower-potential whitespace that doesn't justify human coverage. Risk: lower conversion rates. Mitigation: graduate accounts to human coverage when engagement signals cross a threshold.

**Strategy 5: Channel/Partner Coverage** — Assign whitespace accounts to channel partners. Risk: less control over coverage quality. Mitigation: establish partner SLAs and co-sell processes.

## Territory Performance Metrics

### Core Territory Health KPIs

| Metric | Definition | Target | Calculation |
|--------|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **Potential-to-Quota Ratio** | Territory potential ÷ assigned quota | 2.0-3.0x | Total potential $ ÷ Quota $ |
| **Coverage Ratio** | # accounts with active engagement ÷ total assigned accounts | > 70% | Engaged accounts ÷ Total accounts |
| **Pipeline Generation** | $ of new pipeline created per quarter | ≥ 3x quota | Sum of new opportunities |
| **Pipeline Yield** | % of pipeline that converts to closed-won | 15-25% | Closed-won $ ÷ Pipeline $ |
| **Quota Attainment** | Actual revenue ÷ quota | 80-100%+ | Revenue $ ÷ Quota $ |
| **Whitespace %** | Uncovered potential ÷ total potential | < 15% | Uncovered $ ÷ Total $ |
| **Rep Turnover** | % of reps leaving within 12 months | < 20% | Departures ÷ Team size |
| **Balance Score** | Std deviation of P:Q ratios across team | < 0.3 | Statistical calculation |

### Territory Performance Scorecard

Each territory should be evaluated quarterly on a balanced scorecard that measures both inputs and outcomes:

**Pipeline Health (40% of Score):**
- Pipeline coverage ratio (target: ≥ 3x)
- New pipeline generated this quarter (target: ≥ 3x quota)
- Average deal age (target: within 20% of team average)
- Stage distribution (target: healthy progression across stages)

**Revenue Performance (30% of Score):**
- Quota attainment (target: ≥ 85%)
- Revenue vs. prior period (target: growth or maintenance)
- Average deal size (target: within 15% of team average)
- Win rate (target: ≥ team median)

**Activity & Coverage (20% of Score):**
- Accounts touched this quarter (target: ≥ 70% of assignment)
- Meetings held (target: ≥ team average)
- Response rates on outreach (target: ≥ team median)
- Next steps documented (target: 100% of active deals)

**Strategic (10% of Score):**
- Account plans completed for Tier 1 accounts
- Whitespace identified and escalated
- Cross-sell/upsell pipeline generated
- Customer satisfaction for managed accounts

## Territory Rebalancing

### When to Rebalance

Territory rebalancing should be a structured, data-driven process — not a reaction to political pressure. The triggers for rebalancing include:

**Structural Triggers (Require Rebalancing):**
- Annual planning cycle (mandatory review)
- Merger or acquisition (new accounts to integrate)
- Market expansion (new geography or vertical)
- Rep headcount change (> 10% growth or reduction)
- Product launch targeting new segment

**Performance Triggers (May Require Rebalancing):**
- Territory P:Q ratio drifts outside 1.5-4.0x range
- Rep consistently over/under-performs (3+ consecutive quarters)
- Whitespace exceeds 20% of total potential
- Customer churn concentrated in specific territories

**Situational Triggers (Case-by-Case):**
- Rep departure or role change
- Major account win/loss that shifts territory dynamics
- Competitive landscape change in specific vertical
- Regulatory change affecting specific geographies

### Rebalancing Principles

1. **Minimize Disruption**: Move the fewest accounts possible to achieve balance. Every account move breaks existing relationships and pipeline.

2. **Preserve Relationships**: Never move an account where the rep has an active, progressing opportunity without the rep's and customer's input.

3. **Maintain Transparency**: Document every rebalancing decision with data-backed rationale. Reps who understand why changes are made are more likely to accept them.

4. **Time It Right**: The best time for major rebalancing is the start of a new fiscal year or quarter. Mid-quarter changes create accounting and crediting complications.

5. **Communicate Early**: Give reps at least 30 days' notice before territory changes. Provide account-level data for their new territories so they can begin planning immediately.

6. **Provide Transition Support**: For significant changes, offer a 60-90 day transition period where the former rep introduces the new rep to key accounts and shares context.

### The Rebalancing Algorithm

A systematic approach to rebalancing:

1. **Score** every account in every territory using the current scoring model
2. **Calculate** current P:Q ratio for each territory
3. **Identify** territories outside the target P:Q range (1.5-3.0x)
4. **Rank** accounts in over-resourced territories by "moveability" (no active deals, weaker relationships, lower engagement)
5. **Reassign** moveable accounts from over-resourced to under-resourced territories
6. **Recalculate** P:Q ratios after each reassignment
7. **Iterate** until all territories are within the target range
8. **Validate** that no single rep lost more than 10% of their territory potential
9. **Document** all changes with rationale
10. **Communicate** changes to affected reps with 30-day notice

## Advanced Territory Strategies

### Pod-Based Territories

Pod-based territories assign a team of specialists (AE, SDR, SE, CSM) to a shared territory rather than assigning accounts to individual reps. This model maximizes coverage depth and ensures accounts receive multi-dimensional attention.

**Pod Composition:**
- 1 Account Executive (deal closure, relationship ownership)
- 1-2 SDRs (pipeline generation, qualification)
- 1 Solutions Engineer (technical validation, demos)
- 1 Customer Success Manager (post-sale expansion, retention)
- Optional: Marketing pod alignment (dedicated ABM resources)

**Advantages:**
- Holistic account coverage from multiple angles
- Reduces key-person risk (team continuity if one person leaves)
- Enables specialization within account engagement
- Better handoff experience for customers

**Disadvantages:**
- Higher cost per territory (4-5 people vs. 1)
- Coordination overhead requires strong leadership
- Credit and compensation more complex
- Requires larger team size to implement

### Dynamic Territory Assignment

Dynamic territory models use real-time data to assign accounts to the best-positioned rep at the moment of engagement, rather than maintaining static assignments. This is an emerging model enabled by AI and real-time intent data.

**How It Works:**
1. Intent signal detected (e.g., target account visits pricing page)
2. System identifies all reps with relevant expertise and capacity
3. Account is dynamically routed to the best-fit rep based on: vertical expertise, existing relationship, current pipeline capacity, geographic proximity, and activity level
4. Rep receives real-time notification with account context and recommended action
5. If rep doesn't engage within SLA (e.g., 24 hours), account is rerouted

**Advantages:**
- Maximizes response speed to buying signals
- Reduces whitespace (every signal gets attention)
- Matches rep expertise to account need
- Adapts in real-time to rep capacity changes

**Disadvantages:**
- Eliminates long-term relationship building
- Complex to implement and administer
- Reps may resist lack of "their" accounts
- Compensation and crediting challenging

### Territory Carve-Outs

Strategic carve-outs are specialized territories for specific account segments that require different selling motions:

**Common Carve-Outs:**
- **Strategic/Named Accounts**: Top 20-50 accounts with dedicated resources and custom engagement plans
- **Channel/Partner Accounts**: Accounts primarily served through partner ecosystem
- **Federal/Government**: Accounts requiring security clearances and compliance
- **Startup/Developer**: Accounts with high velocity, low-touch selling
- **Competitive Displacement**: Accounts currently using a competitor, requiring specialized plays

## LeadReach Agent Integration for Territory Planning

### Flow Agent: Territory Assignment Automation

The Flow agent automates the operational mechanics of territory management:

- **Account Routing**: Automatically routes new accounts and inbound leads to the correct territory owner based on routing rules (geography, vertical, account tier)
- **Whitespace Detection**: Continuously scans for accounts with no owner or no activity, flagging them for assignment or outreach
- **Balance Monitoring**: Tracks P:Q ratios in real-time and alerts RevOps when territories drift outside target ranges
- **Rebalancing Execution**: When territory changes are approved, Flow automatically updates CRM ownership, routing rules, and reporting structures
- **Transition Workflows**: Creates structured handoff tasks when accounts move between territories, ensuring context transfer

### Echo Agent: Territory Analytics & Insights

Echo provides the analytical intelligence that drives data-driven territory decisions:

- **Potential Scoring**: Continuously updates account potential scores based on the latest firmographic, intent, and engagement data
- **Performance Benchmarking**: Compares territory performance against historical baselines and peer territories, identifying outliers
- **Whitespace Analysis**: Generates regular whitespace reports quantifying uncovered opportunity by segment, geography, and vertical
- **Predictive Territory Modeling**: Simulates the revenue impact of proposed territory changes before they're implemented, enabling what-if analysis
- **Capacity Optimization**: Analyzes rep activity data to identify under-utilized and over-burdened territories, recommending rebalancing actions

### Judge Agent: Territory Plan Validation

Judge ensures territory plans are structurally sound and equitable:

- **Balance Auditing**: Verifies that all territories meet P:Q ratio targets and that no rep is unfairly disadvantaged
- **Compliance Checking**: Ensures territory assignments comply with documented rules (exclusivity, contiguity, capacity limits)
- **Scenario Validation**: When rebalancing is proposed, Judge validates that the new plan improves overall equity and doesn't create new imbalances
- **Historical Fairness**: Analyzes whether territory assignment patterns over time show any systematic bias (e.g., certain reps consistently getting better territories)
- **Change Impact Assessment**: Evaluates the pipeline and revenue risk of proposed territory changes, flagging high-risk moves

### Bard Agent: Territory Engagement Sequences

Bard designs and executes territory-specific engagement sequences:

- **Whitespace Outreach**: Automatically generates and executes outreach campaigns for uncovered accounts until they can be assigned to a human rep
- **New Territory Onboarding**: When a rep receives new territory assignments, Bard creates customized engagement sequences for the top accounts in their new territory, incorporating account intelligence and recommended talking points
- **Tiered Touch Models**: Implements different engagement cadences for Tier 1, 2, and 3 accounts within each territory, optimizing rep time allocation
- **Re-engagement Campaigns**: Identifies accounts that have gone cold within a territory and creates re-engagement sequences to revive them
- **Vertical-Specific Sequences**: For vertical territories, Bard deploys industry-specific messaging, case studies, and value propositions tailored to each vertical's unique pain points

## Implementation Checklist

### Pre-Planning (4-6 Weeks Before New Fiscal Year)

- [ ] Gather updated account data from all sources (CRM, data providers, intent platforms)
- [ ] Refresh account scoring model with latest win/loss data
- [ ] Calculate current territory P:Q ratios and balance scores
- [ ] Identify whitespace and coverage gaps
- [ ] Survey reps on territory challenges and suggestions
- [ ] Review competitive landscape changes by territory
- [ ] Confirm next year's headcount plan and quota model
- [ ] Prepare territory planning workshop materials

### Planning Workshop (2-3 Days)

- [ ] Present current state analysis (P:Q ratios, performance, whitespace)
- [ ] Discuss strategic priorities for next year (new segments, markets, products)
- [ ] Review territory model options and select approach
- [ ] Draft new territory structure with account assignments
- [ ] Validate balance and capacity for each territory
- [ ] Identify transition requirements (account moves, new hires, carve-outs)
- [ ] Document decisions and rationale

### Implementation (4-6 Weeks Before Go-Live)

- [ ] Update CRM territory fields and routing rules
- [ ] Configure LeadReach agents with new territory assignments
- [ ] Create account transition plans for moved accounts
- [ ] Brief each rep on their new territory with account-level data
- [ ] Update compensation plan if territory changes affect quota
- [ ] Update reporting dashboards for new territory structure
- [ ] Test routing rules with sample leads and accounts
- [ ] Communicate changes to cross-functional partners (marketing, CS, support)

### Post-Implementation (First 90 Days)

- [ ] Monitor pipeline generation by territory weekly
- [ ] Verify routing accuracy for inbound leads
- [ ] Conduct 30-day check-in with each rep on territory coverage
- [ ] Make micro-adjustments for edge cases and errors
- [ ] Document lessons learned for next planning cycle
- [ ] Run quarterly territory health review

## Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

| Pitfall | Impact | Prevention |
|---------|--------|------------|
| Political territory assignment | Unfair allocation, team friction | Use objective scoring model; snake draft allocation |
| Ignoring account complexity | Reps overloaded with complex accounts | Factor account complexity into capacity model |
| Over-indexing on geography | Unequal opportunity across regions | Use potential-based models with geographic overlay |
| Changing territories too frequently | Destroyed relationships, pipeline disruption | Annual rebalance with quarterly micro-adjustments only |
| No whitespace coverage | 20-35% of TAM uncovered | Mandate whitespace analysis in every planning cycle |
| Ignoring rep input | Missed local market knowledge | Incorporate rep surveys and one-on-one input sessions |
| Perfect balance obsession | Analysis paralysis, no plan implemented | Target ±15% variance; accept good enough over perfect |
| No transition planning | Account context lost in moves | 60-90 day structured handoff for all moved accounts |
| CRM routing misconfiguration | Leads routed to wrong rep | Test all routing rules before go-live with sample data |
| Compensation misalignment | Territory changes make quota unachievable | Recalculate quota when territory potential changes significantly |

### C. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **TAM** | Total Addressable Market — total potential revenue opportunity |
| **SAM** | Serviceable Addressable Market — TAM subset reachable by your GTM |
| **SOM** | Serviceable Obtainable Market — SAM subset realistically winnable |
| **P:Q Ratio** | Potential-to-Quota ratio — territory potential divided by assigned quota |
| **Whitespace** | Accounts or segments with no assigned coverage |
| **ICP** | Ideal Customer Profile — firmographic and behavioral criteria defining best-fit accounts |
| **Snake Draft** | Allocation method where picks alternate direction each round |
| **Carve-Out** | Specialized territory segment with different coverage model |
| **Overlay** | Additional coverage resource that works across territories |
| **House Territory** | Unassigned accounts managed by team lead or SDR pool |
