# Compensation Plan Design

Sales compensation design aligns rep incentives with company objectives through structured commission and bonus plans. The fundamental principle is that compensation drives behavior — reps optimize for how they're paid, not what leadership hopes they'll do. Effective compensation plans share these characteristics: 50-60% of total on-target earnings (OTE) comes from base salary, 40-50% from variable compensation; quota is set at a level where 60-65% of reps can achieve it; accelerators kick in above 100% attainment (e.g., 1.5x commission rate at 100-125%, 2x at 125-150%, 3x above 150%); and the plan is simple enough that reps can calculate their earnings for any given outcome without a spreadsheet. Common compensation structures include: straight commission (high risk, high reward — rare in B2B), base + commission (most common, 50/50 split), base + commission + bonus (adds MBO or quarterly bonus components), and tiered commission (increasing rates at higher attainment levels). Compensation plan changes should be made annually, not mid-year, to maintain rep trust.

## Why Compensation Design Is Critical

Compensation is the single most powerful lever that sales leadership has to shape rep behavior. Research from the Alexander Group shows that compensation plan design accounts for 30-40% of the variance in rep performance outcomes across organizations. A well-designed plan aligns individual financial incentives with company strategic objectives so powerfully that reps naturally gravitate toward the activities and outcomes the company most values, without requiring constant managerial oversight.

Conversely, a poorly designed plan creates perverse incentives that drive exactly the wrong behaviors. A plan that pays the same commission rate on all deals regardless of size incentivizes reps to close many small deals rather than pursuing larger, more strategic opportunities. A plan with a hard cap on earnings causes top performers to coast after hitting the cap, redirecting their effort to their next job search. A plan with complex multi-variable formulas that reps can't understand leads to confusion, distrust, and default optimization toward whatever the rep perceives as the easiest path to a paycheck.

The cost of compensation extends far beyond the direct payout. Total compensation expense for a B2B sales organization typically represents 25-35% of total revenue. Commission miscalculations — overpayments due to plan complexity, disputes requiring manual review, and corrections after the fact — add 3-7% on top of that. The administrative burden of managing a complex comp plan consumes 15-25% of a sales ops team's capacity. Getting the plan right is therefore not just a motivational issue; it's a material financial issue that directly impacts company economics.

### The Behavioral Economics of Compensation

Understanding how reps psychologically respond to compensation structures is essential for effective plan design:

**Loss Aversion**: Reps are roughly twice as motivated by the fear of losing income they already count on as they are by the prospect of earning additional income. This means threshold-based plans (earn nothing below 60% attainment) are more motivating than plans with linear earnings from zero, even if total expected payout is the same. However, thresholds also create discontinuities — reps at 58% attainment may engage in unethical behavior to close one more deal to cross the threshold.

**Diminishing Sensitivity**: The motivational impact of each additional dollar of commission decreases as total earnings increase. A $10K commission bump is highly motivating for a rep earning $80K OTE but barely noticed by one earning $300K OTE. This is why accelerator multipliers (which create increasing marginal returns) are more effective than flat-rate increases for driving overperformance.

**Reference Dependence**: Reps evaluate their compensation relative to a reference point — typically their OTE, their prior year earnings, or their peers' earnings. A plan that pays $120K at 100% attainment feels different depending on whether OTE is $100K (20% upside) or $150K (30% downside). This is why setting appropriate OTE and quota levels is as important as the commission structure itself.

**Social Comparison**: Reps care deeply about relative compensation, not just absolute earnings. A plan that allows top performers to earn 2-3x their OTE while bottom performers earn 50-60% of OTE is healthy. A plan where all reps cluster within 10% of OTE suggests insufficient performance differentiation and will fail to retain top talent.

## Compensation Plan Architecture

### Base-to-Variable Ratio

The ratio of base salary to variable compensation (the "split") is the foundational decision in compensation design. It determines the risk profile, the degree of performance differentiation, and the type of rep the plan attracts.

| Split (Base:Variable) | Plan Type | Risk Level | Attracts | Best For |
|-----------------------|-----------|------------|----------|----------|
| 80:20 | High-base | Low | Security-seeking, consultative reps | New products, long cycles, relationship selling |
| 70:30 | Moderate-high base | Low-Medium | Balanced sellers | Enterprise, complex sales, team selling |
| 60:40 | Balanced-lean | Medium | Performance-oriented | Mid-market, hybrid sales motions |
| 50:50 | Balanced | Medium-High | Competitive, results-driven | Mid-market, SMB, transactional |
| 40:60 | High-variable | High | Aggressive hunters | High-velocity, short-cycle, new logo focused |
| 20:80 | Commission-heavy | Very High | Entrepreneurs, risk-takers | Pure hunting roles, new market entry |

**Industry Benchmarks by Role:**

| Role | Typical OTE | Base:Variable | Notes |
|------|-----------|---------------|-------|
| SDR/BDR | $55-75K | 70:30 to 60:40 | Activity-based metrics (meetings, SQLs) |
| SMB AE | $80-120K | 50:50 | High velocity, shorter cycles |
| Mid-Market AE | $120-180K | 55:45 to 50:50 | Balanced hunting and closing |
| Enterprise AE | $180-300K | 60:40 to 55:45 | Complex, relationship-driven |
| Strategic/Named Account AE | $250-400K | 65:35 to 60:40 | Large accounts, expansion focus |
| Sales Engineer | $130-200K | 75:25 to 70:30 | Overlay, supporting role |
| Sales Manager | $150-250K | 65:35 to 60:40 | Team attainment-based |
| VP Sales | $250-400K | 60:40 to 50:50 | Company/region attainment |

### Quota Setting Methodology

Quota is the target revenue (or other primary metric) that a rep is expected to achieve to earn their full OTE. Setting quota correctly is the linchpin of the entire compensation plan — too high and most reps fail, too low and the company overpays for underperformance.

**Method 1: Top-Down (Affordability-Based)**

```
Company revenue target × (1 + growth rate) = Total revenue goal
Total revenue goal × (sales cost as % of revenue) = Total compensation budget
Total compensation budget ÷ (OTE × # of reps) = Required average attainment
Required average attainment × 100 = Quota per rep
```

Example: Company targets $50M revenue, sales cost budget is 25% ($12.5M), team of 50 reps at $250K OTE ($12.5M total comp). Required average attainment = 100%. Quota per rep = $50M / 50 = $1M.

**Method 2: Bottom-Up (Territory Potential-Based)**

```
Territory potential × Expected win rate × Expected penetration rate = Quota
```

Example: Territory has $5M potential, historical win rate is 20%, expected penetration is 12%. Quota = $5M × 0.20 × 0.12 = $120K... this seems too low. Adjusted: Account for multi-year building. Year 1 quota = 60% of full potential-based quota, ramping to 100% by Year 2.

**Method 3: Historical (Performance-Based)**

```
Prior year average attainment × (1 + growth factor) = New quota
```

Example: Average rep attainment was $820K last year. Company targets 15% growth. New quota = $820K × 1.15 = $943K.

**Method 4: Benchmark (Market-Based)**

```
Industry median quota for similar role and segment = Quota
```

Adjust for your company's specific factors: brand strength (stronger brand = higher achievable quota), product maturity (newer product = lower achievable quota), territory quality, and support resources.

### The 60-65% Rule

The most important quota-setting principle: **60-65% of reps should achieve quota** in a well-designed plan. This is the "golden zone" that balances company affordability with rep motivation:

| % at Quota | Consequence | Rep Impact | Company Impact |
|-----------|-------------|------------|----------------|
| < 40% | Quota too high / plan broken | Demoralization, high turnover | Underpay on comp, overpay on recruiting |
| 40-50% | Quota aggressive | Only top performers succeed | Short-term savings, long-term attrition |
| 50-60% | Quota challenging but fair | Good performers can hit | Balanced comp costs |
| **60-65%** | **Optimal zone** | **Right mix of achievability and stretch** | **Predictable comp costs, strong retention** |
| 65-75% | Quota too easy | Most reps coast after hitting | Overpayment risk, underperformance |
| > 75% | Quota much too easy | Low urgency, complacency | Significant comp over-spend |

## Commission Structure Models

### Model 1: Flat-Rate Commission

The simplest model: a fixed percentage of revenue (or fixed dollar amount per unit) regardless of attainment level.

**Formula**: Commission = Revenue × Commission Rate

**Example**: 8% commission on all closed revenue. Rep closes $1.2M, earns $96K in commission.

**When to Use**: Transactional sales, product-led growth motions, inside sales teams, and any scenario where deal size is relatively uniform and the goal is pure volume.

**Advantages**: Simple to understand and calculate; no threshold effects; every deal matters equally
**Disadvantages**: No incentive for overperformance; top performers capped economically; doesn't differentiate strategic deals

### Model 2: Tiered Commission with Accelerators

The most common model in B2B: commission rate increases as attainment rises above threshold levels, creating accelerating marginal returns that reward overperformance.

**Formula**: Different commission rates at different attainment bands

**Example Tiered Structure:**

| Attainment Band | Commission Rate | Example: $1M Quota |
|----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| 0-60% | 5% | $0-$600K revenue → $30K commission |
| 60-100% | 10% | $600K-$1M revenue → $40K commission |
| 100-125% | 15% | $1M-$1.25M revenue → $37.5K commission |
| 125-150% | 20% | $1.25M-$1.5M revenue → $50K commission |
| 150%+ | 25% | $1.5M+ revenue → unlimited upside |

At 100% attainment ($1M): Total commission = $30K + $40K = $70K
At 125% attainment ($1.25M): Total commission = $30K + $40K + $37.5K = $107.5K
At 150% attainment ($1.5M): Total commission = $30K + $40K + $37.5K + $50K = $157.5K

**Key Design Principles for Accelerators:**
- The "ramp rate" (rate of increase between tiers) should be at least 1.5x to create meaningful incentive
- Never cap earnings — top performers should have unlimited upside
- The total commission at 100% attainment should equal the variable component of OTE
- The lowest tier (0-60%) should pay enough that reps don't quit if they're having a bad quarter
- The top tier should be realistically achievable — at least 10-15% of reps should reach it each year

### Model 3: Multi-Metric Composite Plans

Plans that pay on multiple metrics simultaneously, weighting each according to strategic importance. Essential when reps must balance competing priorities (new business vs. expansion, revenue vs. margin, volume vs. strategic accounts).

**Example Composite Plan:**

| Component | Weight | Metric | Payout Rate |
|-----------|--------|--------|-------------|
| New Business Revenue | 50% | New logo ACV | 8% of ACV |
| Expansion Revenue | 25% | Existing account growth | 6% of incremental ACV |
| Deal Quality | 15% | Multi-year contracts | $2K bonus per multi-year deal |
| Strategic Objectives | 10% | Named account penetration | MBO-based, manager discretion |

**Composite Plan Calculation Example:**
- Rep closes $800K new business (at 80% of $1M new biz quota): 50% × 80% × $60K = $24K
- Rep drives $300K expansion (at 100% of $300K expansion quota): 25% × 100% × $30K = $7.5K
- Rep closes 8 multi-year deals: 15% × $2K × 8 = $2.4K
- Named account MBO: 10% × $12K = $1.2K
- Total variable: $35.1K (of $120K OTE variable)

**Warning**: Composite plans should have no more than 3-4 components. Beyond that, reps can't optimize their behavior effectively and default to focusing on whichever component is easiest to understand. Each component should represent at least 10% of variable comp.

### Model 4: SPIF and Bonus Overlay

Short-Term Incentive Funds (SPIFs) and bonuses are temporary overlays on the base comp plan, designed to drive specific behaviors for a limited time.

**Common SPIF Types:**

| SPIF Type | Duration | Purpose | Example |
|-----------|----------|---------|---------|
| New Product Launch | 90 days | Accelerate adoption of new offering | 2x commission on new product deals for first quarter |
| Competitive Displacement | 60 days | Win deals from specific competitor | $5K bonus per competitive win vs. Competitor X |
| Quarter-End Push | 2-4 weeks | Meet quarterly revenue target | 1.5x commission on deals closed in last 2 weeks |
| Strategic Account | Ongoing | Penetrate named target accounts | $10K bonus for first meeting, $25K for first deal |
| Multi-Year Commitment | Ongoing | Increase deal quality and predictability | 20% bonus on commission for 2+ year contracts |

**SPIF Best Practices:**
- Never run more than 2-3 SPIFs simultaneously (dilutes focus)
- Each SPIF should have a clear start and end date
- Pre-fund SPIFs from a separate budget (don't claw back from base plan)
- Measure SPIF ROI: incremental revenue should exceed SPIF cost by 5x+
- Avoid creating SPIF dependency — if reps always expect quarter-end bonuses, they'll sandbag early weeks

### Model 5: MBO (Management by Objectives)

MBOs are discretionary bonuses tied to qualitative or strategic objectives that are difficult to measure through standard commission metrics. They typically represent 10-20% of variable compensation.

**Common MBO Objectives:**
- Complete account plans for all Tier 1 accounts
- Achieve CRM hygiene score above 90%
- Complete product certification program
- Mentor 2 junior reps through ramp period
- Develop vertical expertise with 3 customer references
- Contribute 2 competitive intelligence reports per quarter

**MBO Design Principles:**
- Objectives must be SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound
- Scoring should be binary or simple scale (0%, 50%, 100%) to avoid subjectivity disputes
- MBOs should not exceed 20% of total variable — reps need predictable income
- Manager discretion should be limited to ±10% of MBO payout to prevent favoritism concerns
- Document MBO objectives and scoring criteria before the period begins

## Compensation Plan Design Process

### Step-by-Step Design Methodology

**Step 1: Define Strategic Objectives (Week 1)**
- What are the top 3 revenue priorities for the coming year?
- Which metrics directly connect to those priorities?
- What behaviors need to change vs. continue?
- What is the target revenue and margin mix?
- Document these in a compensation design brief

**Step 2: Benchmark Market Compensation (Week 1-2)**
- Collect OTE benchmarks for each role from at least 3 sources (Radford, Alexander Group, repVue)
- Benchmark base:variable splits by role
- Understand competitor compensation structures (through recruiting intelligence)
- Identify the comp position: market median, 65th percentile, or 75th percentile
- Target at least 65th percentile for AE roles to attract and retain talent

**Step 3: Set Quota (Week 2-3)**
- Apply quota-setting methodology (top-down, bottom-up, or hybrid)
- Validate quota against territory potential (P:Q ratio)
- Compare proposed quota to market benchmarks
- Ensure 60-65% achievability using historical data
- Document quota rationale for each role

**Step 4: Design Commission Structure (Week 3-4)**
- Select compensation model (tiered, flat, composite)
- Define commission rates at each attainment tier
- Calculate break-even point (attainment where comp cost = expected revenue contribution)
- Model 5-10 scenarios from 50% to 200% attainment
- Verify that top performers can earn 2-3x OTE without breaking company economics

**Step 5: Stress Test the Plan (Week 4-5)**
- Run Monte Carlo simulation on proposed plan using historical attainment distribution
- Calculate total comp expense at 1st, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 99th percentile outcomes
- Verify comp cost as % of revenue stays within target range (typically 20-30%)
- Identify "gaming" opportunities — loopholes that reps could exploit for outsized earnings
- Test edge cases: one huge deal, all small deals, early-year vs. late-year closes

**Step 6: Socialize and Refine (Week 5-6)**
- Present draft plan to sales leadership for strategic alignment
- Share anonymized model with 3-5 trusted reps for feedback on understandability
- Revise based on feedback — but don't negotiate on fundamental design principles
- Prepare formal communication materials

**Step 7: Implement and Communicate (Week 6-8)**
- Configure plan in compensation management system (CaptivateIQ, Spiff, Xactly)
- Create personalized comp cards for each rep showing their specific plan
- Conduct comp plan rollout meetings (1 hour per team, with Q&A)
- Distribute written plan documents with examples and FAQs
- Provide a comp calculator tool so reps can model scenarios

## Advanced Compensation Topics

### Commission Credit Timing

When a rep earns commission — at booking, at billing, or at cash collection — has significant implications for both rep motivation and company cash flow.

| Timing | Rep Perspective | Company Perspective | Best For |
|--------|----------------|--------------------|----------|
| **Booking** (contract signed) | Immediate reward, highest motivation | Cash flow risk if deal cancels or payment delayed | High-velocity, low-risk deals |
| **Billing** (invoice sent) | Slight delay, still motivating | Better aligned with revenue recognition | Most B2B scenarios |
| **Cash Collection** (payment received) | Delayed gratification, may not drive closing urgency | Best cash flow alignment | Enterprise, large deals with payment risk |
| **Hybrid** (partial at booking, remainder at collection) | Some immediate reward + alignment | Balanced risk | High-value deals with extended payment terms |

**Clawback Provisions**: Most plans include clawback terms that allow the company to recover commissions on deals that churn or cancel within a specified period (typically 90-180 days). Common clawback structures:
- Full clawback if customer cancels within 90 days
- Proportional clawback (prorated by months active) for 90-180 day cancellations
- No clawback after 180 days (risk absorbed by company)

### Deal-Level Adjustments

Not all revenue dollars are equally valuable to the company. Deal-level adjustments allow the comp plan to differentiate:

**Product Mix Adjustment**: Higher commission rates on strategic products, lower on commoditized ones. Example: 12% on platform deals, 6% on add-on modules.

**Term Length Adjustment**: Bonus for multi-year commitments. Example: 20% commission bonus on 2-year deals, 40% bonus on 3-year deals.

**Discount Penalty**: Reduced commission on heavily discounted deals to protect margin. Example: Full commission at ≤ 15% discount, 75% commission at 15-25% discount, 50% commission at > 25% discount.

**New Logo Premium**: Extra commission on first deals with new customers. Example: $5K bonus per new logo closed.

**Expansion vs. Renewal**: Different rates for growth vs. maintenance. Example: 8% on expansion ACV, 3% on renewal ACV.

### Ramp Plans for New Hires

New reps need time to build pipeline before they can close deals. Ramp plans provide guaranteed income during the ramp period while still creating incentive to accelerate pipeline building.

**Standard Ramp Structure (6 months for AE, 3 months for SDR):**

| Month | Quota | Guarantee | Commission | Total Expected |
|-------|-------|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Month 1 | 0% | 100% of variable | None | Full OTE prorated |
| Month 2 | 25% | 75% of variable | Earned on 25% quota | Near OTE |
| Month 3 | 50% | 50% of variable | Earned on 50% quota | Near OTE |
| Month 4 | 75% | 25% of variable | Earned on 75% quota | Near OTE |
| Month 5 | 100% | 0% | Full plan | Plan earnings |
| Month 6+ | 100% | 0% | Full plan | Plan earnings |

**Ramp Guarantee**: If total earnings (guarantee + commission) in any ramp month fall below the guarantee amount, pay the guarantee. If commission exceeds the guarantee, pay the higher amount. This ensures reps don't suffer financially during ramp while still rewarding early pipeline building.

### Compensation for Overlay Roles

Overlay specialists (Solutions Engineers, Product Specialists, Vertical Leads) support primary reps on deals that require specific expertise. Their compensation must incentivize collaboration without creating duplicate costs.

**Overlay Compensation Models:**

| Model | Overlay Payout | Primary Rep Impact | Complexity |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|------------|
| **Shared Credit** | Overlay earns 15-25% of deal commission | Primary rep earns 75-85% | Medium — need split rules |
| **Overlay Bonus** | Overlay earns flat bonus per supported deal ($500-$5K) | Primary rep earns full commission | Low — simple |
| **Overlay Quota** | Overlay has own quota based on supported revenue | No impact on primary | Medium — need tracking |
| **Team Quota** | Overlay and primary share team quota | Both accountable | High — team dynamics |

**Best Practice**: Use the Overlay Bonus model for occasional support (SEs who demo on 30-40% of deals). Use Shared Credit for dedicated overlays who are deeply involved in specific deal types. Avoid Team Quota unless the overlay is truly a co-seller.

## Compensation Plan Governance

### Annual Review Process

Compensation plans should be reviewed and potentially redesigned annually. The annual review should follow a structured process:

**Phase 1: Performance Analysis (November)**
- What % of reps attained quota? (Target: 60-65%)
- What was the payout distribution? (Target: 50th percentile at ~100% attainment)
- What was total comp cost as % of revenue? (Target: 20-30%)
- Did the plan drive desired behaviors? (Survey reps and managers)
- Were there gaming or loophole issues? (Audit exception reports)

**Phase 2: Strategic Alignment (December)**
- What are next year's strategic priorities?
- Do current comp metrics align to those priorities?
- What changes in market, product, or GTM require comp adjustments?
- What feedback from reps and managers should inform changes?

**Phase 3: Design & Modeling (January)**
- Draft revised plan structure
- Model scenarios using historical attainment data
- Run financial impact analysis at team and company level
- Identify and close loopholes

**Phase 4: Communication & Implementation (February)**
- Finalize plan documentation
- Create personalized comp cards
- Conduct rollout meetings with each team
- Configure in comp management system
- Begin new plan on March 1 or start of fiscal year

### Change Management Principles

1. **Never change the plan mid-year** unless there's a legal or existential business reason. Mid-year changes destroy rep trust and may trigger legal claims in some jurisdictions.

2. **Changes must be grandfathered** when possible. If you're changing the accelerator structure, allow reps to choose the old or new plan for the transition period.

3. **Communicate early and often**. Reps should hear about changes from their manager in a structured meeting, not through rumors or email blasts.

4. **Explain the "why"**. Every change should be accompanied by a clear explanation of the business rationale, not just the mechanics.

5. **Model the impact for every rep**. Show each rep exactly how the new plan affects their earnings at their current attainment level.

6. **Provide a transition period**. When making significant structural changes, allow 30-60 days for reps to understand and adjust to the new plan before it takes effect.

### Dispute Resolution

Compensation disputes are inevitable. A clear resolution process prevents disputes from escalating into legal claims or relationship damage:

**Tier 1: Manager Review** — Rep raises concern with direct manager within 30 days of commission statement. Manager reviews and resolves or escalates.

**Tier 2: Sales Ops Review** — Manager escalates to Sales Ops for calculation verification and plan interpretation. Resolution within 5 business days.

**Tier 3: VP/CRO Decision** — If interpretation is ambiguous or involves significant amount (>$10K), VP Sales makes final determination. Resolution within 10 business days.

**Tier 4: Legal Review** — If rep disputes the decision or alleges contractual violation, legal team reviews. This should be extremely rare (< 1% of disputes).

## Compensation Plan Anti-Patterns

### Common Plan Failures

| Anti-Pattern | Symptom | Root Cause | Fix |
|-------------|---------|-----------|-----|
| **The Cliff** | Reps sandbag to avoid threshold | Hard threshold below which commission = $0 | Use ramped rates from 0% instead of hard cliffs |
| **The Cap** | Top performers coast after hitting cap | Maximum earnings limit | Remove caps; use decelerators only if absolutely needed |
| **The Maze** | Reps can't calculate their own earnings | Too many components and variables | Simplify to ≤ 3 variable components |
| **The Sandbox** | Every rep hits quota by March | Quota too easy | Rebenchmark quota using market data |
| **The Desert** | < 30% of reps attain quota | Quota too high or territories weak | Recalibrate quota to 60-65% attainability |
| **The Treadmill** | High attainment but low earnings | Commission rates too low | Benchmark OTE against market; adjust rates |
| **The Casino** | Massive earnings variance, unpredictable | Over-reliance on SPIFs and bonuses | Increase base comp predictability; reduce SPIF dependence |
| **The Maze²** | Reps need a spreadsheet to understand plan | Complex multi-variable formulas with cross-dependencies | Simplify; each component should be independently calculable |
| **The Penalty Box** | Reps penalized for factors outside their control | Commission clawed back for CS failures | Only claw back for rep-controllable factors |
| **The Democracy** | All reps paid the same regardless of performance | Insufficient variable differentiation | Increase variable %; add accelerators |

Echo provides the analytical intelligence that drives comp plan design and optimization:

- **Attainment Distribution Analysis**: Shows the current distribution of rep attainment, highlighting whether the 60-65% target is being met and identifying structural issues
- **Comp Cost Modeling**: Projects total compensation expense under various attainment scenarios, enabling financial planning and plan stress testing
- **Pay-for-Performance Correlation**: Analyzes whether higher-performing reps actually earn proportionally more, validating plan alignment
- **Behavioral Impact Tracking**: Measures whether plan changes actually drive the intended behavioral shifts (e.g., more multi-year deals after introducing term bonuses)
- **Market Benchmark Comparison**: Compares current OTE and split ratios against market data, flagging roles that may be under- or over-compensated relative to market
- **Attrition Risk Scoring**: Identifies reps whose compensation trajectory suggests flight risk (top performers near cap, bottom performers below threshold for 2+ quarters)

Judge ensures compensation plans are fair, legal, and structurally sound:

- **Plan Compliance Audit**: Verifies that comp plans comply with local labor laws, including minimum wage, overtime, and commission payment timing requirements
- **Gaming Detection**: Identifies loopholes and gaming opportunities in proposed plan designs before they're implemented (e.g., commission structures that incentivize deal splitting)
- **Equity Analysis**: Analyzes whether compensation outcomes show any systematic disparities by demographic group, flagging potential discrimination risk
- **Quota Feasibility Validation**: Evaluates whether proposed quotas are achievable given territory potential, historical performance, and market conditions
- **Clawback Compliance**: Ensures clawback provisions comply with state laws (some states restrict or prohibit commission recovery)
- **Plan Document Review**: Validates that plan documents clearly define all terms, rates, thresholds, and conditions, reducing dispute risk

- **Earnings Projection Notifications**: Sends periodic updates showing current attainment trajectory and projected earnings, keeping reps motivated and informed
- **SPIF Launch Communications**: Creates compelling SPIF announcements that clearly explain the bonus opportunity, qualifying criteria, and timeline

## Implementation Checklist

### Pre-Design Phase

- [ ] Document strategic objectives for the compensation plan
- [ ] Collect market benchmark data (3+ sources per role)
- [ ] Analyze prior year plan performance (attainment distribution, cost, gaming)
- [ ] Survey reps on plan satisfaction and improvement suggestions
- [ ] Confirm headcount plan and territory structure for next year
- [ ] Identify legal/compliance requirements by jurisdiction

### Design Phase

- [ ] Set OTE levels by role (benchmark to 65th percentile minimum)
- [ ] Determine base:variable split for each role
- [ ] Set quotas using validated methodology (60-65% achievability)
- [ ] Design commission structure (tiers, rates, accelerators)
- [ ] Define deal-level adjustments (product mix, term, discount)
- [ ] Design overlay compensation model
- [ ] Create ramp plan for new hires
- [ ] Define SPIF strategy and budget
- [ ] Model 10+ scenarios (50% to 200% attainment)
- [ ] Run Monte Carlo simulation on total comp cost

### B. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **OTE** | On-Target Earnings — total compensation at 100% quota attainment |
| **ACV** | Annual Contract Value — annualized value of a subscription contract |
| **ARR** | Annual Recurring Revenue — total annualized recurring revenue |
| **TCV** | Total Contract Value — total value over the full contract term |
| **Clawback** | Recovery of previously paid commission (typically on churned deals) |
| **Accelerator** | Increased commission rate above 100% attainment |
| **Decelerator** | Decreased commission rate below a threshold (less common) |
| **SPIF** | Short-Term Incentive Fund — temporary bonus for specific behavior |
| **MBO** | Management by Objective — discretionary bonus for qualitative goals |
| **Draw** | Advance against future commissions (repayable if commissions don't cover it) |
| **Guarantee** | Non-recoverable minimum payment during ramp period |
| **Comp Card** | Personalized one-page document summarizing a rep's specific plan |
