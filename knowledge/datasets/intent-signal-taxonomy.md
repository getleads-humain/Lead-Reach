# Intent Signal Taxonomy

The intent signal taxonomy classifies the various indicators that a prospect is actively researching or considering a purchase. Signals are categorized by source type: first-party intent (direct engagement with LeadReach digital properties — website visits, content downloads, demo requests, pricing page views), second-party intent (data from partner networks — publisher networks showing research behavior, community activity, review site engagement), and third-party intent (observable market events — funding rounds, M&A activity, leadership changes, job postings, product launches). Signal strength is scored on a 1-100 scale based on: recency (signals within 7 days score 80-100, 7-30 days score 50-79, 30-90 days score 20-49, 90+ days score below 20), specificity (category-specific research scores higher than general research), and corroboration (multiple signals from the same account increase the score). The taxonomy maps signal types to buying journey stages: awareness signals (general category research, content consumption), consideration signals (vendor comparison, pricing research, demo requests), and decision signals (contract review indicators, procurement activity, security questionnaire requests). LeadReach agents use the intent signal taxonomy to: prioritize outreach based on signal strength and recency, craft messaging that references the specific intent signal, and time outreach within the optimal window after signal detection.
