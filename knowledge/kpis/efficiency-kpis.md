# Efficiency KPIs

Comprehensive KPI reference for the LeadReach platform covering efficiency-related performance metrics.
These benchmarks enable agents to assess prospect performance, identify improvement opportunities, and deliver data-driven recommendations.

## Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

CAC measures the total cost of acquiring a new customer, including sales compensation, marketing spend, tools, and overhead allocated to acquisition. The formula: (Total Sales + Marketing Costs) / Number of New Customers Acquired. B2B SaaS CAC benchmarks: SMB $5K-15K, mid-market $15K-50K, enterprise $50K-200K+. The CAC payback period — months to recoup CAC from gross margin — should be under 12 months for efficient businesses and under 18 months for enterprise-heavy models. CAC should be tracked by channel (outbound, inbound, partner, events) and by segment. Outbound CAC is typically 1.5-2x inbound CAC but produces 1.3x higher ACV. The LTV:CAC ratio should exceed 3:1, with top-performing SaaS companies achieving 5:1 or higher.

## Sales Cycle Length

Sales cycle length measures the average time from opportunity creation to closed-won (or closed-lost). Benchmarks by segment: SMB 15-45 days, mid-market 45-90 days, enterprise 90-180 days, strategic enterprise 180-365 days. Cycle length correlates strongly with ACV, number of stakeholders, and deal complexity. Key factors that extend cycles: multi-threading gaps (single point of contact), security review requirements (adds 2-6 weeks), procurement processes (adds 2-8 weeks), and lack of executive sponsorship. Organizations that implement structured close plans, multi-thread from day one, and run parallel workstreams (security review while negotiation continues) reduce cycle times by 20-35%. Tracking cycle length by rep, segment, and source identifies where process improvements have the greatest impact.

## Win Rate

Win rate measures the percentage of opportunities that close as won. Overall win rates for B2B SaaS: 15-30% depending on segment and deal size. SMB win rates tend to be 25-35%, mid-market 20-30%, enterprise 15-25%. Win rate should be analyzed by source (outbound vs. inbound vs. partner), by competitor present vs. no competitor, and by deal size bucket. The most actionable win rate analysis breaks opportunities into 'close reasons' — budget won, lost to competitor, no decision, timing, etc. 'No decision' (the prospect does nothing) accounts for 40-60% of losses and represents the largest improvement opportunity. Rep-level win rates vary from 10% to 40%+ — the gap is primarily explained by qualification rigor, multi-threading discipline, and executive engagement.

## Quota Attainment

Quota attainment measures the percentage of assigned revenue target achieved by a rep, team, or organization. The benchmark distribution: 60-65% of reps should attain quota, with a median attainment of 85-95%. Only 10-15% of reps should significantly over-achieve (>120%), and 20-25% should underperform (<70%). Setting quotas requires balancing ambition (driving stretch performance) with achievability (maintaining rep morale and retention). Quota capacity planning should account for: ramp time (new reps need 3-9 months), territory maturity, pipeline coverage requirements, and seasonal patterns. Organizations that set quotas based on data-driven territory scoring and historical attainment patterns see 15-20% higher overall attainment compared to top-down quota allocation.
