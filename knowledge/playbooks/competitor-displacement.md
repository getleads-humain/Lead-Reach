# Competitor Displacement — Playbook

This playbook is the canonical reference for identifying prospects using competing outbound platforms and executing structured displacement motions. It covers competitive intelligence gathering, vulnerability assessment, switch-cost mitigation, and migration execution. Used by Scout (Prospecting), Sage (Web Research), Bard (Outreach Composer), Judge (Scoring), and Flow (Pipeline Coordination).

## 1. Why Competitor Displacement Matters

Competitor displacement is the highest-ACV motion in B2B SaaS sales. A prospect already using a competitor has:
- **Confirmed budget** for outbound tooling
- **Confirmed need** — they've already bought the category
- **Confirmed urgency** when they're unhappy (switching is painful; they only do it when pain exceeds switching cost)

The challenge: **switching costs are real and often underestimated**. Data migration, team retraining, sequence rebuilding, integration reconfiguration — these create inertia that keeps customers on inferior platforms. A successful displacement motion must simultaneously **increase the perceived cost of staying** and **reduce the perceived cost of switching**.

LeadReach's displacement win rate is 35-40% when the motion is structured (vs. 10-15% for opportunistic competitive deals). Structure is the difference.

## 2. Competitive Intelligence Framework

### Tier 1 Competitors (Primary Displacement Targets)

| Competitor | Typical Weakness | Displacement Angle |
|------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Outreach | Price; complexity; slow support for mid-market | Simplicity + value + responsive support |
| Salesloft | Feature gaps in analytics; pricing tiers | Advanced analytics at transparent pricing |
| Reply.io | Limited enterprise features; scale issues | Enterprise-grade reliability + scalability |
| Apollo | Data quality concerns; support gaps | Superior data hygiene + dedicated CSM |
| Lemlist | Limited sequencing; no enterprise tier | Full sequencing platform + enterprise governance |
| Instantly | Mailbox-only; no multi-channel | Multi-channel orchestration + CRM integration |

### Tier 2 Competitors (Opportunistic Displacement)

| Competitor | Typical Weakness | When to Target |
|------------|-----------------|----------------|
| HubSpot Sales Hub | Shallow outbound features | When customer outgrows HS outbound |
| Salesforce Engage | Overpriced; basic sequencing | When contract renewal approaches |
| Woodpecker | Limited scale; basic analytics | When team grows beyond 10 SDRs |
| Mixmax | Gmail-only; no enterprise | When customer needs cross-platform support |

### Vulnerability Signals by Competitor

**Outreach users show vulnerability when:**
- Complaining about UI complexity on G2/Reddit
- Downgrading from Enterprise to lower tier (budget pressure)
- Support ticket volume spikes (visible in community forums)
- Key feature requests unaddressed for 6+ months (check public roadmap)

**Salesloft users show vulnerability when:**
- Requesting advanced reporting features (available in LeadReach)
- Experiencing data quality issues (visible in reviews)
- Leadership changes in revenue org (new CRO may re-evaluate stack)

**Apollo users show vulnerability when:**
- Data accuracy complaints surface (G2, community)
- Customer hits scale ceiling (performance degradation)
- Security/compliance concerns (data sourcing practices)

## 4. The 90-Day Displacement Workflow

### Phase 1: Reconnaissance (Days 1-14)

**Scout agent** identifies competitive users through:
- Job postings mentioning competitor tools ("Outreach experience required")
- LinkedIn profiles listing competitor tools in skills
- G2 reviews of competitor products (reviewer = prospect)
- Community forum participation (competitor's community)
- Technology intent data (Bombora, G2 Buyer Intent)
- Conference attendee lists (competitor's events)

**Sage agent** researches each identified account:
- Current contract likely end date (from procurement timelines, fiscal year)
- Recent complaints or issues on public forums
- Organizational changes that might trigger vendor review
- Peer companies that already switched to LeadReach

**Output**: Displacement Target List with vulnerability notes and recommended approach.

### Phase 2: Seeding (Days 14-45)

**Bard agent** runs a competitive seeding sequence that does NOT directly attack the competitor:

| Day | Channel | Content |
|-----|---------|---------|
| 0 | LinkedIn | Connection request; engage with prospect's content |
| 7 | Email | Value-led content — "how [industry] teams improved outbound by 40%" (no competitor mention) |
| 14 | Email | Peer case study — from company that switched from same competitor |
| 21 | LinkedIn | Share competitive-neutral content (outbound best practices) |
| 28 | Email | Industry benchmark report (subtle: shows LeadReach data outperforms category average) |
| 35 | Email | "Worth a 15-minute call?" — soft CTA, no competitor mention |
| 42 | LinkedIn DM | Direct message referencing shared content engagement |

**Key principle**: Never badmouth the competitor in writing. Let the prospect reach their own conclusion. The case study from a peer who switched is your most powerful weapon — it provides social proof without requiring you to attack.

### Phase 3: Engagement (Days 45-70)

When the prospect engages (replies, takes a call, downloads content):

1. **Discovery meeting**: Focus 80% on their goals, 20% on LeadReach. Ask:
   - "What's working well with your current platform?"
   - "What would you change if you could?"
   - "What does your ideal outbound stack look like?"
2. **Competitive gap analysis**: Privately map their stated needs against competitor gaps. Do not share this analysis with the prospect — use it to tailor your demo.
3. **Tailored demo**: Show LeadReach doing exactly what the competitor can't — no more, no less. The demo should create an "aha moment" where the prospect sees a solution to their unaddressed pain.
4. **Switch-cost mitigation plan**: Present a detailed migration plan that addresses:
   - Sequence migration (LeadReach imports competitor sequences)
   - Data migration (contact data transfer)
   - Integration reconfiguration (CRM, calendar, email)
   - Team training (structured onboarding with dedicated CSM)
   - Timeline (typically 2-4 weeks for full migration)
   - Running parallel during transition

### Phase 4: Close (Days 70-90)

1. **ROI of switching**: Quantify the cost of staying (lost productivity, missed pipeline, support pain) vs. the cost of switching (migration time, training, contract overlap).
2. **Competitive buyout**: Offer to buy out the remaining competitor contract (up to 6 months) for high-ACV deals.
3. **Risk reversal**: 90-day money-back guarantee on LeadReach if they're not satisfied after migration.
4. **Champion enablement**: Provide the internal champion with a switch justification document they can present to their leadership.
5. **Urgency creation**: If their competitor contract renewal is approaching, time the proposal to create a natural decision point.

- **Title**: Chief Revenue Officer, VP Sales
- **Pain**: Inherited a tech stack that isn't delivering; needs to show results in first 90 days
- **Goal**: Quick wins on pipeline generation; stack rationalization
- **Buying trigger**: New hire in role; first QBR reveals outbound underperformance

## 8. Email / Sequence Templates

### Template 1: Empathy-Led Outreach (Post-Negative Signal)

**Subject**: `[competitor] alternative?`

> Hi [first name],
>
> Saw your team is using [competitor] — and I imagine the recent [price increase / feature gap / outage] wasn't welcome news.
>
> Not here to bash [competitor]. But I do work with 12 teams who made the switch from [competitor] to LeadReach in the last year. The most common reason: they wanted [specific capability] without [specific pain point].
>
> Worth a 15-minute call to see if it's worth exploring?
>
> [AE name]

### Template 2: Peer Case Study Seeding

**Subject**: `how [peer company] improved outbound 40%`

> Hi [first name],
>
> [Peer company] was running [competitor] for 18 months before they made a change. Their SDR team was hitting 8% reply rates but struggling with [specific pain — e.g., "sequence analytics that didn't go deep enough"].
>
> After switching, they hit 14% reply rates in the first quarter. I can share the details if you're curious — no pitch, just the story.
>
> [AE name]

### Template 3: Switch Justification Document (Champion Enablement)

**Subject**: `internal business case for stack evaluation`

> Hi [first name],
>
> Attached is a one-page switch evaluation framework. It covers:
> - Current state cost (license + opportunity cost of [pain point])
> - Migration timeline and resource requirements
> - Projected ROI based on peer benchmarks
> - Risk mitigation (parallel run, money-back guarantee)
>
> This is designed to help you have the conversation internally — not to sell. Happy to customize it with your specific numbers.
>
> [AE name]

## 9. Objection Handling

| Objection | Root Cause | Response |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| "Switching is too much work" | Switching cost fear | "We've done 50+ migrations from [competitor]. Average timeline is 2 weeks. We handle the heavy lifting — sequence import, data migration, integration setup. Your team just needs to show up for a 2-hour training." |
| "We just renewed our contract" | Sunk cost fallacy | "Understood. Two options: (1) we can buy out the remaining months, or (2) we can start the evaluation now and time the switch for renewal. Either way, knowing your options costs nothing." |
| "Our team is used to [competitor]" | Training cost fear | "Most SDRs are productive on LeadReach within 2 days — the interface is simpler by design. We also provide a dedicated CSM for the first 90 days to ensure adoption." |
| "We built custom integrations" | Technical lock-in | "We have pre-built integrations for [CRM, calendar, analytics tools]. For custom integrations, our API is [more accessible/better documented]. We'll handle migration at no extra cost." |
| "Leadership won't approve a switch" | Political risk | "That's why we prepare an internal business case document. It quantifies the cost of staying vs. switching — so it's a data-driven decision, not a vendor preference." |
| "We haven't had problems with [competitor]" | No pain surfaced | "That's great. Out of curiosity, what would make you evaluate alternatives? Knowing that helps me understand if LeadReach is ever relevant for your team." |
| "We're locked in for 2 years" | Contract constraint | "Let me review your contract terms. Many customers have early termination clauses they aren't aware of. And we can structure our contract to align with your remaining term." |

## 11. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

| Pitfall | Cause | Prevention |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| Attacking the competitor directly | AE gets emotional or desperate | Never badmouth a competitor in writing or in front of the prospect. Let peer stories and data speak. |
| Underestimating switching costs | Migration plan too optimistic | Build a detailed migration plan with buffer time. Expect 2-4 weeks, quote 3-5 weeks. |
| Ignoring the champion's political risk | Champion wants to switch but can't sell it internally | Provide the internal business case document. Offer to join an internal call as a "technical resource." |
| Winning the deal then losing the customer | Migration fails; customer regrets switching | Dedicated migration CSM. 90-day money-back guarantee. Parallel running period. |
| Slow follow-up on competitive signals | Signal detected but no action for weeks | 72-hour SLA from signal detection to first outreach. Flow agent enforces. |
| No competitive battlecard updates | Competitor releases new features; battlecard is stale | Sage agent reviews and updates battlecards monthly. |
| Over-promising on migration speed | AE promises 1-week migration; reality is 3 weeks | Use standardized migration timeline template. Never custom-promise faster. |

## 13. Example Scenario

**Account**: Vertix Analytics, a Series C data analytics company

**Current vendor**: Outreach (Enterprise tier, 60 seats, $180K/year)

**Signals detected**:
- VP RevOps posted a frustrated G2 review about Outreach's UI complexity (Scout, Day 0)
- 3 LinkedIn posts from SDRs at Vertix complaining about "too many clicks" in Outreach (Scout, Day 1)
- Vertix's contract renewal is in 4 months (Sage research, Day 2)
- New CRO started 2 months ago (Scout, Day 3)

**DisplacementReadinessScore**: 82 (Priority target)

**Motion executed**:
1. **Day 3**: Bard sends empathy-led outreach to VP RevOps — "saw your G2 review; ouch. Worth a 15-minute call?" VP replies next day.
2. **Day 5**: Discovery call — VP confirms: "Outreach is powerful but our SDRs hate using it. Support is slow. And the price increase last quarter stung."
3. **Day 8**: Bard sends peer case study — Vertix's competitor (similar size, switched from Outreach 6 months ago, saw 35% productivity improvement).
4. **Day 12**: CRO outreach — executive-to-executive email about "outbound stack rationalization." CRO's EA schedules a call.
5. **Day 15**: Joint meeting with VP RevOps + CRO. Demo focused on simplicity and productivity. Key moment: showing how a 12-step Outreach sequence maps to a 5-step LeadReach sequence with the same results.
6. **Day 20**: Migration plan presented — 3-week timeline, parallel running, dedicated migration CSM, data import included.
7. **Day 25**: Internal business case document provided to VP RevOps for CFO conversation.
8. **Day 35**: CFO approves budget. Contract signed at $156K/year (60 seats, Enterprise tier — 13% savings vs. Outreach renewal).
9. **Day 38-56**: Migration executed. 60 SDRs onboarded in 2 waves. 100% adoption by day 50.
10. **Day 90**: Post-switch review — reply rates up 28%, SDR satisfaction up 40% (internal survey), support tickets down 65%.
