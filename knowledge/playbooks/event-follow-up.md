# Event Follow-Up — Playbook

This playbook is the canonical reference for converting event interactions (conferences, trade shows, webinars, roundtables, dinners) into qualified pipeline. It covers lead capture, prioritization, speed-to-lead execution, multi-channel follow-up sequences, and attribution tracking. Used by Scout (Lead Capture), Bard (Outreach Composer), Flow (Sequence Orchestration), and Echo (Attribution Reporting).

## 1. Why Event Follow-Up Matters

Events are the most expensive top-of-funnel channel in B2B SaaS. A typical industry conference costs $50K-$200K for booth, sponsorship, travel, and staffing. The ROI depends entirely on what happens **after** the event.

Research is brutal: **78% of event leads never receive follow-up** (CEIR, 2024). Of those that do, the average response time is 4-5 business days. Yet the probability of qualifying a lead drops **80% when follow-up takes more than 5 minutes vs. 5 minutes** (Harvard Business Review). Speed-to-lead is not optional — it is the single biggest lever on event ROI.

LeadReach's event follow-up philosophy: **capture everything, prioritize ruthlessly, follow up within 1 hour, and sequence for 21 days**. Every badge scan, business card, and conversation becomes a data point. The agents turn raw event data into pipeline within hours, not weeks.

## 2. Event Lead Taxonomy

Not all event leads are equal. Categorize immediately:

### Tier 1: Hot Leads (10-15% of event leads)

**Definition**: Prospects who expressed explicit interest in buying or evaluating LeadReach.

**Signals**:
- "We're actively looking for a new outbound platform"
- "Can you set up a demo for our team next week?"
- "What's your pricing for 50 seats?"
- Handed business card with "call me" note
- Scheduled a meeting at the booth

**Follow-up SLA**: Within 30 minutes of lead capture.

### Tier 2: Warm Leads (25-35% of event leads)

**Definition**: Prospects who engaged meaningfully but did not express immediate buying intent.

**Signals**:
- Watched a full product demo at the booth
- Asked detailed questions about features or integrations
- Expressed a pain point that LeadReach solves
- Attended a LeadReach-sponsored session
- Engaged in conversation >5 minutes

**Follow-up SLA**: Within 2 hours of lead capture.

### Tier 3: Lukewarm Leads (30-40% of event leads)

**Definition**: Prospects who stopped by the booth or attended a session but showed limited engagement.

**Signals**:
- Badge scan with brief conversation
- Took swag but didn't engage deeply
- Attended session but didn't ask questions
- General "tell me about LeadReach" interaction

**Follow-up SLA**: Within 24 hours of lead capture.

### Tier 4: Cold / Unqualified (15-25% of event leads)

**Definition**: Prospects who are not in ICP or showed no meaningful engagement.

**Signals**:
- Badge scan only, no conversation
- Student, job seeker, vendor (not prospect)
- Company too small or wrong industry
- International prospect with no US/EU buying authority

**Follow-up SLA**: Add to nurture database; no personalized outreach.

## 3. Pre-Event Preparation (1-4 Weeks Before)

### 3.1 Target Account List (Sage + Scout, Week -4)

Before the event, Sage agent builds a target account list:
- **Attending companies**: cross-reference attendee list (if available) with ICP
- **Speaking companies**: executives speaking at the event are high-value targets
- **Sponsoring companies**: competitors (monitor) and partners (coordinate)
- **Local accounts**: companies headquartered near the event venue

Output: **Event Target List** with 50-200 accounts, prioritized by ICP fit.

### 3.2 Pre-Event Outreach (Bard, Week -2)

Bard agent sends pre-event outreach to target accounts:

**Template — Pre-Event Connection**:
**Subject**: `at [event] next week?`

> Hi [first name],
>
> Looks like we'll both be at [Event] next week. I'd love to connect — especially if your team is thinking about outbound optimization.
>
> We're at booth [number] and hosting a [breakfast/happy hour/roundtable] on [day]. Worth grabbing 15 minutes?
>
> [AE name]

### 3.3 Event Landing Page (Forge, Week -1)

Forge agent creates an event-specific landing page:
- Personalized URL: `leadreach.io/[event-name]`
- Event-specific offer: "Mention this page for 20% off first year"
- Lead capture form: name, email, company, title, biggest outbound challenge
- Auto-responder email confirming the meeting/conversation

### 3.4 Team Briefing (Atlas, Day -1)

Atlas agent prepares the event team briefing:
- Target account list with ICP notes and relevant triggers
- Competitive intelligence summary (which competitors are present)
- Key messages and feature demos for the event
- Lead capture protocol (how to tag each lead tier)
- Follow-up SLA assignments

## 4. During-Event Execution

### 4.1 Lead Capture Protocol

Every interaction must be captured with:
- **Contact info**: name, title, company, email, phone
- **Lead tier**: Hot / Warm / Lukewarm / Cold
- **Conversation notes**: key pain points, questions, interests
- **Next step**: agreed follow-up (demo, call, send info)
- **Timestamp**: when the interaction occurred
- **Staff member**: who had the conversation

**Tools**: Badge scanner app, CRM mobile app, business card photo + OCR (Forge agent).

### 4.2 Real-Time Lead Routing

As leads are captured during the event:

| Lead Tier | Routing | Action |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| Hot | Immediate push to AE's phone | AE calls/texts within 30 minutes |
| Warm | Push to AE's queue | AE emails within 2 hours |
| Lukewarm | Push to SDR queue | SDR sequences within 24 hours |
| Cold | Add to marketing nurture | Automated drip campaign |

### 4.3 Live Social Engagement (Scout + Bard)

During the event, Scout agent monitors social:
- LinkedIn posts from attendees mentioning the event
- Twitter/X posts with event hashtag
- Photo tags, check-ins, booth visits

Bard agent composes real-time social responses:
- Like + comment on prospect's event posts
- Share relevant event content with @mentions
- DM to prospects who are at the event but haven't visited the booth

## 5. Post-Event Follow-Up Sequences

### 5.1 Hot Lead Sequence (Bard, starts within 30 minutes)

| Timing | Channel | Content |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 30 min | Email | Personalized recap of booth conversation; reference specific pain point; propose specific meeting time |
| 2 hours | LinkedIn | Connection request with personal note referencing conversation |
| Day 1 | Phone | AE call: "Great meeting you at [Event]. Following up on [specific topic]..." |
| Day 3 | Email | Case study from same industry; reference conversation |
| Day 5 | Email | Meeting re-confirm or re-propose if no response |
| Day 7 | LinkedIn DM | Soft re-engagement |
| Day 10 | Email | "Should I close your file?" breakup email |

### 5.2 Warm Lead Sequence (Bard, starts within 2 hours)

| Timing | Channel | Content |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 2 hours | Email | Thank you for visiting; recap key feature interest; offer demo |
| Day 1 | LinkedIn | Connection request with event reference |
| Day 3 | Email | Industry-specific content (report, benchmark) |
| Day 5 | Phone | SDR call referencing event + email |
| Day 7 | Email | Peer case study + soft CTA |
| Day 10 | LinkedIn DM | Engage with their content; share relevant insight |
| Day 14 | Email | New trigger-based message (if applicable) |
| Day 21 | Email | Breakup email |

### 5.3 Lukewarm Lead Sequence (Bard, starts within 24 hours)

| Timing | Channel | Content |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 24 hours | Email | Thank you for stopping by; general value prop; offer content |
| Day 3 | LinkedIn | Connection request |
| Day 7 | Email | Top 3 outbound benchmarks for [industry] |
| Day 14 | Email | Case study + soft CTA |
| Day 21 | Email | Breakup or nurture transition |

### 5.4 Cold Lead Sequence (Automated, starts within 48 hours)

| Timing | Channel | Content |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 48 hours | Email | Generic "great meeting you at [Event]" + newsletter signup |
| Monthly | Email | Marketing nurture drip |

## 7. Target Persona and ICP Criteria

### Ideal Event Lead

| Criterion | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Title | VP+ in Sales, Revenue Ops, or Marketing |
| Company size | 50-5000 employees |
| Industry | SaaS, FinTech, HealthTech, Professional Services |
| Current tool | Using a competitor or manual outbound |
| Budget authority | Can approve or influence $50K+ purchase |
| Engagement | Visited booth, attended session, or engaged on social |

### Key Event Personas

**The Evaluator** (40% of qualified leads):
- Actively comparing outbound tools
- Asking pricing and feature questions
- Ready for demo and trial
- Follow-up: fast-track to demo → proposal

**The Curious Leader** (35% of qualified leads):
- Attended session or stopped by booth out of curiosity
- Has a pain point but hasn't started evaluating
- Needs education before selling
- Follow-up: content nurture → discovery call

**The Networker** (15% of qualified leads):
- At the event for networking, not buying
- Valuable for referral potential and brand awareness
- Follow-up: relationship building → future opportunity

**The Competitor / Analyst** (10% of leads):
- Gathering intelligence or market research
- Not a prospect but valuable for competitive insights
- Follow-up: engage carefully; gather intelligence

## 9. Email / Sequence Templates

### Template 1: Hot Lead — Booth Conversation Follow-Up

**Subject**: `great chatting at [event] — [specific topic]`

> Hi [first name],
>
> Really enjoyed our conversation at [Event] about [specific pain point they mentioned, e.g., "scaling your SDR team's outbound without sacrificing personalization"].
>
> As promised, here's the [case study / benchmark report / feature overview] we discussed. I'd also love to show you how [peer company] solved exactly this — they went from 8% to 14% reply rates in one quarter.
>
> Worth a 15-minute demo next Tuesday or Wednesday?
>
> [AE name]

### Template 2: Warm Lead — Session Attendee Follow-Up

**Subject**: `[event] session follow-up — outbound benchmarks`

> Hi [first name],
>
> Saw you at the [session name] session at [Event]. The Q&A about [topic] was great — especially the point about [specific insight].
>
> I've attached the benchmark report we referenced in the session. One stat that might resonate: teams using multi-channel sequences see 2.3x the reply rate of email-only.
>
> Worth a 15-minute conversation about how this applies to [company]?
>
> [AE name]

### Template 3: Lukewarm Lead — General Follow-Up

**Subject**: `met at [event] — leadreach + [company]`

> Hi [first name],
>
> We met briefly at the LeadReach booth at [Event]. Quick thought based on your industry: most [industry] teams we work with are struggling with [common pain point].
>
> I put together a short report on how top-performing outbound teams in [industry] are solving this. Mind if I send it over?
>
> [AE name]

## 10. Objection Handling

| Objection | Root Cause | Response |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| "I don't remember meeting you" | Brief interaction or many booths visited | "We met at [Event] at the LeadReach booth — we talked about [specific topic]. It was a busy event! Quick context on why I'm reaching out..." |
| "We're not looking to switch tools right now" | No active buying intent | "Totally understand. Would it be useful to see how your current outbound benchmarks compare to industry averages? No pitch — just data." |
| "I just collected a lot of info; I need time to process" | Overwhelmed post-event | "Of course. I'll send one resource that's most relevant to [their challenge] and circle back in 2 weeks. Fair?" |
| "Send me some info and I'll review" | Polite deflection | "Happy to. What's most relevant: [option A: ROI benchmarks], [option B: feature comparison], or [option C: case study from your industry]?" (Give specific choices) |
| "We already have an outbound tool" | Category satisfied (not necessarily well) | "Good to hear. Out of curiosity, what's working well? And what would you change? Most teams have at least one thing they'd improve." |
| "I'm not the right person for this" | Not the decision-maker | "Who would be the best person to chat with? I'm happy to send them a short overview — and if you think it's worth a conversation, an intro would be amazing." |

## 12. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

| Pitfall | Cause | Prevention |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| Slow follow-up (>24 hours) | No SLA enforcement; team busy or traveling | Flow agent enforces SLAs with escalation to manager |
| Generic follow-up emails | No conversation notes captured; mass-send | Bard agent requires conversation notes to compose follow-up; no generic templates for Tier 1-2 |
| Losing leads in the CRM | Leads captured on paper/phones but not entered | Forge agent deduplicates and enters within 1 hour of capture |
| No lead prioritization | All leads treated equally; AE overwhelmed by volume | Judge agent scores and tiers every lead before routing |
| No attribution tracking | Can't prove which events drive pipeline | Every lead tagged with event source; Echo agent tracks attribution |
| Ignoring social signals | Only tracking booth interactions | Scout agent monitors event hashtag, LinkedIn posts, check-ins |
| Pre-event targeting skipped | Team shows up unprepared; wastes booth time | Atlas agent enforces pre-event preparation checklist |
| No post-event nurture for Tier 3-4 leads | Lukewarm leads forgotten after initial sequence | Automated nurture sequence for all non-converted leads |

## 14. Example Scenario

**Event**: SaaStr Annual 2026, San Francisco

**LeadReach investment**: $120K (platinum booth + sponsorship + party)

**Pre-event (2 weeks before)**:
- Sage identifies 180 target accounts from attendee list
- Bard sends pre-event outreach to 120 contacts at target accounts
- 25 pre-event meetings booked before the event starts

**During event (3 days)**:
- Day 1: 85 badge scans, 6 hot leads, 28 warm leads
- Day 2: 62 badge scans, 4 hot leads, 22 warm leads (keynote day)
- Day 3: 41 badge scans, 2 hot leads, 15 warm leads
- Social: Scout identifies 34 additional prospects posting about the event

**Total leads**: 188 badge scans + 34 social leads = 222 total leads
- Hot: 12 (5%)
- Warm: 65 (29%)
- Lukewarm: 95 (43%)
- Cold: 50 (23%)

**Post-event follow-up**:
- **Hot leads**: All 12 contacted within 30 minutes. 8 meetings booked by Day 2. 4 proposals sent by Day 14. 2 closed-won by Day 45.
- **Warm leads**: All 65 contacted within 2 hours. 18 meetings booked by Day 7. 6 proposals sent by Day 30. 3 closed-won by Day 75.
- **Lukewarm leads**: All 95 in sequence by Day 1. 7 meetings booked by Day 14. 1 closed-won by Day 90.

**Results at 90 days**:
- Pipeline generated: $680K
- Closed-won: $240K (6 deals)
- Event ROI: $240K / $120K = 2x (pipeline ROI: 5.7x)
- Additional pipeline expected from nurture: $150K over next 6 months

## 15. Escalation Paths

| Situation | Escalation Level | Action |
|-----------|-----------------|--------|
| Hot lead not contacted within 30 min | AE Manager | Immediate reassignment; manager calls directly |
| Follow-up SLA missed for >10% of leads | VP Sales | Root cause analysis; process remediation |
| Lead quality significantly below expectations | Marketing + Sales | Review targeting; adjust ICP for future events |
| Event ROI below 1x at 90 days | VP Sales + Marketing | Evaluate event for next year; adjust strategy |
| Competitive confrontation at event | CMO + VP Sales | Debrief; adjust competitive messaging |
| Data quality issues (bad emails, duplicates) | RevOps | Forge agent audit; CRM cleanup process |
| Customer complaint from event interaction | VP CS | Immediate outreach; service recovery |
