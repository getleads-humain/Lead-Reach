# Executive Engagement Playbook

This playbook provides a systematic framework for engaging C-suite and senior executives in enterprise accounts. Executive engagement is the single highest-leverage activity in B2B sales — an executive sponsor can accelerate deals by 40%, expand deal sizes by 3x, and virtually eliminate competitive displacement. Yet most sales teams fail to engage executives effectively because they lead with features instead of strategic outcomes.

LeadReach's AI agent pipeline enables precision executive engagement through persona-specific intelligence, strategic alignment analysis, and AI-generated executive insights. This playbook transforms those capabilities into repeatable executive engagement motions.

## 1. C-Suite Engagement Strategies

### 1.1 The Executive Engagement Maturity Model

#### Level 1: No Executive Access

- Sales team has zero executive relationships in the account
- All conversations happen at manager and director level
- Deals stall in procurement without executive champion
- Highly vulnerable to competitor executive relationships

**Assessment**: If your account has no executive access, this is your #1 risk factor. 67% of lost deals trace back to lack of executive engagement.

#### Level 2: Peripheral Awareness

- Executives know your company name but have no direct relationship
- Awareness comes from marketing, not personal engagement
- Executives may approve budget but don't actively sponsor
- Moderate vulnerability — accessible to competitor executive sellers

**Assessment**: Progressing but insufficient. Move to Level 3 within 90 days.

#### Level 3: Executive Familiarity

- At least one executive has met your team in a briefing or event
- The executive has a positive but passive impression of LeadReach
- The executive will take your calls but doesn't proactively advocate
- Lower vulnerability but lacks the active sponsorship that drives large deals

**Assessment**: Good foundation. Cultivate into active sponsorship within 6 months.

#### Level 4: Active Executive Sponsor

- A named executive actively champions LeadReach internally
- The sponsor removes obstacles, provides access, and advocates for budget
- The sponsor connects you to other executives in the organization
- Low vulnerability — strong internal defense against competitors

**Assessment**: Target state for all Tier 1 accounts. Maintain and expand to multi-sponsor.

#### Level 5: Multi-Executive Coalition

- Multiple executives across functions actively support LeadReach
- The coalition creates organizational gravity that's extremely hard to displace
- Deal sizes expand naturally as executives see cross-functional value
- Very low vulnerability — requires systemic failure to displace

**Assessment**: Target state for strategic accounts. This creates durable competitive advantage.

## 2. Executive Communication Frameworks

### 2.1 The S.U.C.C.E.S.S. Framework for Executive Communication

All executive communications at LeadReach follow the S.U.C.C.E.S.S. framework:

**S — Specific**: Lead with a specific, quantified outcome — never generalities
**U — Urgent**: Create a time-bound reason to act now
**C — Credible**: Back claims with data, references, or third-party validation
**C — Concise**: Executives allocate 2-3 minutes to initial evaluation
**E — Executable**: Include a clear, simple next step
**S — Strategic**: Frame in terms of their strategic priorities, not your product
**S — Surprising**: Offer an insight they don't already have

### 2.4 Executive Communication Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Lead with business outcomes | Lead with product features |
| Use their language and metrics | Use your internal jargon |
| Reference their public statements | Make generic industry observations |
| Provide specific, quantified results | Use vague claims ("improve efficiency") |
| Keep emails under 150 words | Write multi-paragraph emails |
| Include one clear call to action | Give multiple options for next steps |
| Reference mutual connections | Send cold emails without context |
| Follow up within 24 hours | Wait a week to follow up |
| Send insights they can't get elsewhere | Forward your marketing newsletter |
| Ask about their priorities | Pitch your solution immediately |

## 3. Board-Level Presentations

### 3.1 When to Pursue Board-Level Engagement

Board-level engagement is appropriate when:

1. **Strategic partnership opportunity**: The relationship has potential beyond a vendor-customer dynamic
2. **Major expansion proposal**: Multi-year, multi-million dollar expansion requiring board visibility
3. **Competitive threat**: A competitor is making board-level plays in your account
4. **Industry transformation**: Your solution is critical to their digital transformation journey
5. **Investor relationship**: You have connections to their investors or board members

### 3.2 Board Presentation Structure

Board presentations must be radically different from standard sales presentations. Board members care about:

```
Board Presentation Framework (20 minutes max):
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Section 1: Market Context (3 minutes)
├── Macro trend creating strategic imperative
├── Competitive landscape shift
├── Risk of inaction (quantified)
└── Opportunity of action (quantified)

Section 2: Strategic Alignment (5 minutes)
├── How LeadReach maps to their stated strategic priorities
├── Proof points from similar-scale organizations
├── Executive sponsor perspective (if available)
└── Board member reference (if available)

Section 3: Business Case (7 minutes)
├── Investment required (clear, simple)
├── Projected ROI with confidence intervals
├── Time to value and payback period
├── Risk mitigation plan
└── Competitive advantage created

Section 4: Execution Plan (3 minutes)
├── Phased implementation timeline
├── Quick wins in first 90 days
├── Governance and success metrics
└── Executive sponsor assignment

Section 5: Ask (2 minutes)
├── Clear, specific request
├── Decision timeline needed
└── What happens next based on their decision
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### 3.3 Board Presentation Visual Guidelines

- **Maximum 12 slides** for a 20-minute presentation
- **One idea per slide** — no complex multi-concept slides
- **Data visualization over text** — executives scan, they don't read
- **Red/amber/green status indicators** for progress tracking
- **No product screenshots** — this is a business conversation, not a demo
- **Include a one-page executive summary** as a leave-behind

## 6. Executive Sponsor Cultivation

### 6.1 The Executive Sponsor Development Path

```
Executive Sponsor Development Path:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Phase 1: Introduction (Months 1-2)
├── Initial meeting through executive briefing or warm intro
├── Share industry insights and strategic perspective
├── Demonstrate understanding of their priorities
├── No ask — pure value delivery
└── Goal: Second meeting scheduled

Phase 2: Value Demonstration (Months 2-4)
├── Deliver on promises from first meeting
├── Share custom analysis or research relevant to their priorities
├── Connect them with peers facing similar challenges
├── Introduce them to satisfied LeadReach customers at their level
└── Goal: They proactively reach out to you

Phase 3: Trust Building (Months 4-6)
├── Provide counsel on strategic decisions (not just your product)
├── Help them navigate internal politics when appropriate
├── Offer early access to product innovations relevant to them
├── Advocate for their priorities within LeadReach
└── Goal: They view you as a trusted advisor, not a vendor

Phase 4: Active Sponsorship (Months 6+)
├── They advocate for LeadReach in internal meetings
├── They remove obstacles to expansion or renewal
├── They connect you to other executives in the organization
├── They provide candid feedback and strategic guidance
└── Goal: Multi-threaded executive coalition
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### 6.2 Executive Sponsor Maintenance

Maintaining an executive sponsor requires ongoing investment:

**Monthly Touchpoints**:
- Send one relevant industry insight or article (not about your product)
- Share one customer success story from their industry
- Provide a brief update on value delivered to their organization

**Quarterly Touchpoints**:
- Executive QBR or strategic review meeting
- Product roadmap preview relevant to their priorities
- Industry event or dinner invitation

**Annual Touchpoints**:
- Strategic planning session alignment
- Executive briefing center visit
- Peer executive connection facilitation

### 6.3 Executive Sponsor Risk Indicators

Watch for these signs that your executive sponsor relationship is weakening:

- Meeting cancellations or postponements (2+ in a row)
- Delegation of meetings to direct reports without explanation
- Reduced responsiveness to emails or calls
- Less engagement in strategic discussions
- Declining attendance at QBRs or executive events
- Lack of follow-through on commitments made in meetings

**LeadReach Configuration**: Flow Agent tracks executive engagement patterns and alerts CSM/AE when interaction frequency declines below threshold.

### 8.2 Industry Conference Strategy

Maximize executive engagement at industry conferences:

**Pre-Conference**:
1. Identify target executives attending (Scout Agent monitors registration lists and social signals)
2. Research their speaking topics and panel participation
3. Prepare personalized insights relevant to their stated interests
4. Schedule meetings 2-3 weeks before the event
5. Coordinate with partner executives who may have relationships

**At-Conference**:
1. Attend their speaking sessions and prepare thoughtful questions
2. Connect during networking events with context-relevant insights
3. Host a private executive breakfast or lunch for top targets
4. Facilitate peer introductions that add value to the executive
5. Take notes on their stated priorities and challenges

**Post-Conference**:
1. Follow up within 48 hours with meeting-specific insights
2. Share relevant content that builds on your conversation
3. Propose a deeper briefing based on their expressed interests
4. Document all intelligence in CRM for team-wide benefit
5. Track engagement progression via Flow Agent
