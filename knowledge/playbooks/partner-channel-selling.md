# Partner & Channel Selling — Playbook

This playbook is the canonical reference for building, managing, and scaling LeadReach's partner and channel ecosystem. It covers partner identification, onboarding, co-selling motions, revenue sharing, and partner lifecycle management. Used by Atlas (Orchestrator), Scout (Partner Discovery), Bard (Partner Communication), Flow (Deal Registration), and Echo (Partner Analytics).

## 1. Why Partner & Channel Selling Matters

Partner-sourced and partner-influenced revenue is the most capital-efficient growth lever in B2B SaaS. Companies with mature partner programs generate **27% of revenue through channels** (Forrester, 2025) at a CAC that is 40-60% lower than direct sales.

For LeadReach specifically, partners create value in three ways:

1. **Pipeline generation**: Partners refer clients who need outbound tooling, creating net-new pipeline at zero marginal CAC.
2. **Deal acceleration**: Partners who implement LeadReach reduce deployment friction, shortening sales cycles by 25-40%.
3. **Market expansion**: Partners with presence in underserved geographies or verticals extend LeadReach's reach without headcount investment.

The challenge: most partner programs fail because they treat partners as a free sales team rather than a business that needs to be built, enabled, and incentivized like any other revenue channel.

## 3. Partner Lifecycle Management

### 3.1 Partner Recruitment (Scout + Atlas, Ongoing)

Scout agent identifies potential partners through:
- LinkedIn: companies listing "RevOps consulting," "sales enablement," "outbound optimization" in their services
- Conference attendee lists: implementation partners at SaaStr, Dreamforce, HubSpot INBOUND
- Customer recommendations: "Who helped you implement LeadReach?"
- Technology ecosystem: companies with certified integrations on LeadReach's marketplace

Atlas agent evaluates partner fit using a **Partner Scorecard**:

| Criterion | Weight | Scoring |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| Client overlap (same ICP) | 25% | High: >50% clients in LeadReach ICP |
| Service capability (can they implement?) | 20% | Certified consultants; proven methodology |
| Market reach (geography / vertical) | 20% | Access to LeadReach's underserved markets |
| Complementary technology | 15% | CRM, data, intent integrations |
| Financial stability | 10% | Revenue, growth, funding |
| Cultural alignment | 10% | Values, customer-first approach |

Partners scoring >70 are prioritized for recruitment outreach.

## 4. Co-Selling Motions

### 4.1 Partner-Sourced Deal (Partner finds the opportunity)

**Workflow**:
1. Partner identifies an opportunity in their client base
2. Partner registers the deal in LeadReach partner portal (Flow agent validates: no duplicate in CRM)
3. Deal is routed to AE in the prospect's territory with "partner-sourced" tag
4. AE and partner jointly execute the sales process:
   - Partner: introduces LeadReach; provides client context; manages relationship
   - AE: runs demo, handles pricing, manages procurement
5. Deal closes: partner receives revenue share per tier

**Revenue share calculation**: 
- Partner-sourced: Revenue share × first-year ACV
- Example: Gold partner, $100K ACV = 20% × $100K = $20K partner payout

### 4.2 Partner-Influenced Deal (AE finds the opportunity; partner adds value)

**Workflow**:
1. AE identifies an opportunity that would benefit from partner involvement
2. AE requests partner engagement through the partner portal
3. Partner provides: implementation planning, technical assessment, or client reference
4. Deal closes: partner receives reduced revenue share (50% of sourced rate)

**Revenue share calculation**:
- Partner-influenced: 50% of sourced rate × first-year ACV
- Example: Gold partner influenced, $100K ACV = 10% × $100K = $10K partner payout

### 4.3 Co-Sell to Existing Customer (Partner sells services to LeadReach customer)

**Workflow**:
1. LeadReach CSM identifies a customer who could benefit from partner services (implementation, optimization, training)
2. CSM makes a warm introduction to partner
3. Partner sells services to the LeadReach customer
4. No revenue share to LeadReach (this is a value-add for customer retention)

### 4.4 Reseller Deal (Partner resells LeadReach under their brand)

**Workflow**:
1. Partner identifies opportunity and handles the entire sales process
2. Partner issues their own contract to the end customer
3. LeadReach provides the platform at a discounted rate (partner margin: 20-30%)
4. Partner handles onboarding, support (Tier 1), and billing
5. LeadReach provides Tier 2/3 support and platform access

## 5. Deal Registration and Conflict Resolution

### 5.1 Deal Registration Rules

| Rule | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Registration window | Deal must be registered before first AE contact |
| Exclusivity | First registrant gets 90-day exclusive on the deal |
| Validation | Flow agent validates against CRM: no duplicate, prospect fits ICP |
| Minimum deal size | $25K ACV to qualify for deal registration |
| Registration completeness | Must include: company name, contact name, title, estimated deal size, expected close date |
| Renewal protection | LeadReach direct team owns renewals; partners can influence but not register |

### 5.2 Conflict Resolution

| Conflict Type | Resolution |
|---------------|-----------|
| Two partners register the same deal | First registrant wins; second is notified and offered co-sell opportunity |
| Partner registers a deal already in direct pipeline | Direct pipeline wins; partner offered influence role at reduced share |
| Partner-sourced deal goes to direct team | Partner retains sourced credit; revenue share honored |
| Customer prefers direct relationship | Partner credit maintained; customer preference respected |
| Partner dispute on deal ownership | Atlas mediates; VP Partnerships makes final call within 5 business days |

## 10. Objection Handling

| Objection | Root Cause | Response |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| "We don't want to refer tools we haven't used extensively" | Product familiarity gap | "We offer a free partner sandbox and certification program. Most partners feel confident after 2-3 implementations." |
| "Our clients don't ask for outbound tools" | Market positioning mismatch | "That might be an opportunity. When you're implementing CRM or RevOps, outbound is often the next question. We can help you proactively offer it." |
| "The revenue share isn't worth the effort" | Incentive misalignment | "Our top partners earn $200K+ annually. And that's just direct revenue — the bigger win is client retention and expansion. Clients who adopt LeadReach with your implementation stay longer." |
| "We already recommend [competitor]" | Competitive lock-in | "We're not asking you to switch — we're asking you to offer choice. Many clients prefer [LeadReach differentiator]. Let's discuss a co-existence model." |
| "We don't have bandwidth for certification" | Resource constraint | "Certification takes 8-10 hours and can be completed online. We also offer a 'referral-only' track if you prefer to start without implementation capabilities." |
| "Deal registration creates conflict with our process" | Process friction | "We've streamlined deal registration to take <5 minutes. And it protects your investment — without registration, another partner or our direct team could engage the same prospect." |

## 12. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

| Pitfall | Cause | Prevention |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| Partner recruited but never activated | No onboarding support; partner loses interest | 30-60-90 day onboarding plan; dedicated partner manager for Platinum/Gold |
| Channel conflict with direct sales | Partner registers deal AE is already working | Flow agent validates every registration against CRM; clear conflict resolution rules |
| Partners referring low-quality deals | No qualification criteria for registered deals | Judge agent scores every registered deal; reject deals below ICP threshold |
| Revenue share disputes | Unclear attribution; deals close without partner credit | Mandatory deal registration before engagement; Flow agent tracks attribution |
| Partner certification lapses | No recertification process; partners forget | Forge agent tracks certification dates; automated reminders 60/30/14 days before expiry |
| No partner marketing support | Partners expected to market on their own | Co-marketing fund for Platinum/Gold; joint webinars, content, events |
| Over-reliance on a few partners | 80% of partner revenue from 3 partners | Scout continuously recruits new partners; diversification target: no partner >15% of channel revenue |

## 14. Example Scenario

**Partner**: Velocity RevOps, a 25-person RevOps consultancy (Gold tier partner)

**Partner profile**: Implements CRM + sales tech for mid-market SaaS companies. 60% client overlap with LeadReach ICP. 3 certified LeadReach consultants.

**Co-sell motion executed**:
1. **Day 0**: Velocity RevOps identifies an opportunity at DataSync Corp (200 employees, SaaS, evaluating outbound platforms). Partner registers the deal in the portal.
2. **Day 0**: Flow agent validates — DataSync is not in LeadReach CRM. Deal registration approved. 90-day exclusivity granted.
3. **Day 1**: Deal routed to AE Sarah (DataSync's territory). Sarah reaches out to Velocity's lead consultant, Mark, for context.
4. **Day 2**: Mark provides context: DataSync's VP Sales is frustrated with current manual outbound; 15 SDRs need better tooling; budget approved for Q3. Mark offers to make the introduction.
5. **Day 3**: Mark sends warm intro email: VP Sales at DataSync → AE Sarah. VP Sales replies same day.
6. **Day 5**: Discovery call. Sarah runs the meeting; Mark joins to provide RevOps context. Strong fit identified.
7. **Day 10**: Demo with DataSync team. Mark provides implementation perspective: "We can have your team fully migrated and operational in 3 weeks."
8. **Day 18**: Proposal sent. DataSync negotiates pricing; Sarah handles with Atlas support.
9. **Day 25**: Contract signed. 20 seats, Growth tier, $46K ACV.
10. **Day 26**: Flow agent triggers revenue share: 20% × $46K = $9,200 to Velocity RevOps.
11. **Day 27**: Velocity begins implementation. 3-week timeline. Dedicated CSM assigned.
12. **Day 48**: Implementation complete. DataSync live on LeadReach. Mark's team provides training.

**Outcome**: $46K ACV deal with partner-sourced pipeline at zero direct CAC. Velocity earns $9,200. DataSync gets seamless implementation. LeadReach gets a reference-able customer.
