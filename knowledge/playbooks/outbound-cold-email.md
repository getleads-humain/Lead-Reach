# Outbound Cold Email — Playbook

This playbook is the canonical reference for the Bard agent's email-composition skills. It covers structure, sequencing, personalization, compliance, and measurement for B2B cold email across regions.

## 1. The Anatomy of a Cold Email

A high-converting cold email has six elements, in this order:

1. **Subject line** — 3–7 words; lowercase; no caps; no exclamation marks; promise a specific outcome, not a product.
2. **Personalization hook** — 1–2 lines referencing a recent, verifiable event in the prospect's world (funding, hiring, product launch, leadership change, earnings call).
3. **Problem framing** — 2–3 lines articulating the problem the prospect likely faces, anchored in the personalization hook.
4. **Solution positioning** — 1–2 lines positioning LeadReach as the answer, with one quantified proof point.
5. **Soft CTA** — 1 line asking for a 15-minute call (not a demo, not a "quick chat").
6. **Signature** — Name, title, company, phone, physical address (CAN-SPAM), LinkedIn profile link.

Total target length: 80–130 words. Shorter is better.

### Subject Line Patterns That Convert

| Pattern | Example | Open Rate | When to Use |
|---------|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Question | `quick question about [company]'s outbound` | 45–60% | Default; works across verticals |
| Reference | `[peer company] / [vendor]` | 40–55% | When you have a strong peer reference |
| Curiosity | `worth a 15-min call?` | 35–50% | Last resort; no trigger available |
| Trigger-based | `saw your Series B — congrats` | 50–70% | When you have a fresh trigger event |
| Personal | `[first name] — quick one` | 35–45% | Follow-up emails; familiar contacts |
| Direct | `pipeline for [company]` | 30–40% | When targeting ops/finance roles |
| Value-prop | `30% more pipeline in 60 days` | 25–40% | Performance-oriented buyers (VP Sales) |
| Social proof | `[peer] just did this` | 40–55% | When peer reference is compelling |

Avoid: ALL CAPS, exclamation marks, the word "free", product names in subject lines, emoji in B2B, question marks in follow-ups (feels pushy), "Re:" prefix (deceptive unless actually replying).

### Subject Line A/B Testing Framework

Every new sequence should launch with at least 2 subject line variants. Follow this testing protocol:

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Write 4–6 candidate subject lines | Pre-launch |
| 2 | Select top 2 based on pattern match | Pre-launch |
| 3 | Split test: 50/50 across first 200 sends | Day 0–3 |
| 4 | Measure open rates (minimum 100 per variant) | Day 3–5 |
| 5 | Declare winner if delta >5% and statistically significant | Day 5 |
| 6 | Run winner; archive loser with performance data | Day 5+ |
| 7 | Quarterly: analyze all subject line data for pattern trends | Quarterly |

**Statistical significance calculator**: Use a two-proportion z-test. Minimum confidence level: 90% (p < 0.10). For marginal results, extend the test to 300+ per variant.

### Personalization Hook Examples

**Funding trigger:**
> "Saw the news about Acme's $40M Series B — congrats. With GTM scaling, most SDR teams at that stage struggle with research time..."

**Hiring trigger:**
> "Noticed you're hiring 3 SDRs in Austin — looks like outbound is a focus. Quick thought: most SDRs spend 60% of their day on research..."

**Product launch trigger:**
> "Saw the launch of [Product] — interesting positioning against [Competitor]. One question that came to mind: how are you sourcing the ICP accounts for the launch?"

**Leadership change trigger:**
> "Congrats on the new CRO role at Acme. Most new CROs in their first 90 days focus on pipeline coverage — quick thought..."

**Earnings call trigger:**
> "Heard [CEO name] mention 'sales efficiency' on the Q2 call — sounds like that's a priority. One approach that's helped [peer] improve SDR efficiency by 40%..."

**Competitor displacement trigger:**
> "Saw [competitor] had an outage last week — rough. If your team's evaluating alternatives for [use case], worth a quick conversation..."

**Conference/event trigger:**
> "Great talk at [conference] on [topic]. The point about [specific insight] resonated — most teams we work with face the same challenge..."

### Problem Framing Techniques

The problem framing must bridge from the personalization hook to your solution. Use one of these three techniques:

**Technique 1: Statistical Anchor**
Cite a specific, credible statistic that frames the problem:
> "Most SDR teams spend 60% of their day on research, not selling (TOPO benchmark, 2024). That's 4.8 hours per rep per day on manual account research."

**Technique 2: Peer Mirror**
Reference a peer company that had the same problem:
> "When [peer company] was at your stage, they found their SDRs were spending most of their day on research instead of conversations..."

**Technique 3: Consequence Chain**
Trace the consequence of the problem to a business outcome the prospect cares about:
> "When SDRs spend 60% of their time on research, the math is brutal: a $60K SDR costs $24K/year in research time, and books 40% fewer meetings than a research-supported rep."

### Soft CTA Examples

**Standard CTAs (first email):**
- "Worth a 15-minute call next Tuesday or Wednesday?"
- "Open to a 15-minute conversation next week?"
- "If this resonates, would 15 minutes next Tuesday work?"

**Interest-check CTAs (lower friction):**
- "Mind if I send over a short case study first?"
- "Curious if this is a priority right now — worth a quick note back either way?"
- "Want me to send a 2-min video showing how this works?"

**Breakup CTAs (final email):**
- "Should I close your file on this?"
- "Totally understand if timing's off — should I circle back in Q3?"
- "No worries if this isn't a fit right now. Just let me know so I can update my records."

### What NOT to Do

- **Don't pitch in the first email.** The goal of email #1 is a reply, not a sale.
- **Don't use "I hope this finds you well"** — clichéd, immediately ignored.
- **Don't ask for a 30-minute demo** — too high friction. Ask for 15 minutes.
- **Don't send attachments in cold email #1** — triggers spam filters.
- **Don't use tracking pixels in GDPR-regulated markets** without consent.
- **Don't follow up the same day** with multiple touches — feels desperate.
- **Don't use "Just checking in"** — provides zero value, signals laziness.
- **Don't use HTML-heavy templates** — plain text outperforms HTML in cold email 2:1.
- **Don't include multiple CTAs** — one ask per email, always.
- **Don't reference the prospect's competitors negatively** — unprofessional.
- **Don't use "quick" or "brief" to describe your product** — undermines value.
- **Don't apologize for reaching out** — you're offering value, not intruding.

## 2. The 21-Day Sequence (SaaS B2B — US)

This is the canonical Bard sequence for SaaS B2B in the US. Adapt for region and industry.

| Day | Channel | Content |
|-----|---------|---------|
| 0 | LinkedIn | Connection request, 2-line personalized note (no pitch) |
| 2 | Email | Email #1 — problem-led, soft CTA |
| 5 | LinkedIn | Like + thoughtful comment on recent post |
| 7 | Email | Email #2 — case study from same industry |
| 10 | Cold Call | Reference the emails; ask for 15 minutes |
| 14 | Email | Email #3 — new case study + soft CTA |
| 17 | LinkedIn DM | Soft re-engagement |
| 21 | Email | Breakup email ("should I close your file?") |

Expected performance (with tight ICP, fresh data, well-written copy):
- **Open rate**: 50–65%
- **Reply rate**: 4–8%
- **Meeting-booked rate**: 2.5–4%
- **Positive reply rate**: 60–75% of replies

## 3. Compliance Checklist (Per Region)

### US (CAN-SPAM + state privacy laws)

- [ ] Physical postal address in every email
- [ ] Clear, working unsubscribe link in every email
- [ ] Honor unsubscribes within 10 business days
- [ ] No deceptive subject lines
- [ ] Accurate From name and email
- [ ] CCPA: opt-out link for "sale" or "sharing" of personal data
- [ ] DNC scrub for cold calls (every 31 days)
- [ ] Colorado Privacy Act: consent for targeted advertising
- [ ] Virginia CDPA: data protection assessment for high-risk processing
- [ ] CT Data Privacy Act: universal opt-out mechanism recognition

### EU (GDPR + ePrivacy)

- [ ] Documented Legitimate Interests Assessment (LIA) for B2B outreach
- [ ] Consent for B2B cold email in Germany, Hungary (where required)
- [ ] Clear opt-out in every email
- [ ] No tracking pixels without consent
- [ ] SCCs or EU-US DPF for US data transfers
- [ ] Appoint EU representative (Article 27) if processing at scale
- [ ] Record of Processing Activities (ROPA) maintained
- [ ] Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) for high-volume profiling
- [ ] Privacy notice accessible from email footer
- [ ] Breach notification procedure documented (72-hour BSA threshold)

### UK (UK GDPR + PECR)

- [ ] Documented LIA for B2B outreach
- [ ] Clear opt-out in every email
- [ ] Sender ID not concealed
- [ ] IDTA or UK Addendum for US data transfers
- [ ] TPS scrub for cold calls
- [ ] UK representative appointed if no UK establishment

### APAC (PDPA Singapore + variants)

- [ ] Deemed consent source documented for each contact
- [ ] Clear opt-out in every email
- [ ] DNC scrub (Singapore) for cold calls
- [ ] PDPA-compliant DPA template ready
- [ ] Japan APPI: honor opt-out requests promptly
- [ ] Australia Spam Act: consent or existing relationship documented

## 4. Measurement Framework

Track the following metrics for every sequence:

**Top-of-funnel:**
- **Sent volume** — emails sent per sequence per week
- **Open rate** — unique opens / delivered
- **Reply rate** — replies / delivered
- **Bounce rate** — bounces / sent (target < 2%)
- **Spam complaint rate** — complaints / delivered (target < 0.1%)

**Mid-funnel:**
- **Positive reply rate** — positive replies / total replies (target > 60%)
- **Meeting-booked rate** — meetings / delivered
- **Meeting-attended rate** — attended / booked (target > 70%)

**Bottom-funnel:**
- **SQL conversion rate** — SQLs / meetings attended
- **Win rate** — closed-won / SQLs
- **Cycle time** — days from first touch to closed-won

### Diagnostic Patterns

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| Low open rate (<30%) | Subject line / sender reputation / spam filter | Test subject lines; check domain reputation; warm up new sending domains |
| High open + low reply | Email content not resonating | Rewrite copy; tighter ICP; better personalization |
| High reply + low positive reply | ICP wrong / messaging wrong | Re-examine ICP; rewrite copy |
| High positive reply + low meeting booked | CTA too soft / friction too high | Tighter CTA; reduce scheduling friction (Calendly link) |
| High meeting booked + low attended | Confirmation process weak | Add reminder emails 24h and 1h before |
| High attended + low SQL | Discovery process weak | Train AEs on discovery; tighter qualification criteria |
| High SQL + low win rate | Value prop / pricing misalignment | Review competitive losses; adjust pricing/positioning |
| Reply rate declining over time | Sender reputation decay / ICP fatigue | Rotate sending domains; refresh ICP; new messaging angles |

### Advanced Diagnostics — Cohort Analysis

Beyond simple diagnostic patterns, track performance by **cohort** to identify systemic issues:

| Cohort Dimension | What It Reveals | Action If Underperforming |
|-----------------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| By ICP segment | Which segments convert best | Shift volume to best segments; revise ICP for worst |
| By trigger type | Which triggers produce most meetings | Prioritize highest-signal triggers in Sage |
| By sending domain | Domain reputation issues | Warm up underperforming domains; retire damaged ones |
| By day of week | Optimal send timing | Shift volume to best days |
| By time of day | Optimal send window | Schedule sends for best window |
| By rep | Individual rep performance | Coach underperformers; study top performers |
| By sequence version | Copy/sequence effectiveness | A/B test improvements; promote winners |
| By industry vertical | Vertical-specific messaging fit | Customize sequences for weak verticals |

## 5. Bard's Email Composition Workflow

When the Bard agent is invoked to compose an email:

1. **Read the prospect context** — name, title, company, industry, region, recent triggers, peer references.
2. **Select a personalization hook** — choose the highest-signal trigger (funding > hiring > product > leadership > generic).
3. **Select a peer reference** — from the industry doc, choose a case study from the same industry, ideally same sub-vertical and similar size.
4. **Draft subject line** — 3–7 words, lowercase, no exclamation, promise outcome not product.
5. **Draft body** — 80–130 words, follow the six-element structure above.
6. **Compliance check** — verify physical address, unsubscribe link, accurate sender, opt-out language per region.
7. **Personalization review** — verify the personalization hook is accurate (e.g., funding date is correct).
8. **Length check** — under 130 words.
9. **CTA review** — single CTA, 15-minute ask, soft phrasing.
10. **Output** — return subject + body + signature, ready for the sequencing tool.

## 6. Email Deliverability

Deliverability is the foundation of cold email. No amount of copywriting skill matters if emails land in spam.

### Domain Warmup Protocol

New sending domains must be warmed up over 14–21 days:

| Day | Daily Volume | Ramp Rate | Monitoring |
|-----|-------------|-----------|------------|
| 1–3 | 10–20 | Start low | Check spam placement daily |
| 4–7 | 30–50 | +10/day | Monitor bounce rate <2% |
| 8–11 | 50–80 | +10/day | Monitor complaint rate <0.05% |
| 12–14 | 80–100 | +10/day | Full deliverability test |
| 15+ | Scale to target | +20/day max | Weekly reputation check |

### Sender Reputation Checklist

- **SPF record** configured for sending domain
- **DKIM signature** enabled (1024-bit minimum, 2048-bit recommended)
- **DMARC policy** set to `p=reject` or `p=quarantine` with `rua` reporting
- **Reverse DNS (PTR)** record matches sending IP
- **IP reputation** clean (check via Talos, Sender Score, Barracuda)
- **Dedicated IP** for high-volume sending (>5K emails/day)
- **Domain age** >30 days before cold outreach
- **Google Postmaster Tools** configured and monitoring
- **Bounce rate** maintained below 2%
- **Spam complaint rate** maintained below 0.1%
- **Unsubscribe rate** below 1%

### Deliverability Troubleshooting

| Issue | Symptom | Diagnosis | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----------|-----|
| Spam placement | 0% open rate on verified sends | Test with seed addresses across providers | Check SPF/DKIM/DMARC; warm up domain; reduce volume |
| Inbox clipping | Gmail clips email at 102KB | Email HTML too large | Switch to plain text; reduce image count |
| Bulking (Promotions tab) | Opens low, not in spam | HTML-heavy content + tracking | Use plain text; remove tracking pixels; shorten |
| Bounce spike | >5% bounce rate | Stale list or poor data quality | Re-verify emails; use Forge enrichment; check email syntax |
| Throttling | Sends delayed by ESP | Sending too fast from new domain | Reduce send rate; add delays between sends |
| Blacklist | 0% delivery to specific ISP | IP or domain on blacklist | Check blacklist status; request delisting; switch IP |

## 7. Advanced Tactics

### 7.1 The "Interest Bait" Sequence

Instead of asking for a meeting in email #1, offer value and gauge interest:

**Email #1**: "Published a benchmark report on [topic relevant to their role]. Want me to send it?" — If they reply "yes", they've self-qualified interest.

**Email #2** (after yes): Send the report + "Worth 15 minutes to discuss how these numbers apply to [company]?"

This two-step approach produces 2-3x higher positive reply rates because the prospect has already engaged with your content before being asked for a meeting.

### 7.2 The "Champion Multiplier" Sequence

When one contact at an account engages, ask for introductions to other stakeholders:

> "Great connecting, [name]. Two quick questions:
> 1. Who else on your team would benefit from this conversation?
> 2. Is there anyone I should brief separately — your VP, IT team, procurement?"

This seeds multi-threaded engagement from the first positive interaction.

### 7.3 The "Competitor Displacement" Play

When Sage detects a competitor problem (outage, bad review, contract expiry):

> "Saw [competitor]'s [issue] — that's rough. If you're evaluating alternatives, [peer company] switched from [competitor] to us 6 months ago and saw [specific result]. Worth a 15-min conversation?"

Timing is critical: this must be sent within 48 hours of the competitor event for maximum relevance.

### 7.4 The "Executive Ghostwriter" Play

For C-suite outreach, write as a peer executive:

**Subject**: `[company]'s pipeline efficiency`

> Hi [first name],
>
> As a [peer role — e.g., fellow CEO], I know pipeline coverage is the #1 thing that keeps us up at night. We built LeadReach because our own SDR team was spending 60% of their day on research.
>
> [Peer CEO] at [peer company] mentioned at [event] that they improved pipeline coverage from 2.5x to 4x after implementing our approach.
>
> Worth a 15-minute call — peer to peer?
>
> — [CEO name]

### 7.5 The "Video Prospecting" Play

Personalized video (60–90 seconds) embedded in email increases reply rates by 3x:

- Record a screen share walking through the prospect's website/LinkedIn
- Highlight 2-3 specific observations
- Close with the soft CTA
- Thumbnail shows the prospect's website (increases open rate)
- Tools: Vidyard, Loom, Hippo Video

### 7.6 The "Trigger Cascade" Play

When multiple trigger events hit the same account in a short window, escalate the approach:

1 trigger → Standard sequence
2 triggers → Fast-track sequence (7-day cycle) + phone call on day 1
3+ triggers → AE direct outreach + executive-to-executive intro attempt

This prevents high-signal accounts from languishing in the standard sequence cadence.

## 9. Objection Handling Scripts

### Objection: "Not interested"
> "Totally understand — timing matters. Mind if I circle back in Q[quarter]? Or if there's a better person to chat with, I'm happy to redirect."

### Objection: "We already use [competitor]"
> "Got it — [competitor] is solid. Out of curiosity, how's the research/enrichment side working for you? We hear from teams that [competitor's weakness] is a gap. No pressure — just curious if that matches your experience."

### Objection: "Send me some info"
> "Happy to. What specifically are you curious about — the research automation, the scoring, or the sequencing? Want to make sure I send something relevant." [Then send a targeted 1-pager, not a generic brochure]

### Objection: "No budget right now"
> "Makes sense — budget cycles are real. When does your next budget cycle start? And is this something that could be funded from a different bucket — like SDR tooling vs. sales tech?"

### Objection: "I'm too busy"
> "I hear that. This won't save you time tomorrow — it saves your SDRs 4 hours a day starting next week. Worth 15 minutes to see if that's worth pursuing when things slow down?"

### Objection: "We build this in-house"
> "Respect that — a lot of teams try. Two questions: how much eng time did it take? And does it cover enrichment, scoring, and sequencing, or just one piece? We find most in-house builds cover 30% of what's needed and cost 3x in eng hours what a subscription would."

## 11. Failure Mode Analysis

| Failure Mode | Frequency | Impact | Root Cause | Detection | Prevention |
|-------------|-----------|--------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Generic personalization | Very high | Low reply rate | Bard using L0-L1 in quality mode | Reply rate <2% | Enforce L3+ for quality sequences |
| Wrong trigger event | Medium | Credibility damage | Stale Sage data | Prospect correction reply | Trigger freshness check (<30 days) |
| Over-personalization | Low | "Creepy" perception | Too many personal details | Negative reply ("How do you know that?") | Max 3-5 personal details per email |
| Compliance violation | Low | Legal risk | Missing unsubscribe/address | Automated compliance check fails | Block send on compliance failure |
| Sender reputation damage | Medium | Deliverability collapse | High bounce/complaint rate | Bounce rate >3%; complaint >0.2% | Email verification pre-send; domain warmup |
| Sequence fatigue | Medium | Declining performance over time | Same prospects seeing same sequence | Reply rate declining 20%+ per quarter | Rotate sequences; refresh messaging quarterly |
| ICP mismatch | High | Low positive reply rate | ICP too broad | Positive reply rate <40% | Tighten ICP; re-score leads |
