# Pricing Negotiation — Playbook

This playbook is the canonical reference for pricing negotiations in LeadReach deals. It covers value anchoring, discount governance, deal structuring, multi-year contracts, and procurement navigation. Used by Atlas (Orchestrator), Judge (Pricing Scoring), Bard (Proposal Composition), and Flow (Deal Tracking).

## 1. Why Pricing Negotiation Matters

Pricing negotiation is where deals are won or lost — not because of the number, but because of the **process**. Poorly managed negotiations:

- **Destroy margin**: A 10% discount on a 3-year deal costs more than the entire CAC.
- **Signal weakness**: Leading with discounts trains buyers to always negotiate.
- **Create bad precedents**: One-off deals become the baseline for renewals.
- **Delay closes**: Unstructured negotiation extends cycles by 2-4 weeks.

Well-managed negotiations:
- **Protect margin** while maximizing close rate
- **Reinforce value** by trading price concessions for commitment (volume, term, referrals)
- **Create urgency** through time-bound offers and multi-year incentives
- **Build trust** through transparency, consistency, and professionalism

LeadReach's negotiation philosophy: **never discount without getting something in return, and never negotiate against yourself.**

## 3. The Value-First Negotiation Framework

### 3.1 Principle: Anchor on Value, Not Price

The buyer's frame: "How much does it cost?"
Your frame: "What is it worth to you?"

Before any pricing discussion, the AE must have quantified:
1. **Current state cost**: What the prospect is losing by NOT having LeadReach (inefficiency, missed pipeline, manual research time)
2. **Future state value**: What LeadReach delivers (more meetings, higher reply rates, faster ramp)
3. **ROI**: Future state value / LeadReach cost — target 3-5x ROI

**If ROI is not quantified, do not send a proposal.** The proposal is a value document, not a price quote.

### 3.2 The TRAC Framework for Negotiation

| Step | Action | Example |
|------|--------|---------|
| **T** — Translate | Translate the objection into a value question | "Is the concern about the total investment, or about the value you'll receive?" |
| **R** — Reframe | Reframe price as investment with return | "At $89/seat, you're investing $1,068/year per SDR. If each SDR books 2 additional meetings per month, that's 24 meetings/year. What's a meeting worth to you?" |
| **A** — Anchor | Anchor on the value, not the list price | "Companies like [peer] see 3.5x ROI in the first quarter. That means for every $1 you invest, you get $3.50 back." |
| **C** — Close | Close on a trade, not a concession | "I can get closer to your number if you can commit to [2-year term / 100+ seats / case study participation]." |

### 3.3 Never Negotiate Against Yourself

Common mistake: the AE offers a discount before the buyer asks. This is negotiating against yourself.

**Rules**:
1. Never volunteer a discount. Wait for the buyer to ask.
2. When the buyer asks for a discount, ask: "What number did you have in mind?" (Let them anchor first.)
3. If the buyer says "we need a better price," respond: "I understand budget is a consideration. Can you help me understand — is this about the total investment, or about fitting it into this quarter's budget?" (Diagnose the real objection.)
4. If you must discount, always trade: "I can offer X if you can do Y." Never give without getting.

## 4. Common Negotiation Scenarios and Playbooks

### Scenario 1: "We need a 30% discount"

**Buyer's real position**: Usually anchoring low. They expect to settle at 10-15%.

**Playbook**:
1. **Acknowledge**: "I appreciate you being direct about budget."
2. **Re-anchor on value**: "At list price, you're looking at a 4x ROI based on our analysis. A 30% discount would make it a 5.7x ROI — which is exceptional."
3. **Trade**: "I can't get to 30%, but here's what I can do: 15% off for a 2-year commitment, with a 5% additional discount if you can commit to 100+ seats. That brings the effective discount to 20% — and you lock in pricing for 2 years."
4. **Urgency**: "This pricing is valid through end of quarter. After that, pricing adjusts with our annual increase."

### Scenario 2: "Your competitor is cheaper"

**Buyer's real position**: They want justification to pay more for LeadReach.

**Playbook**:
1. **Acknowledge**: "I'm not surprised — different platforms price differently."
2. **Differentiate on value, not features**: "The question isn't whether [competitor] is cheaper. It's whether you're comparing equivalent outcomes. Let me show you what [peer company] found when they evaluated both on actual results, not list price."
3. **TCO comparison**: "When you factor in [implementation cost, training time, support quality, data accuracy], the total cost of ownership often favors LeadReach despite a higher per-seat price."
4. **Risk reversal**: "If you choose LeadReach and don't see [specific outcome] in 90 days, we'll refund your investment."

### Scenario 3: "We can only afford $X"

**Buyer's real position**: Budget constraint is real, but often negotiable.

**Playbook**:
1. **Acknowledge**: "I respect your budget. Let's work within it."
2. **Scope adjustment**: "At $X, we'd be looking at [smaller tier / fewer seats / fewer add-ons]. Here's what that looks like..."
3. **Payment structure**: "We can also restructure the payment — quarterly instead of annual, or a phased rollout starting with [core team] and expanding in Q3."
4. **Value threshold**: "I want to make sure you get enough value at this investment level. With [X seats], your team should see [specific outcome]. If we can't deliver that, I'd rather you invest elsewhere."

### Scenario 4: "We need month-to-month, not annual"

**Buyer's real position**: Risk aversion; not ready to commit.

**Playbook**:
1. **Acknowledge**: "I understand wanting flexibility."
2. **Annual incentive**: "Annual pricing is 20% lower than month-to-month. For your team size, that's [$X] in savings."
3. **Risk reversal**: "We offer a 90-day opt-out on annual contracts. If you're not satisfied in the first 90 days, you can cancel with a full refund. After that, you're locked in at the annual rate."
4. **Middle ground**: "Alternatively, we can do a 6-month commitment at a 10% discount. It gives you a shorter commitment while still saving."

### Scenario 5: Multi-Year Deal Negotiation

**Buyer's position**: Willing to commit long-term for deeper discount.

**Playbook**:
1. **Anchor on Year 1**: "Year 1 pricing is [list price]. For a 2-year commitment, we offer 15% off. For 3 years, 20% off."
2. **Escalator clause**: "Annual increases are capped at 5% (vs. our standard 8%). This protects you from future price increases."
3. **Growth clause**: "If you expand beyond [X seats] during the term, additional seats are priced at the same per-seat rate — no tier jump."
4. **Early termination**: "Early termination fee is 50% of remaining contract value. But with our 90-day opt-out, you have a full quarter to validate the investment."
5. **Value stacking**: Show the cumulative savings over the full term: "Over 3 years, this contract saves you [$X] vs. annual renewals at list price."

## 5. Deal Structuring Options

### 5.1 Standard Annual Contract

- List price per seat × number of seats × 12 months
- 0-10% discount for annual commitment
- Standard payment: Net 30, annual upfront
- Quarterly payments available at 5% premium

### 5.2 Multi-Year Contract (2-3 Years)

- 15-20% discount for 2-year commitment
- 20-25% discount for 3-year commitment
- Annual price escalator: 5% (vs. standard 8%)
- Growth clause: additional seats at same per-seat rate
- Early termination: 50% of remaining value

### 5.3 Phased Rollout

- Start with pilot team (e.g., 15-20 seats)
- Expansion milestone: if [success criteria met] within [timeframe], expand to full team
- Pricing: pilot at standard rate; expansion at committed rate (locked in at signing)
- Useful for customers who want to prove value before full commitment

### 5.4 Budget-Allocated Structure

- Split payment across fiscal years (e.g., 60% in Year 1 budget, 40% in Year 2 budget)
- Full contract value committed upfront
- Useful for customers with rigid budget cycles

### 5.5 Partner-Sponsored Deal

- Implementation partner buys LeadReach on behalf of their client
- 15-20% partner discount
- Partner handles onboarding and training
- Useful for customers who prefer vendor consolidation

## 7. Target Persona and ICP Criteria

### Negotiation Personas

**The Procurement Professional (35% of negotiations)**:
- Trained negotiator; follows structured process
- Motivated by cost reduction and risk mitigation
- Strategy: Lead with ROI; trade concessions for commitment; use benchmarking data

**The Budget-Conscious Executive (30% of negotiations)**:
- CRO or VP Sales who wants LeadReach but has limited budget
- Motivated by outcomes but constrained by reality
- Strategy: Scope adjustment; phased rollout; payment restructuring

**The Value Buyer (20% of negotiations)**:
- Wants to understand the ROI before committing
- Motivated by proof and data
- Strategy: Quantified ROI model; reference calls; pilot program; money-back guarantee

**The Price Shopper (15% of negotiations)**:
- Comparing on price alone; not considering total value
- Motivated by the lowest number
- Strategy: TCO comparison; risk reversal; willingness to walk away

## 10. Objection Handling

| Objection | Root Cause | Response |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| "That's too expensive" | Value not established | "Compared to what? If you're comparing to [competitor], let's look at total cost of ownership. If you're comparing to doing nothing, let's look at what you're losing each month without a solution." |
| "We don't have budget this quarter" | Timing constraint | "When does your next budget cycle start? We can structure the contract to align — sign now, bill later. This locks in current pricing." |
| "Can you match [competitor]'s price?" | Price shopping | "We don't price-match because we're not the same product. Let me show you the differences in outcomes — [peer company] evaluated both and found LeadReach delivered [X] more value at a slightly higher price." |
| "We need a PO by end of week but need 25% off" | Procurement pressure | "I can get to 15% with a 2-year commitment by end of week. Beyond that requires VP approval, which takes 3-5 business days. Want me to submit the 15% deal now?" |
| "Our board wants to see three options" | Process requirement | "Happy to provide three options: [Standard tier at list], [Growth tier with annual discount], [Enterprise tier with multi-year discount]. Each includes an ROI projection." |
| "If we can't get 30% off, we'll go with [competitor]" | Negotiation tactic / real competitive threat | "I respect that. Before you decide, let me share one thing: [specific LeadReach advantage that competitor can't match]. If that matters, it might be worth reconsidering the savings." |
| "We want month-to-month with no commitment" | Risk aversion | "We offer a 90-day opt-out on annual contracts. That gives you the flexibility of month-to-month with the savings of an annual commitment. Best of both worlds." |

## 12. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

| Pitfall | Cause | Prevention |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| Discounting too early | AE leads with discount to "build goodwill" | No discount before value is established. Atlas agent verifies ROI model exists before proposal. |
| No approval workflow | AE offers 20% discount without manager approval | Flow agent blocks proposal generation for discounts >10% without approval |
| Inconsistent pricing | Same deal size gets different discounts from different AEs | Standard discount matrix; Judge agent validates every proposal against policy |
| Price anchoring too low | AE starts negotiation at the lowest acceptable price | Start at list price (or standard 10% annual discount); negotiate from there |
| Giving without getting | AE makes concessions without trades | Every concession must have a corresponding ask (commitment, seats, term, case study) |
| No BATNA awareness | AE doesn't know their walk-away point | Atlas agent sets walk-away price before negotiation begins |
| Ignoring the procurement timeline | Deal stalls because procurement needs 6 weeks | Sage researches procurement timeline early; build it into the deal plan |

## 14. Example Scenario

**Account**: TechVista Solutions, 120-person SaaS company

**Deal**: 30 seats, Growth tier, list price $46,440/year

**Negotiation timeline**:
1. **Day 0**: AE sends value-anchored proposal at list price ($129/seat × 30 × 12 = $46,440). ROI model shows 4.2x return.
2. **Day 3**: VP Sales responds: "We like it but need it closer to $35K." (That's a 25% discount request.)
3. **Day 3**: AE uses TRAC framework: "I appreciate the direct feedback. Can you help me understand — is this about fitting it into this quarter's budget, or about the value you're getting?" VP: "Honestly, both."
4. **Day 4**: AE proposes trade: "I can get to $39,474 (15% off) with a 2-year commitment. That locks in pricing and saves you [$X] vs. two annual renewals. For the budget concern, I can split payment: 60% in Q2, 40% in Q3."
5. **Day 5**: VP: "We can do 2 years, but we need it under $38K." (18% discount request.)
6. **Day 5**: AE escalates to Sales Manager (discount 15-20% requires manager approval). Manager approves 18% with conditions: 2-year commitment + case study participation.
7. **Day 6**: AE sends revised proposal: $38,080/year (18% off), 2-year term, annual escalator 5%, case study participation required, payment 60/40 split across quarters.
8. **Day 8**: VP approves. Contract routed to procurement.
9. **Day 15**: Procurement completes review. Contract signed.
10. **Day 16**: Deal closed. Margin: 72% (within Growth tier target).

**Outcome**: 18% discount (within policy), 2-year commitment secured (higher LTV), case study commitment (marketing value), payment structure accommodates budget.
