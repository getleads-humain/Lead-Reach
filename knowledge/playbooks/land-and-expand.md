# Land and Expand — Playbook

This playbook is the canonical reference for LeadReach's land-and-expand growth strategy — acquiring customers with a small initial deployment and systematically expanding across teams, departments, and geographies. It covers initial land strategy, adoption acceleration, expansion signal detection, multi-department selling, and land-and-expand metrics. Used by Atlas (Orchestrator), Echo (Adoption Monitoring), Scout (Stakeholder Discovery), Bard (Expansion Outreach), and Flow (Expansion Tracking).

## 1. Why Land and Expand Matters

Land and expand is the most capital-efficient growth model in enterprise SaaS. The principle is simple: **sell small, prove value, then expand.** The economics are compelling:

- **Lower initial CAC**: A small deployment (5-15 seats) is easier to sell than a company-wide rollout (100+ seats).
- **Higher win rate**: The initial land deal has less friction, fewer stakeholders, and a shorter cycle.
- **Expansion is 5-7x cheaper**: Selling to an existing customer who's already seeing value is dramatically easier than new-logo acquisition.
- **Compounding growth**: Each expansion creates more internal advocates, making the next expansion easier.
- **Higher NRR**: Companies that master land-and-expand achieve 120-140% net revenue retention.

The challenge: most companies "land" well but fail to "expand." They close the initial deal, deliver value to a small team, and never systematically drive adoption across the organization. The expansion happens accidentally — or not at all.

LeadReach's approach: **the expansion motion begins on Day 1 of the land deal.** Every customer interaction, onboarding milestone, and success metric is designed to create the conditions for expansion.

## 2. The Land-and-Expand Model

### 2.1 Land Strategy

| Land Type | Typical Entry Point | ACV Range | Sales Motion | Expansion Potential |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **PLG Land** | Single SDR signs up for free trial | $0-$3K | Self-serve | High — can expand to whole team |
| **Team Land** | SDR team (5-15 seats) purchases | $3K-$20K | SDR/AE | High — can expand to other teams |
| **Department Land** | Sales department (15-50 seats) purchases | $20K-$75K | AE | Medium — can expand to marketing, CS |
| **Division Land** | Full sales org (50-200 seats) purchases | $75K-$300K | AE + SE | Medium — can expand to other regions |

### 2.2 Expansion Paths

| Expansion Path | From | To | Typical Timeline | ACV Growth |
|---------------|------|----|-----------------|-----------|
| **Seat expansion** | 5-15 seats | 30-100 seats | 3-6 months | 3-6x |
| **Department expansion** | Sales | Marketing, CS, Partners | 6-12 months | 2-4x |
| **Geography expansion** | US team | EMEA, APAC teams | 6-18 months | 2-5x |
| **Tier upgrade** | Professional | Growth, Enterprise | 3-12 months | 2-3x |
| **Feature expansion** | Core sequences | Analytics, SSO, API | 3-12 months | 1.5-2x |

### 2.3 The Expansion Flywheel

```
Land → Prove Value → Create Advocates → Identify Expansion Signal → Sell Expansion → Prove Value (again) → ...
```

Each successful expansion reinforces the next:
1. **Land**: Small team adopts LeadReach
2. **Prove Value**: Team achieves measurable outcomes (higher reply rates, more meetings)
3. **Create Advocates**: Team members become internal champions
4. **Identify Signal**: Other teams/departments notice the results
5. **Sell Expansion**: Leverage advocates to sell to new teams
6. **Prove Value (again)**: New team achieves outcomes; cycle repeats

## 3. The Land-and-Expand Workflow

### Phase 1: The Land (Days 0-30)

#### Step 1.1: Strategic Land Planning (Atlas, Pre-Deal)

Before closing the initial deal, Atlas agent evaluates the expansion potential:

| Factor | High Expansion Potential | Low Expansion Potential |
|--------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Company size | 200+ employees | <50 employees |
| Number of sales teams | 3+ | 1 |
| Geography | Multi-region | Single office |
| Industry | SaaS, FinTech (high outbound) | Small local business |
| Champion level | VP+ | IC |
| Procurement | Flexible | Rigid, enterprise-wide mandate |

If expansion potential is high, the initial deal is priced strategically:
- **Lower entry price**: Reduce friction to close; the real value is in expansion
- **Shorter commitment**: Monthly or 6-month; easier to approve
- **Success criteria defined**: Pre-agreed metrics that trigger expansion conversations
- **Expansion option built into contract**: "Customer may add up to [X] seats at [Y] per-seat rate for 12 months"

#### Step 1.2: Onboarding for Expansion (CSM, Days 0-14)

Onboarding is designed not just to activate the initial team, but to create visibility for expansion:

| Day | Activity | Expansion Purpose |
|-----|----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Kickoff call: set success criteria | Defines the metrics that prove value |
| 3 | Email + CRM integration | Creates cross-team visibility |
| 7 | First sequence sent | Quick win for advocacy |
| 10 | First results review | Document outcomes for expansion case |
| 14 | Success criteria check | Validate value; introduce expansion concept |

**CSM conversation at Day 14**:
> "Your team is hitting [X% reply rate] — great results. I'm curious: are other teams at [company] doing similar outbound? We often see marketing and customer success teams adopt LeadReach after the sales team proves the value."

#### Step 1.3: Quick Wins Documentation (Echo, Days 14-30)

Echo agent documents every quick win:
- First reply received
- First meeting booked
- First deal influenced by LeadReach-sourced meeting
- Reply rate improvement vs. pre-LeadReach baseline
- Time saved per SDR per week

Output: **Quick Wins Report** — a one-page summary of results that the CSM shares with the champion and their leadership.

### Phase 2: Prove Value and Create Advocates (Days 30-90)

#### Step 2.1: Value Realization Milestone (Echo + CSM, Day 30)

At Day 30, Echo generates a **Value Realization Report**:

| Metric | Before LeadReach | After LeadReach | Improvement |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|-------------|
| Reply rate | X% | Y% | +Z% |
| Meetings booked per SDR per week | X | Y | +Z |
| Time spent on research per sequence | X hours | Y hours | -Z hours |
| Pipeline sourced from outbound | $X | $Y | +Z% |
| SDR ramp time | X weeks | Y weeks | -Z weeks |

CSM shares this with the champion and asks: **"Who else should see this?"**

This question is the single most important expansion trigger. It's not pushy — it's a natural follow-up to a success story.

#### Step 2.2: Advocate Identification (Scout, Days 30-60)

Scout agent identifies potential advocates beyond the original champion:

| Signal | Source | Expansion Implication |
|--------|--------|----------------------|
| Non-champion user achieving high results | Product analytics | Potential advocate in another team |
| Executive mentioned LeadReach in meeting | CSM notes | Executive sponsor for expansion |
| Team member shared LeadReach on social | Scout social monitoring | Public advocate; high credibility |
| Other department asked about LeadReach | Support tickets / CSM notes | Organic expansion demand |
| Champion recommended LeadReach to peer | CSM notes | Referral + expansion signal |

#### Step 2.3: Executive Business Review (CSM + AE, Day 60-90)

A formal EBR with the champion's leadership:

1. **Results presentation**: Value Realization Report + ROI quantification
2. **Peer benchmarking**: How the customer compares to industry peers
3. **Expansion opportunity**: Other teams/departments that could benefit
4. **Success roadmap**: What the next 90 days looks like
5. **Ask**: "Would it make sense to explore how [other team] could benefit from similar results?"

### Phase 3: Expand (Days 90-365)

#### Step 3.1: Expansion Signal Detection (Echo, Continuous)

Echo monitors for expansion signals across all customer accounts:

| Signal | Weight | Expansion Type |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| Seat utilization >85% | 20 | Seat expansion |
| Users in 2+ departments | 20 | Department expansion |
| Requests for multi-region features | 15 | Geography expansion |
| SSO/SAML request | 15 | Tier upgrade |
| API usage >80% of limit | 10 | Feature expansion |
| Executive engagement increase | 10 | Any expansion |
| Champion promoted/moved internally | 10 | Department expansion |

#### Step 3.2: Expansion Motion Execution (AE + Bard, Days 90+)

When an expansion signal is detected:

1. **AE briefs on expansion opportunity**: Sage provides company context; Forge provides usage data
2. **Champion outreach**: CSM asks champion for introduction to new department/team lead
3. **Bard composes expansion outreach**: Personalized to the new team's needs
4. **Discovery with new team**: Focus on their pain points (not a re-demo of the full product)
5. **Proposal**: Expansion pricing (often per-seat addendum to existing contract)
6. **Close and onboarding**: Quick — infrastructure is already in place

#### Step 3.3: Repeat the Cycle

Each expansion resets the clock on Phase 2: prove value to the new team, create advocates, expand again.

## 8. Objection Handling

| Objection | Root Cause | Response |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| "We don't have budget for expansion" | Budget constraint | "What if we structure it as an amendment to your current contract? Same per-seat rate, added to your next invoice. No new approval needed if it's within your department's budget." |
| "Other teams have different needs" | Perceived misfit | "That's exactly right — and LeadReach serves different use cases for different teams. [CS team uses it for renewals; Marketing uses it for ABM; Recruiting uses it for sourcing]. Want me to show you how?" |
| "We need to see more results first" | Value not proven enough | "Fair point. Let's schedule an EBR at the [60/90]-day mark with a full ROI analysis. If the numbers don't justify expansion, we'll pause. But if they do — and they usually do — we'll have the data to make it happen." |
| "Our IT team won't support another department on the platform" | IT resistance | "Good news — your IT team already approved LeadReach. Adding departments doesn't require a new security review. It's the same platform, same compliance, just more users." |
| "We're evaluating a company-wide tool instead" | Competitor / consolidation threat | "Company-wide is a great goal. LeadReach can serve multiple departments — and we can prove it with your team first. Want to compare what a multi-department LeadReach deployment looks like vs. [alternative]?" |
| "We can't justify the ROI for another team" | Unclear value for new team | "Let me prepare a specific ROI analysis for [department]'s use case, using [champion team]'s actual results as the baseline. Data first, decision second." |

## 10. Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

| Pitfall | Cause | Prevention |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| Landing too small (1-2 seats) with no expansion potential | Deal doesn't create enough internal visibility | Minimum land size: 5 seats; Atlas evaluates expansion potential before pricing |
| No value documentation | CSM doesn't track results; no data to justify expansion | Echo auto-generates Value Realization Reports; CSM presents at Day 30 |
| Forgetting the expansion motion after land | CSM/AE moves to next deal; customer left to self-serve | Flow agent enforces Day 30, 60, 90 expansion check-ins |
| Wrong champion (IC with no influence) | Champion can't sell internally | Judge agent scores champion influence during land deal |
| Expanding too fast (before value is proven) | AE pushes expansion before the land team has results | Minimum 30-day value realization before expansion motion |
| No cross-department visibility | Each team uses LeadReach independently | Shared analytics dashboard; company-wide benchmarks |
| Pricing becomes an expansion barrier | Per-seat price increases scare off expansion | Lock per-seat rate for 12 months; expansion at same rate |

## 12. Example Scenario

**Account**: Apex Solutions, 800-person SaaS company

**Land deal**: 15 SDR seats, Professional tier, $16,020/year (AE closes in 21 days)

**Expansion timeline**:

**Phase 1: Land (Month 1)**:
- 15 SDRs onboarded
- Day 7: First sequences sent
- Day 14: 10% reply rate achieved (up from 6% baseline)
- Day 30: Value Realization Report — 15 SDRs booking 25% more meetings

**Phase 2: Prove Value (Months 2-3)**:
- Day 45: CSM asks champion (SDR Manager): "Who else should see these results?" Champion introduces Marketing ABM team.
- Day 60: EBR with VP Sales. VP asks: "Can we use this for our EMEA team?"
- Day 75: Marketing ABM team (8 seats) signs on. Expansion: $8,544/year.
- Day 90: EMEA Sales team (12 seats) signs on. Expansion: $12,816/year.

**Phase 3: Expand (Months 4-12)**:
- Month 4: CS team explores LeadReach for proactive outreach. Scout identifies CS Director.
- Month 5: CS team (10 seats) signs on. Expansion: $10,680/year.
- Month 7: Seat utilization hits 88%. CSM initiates seat expansion for all teams.
- Month 8: 15 additional seats added across teams. Expansion: $16,020/year.
- Month 10: Enterprise upgrade (SSO, analytics, dedicated CSM). Tier upgrade: +40% per seat.
- Month 12: Total ACV: $89,244/year (5.6x the initial land deal).

**Outcome**: NRR of 560% in Year 1. Customer now has 60 seats across 4 departments on Enterprise tier. Next expansion: APAC team (estimated 20 additional seats).
