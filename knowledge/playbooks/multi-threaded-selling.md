# Multi-Threaded Selling — Playbook

This playbook is the canonical reference for the Flow agent's pipeline-management skills in consensus-driven enterprise buying. It covers stakeholder mapping, multi-thread entry points, sequence orchestration, and consensus-building tactics.

## 1. Why Multi-Threading Matters

Modern B2B buying is consensus-driven. Gartner research shows the average enterprise deal involves **6.8 decision-makers**, up from 5.4 in 2014. Deals with single-threaded engagement (one contact at the account) have a **30% close rate**; deals with 4+ contacts engaged have a **70%+ close rate**.

The single biggest predictor of deal slippage is single-threaded engagement. A champion leaves, gets fired, or loses budget — and the deal stalls. Multi-threading creates redundancy: if one contact disengages, others carry the deal forward.

## 2. The Stakeholder Map

Every enterprise opportunity should have a stakeholder map within 7 days of first meeting. The map identifies:

| Role | Definition | Example Title |
|------|------------|---------------|
| **Economic Buyer** | Has authority to sign the contract | CEO, CFO, CRO, VP (depending on ACV) |
| **Technical Buyer** | Evaluates technical fit | CTO, VP Engineering, Director of IT |
| **User Buyer** | Will use the product day-to-day | Director of Sales Ops, Sales Manager |
| **Champion** | Internal advocate; willing to sell on your behalf | Senior IC, manager-level |
| **Coach** | Provides information but doesn't advocate | Analyst, junior employee |
| **Blocker** | Actively or passively opposes the deal | IT Director worried about security, Procurement |
| **Influencer** | Trusted by Economic Buyer; opinion matters | Outside consultant, board member |

For each stakeholder, capture:

- **Name, title, role**
- **Reporting line** (who they report to)
- **Tenure** (how long in role)
- **Engagement level** (cold → warm → engaged → champion)
- **Sentiment** (negative → neutral → positive)
- **Key question/concern**
- **Next action**

## 3. Multi-Thread Entry Points

There are four common entry points to start multi-threading:

### Entry Point 1: Champion-Mediated Introduction

The champion introduces you to other stakeholders.

**Tactics:**
- "Who else should I meet to make sure this fits your team's needs?"
- "Can you intro me to [IT/Security] so we can start the security review in parallel?"
- "Can you intro me to [Finance] so we can align on procurement timelines?"

**Conversion rate**: 60–80% when champion is engaged.

### Entry Point 2: Top-Down from Economic Buyer

The Economic Buyer directs you to other stakeholders.

**Tactics:**
- "To make sure we're aligned with your team's workflow, who else should I involve?"
- "Who else will be involved in the evaluation?"

**Conversion rate**: 80%+ when EB is engaged.

### Entry Point 3: Cold Outbound to Other Stakeholders

You reach out directly to other stakeholders at the same account.

**Tactics:**
- LinkedIn connection with reference to the deal: "Working with [Champion] on [project] at [Company] — wanted to connect."
- Cold email referencing the deal: "[Champion] and I are discussing [use case] — quick question about [relevant topic to recipient]."

**Conversion rate**: 30–50% when champion is informed; 10–20% when champion is bypassed.

### Entry Point 4: Event-Based Touch

Multiple stakeholders attend the same event (webinar, conference, roundtable).

**Tactics:**
- Invite the champion to a roundtable; ask them to bring a peer.
- Host a private dinner; champion brings 1–2 colleagues.
- Conference booth: ask champion to introduce peers.

**Conversion rate**: 40–60% for events with right targeting.

## 4. Sequence Orchestration for Multi-Threaded Deals

For an enterprise opportunity with 4+ stakeholders, Flow orchestrates the following sequence:

| Day | Stakeholder | Channel | Content |
|-----|-------------|---------|---------|
| 0 | Champion | Email | Recap of meeting #1; meeting #2 agenda |
| 3 | Champion | Email | Intro to Technical Buyer requested |
| 5 | Technical Buyer | LinkedIn | Connection request, reference champion |
| 7 | Technical Buyer | Email | Technical brief; offer tech review session |
| 10 | Champion | Email | Recap of tech review; ask for intro to Economic Buyer |
| 14 | Economic Buyer | Email | Executive summary; ROI model; reference call invitation |
| 17 | Economic Buyer | LinkedIn | Connection request; engage with their content |
| 21 | User Buyer | Email | Pilot program offer; case study |
| 24 | Blocker (if any) | Email | Proactively address concern; offer call |
| 28 | Economic Buyer | Email | Pricing proposal; success criteria |
| 35 | All stakeholders | Email | Recap; mutual action plan |

## 5. Consensus-Building Tactics

### Tactic 1: Mutual Action Plan (MAP)

A shared document outlining the path to close. Includes:
- **Milestones** with dates
- **Owner** for each milestone (prospect or seller)
- **Required artifacts** (security review, legal review, ROI model, executive summary)
- **Decision criteria** and how they'll be evaluated

The MAP creates shared accountability and surfaces blockers early.

### Tactic 2: Reference Call

Connect the prospect to a peer reference. Effective when:
- Prospect is uncertain about outcomes
- Blocker is concerned about risk
- Economic Buyer needs social proof

Best practices:
- Match on industry, size, use case
- Brief the reference in advance
- Have the prospect drive the questions

### Tactic 3: Pilot Program

A time-boxed, success-criteria-defined pilot. Effective when:
- Technical Buyer needs proof
- User Buyer needs to feel ownership
- Economic Buyer needs ROI evidence

Pilot structure:
- 30–90 days
- Pre-agreed success criteria (e.g., "5 SDRs book 20+ meetings each in 30 days")
- Pre-agreed conversion criteria (e.g., "if success criteria met, convert to annual contract")
- Pricing: paid pilot (small fee, often credited against annual contract)

### Tactic 4: Executive Alignment Meeting

A meeting with Economic Buyer + Champion to align on:
- Business case
- Success criteria
- Procurement timeline
- Mutual action plan

Best held after Technical Buyer has signed off, before procurement.

### Tactic 5: ROI Model

A quantified business case. Includes:
- Current state costs (hard + soft)
- Future state savings
- Implementation cost
- Payback period
- 3-year NPV

The ROI model gives the Economic Buyer ammunition to defend the purchase internally.

## 6. Anti-Patterns

1. **Single-threaded too long**: Champion is engaged; you wait 60 days before involving others. Champion leaves; deal dies.
2. **Going over champion's head**: Cold-outreaching the EB without telling the champion. Champion feels betrayed; withdraws support.
3. **No stakeholder map**: You engage with whoever replies; miss the Blocker. Blocker kills the deal at the 11th hour.
4. **No MAP**: Each stakeholder has different expectations. Deal stalls in procurement.
5. **No ROI model**: Economic Buyer can't defend internally. Deal dies in budget cycle.
6. **Pilot with no success criteria**: Pilot runs forever; never converts.
7. **No reference call**: Prospect decides based on vendor pitch alone; loses to competitor who provided a reference.

## 7. Measurement Framework

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Stakeholders engaged per opp | 4+ | Strong predictor of close rate |
| Time to first multi-thread | <7 days | Avoids single-threaded deals stalling |
| Mutual Action Plan adoption | >70% | Surfaces blockers early |
| Reference call rate | >40% | Provides social proof |
| Pilot conversion rate | >60% | Pre-qualifies with success criteria |

## 8. Flow's Multi-Thread Orchestration Workflow

When Flow identifies an enterprise opportunity:

1. **Stakeholder identification** — read prospect context, identify likely roles.
2. **Champion engagement** — confirm champion's role and engagement level.
3. **Multi-thread request** — ask champion for introductions.
4. **Direct outreach** — for stakeholders champion won't introduce.
5. **Stakeholder map** — record all stakeholders, roles, engagement.
6. **Mutual Action Plan** — propose MAP at meeting #2.
7. **Reference call** — offer reference call when prospect shows hesitation.
8. **Pilot proposal** — offer pilot when technical evaluation is needed.
9. **Executive alignment** — schedule when technical sign-off is near.
10. **ROI model** — deliver before procurement.
