# Multi-Threaded Selling — Playbook

This playbook is the canonical reference for the Flow agent's pipeline-management skills in consensus-driven enterprise buying. It covers stakeholder mapping, multi-thread entry points, sequence orchestration, and consensus-building tactics.

## 1. Why Multi-Threading Matters

Modern B2B buying is consensus-driven. Gartner research shows the average enterprise deal involves **6.8 decision-makers**, up from 5.4 in 2014. Deals with single-threaded engagement (one contact at the account) have a **30% close rate**; deals with 4+ contacts engaged have a **70%+ close rate**.

The single biggest predictor of deal slippage is single-threaded engagement. A champion leaves, gets fired, or loses budget — and the deal stalls. Multi-threading creates redundancy: if one contact disengages, others carry the deal forward.

### 1.1 The Multi-Threading ROI Model

The business case for multi-threading is quantifiable:

| Metric | Single-Threaded | Multi-Threaded (4+ contacts) | Improvement |
|--------|-----------------|------------------------------|-------------|
| Close rate | 30% | 70%+ | +133% |
| Average deal cycle | 120 days | 90 days | -25% |
| Deal value (ACV) | $60K | $85K | +42% |
| Champion departure risk | Deal dies | Deal continues | Infinite |
| Forecast accuracy | ±40% | ±15% | +63% |

The mechanism: multi-threaded deals move faster because parallel conversations eliminate sequential bottlenecks (waiting for the champion to brief IT, then IT to brief procurement). They close larger because more stakeholders see value (expansion use cases emerge). They forecast better because multiple data points confirm deal progress.

### 1.2 When to Multi-Thread

Not every deal warrants multi-threading. The effort should match the deal size and complexity:

| Deal ACV | Minimum Contacts | Multi-Thread Level | Effort Investment |
|----------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| < $15K | 1–2 | Light (champion + 1) | Low |
| $15K–$50K | 2–3 | Standard (champion + 2) | Medium |
| $50K–$150K | 3–4 | Full (champion + 3) | High |
| > $150K | 4+ | Deep (all buying committee) | Very High |

## 2. The Stakeholder Map

Every enterprise opportunity should have a stakeholder map within 7 days of first meeting. The map identifies:

| Role | Definition | Example Title | Key Concern | Messaging Angle |
|------|------------|---------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Economic Buyer** | Has authority to sign the contract | CEO, CFO, CRO, VP (depending on ACV) | "Is the ROI justified?" | ROI model, payback period, competitive risk |
| **Technical Buyer** | Evaluates technical fit | CTO, VP Engineering, Director of IT | "Is this safe and compatible?" | Security docs, architecture review, integration plan |
| **User Buyer** | Will use the product day-to-day | Director of Sales Ops, Sales Manager | "Will this make my job easier?" | Workflow demo, training plan, pilot program |
| **Champion** | Internal advocate; willing to sell on your behalf | Senior IC, manager-level | "Can I look smart for recommending this?" | Arm with internal pitch deck, ROI data, peer references |
| **Coach** | Provides information but doesn't advocate | Analyst, junior employee | "Am I doing the right thing by sharing info?" | Keep informed; respect their risk; provide value |
| **Blocker** | Actively or passively opposes the deal | IT Director worried about security, Procurement | "What's the risk to me?" | Address concerns directly; offer pilot; provide references |
| **Influencer** | Trusted by Economic Buyer; opinion matters | Outside consultant, board member | "Does this align with my expertise/recommendations?" | Brief separately; provide thought leadership |

For each stakeholder, capture:

- **Name, title, role**
- **Reporting line** (who they report to)
- **Tenure** (how long in role)
- **Engagement level** (cold → warm → engaged → champion)
- **Sentiment** (negative → neutral → positive)
- **Key question/concern**
- **Next action**
- **Preferred communication channel** (email, phone, LinkedIn, in-person)
- **Personality type** (analytical, driver, amiable, expressive) — influences messaging style
- **Decision influence score** (1-5 scale: how much weight their opinion carries with the Economic Buyer)

### 2.2 How to Discover Stakeholders

Stakeholders aren't always obvious from the first meeting. Use these methods to build the complete map:

**Method 1: Ask the Champion**
- "Who else will be involved in this decision?"
- "Who else should I meet to make sure this fits your team's needs?"
- "Can you intro me to [IT/Security] so we can start the security review in parallel?"
- "Who else will need to sign off on this?"

**Method 2: Research (Forge + Sage)**
- LinkedIn org chart: check who reports to the Economic Buyer
- Company "About" page: lists C-suite and VPs
- SEC filings (public companies): executive compensation table lists top 5 officers
- Job postings: open roles reveal department structure
- Conference speaker lists: identify senior leaders who are externally visible
- Podcast/webinar appearances: thought leaders at the company

**Method 3: CRM/Email Signals**
- Who was cc'd on emails from the champion?
- Who attended the demo from the prospect side?
- Who signed the NDA?
- Who requested security documentation?

**Method 4: Direct Outreach (Bard)**
- Cold LinkedIn connection to likely stakeholders
- Cold email referencing the deal context
- Inviting additional stakeholders to meetings explicitly

## 3. Multi-Thread Entry Points

There are four common entry points to start multi-threading:

### Entry Point 1: Champion-Mediated Introduction

The champion introduces you to other stakeholders.

**Tactics:**
- "Who else should I meet to make sure this fits your team's needs?"
- "Can you intro me to [IT/Security] so we can start the security review in parallel?"
- "Can you intro me to [Finance] so we can align on procurement timelines?"
- "I'd love to get [VP Eng's] perspective on the technical fit — could you make an intro?"

**Conversion rate**: 60–80% when champion is engaged.

**Best practice**: Always give the champion a reason for the intro that makes them look good. Don't say "I need to sell to more people" — say "I want to make sure the technical team gets their questions answered early so nothing blocks your timeline."

### Entry Point 2: Top-Down from Economic Buyer

The Economic Buyer directs you to other stakeholders.

**Tactics:**
- "To make sure we're aligned with your team's workflow, who else should I involve?"
- "Who else will be involved in the evaluation?"
- "I'd like to brief your IT team on security — who's the right contact?"

**Conversion rate**: 80%+ when EB is engaged.

**Best practice**: The Economic Buyer's endorsement is the strongest possible introduction. Reference it explicitly: "[EB name] suggested I reach out to discuss..."

### Entry Point 3: Cold Outbound to Other Stakeholders

You reach out directly to other stakeholders at the same account.

**Tactics:**
- LinkedIn connection with reference to the deal: "Working with [Champion] on [project] at [Company] — wanted to connect."
- Cold email referencing the deal: "[Champion] and I are discussing [use case] — quick question about [relevant topic to recipient]."
- Comment on their LinkedIn content: Thoughtful comment that demonstrates expertise, then DM.

**Conversion rate**: 30–50% when champion is informed; 10–20% when champion is bypassed.

**Critical rule**: ALWAYS inform the champion before cold-outreaching other stakeholders. Going over the champion's head destroys trust. Frame it positively: "I noticed [Name] leads [relevant function] — would it be helpful if I reached out to them directly to [answer their technical questions / align on procurement]?"

### Entry Point 4: Event-Based Touch

Multiple stakeholders attend the same event (webinar, conference, roundtable).

**Tactics:**
- Invite the champion to a roundtable; ask them to bring a peer.
- Host a private dinner; champion brings 1–2 colleagues.
- Conference booth: ask champion to introduce peers.
- Webinar Q&A: multiple stakeholders from same company attend; follow up with each.
- Executive briefing: invite EB to a private session; they bring their team.

**Conversion rate**: 40–60% for events with right targeting.

**Best practice**: Events are the lowest-risk multi-threading approach because they don't require explicit introductions. The event serves as a natural context for connecting with multiple stakeholders.

### Entry Point Selection Decision Tree

```
Need to engage additional stakeholders?
├── Champion is strong (engaged + advocating)?
│   ├── Yes → Champion-mediated intro (Entry Point 1) — highest conversion
│   └── No → Is Economic Buyer engaged?
│       ├── Yes → Top-down intro (Entry Point 2)
│       └── No → Cold outbound (Entry Point 3) — inform champion first
├── Is there an upcoming event?
│   ├── Yes → Event-based touch (Entry Point 4) — lowest risk
│   └── No → Revert to Entry Point 1 or 3
└── No relationship yet at account?
    └── Start with champion discovery before multi-threading
```

## 4. Sequence Orchestration for Multi-Threaded Deals

For an enterprise opportunity with 4+ stakeholders, Flow orchestrates the following sequence:

| Day | Stakeholder | Channel | Content |
|-----|-------------|---------|---------|
| 0 | Champion | Email | Recap of meeting #1; meeting #2 agenda |
| 3 | Champion | Email | Intro to Technical Buyer requested |
| 5 | Technical Buyer | LinkedIn | Connection request, reference champion |
| 7 | Technical Buyer | Email | Technical brief; offer tech review session |
| 10 | Champion | Email | Recap of tech review; ask for intro to Economic Buyer |
| 14 | Economic Buyer | Email | Executive summary; ROI model; reference call invitation |
| 17 | Economic Buyer | LinkedIn | Connection request; engage with their content |
| 21 | User Buyer | Email | Pilot program offer; case study |
| 24 | Blocker (if any) | Email | Proactively address concern; offer call |
| 28 | Economic Buyer | Email | Pricing proposal; success criteria |
| 35 | All stakeholders | Email | Recap; mutual action plan |

### 4.1 Thread-Specific Messaging Matrix

Each thread requires messaging tailored to the stakeholder's role and concerns:

| Thread | Stakeholder | Primary Message | Proof Points | CTA |
|--------|------------|----------------|-------------|-----|
| Executive | Economic Buyer | Business outcomes, ROI, risk mitigation | ROI model, peer reference call, case study | "Review the business case together" |
| Technical | Technical Buyer | Integration, security, architecture | Security docs, architecture diagram, sandbox | "Technical review session" |
| User | User Buyer | Workflow improvement, productivity | Product demo, pilot program, training plan | "See it in action" |
| Champion | Champion | Arm for internal advocacy | Internal pitch deck, ROI calculator, peer story | "Forward this to [EB]" |
| Blocker | Blocker | Address specific concerns | Security audit, reference from similar org, trial | "Let me address your concerns" |

### 4.2 Thread Coordination Rules

When managing multiple threads simultaneously, Flow follows these rules:

1. **Never let threads contradict**: All stakeholders at the same account should hear consistent messaging, adapted for their role but not contradictory.
2. **Sync before major touchpoints**: Before sending pricing to the EB, ensure the Technical Buyer has signed off. Before offering a pilot, ensure the Champion has previewed it.
3. **Share intelligence across threads**: If the Technical Buyer raises a security concern, proactively address it with the Champion before it becomes a blocker.
4. **Sequence by influence**: Engage stakeholders in order of their influence on the decision. Champion → User Buyer → Technical Buyer → Economic Buyer → Blocker → Procurement.
5. **Calendar alignment**: When possible, schedule stakeholder meetings in sequence (Champion briefs TB on Monday; TB meets you on Tuesday; TB briefs EB on Wednesday).

### 4.3 Thread Health Monitoring

Flow agent monitors the health of each thread and flags issues:

| Signal | Green | Yellow | Red |
|--------|-------|--------|-----|
| Response time | <24 hours | 24–72 hours | >72 hours |
| Meeting attendance | Attends all | Reschedules once | No-shows or cancels |
| Engagement depth | Asks questions, shares context | Brief replies | One-word replies |
| Proactivity | Introduces you to others | Responds when asked | Doesn't respond |
| Sentiment | Positive language | Neutral | Negative or skeptical |
| Internal advocacy | Forwards your content | Acknowledges value | Questions value |

## 5. Consensus-Building Tactics

### Tactic 1: Mutual Action Plan (MAP)

A shared document outlining the path to close. Includes:
- **Milestones** with dates
- **Owner** for each milestone (prospect or seller)
- **Required artifacts** (security review, legal review, ROI model, executive summary)
- **Decision criteria** and how they'll be evaluated

The MAP creates shared accountability and surfaces blockers early.

#### MAP Template

```
MUTUAL ACTION PLAN — [Company] × LeadReach
============================================
Opportunity: [Deal Name]
ACV: $[X]
Target Close: [Date]

MILESTONES:
┌────┬───────────────────────────────┬────────────┬───────────┬──────────┐
│ #  │ Milestone                      │ Owner      │ Target    │ Status   │
├────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────┼───────────┼──────────┤
│ 1  │ Technical review complete      │ Tech Buyer │ [Date]    │ Complete │
│ 2  │ Security questionnaire done    │ IT Director│ [Date]    │ In Prog  │
│ 3  │ Reference call with [peer]     │ Champion   │ [Date]    │ Scheduled│
│ 4  │ Pilot program launched         │ User Buyer │ [Date]    │ Pending  │
│ 5  │ ROI model reviewed by CFO      │ Econ Buyer │ [Date]    │ Not Start│
│ 6  │ Legal / procurement review     │ Procurement│ [Date]    │ Not Start│
│ 7  │ Contract signed                │ Econ Buyer │ [Date]    │ Pending  │
└────┴───────────────────────────────┴────────────┴───────────┴──────────┘

DECISION CRITERIA:
1. [Criterion 1 — how it will be evaluated]
2. [Criterion 2 — how it will be evaluated]
3. [Criterion 3 — how it will be evaluated]

NEXT STEPS (this week):
- [Action 1] — [Owner] — by [Date]
- [Action 2] — [Owner] — by [Date]
```

#### MAP Best Practices

- **Propose the MAP at meeting #2** — early enough to set expectations, late enough that the champion is engaged.
- **Share via collaborative tool** (Google Doc, Notion, Recapped) — not a PDF. The prospect should be able to edit.
- **Review at every meeting** — "Let's check the MAP. Are we on track? Any blockers?"
- **Flag delays immediately** — if a milestone slips, communicate the impact on the close date.
- **Get Economic Buyer buy-in** — the MAP should be reviewed with the EB at least once before procurement.

### Tactic 2: Reference Call

Connect the prospect to a peer reference. Effective when:
- Prospect is uncertain about outcomes
- Blocker is concerned about risk
- Economic Buyer needs social proof

Best practices:
- Match on industry, size, use case
- Brief the reference in advance
- Have the prospect drive the questions
- Record the call (with permission) for internal sharing

#### Reference Call Matching Matrix

| Prospect Attribute | Reference Match Priority | Why |
|-------------------|------------------------|-----|
| Industry / vertical | ★★★★★ | Same industry = same pain |
| Company size (FTE) | ★★★★☆ | Similar size = similar complexity |
| Use case | ★★★★★ | Same use case = same outcomes |
| Geography | ★★★☆☆ | Same region = same compliance context |
| Tech stack | ★★★★☆ | Same stack = same integration experience |
| Competitor displaced | ★★★★★ | Displaced same competitor = highest credibility |

#### Reference Call Script Template

**Pre-brief with reference (5 min):**
> "Thanks for doing this. Quick context: [Prospect] is evaluating LeadReach for [use case]. They're at [stage in process]. Their main concern is [concern]. Would be great if you could speak to that specifically. Let them drive the questions — you're the expert on your experience, not the product."

**Intro on the call:**
> "[Prospect], [Reference] is [title] at [company]. They were in a similar spot [X months ago] and have been using LeadReach since. I'll let you two connect — I'm here if you have product questions, but this is your conversation."

**Post-call follow-up with prospect:**
> "Great conversation with [Reference]. Any other questions that came up? Here's the [relevant case study / ROI data] that addresses [concern raised]."

### Tactic 3: Pilot Program

A time-boxed, success-criteria-defined pilot. Effective when:
- Technical Buyer needs proof
- User Buyer needs to feel ownership
- Economic Buyer needs ROI evidence

Pilot structure:
- 30–90 days
- Pre-agreed success criteria (e.g., "5 SDRs book 20+ meetings each in 30 days")
- Pre-agreed conversion criteria (e.g., "if success criteria met, convert to annual contract")
- Pricing: paid pilot (small fee, often credited against annual contract)

#### Pilot Design Framework

| Pilot Parameter | Recommendation | Rationale |
|----------------|---------------|-----------|
| Duration | 30 days (standard) or 60 days (enterprise) | Long enough to see results; short enough to maintain urgency |
| Scope | 3–5 users (representative sample) | Enough for meaningful data; small enough for focused support |
| Success criteria | 2–3 measurable outcomes | Must be specific, measurable, and pre-agreed |
| Pricing | 50% of monthly rate; credited against annual | Skin in the game; removes "free" perception |
- Support | Dedicated CSM + weekly check-ins | High-touch ensures pilot success |
- Conversion trigger | Auto-convert if success criteria met | Removes ambiguity; creates momentum |
| Exit criteria | Either party can end with 5 days notice | Low risk for prospect; demonstrates confidence |

#### Pilot Success Criteria Templates

**SDR Productivity Pilot:**
- Criterion 1: SDRs using LeadReach book 30%+ more meetings than baseline
- Criterion 2: Research time per account reduced by 50%+
- Criterion 3: New SDR ramp time reduced by 30%+

**Pipeline Quality Pilot:**
- Criterion 1: ICP fit score of generated leads improves by 25%+ vs. current method
- Criterion 2: Lead-to-meeting conversion rate improves by 20%+
- Criterion 3: Pipeline coverage ratio increases from [current] to 3x+

### Tactic 5: ROI Model

A quantified business case. Includes:
- Current state costs (hard + soft)
- Future state savings
- Implementation cost
- Payback period
- 3-year NPV

The ROI model gives the Economic Buyer ammunition to defend the purchase internally.

#### ROI Model Framework

| Component | Description | Data Source |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Current state costs** | Hard costs (current tooling, manual processes) + soft costs (SDR time on research, missed pipeline) | Prospect interview + industry benchmarks |
| **Future state savings** | Reduced research time, higher SDR output, faster ramp, better pipeline quality | Case study data + pilot results |
| **Implementation cost** | Onboarding, training, integration effort | LeadReach implementation guide |
| **Ongoing cost** | Annual subscription + any add-ons | Pricing proposal |
| **Net benefit** | Future state savings - (implementation + ongoing cost) | Calculation |
| **Payback period** | Months to recoup investment | Calculation |
| **3-year NPV** | Net present value of 3-year benefits minus costs | Calculation (10% discount rate) |

## 6. Anti-Patterns

1. **Single-threaded too long**: Champion is engaged; you wait 60 days before involving others. Champion leaves; deal dies.
2. **Going over champion's head**: Cold-outreaching the EB without telling the champion. Champion feels betrayed; withdraws support.
3. **No stakeholder map**: You engage with whoever replies; miss the Blocker. Blocker kills the deal at the 11th hour.
4. **No MAP**: Each stakeholder has different expectations. Deal stalls in procurement.
5. **No ROI model**: Economic Buyer can't defend internally. Deal dies in budget cycle.
6. **Pilot with no success criteria**: Pilot runs forever; never converts.
7. **No reference call**: Prospect decides based on vendor pitch alone; loses to competitor who provided a reference.
8. **Treating all stakeholders the same**: Sending the same deck to the CRO and the IT Director. Neither feels addressed.
9. **Ignoring the Blocker**: Hoping the Blocker will go away. They won't. They'll surface at the worst possible time.
10. **Over-messaging**: Contacting 6 stakeholders simultaneously with the same message. Feels like spam, not consultation.
11. **Skipping the champion**: Direct outreach to multiple stakeholders before the champion is engaged. No internal advocate to coordinate.
12. **No thread sync**: Different stakeholders hear different things. The deal loses credibility.

### Anti-Pattern Detection by Flow Agent

Flow agent monitors for these anti-patterns and alerts the AE:

| Anti-Pattern | Detection Signal | Alert Level | Recommended Fix |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Single-threaded >14 days | Only 1 contact at account with activity | Red | Request champion intro to 2nd stakeholder |
| Champion bypassed | AE contacted EB without champion CC | Yellow | Inform champion immediately; apologize if needed |
| No stakeholder map | No map in CRM after 7 days | Yellow | Create map; schedule mapping session with champion |
| No MAP | No mutual action plan after 14 days | Yellow | Propose MAP at next meeting |
| No ROI model | No ROI document shared with EB | Yellow | Build and deliver ROI model before procurement |
| Pilot with no criteria | Pilot started without success metrics | Red | Pause pilot; define criteria; restart with agreement |
| Blocker unaddressed | Known blocker not contacted in 14 days | Red | Proactive outreach to address blocker's concern |
| Thread contradiction | Different stakeholders told different things | Red | Sync messaging; brief champion on consistent narrative |

## 8. Flow's Multi-Thread Orchestration Workflow

When Flow identifies an enterprise opportunity:

1. **Stakeholder identification** — read prospect context, identify likely roles (Forge + Sage research).
2. **Champion engagement** — confirm champion's role and engagement level.
3. **Multi-thread request** — ask champion for introductions (Bard drafts the ask).
4. **Direct outreach** — for stakeholders champion won't introduce (Bard composes tailored outreach).
5. **Stakeholder map** — record all stakeholders, roles, engagement in CRM.
6. **Mutual Action Plan** — propose MAP at meeting #2 (Bard drafts; AE presents).
7. **Reference call** — offer reference call when prospect shows hesitation (Sage matches best reference).
8. **Pilot proposal** — offer pilot when technical evaluation is needed (Bard drafts proposal).
9. **Executive alignment** — schedule when technical sign-off is near.
10. **ROI model** — deliver before procurement (Sage provides benchmarks; Bard produces document).
11. **Thread health monitoring** — Flow continuously tracks engagement across all threads.
12. **Blocker intervention** — proactive outreach when a blocker is identified.

## 9. Objection Handling for Multi-Threading

### Champion's Objection: "I don't want you talking to my boss"
> "Totally understand — your boss's time is valuable. Here's what I'm thinking: I'll put together a one-page executive summary that you can forward. That way, it comes from you, and you control the narrative. Want me to draft that?"

### Technical Buyer's Objection: "We haven't asked for this evaluation"
> "You're right — this came from [Champion]'s initiative. I'm reaching out proactively because technical fit is critical for success, and I'd rather answer your questions now than have them become blockers later. 15 minutes — technical questions only?"

### Economic Buyer's Objection: "My team hasn't brought this to me"
> "They're evaluating it now — [Champion] can share the details. I'm reaching out because at this ACV level, I want to make sure we're aligned on business outcomes from the start. 15 minutes to review the business case?"

### Blocker's Objection: "We don't need another tool"
> "I hear that concern. Can I ask — is it the tool itself, or the integration/maintenance burden? Because we've specifically designed LeadReach to integrate with [their existing stack] with zero additional maintenance. Worth 15 minutes to see if that changes your assessment?"

## 10. Advanced Multi-Threading Tactics

### 10.1 The "Executive Sponsor" Play

Assign an executive on your side to mirror the Economic Buyer:

- Your CRO ↔ Their CRO
- Your VP Eng ↔ Their VP Eng
- Your CEO ↔ Their CEO

Executive-to-executive alignment creates peer-level trust that no AE can replicate. Use sparingly — only for Tier 1 deals.

### 10.2 The "Champion Armament" Play

Equip your champion to sell internally on your behalf:

1. **Internal pitch deck**: A 5-slide deck the champion can present to the EB without you present.
2. **ROI calculator**: A spreadsheet the champion can customize with their own numbers.
3. **Peer story**: A written case study the champion can forward.
4. **FAQ document**: Answers to the most common internal objections.
5. **Competitor comparison**: Honest comparison that the champion can use in evaluation meetings.

### 10.3 The "Parallel Procurement" Play

Start procurement activities in parallel with technical evaluation:

- Send security questionnaire proactively (don't wait for IT to ask)
- Provide standard DPA/MSA templates (redline-friendly)
- Offer a pre-built integration architecture document
- Connect procurement teams early

This compresses the deal cycle by 30+ days by eliminating the typical "evaluate → procurement → legal" sequential bottleneck.
