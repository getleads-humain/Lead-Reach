# Email Sequence Templates — Master Reference

## 1. Overview

This file provides the canonical library of email sequence templates used by the LeadReach outreach pipeline. Every email generated by Forge (outreach composer) or Bard (content refiner) should draw from these templates as a baseline, then apply personalization logic from the personalization frameworks in §7.

**Template Architecture:**

| Component | Purpose | Owner Agent |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| Base template | Structural skeleton + copy | Forge |
| Personalization layer | Merge fields + dynamic inserts | Forge + Scout data |
| A/B variant | Alternative copy for testing | Bard |
| Compliance check | CAN-SPAM / GDPR / opt-out | Judge |
| Send orchestration | Timing + throttling | Flow |

## 2. Cold Email Sequence Templates

### 2.1 First Touch — The "Insight Lead" Pattern

The first touch must establish credibility within 3 seconds of the subject line being read. Never sell; always lead with a provable insight about the prospect's world.

**Template: `cold_first_touch_insight_lead`**

```
Subject: {{subject_line}}

Hi {{first_name}},

{{personalized_insight_opening}}

{{specific_observation_about_company}}

{{bridge_to_value_proposition}}

{{soft_cta}}

Best,
{{sender_name}}
{{sender_title}}, {{sender_company}}
```

**Filled Example (SaaS):**

```
Subject: Noticed {{company}}'s expansion into EMEA — quick thought

Hi Sarah,

I saw that Podium.io just opened your London office and tripled the sales
team in Q4. Scaling that fast usually means the CRM data quality takes a
hit — we've seen it happen at 4 of the last 5 SaaS companies that grew
50%+ YoY.

At LeadReach, we help high-growth SaaS teams keep their pipeline data
clean and actionable even as the rep count doubles. Would it be useful
if I shared a quick benchmark on what pipeline accuracy looks like at
companies scaling at your pace?

Best,
Alex Chen
VP Growth, LeadReach
```

### 2.2 First Touch — The "Trigger Event" Pattern

Use when a real-time trigger event is detected (funding, leadership change, product launch, acquisition, job change).

**Template: `cold_first_touch_trigger_event`**

```
Subject: {{subject_line}}

Hi {{first_name}},

Congratulations on {{trigger_event_description}}.

{{why_this_matters_to_them}}

{{how_we_help_at_this_stage}}

{{soft_cta}}

Best,
{{sender_name}}
```

**Trigger Event Subject Lines:**

| Trigger Type | Subject Formula | Example |
|-------------|----------------|---------|
| Funding round | "Congrats on the {{round_type}} — {{relevant_implication}}" | "Congrats on the Series C — scaling the SDR team?" |
| New hire | "Welcome to {{company}}, {{first_name}}" | "Welcome to Acme Corp, Sarah" |
| Product launch | "{{product_name}} launch — {{reaction}}" | "Saw the new analytics launch — impressive" |
| Acquisition | "{{acquirer}} + {{company}} — {{implication}}" | "Salesforce + your team — integration headaches?" |
| Job change | "New role at {{company}}? {{relevant_question}}" | "New VP role at Stripe? Quick question" |
| Expansion | "{{company}} expanding into {{region}}?" | "Acme expanding into APAC?" |

### 2.3 First Touch — The "Peer Proof" Pattern

Open with proof that their peers (same title, same industry) have solved the problem you're about to position.

**Template: `cold_first_touch_peer_proof`**

```
Subject: {{subject_line}}

Hi {{first_name}},

{{peer_company_1}} and {{peer_company_2}} — both {{industry}} companies
around {{company}}'s stage — recently solved {{pain_point}} by
{{approach}}.

{{implication_for_prospect}}

{{brief_value_statement}}

{{soft_cta}}

Best,
{{sender_name}}
```

### 2.4 Follow-Up #1 — The "Value Add" (Day 3–4)

Never just "bumping this up." Every follow-up must deliver standalone value.

**Template: `cold_followup_value_add`**

```
Subject: Re: {{original_subject}}

Hi {{first_name}},

{{reference_previous_email_briefly}}

{{deliver_tangible_value — resource, data point, insight, benchmark}}

{{soft_cta_different_from_first}}

Best,
{{sender_name}}
```

**Value Add Options:**

| Value Type | Template Snippet |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Benchmark data | "I pulled the benchmark: {{industry}} companies at {{revenue_range}} typically see {{metric}} of {{number}}. Thought you'd find it useful regardless of next steps." |
| Framework | "I put together a 1-page framework for {{topic}} that {{peer_company}} used. Happy to share." |
| Case study teaser | "{{peer_company}} went from {{before_state}} to {{after_state}} in {{timeframe}}. The key shift was {{key_insight}}." |
| Checklist | "I created a {{checklist_name}} checklist — covers the top 7 things to evaluate when {{scenario}}. Want me to send it over?" |
| Tool comparison | "I recently compared {{tool_category}} options for a client in {{industry}}. Happy to share the breakdown if useful." |

### 2.5 Follow-Up #2 — The "Question Breakup" (Day 7–9)

A brief email that poses a genuine question and gives permission to close the loop.

**Template: `cold_followup_question_breakup`**

```
Subject: Quick question re: {{topic}}

Hi {{first_name}},

{{genuinely_curious_question_related_to_their_role_or_challenge}}

If {{problem_area}} isn't a priority right now, totally understand — I'll
close out my notes on my end.

Best,
{{sender_name}}
```

**Question Bank:**

| Persona | Question |
|---------|----------|
| CRO | "Is hitting the Q3 number dependent on improving pipeline conversion, or is the top-of-funnel the bigger lever?" |
| VP Sales | "Are your reps spending more time on data entry than selling, or is that not the bottleneck right now?" |
| CTO | "Is your engineering team feeling the pain of {{specific_tech_debt}}, or is that further down the roadmap?" |
| VP Marketing | "Has the CAC creep made you rethink the demand gen mix, or is it more about conversion optimization?" |
| Director of Ops | "Is the {{process}} still running on spreadsheets, or have you found a system that works?" |
| CFO | "Are you tracking {{metric}} at the granularity the board wants, or is that still a quarterly roll-up?" |

### 2.6 The Breakup Email (Day 12–14)

The final email in the cold sequence. Paradoxically, breakup emails have the highest reply rate of any touch in a sequence.

**Template: `cold_breakup_email`**

```
Subject: Closing the loop

Hi {{first_name}},

I've reached out a few times about {{value_topic}} and I don't want to
be a pest. I'll assume this isn't a priority for {{company}} right now.

If things change, I'm at {{sender_email}}. Wishing you and the team a
strong {{quarter}}.

Best,
{{sender_name}}
```

**Breakup Variants:**

| Variant | Twist |
|---------|-------|
| Standard close | "I'll close out my notes." |
| Defer to colleague | "If there's someone else on the team I should connect with, let me know." |
| Future timing | "I'll check back in {{timeframe}} — unless you'd prefer I don't." |
| Resource drop | "I'll leave this here in case it's useful down the road: {{resource_link}}" |

## 7. Personalization Frameworks

### 7.1 The 5-Level Personalization Model

Every email should aim for Level 3+ personalization. Level 1–2 emails have significantly lower reply rates.

| Level | Name | Description | Example | Data Source |
|-------|------|-------------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | Merge tag | First name + company name | "Hi Sarah, I noticed Acme Corp..." | CRM |
| 2 | Firmographic | Company size, industry, location | "As a Series B fintech..." | Scout firmographic data |
| 3 | Trigger-based | Recent event or change | "Congrats on the Series C..." | Scout trigger events |
| 4 | Pain-inferred | Inferred from role + context | "Scaling the SDR team usually means..." | ICP + role mapping |
| 5 | Deep research | Specific insight about the person/company | "I read your blog post on X and noticed you mentioned Y..." | Scout deep research |

## 8. A/B Testing Variants

### 8.1 Subject Line A/B Testing Framework

**Testing Protocol:**

| Parameter | Guideline |
|-----------|-----------|
| Minimum sample size | 500 impressions per variant |
| Statistical significance | 95% confidence (p < 0.05) |
| Test duration | 7–14 days |
| Concurrent tests | Max 1 per sequence step |
| Primary metric | Open rate (for subject lines) |
| Secondary metric | Reply rate (for body variants) |

**Subject Line Variant Pairs:**

| Test Name | Variant A | Variant B | Hypothesis |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Question vs Statement | "Is {{pain_point}} still a priority?" | "{{company}} + {{peer}} approach to {{topic}}" | Questions drive curiosity; statements drive credibility |
| Short vs Long | "Quick question" | "{{specific_insight_about_company}}" | Short creates mystery; long proves research |
| Benefit vs Curiosity | "Reduce {{metric}} by {{percent}}" | "Something interesting about {{company}}'s {{area}}" | Benefit is direct; curiosity drives opens |
| First name vs No first name | "{{first_name}}, quick thought" | "Thought about {{company}}" | Personalization vs company focus |
| Time-bound vs Open | "{{topic}} before {{date}}" | "Worth exploring {{topic}}" | Urgency vs low pressure |

### 8.2 Body Copy A/B Testing Variants

| Element | Variant A | Variant B | When to Test |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| CTA type | "Open to a quick call?" | "Mind if I send over {{resource}}?" | Testing commitment level |
| Email length | 3 paragraphs (standard) | 1 paragraph (ultra-short) | Testing attention threshold |
| Proof placement | After pain statement | Before pain statement | Testing authority-first vs empathy-first |
| Social proof | Named peer company | Statistical benchmark | Testing specificity vs scale |
| Tone | Professional / consultative | Casual / conversational | Testing persona alignment |
| P.S. line | With P.S. (urgency/reinforcement) | Without P.S. | Testing recency effect |

### 8.3 CTA A/B Testing Matrix

| CTA Category | Low Commitment | Medium Commitment | High Commitment |
|-------------|----------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Question | "Is this worth exploring?" | "Open to a 15-min call?" | "Can I put 30 min on your calendar?" |
| Resource | "Want me to send a case study?" | "Want me to send a custom analysis?" | "Want me to build a proof-of-concept?" |
| Referral | "Know anyone who'd find this useful?" | "Would your colleague {{name}} find this relevant?" | "Can you intro me to {{target_name}}?" |
| Validation | "Does this resonate?" | "Am I off base here?" | "Is this a top-3 priority for Q{{quarter}}?" |

## 9. Industry-Specific Variants

### 9.1 SaaS Industry

**Key Pain Points to Reference:**

| Persona | Pain Point | Angle |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| CRO | Inefficient pipeline, low conversion rates | Pipeline velocity & forecast accuracy |
| VP Sales | Rep ramp time, inconsistent messaging | SDR productivity & playbook standardization |
| CTO | Tech stack fragmentation, integration debt | API-first architecture, native integrations |
| VP CS | Churn prediction, expansion revenue | Health scoring & proactive outreach |

**SaaS-Specific First Touch:**

```
Subject: {{company}}'s {{metric}} — benchmark data inside

Hi {{first_name}},

I pulled the latest benchmark for {{company_subtype}} SaaS companies
at {{arr_range}}: the median {{metric}} is {{benchmark_number}}.

{{if_company_underperforms}}: Based on your public data, {{company}}
appears to be around {{estimated_number}} — which puts you in the
{{percentile}} percentile.

{{if_company_outperforms}}: You're outperforming the median — impressive.

Either way, we've helped {{peer_company_count}} SaaS companies in your
range improve {{metric}} by {{improvement_percent}}. The biggest lever
is usually {{key_lever}}.

Want me to share the full benchmark report?

Best,
{{sender_name}}
```

**SaaS Subject Line Formulas:**

- `{{company}}'s {{saas_metric}} vs. the benchmark`
- `How {{peer_saas_company}} improved {{metric}} by {{percent}}`
- `Scaling from {{arr_start}} to {{arr_end}} ARR — the data`
- `Your {{stage}} SaaS playbook is probably missing this`
- `The {{number}} SaaS companies winning at {{topic}}`

### 10.2 Subject Line Rules

1. **Never use ALL CAPS** in subject lines (spam filter trigger)
2. **Never use exclamation marks** in cold emails (reduces deliverability)
3. **Keep under 50 characters** when possible (mobile optimization)
4. **Avoid trigger words**: free, guarantee, opportunity, urgent, act now, limited time
5. **Include company name or first name** in at least 60% of sends
6. **A/B test every new subject line** before scaling
7. **Track open rates by subject line formula** and retire underperformers quarterly

## 11. CTAs That Convert

### 11.1 CTA Hierarchy

| Commitment Level | CTA Type | Example | Best Used In |
|-----------------|----------|---------|-------------|
| Zero commitment | Permission to share | "Mind if I send over {{resource}}?" | First touch |
| Low commitment | Interest check | "Worth exploring?" | First touch, follow-up |
| Medium commitment | Time-specific meeting | "Open to 15 min Thursday?" | Follow-up, warm intro |
| High commitment | Custom deliverable | "Want me to build a custom analysis?" | Post-demo, late sequence |
| Referral | Network ask | "Know anyone dealing with {{pain}}?" | Breakup, re-engagement |

### 11.2 CTA Rules

1. **One CTA per email** — never give multiple options
2. **End with a question** — questions get replies; statements get ignored
3. **Make it easy to say yes** — "Worth a chat?" > "I'd like to schedule a demo"
4. **Avoid meeting-centric CTAs in first touch** — reduces reply rate by 40%
5. **Use "if/then" framing** — "If this is relevant, I can share X. If not, no worries."
6. **Make the next step clear and small** — "Reply with a yes and I'll send X"

### 11.3 CTA Templates by Sequence Position

| Position | CTA Template | Rationale |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| First touch | "Would it be useful if I shared {{resource}}?" | Zero pressure, earns permission |
| Follow-up 1 | "Open to a quick 15-minute call?" | Escalates commitment slightly |
| Follow-up 2 | "If {{topic}} isn't a priority, totally understand." | Gives permission to opt out |
| Breakup | "I'll close out my notes." | Paradoxical — drives replies |
| Warm intro | "Open to connecting?" | Social proof lowers resistance |
| Re-engagement | "Worth revisiting?" | Low commitment re-entry |
